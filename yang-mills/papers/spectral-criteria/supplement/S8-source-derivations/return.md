# Return to a prescribed coarse plaquette gate in four-dimensional Wilson theory

Take the isotropic pure SU(2) Wilson measure on an even spatial torus of side $N\ge6$, using the action weight $\exp(\beta\sum_p\Re U_p)$, where $\Re U_p=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}U_p$ and $\beta\ge0$. Use the time vacuum at fixed spatial regulator. On a slice let $A_\theta$ be the event that the straight spacing-two holonomy around a specified coarse spatial plaquette lies within round distance $\theta$ of $-I$, $0\le\theta\le\pi$. This is invariant under the vertex gauge group. For the stationary, vacuum-normalized physical transfer $S_\tau$, at any positive integer lattice lag, multiplication $R=1_{A_\theta}$ satisfies
$$\|R S_\tau R\|\le p_{6\beta}(\theta),\qquad
p_k(\theta)=\frac{\int_0^\theta\sin^2 r\,e^{k(\cos r-1)}\,dr}{\int_0^\pi\sin^2 r\,e^{k(\cos r-1)}\,dr}.$$
The result holds on the full gauge-invariant slice space. Multiplication by a general event need not preserve the centered subspace. A fine projection supported there does, as used in the [inheritance argument](inheritance.md). [Wilson one-link sampling, Creutz](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.21.2308) · [Physical transfer, Lüscher](https://bib-pubdb1.desy.de/record/396349)

**Conditional probability at the later slice.** Freeze every link except one of the eight distinct fine links in the later coarse plaquette. The earlier spatial slice is among the frozen data. The one-link conditional law relative to unit Haar measure has density $\exp(k\,u\cdot v)/Z(k)$ on the unit three-sphere, with $v$ a unit vector and $0\le k\le6\beta$ from the six unit staples. For $k=0$ the direction is immaterial. Here $Z(k)=\int e^{k u\cdot v}\,du$. Left and right multiplication by the fixed parts of the loop and inversion are isometries. Consequently the allowed values of that link form a spherical cap of radius $\theta$. Its Haar probability is $(\theta-\sin\theta\cos\theta)/\pi$.

At fixed tilt, a cap of given radius has greatest probability when centered at $v$. Indeed each superlevel set of the density is a cap centered there. Its intersection with the test cap has measure at most the smaller cap's measure, achieved for concentric caps. Integrate the superlevel sets. For the aligned cap the derivative with respect to $k$ is the covariance of $\mathbf1_{\{\cos r\ge\cos\theta\}}$ with $\cos r$, which is nonnegative. This proves the claimed conditional bound for each exterior. Averaging it holds given any earlier slice. The aligned envelope is optimal among unconstrained one-link tilts with $k\le6\beta$. Attainability by entire Wilson backgrounds is not needed.

To see the operator implication, let $\nu(dx,dy)$ be the stationary reversible two-slice law. On $A_\theta\times A_\theta$, use $2|g(x)g(y)|\le |g(x)|^2+|g(y)|^2$ and exchange endpoints. The return bound gives $|\langle g,RS_\tau Rg\rangle|\le p_{6\beta}(\theta)\|Rg\|^2$. At finite periodic time the row estimate requires the slices to be distinct modulo the period. At fixed $N,\beta$ it passes to the time vacuum by the finite-slab convergence of the Wilson transfer, first for bounded tests, then by truncation. Equivalently the bulk one-link Wilson conditional law holds on any finite interior slab of the vacuum with exterior boundary weights. [Vacuum passage](../full-complement/wilson.md)

**A volume-independent envelope.** The one-link density is at most $e^k/Z(k)$. On $0\le r\le(1+k)^{-1/2}$ the bounds $\sin r\ge 2r/\pi$ and $k(\cos r-1)\ge-1/2$ give
$$e^{-k} Z(k)\ge\frac{8}{3\pi^3\sqrt e}(1+k)^{-3/2}.$$
Using $\sin r\le r$ for the cap numerator gives
$$p_{6\beta}(\theta)\le\min\{1,C(1+6\beta)^{3/2}\theta^3\},\qquad C=\pi^2\sqrt e/4.$$
For $A$ contained in the union of $M$ prescribed plaquette gates of this radius, the same argument gives $\|1_A S_\tau 1_A\|\le\min(1,M p_{6\beta}(\theta))$. No independence between faces is assumed. At the side-eight study's bare coupling $\beta=3.399810242$, the one-link envelope for a single marked plaquette is 0.003072388 for $\theta=0.05$, 0.156180437 for $\theta=0.2$, and 0.934803630 for $\theta=0.6$. These are evaluations of the upper bound in radians. They are not estimates of a measured mass or of a four-dimensional return probability. [Quadrature](checks.py)

For a dyadic sequence of sides, take all $M=3(N/2)^3$ coarse spatial plaquettes and $\theta=c(1+6\beta)^{-1/2}N^{-4/3}$, with $c>0$ chosen so $\theta\le\pi$. The union then costs at most $(3C/8)c^3/N$ at each step, summable over refinements. A normalized observable confined to these gates has physical loss at least one minus this cost. This remains valid if the local updates barely move that observable. Proving adequate mixing on the complement of such narrow gates is a separate interacting problem.
