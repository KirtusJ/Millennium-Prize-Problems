# A compact SU(2) failure test for uniform conditional mixing

A fixed four-face Wilson disk already separates Gaussian mixing from compact mixing on an atypical coarse fibre. With the local updates defined below, the best full-complement constant on this disk tends to zero as $\beta\to\infty$. The exhibited quotient is asymptotic to $5/(3\sqrt2\,\beta)$. The four-dimensional isotropic Wilson inequality uses a different lattice, measure and update family. Moreover deterioration of order $1/\beta$ need not violate the time-spacing summability criterion.

Take an open strip of four squares. Gauge a spanning tree consisting of the lower row and the verticals to the identity. Orient the remaining upper links consecutively, calling them $g_1,\ldots,g_4$. Its face half-traces are $\Re g_i$ and its outer boundary product is $W=g_1g_2g_3g_4$. Add a separate single face sharing the root vertex, with holonomy $B$. Haar invariance under tree gauge gives five independent Haar variables before weighting by $\exp[\beta(\Re B+\sum_i\Re g_i)]$. The remaining gauge action conjugates all variables simultaneously. The coarse orbit of $(W,B)$ is observed. Give equal weights to the three heat baths on adjacent pairs $(1,2),(2,3),(3,4)$, freezing the other two variables, $B$ and the ordered product of the chosen pair. These moves preserve the coarse data. The test $f=\Re(Bg_1)$ is a gauge-invariant closed path observable. The second coarse face supplies an observable relative colour direction. [Related planar face-coordinate constructions](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geomphys.2011.10.005)

First fix $W=-I$ and a traceless $B$. The conditional density on $g_1,g_2,g_3$ is obtained by substituting $g_4=(g_1g_2g_3)^{-1}W$ into the positive Wilson density. The angles $\theta_i=d(I,g_i)$ on the round unit sphere have sum at least $\pi$. The maximum of $\sum\cos\theta_i$ is $2\sqrt2$. To see it, reduce angles to sum $\pi$ if necessary. Pair the first two and the last two; for $u+v\le\pi$, $\cos u+\cos v\le2\cos((u+v)/2)$. Pairing those two averages gives equality only when all four angles equal $\pi/4$. Equality in the distance bound forces the four segments to form one minimizing great semicircle to $-I$. Thus $g_1=\cdots=g_4=q$ with $\Re q=1/\sqrt2$. Conversely all such configurations attain the maximum. Compactness, strict loss away from the maximum set and the positive Haar mass of every open neighbourhood give concentration on this set. At the central boundary the conditional law of $g_1$ is conjugation invariant. For traceless unit $B$, $\operatorname{Var}(f\mid W,B)=\mathbb E[1-(\Re g_1)^2\mid W]/3\to1/6$. Each pair move leaves at least one $g_j$ fixed. $\Re(Bg_j)$ predicts $f$ with vanishing error, since the four matrices align. This proves a vanishing local-update quotient without an asymptotic expansion.

**Why a null fibre matters.** At every fixed finite $\beta$, the preceding conditional variance and each expected pair variance are continuous in $(W,B)$. Conditional integrals are over compact groups with smooth positive densities and nonzero denominators. The coarse law has positive density on group pairs. The errors are conjugation-invariant. Choose a conjugation-invariant neighbourhood of the orbit $(-I,B)$ with positive probability and with quotient arbitrarily close to the central value (the denominator is positive). Multiply the centered test by its indicator. Coarse multipliers commute with every colour prediction. Equivalently the global Rayleigh quotient of this test is the ratio of the local numerator and denominator integrated over the gate. Shrinking the gate separately at each $\beta$ makes the central value an upper bound on any global full-complement constant. This quantifier is essential even when the coarse event is rare. For four-dimensional Wilson it suggests testing weights on coarse holonomy as well as the unweighted observables.

**Size of the effect.** Write $p=g_1g_2$ with angle $\theta$. At the central boundary its angle density is proportional to
$$\sin^2\theta\, Z(2\beta\cos(\theta/2))\,Z(2\beta\sin(\theta/2)),\qquad Z(k)=2I_1(k)/k.$$
Indeed conditional on $p$, $g_1=u$ has weight $\exp[\beta\Re(u+u^{-1}p)]$, a spherical tilt in the direction of $1+p$, and the other pair has product $-p^{-1}$. Set $s=\sin(\theta/2)$, $k=2\beta\cos(\theta/2)$, and $R=I_2(k)/I_1(k)$. Averaging over the uniformly oriented axis of $p$ yields
$$J_{12}=\mathbb E[(1-s^2/3)R/k+(s^2/3)R'],\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(f)=J_{12}+\mathbb E[R^2s^2/3].$$
The other two moves leave $f$ fixed, so the quotient is $J_{12}/(3\operatorname{Var}f)$. The probability outside any fixed neighbourhood of $\pi/2$ decays exponentially, by the maximum-set argument above. Equivalently the angle density has leading exponent $2\beta(\cos(\theta/2)+\sin(\theta/2))$, strictly maximal there. On a fixed neighbourhood $R\to1$ and $R'=O(k^{-2})$ uniformly by the large-argument modified Bessel expansion, while the conditional variance is bounded on the complement. Hence $\beta J_{12}\to5/(6\sqrt2)$ and the stated quotient follows. At $\beta=16,64,256,1024$, direct quadrature gives quotients $0.0663483,0.0178984,0.00457024,0.00114879$. The Gaussian full-complement lemma therefore needs a Wilson-specific transfer argument. A typical-configuration quadratic estimate alone could miss coarse-dependent tests. The disk calculation leaves open whether the four-dimensional updates admit a summable rate on the matched trajectory or are protected against this particular soft direction.
