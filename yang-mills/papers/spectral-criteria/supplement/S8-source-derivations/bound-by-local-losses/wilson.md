# Full conditional Poincaré inequality at small Wilson parameter

Work first on $N^3\times M$ with even $N,M\ge6$. Each elementary face has weight $\exp(\beta\operatorname{Tr}U_p/2)$ and each edge has Haar probability. Let $\mathcal S$ fix the individual spatial skeleton edges on slice zero. At each midpoint the skeleton graph has just two incident edges. Gauge transformations there put the first segment of each length-two link to identity, retaining the product. Even-vertex gauge transformations then act on the products as coarse gauge transformations. In the gauge-invariant measure the conditional mean of a gauge-invariant slice function given $\mathcal S$ is invariant on these skeleton orbits (equivalently average a version over the compact vertex group). Thus it agrees almost surely with the conditional mean given the full coarse orbit algebra $\mathcal C$.

### Random block heat bath on a fixed skeleton

Every nonskeleton spatial edge on slice zero has at least one odd transverse coordinate. The neighbouring face in the plane of that coordinate selects the edge at its odd-odd corner. Assign each such edge to one covering colour once and for all. Within each colour split the assigned edges by their two possible directions. The resulting subblocks contain at most one edge of any selected pair. No Wilson face meets two edges of a subblock, by the [parity geometry](../coarse-plaquette-floor/README.md). Partition the remaining unfrozen spacetime edges by their four directions and by the four parities of their bases. Distinct same-direction links in any action face differ in a transverse parity. Every such block also has a product conditional distribution. Empty blocks can be dropped.

Update a uniformly chosen block by its exact heat bath. Write $m$ for the number of blocks. A changed neighbouring link alters the one-link conditional log density by a function of oscillation at most $4\beta$. The likelihood-ratio bound therefore gives total variation at most $\tanh\beta$. There are at most 18 neighbours per link, counting the other three edges in each of six faces. The symmetric influence bound also controls the sum of changes caused by one edge. [Wilson oscillation calculation](../isotropic-variance/influence.md) · [Pedersen on block heat baths and Dobrushin conditions](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0703461)

Start from configurations differing at edge $i$. Choosing its own block erases the disagreement. At every other block couple independently by maximal one-link couplings. Averaging over blocks gives expected Hamming distance at most $\rho=1-(1-18\tanh\beta)/m$. Joining configurations one edge at a time and using the triangle inequality for the Hamming transportation cost gives contraction by $\rho$ for general pairs. This estimate is uniform in the frozen skeleton values. For $\rho<1$, any bounded function is Lipschitz for Hamming distance, and the oscillation of its $j$-step iterates is bounded by its Lipschitz constant times the finite edge count times $\rho^j$. The reversible kernel is an average of conditional-expectation projections, hence positive. A bounded centered test with spectral mass above $\rho$ would violate this decay of its $L^2$ norm. Bounded tests are dense. Multiplying the resulting gap by $m$ gives a sum of block defects at least $(1-18\tanh\beta)\operatorname{Var}(f\mid\mathcal S)$, on each conditional measure.

For one-slice $f$ all bulk-block defects vanish. A subblock defect is at most the defect for the larger original colour, by the variational definition of conditional variance. Each colour pays at most twice. Integrating over $\mathcal S$ and applying the gauge-orbit identity proves $\gamma=(1-18\tanh\beta)/48$. This is a volume-uniform interacting full-space result within the stated strong-coupling interval. The [weak-exterior tests](../isotropic-variance/weak-exteriors.md) explain why the same worst-case one-link comparison fails at matched weak coupling.

### Arbitrary finite coupling

Fix the skeleton and everything off slice zero. Relative to product Haar on the remaining spatial edges, the conditional density lies between $e^{-18\beta V}$ and $e^{18\beta V}$: at most $3V$ spatial and $6V$ temporal faces touch this slice, and each half-trace lies in $[-1,1]$. The same two bounds apply to the mixture over off-slice variables conditional on the skeleton alone. On each fixed exterior, the variational formula for squared prediction error bounds each colour defect below by $e^{-18\beta V}$ times the corresponding product-Haar defect. In the orthogonal product decomposition on the free edges, every nonconstant component has support meeting at least one colour. Thus the sum of Haar defects is at least the Haar variance. Compare back by using the Haar mean as a trial predictor for the conditional slice distribution given the skeleton. This yields $\gamma=e^{-36\beta V}/24$. The estimate rules out an exact hidden invariant at finite regulator. Its rate is insufficient on the matched trajectory.

### Time vacuum and the domain

At fixed $N,\beta$, normalized time transfer powers converge to the simple positive vacuum projection. Bounded marked finite-slab marginals converge in total variation: on a fixed slab the long closing kernel, divided by its top power, converges uniformly to the product of its two ground-state factors. This follows by sandwiching the norm-convergent middle power with one transfer step at each end; the continuous bounded kernel has uniformly bounded $L^2$ rows. Integration over the remaining Haar links gives the assertion, also when a time link is required in the slab. The argument can be made on gauge-invariant boundary sections since all boundary gauge copies are averaged. [Lüscher's Wilson transfer](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01614090)

For bounded measurable $f$, the colour variance is an expectation over three adjacent slices of the locally integrated squared error; its conditional pair kernels are the same in the periodic and vacuum measures. The coarse or skeleton prediction error converges as well: its variational infimum may restrict trials to the disk of radius $\|f\|_\infty$, giving a uniform bound on squared errors when the slice law changes in total variation. Then truncate in vacuum $L^2$. There is no interchange of weak-coupling and time limits.

For simultaneous endpoints with lattice separation at least two (both cyclic distances at least two on a periodic cylinder), the colour updates use disjoint action factors. The opposite endpoint does not change the conditional pair law. Conditional independence bounds $\operatorname{Re}\langle f,S_df\rangle$ by $\|f\|^2-J_k(f)$ for each colour, using stationarity and Cauchy–Schwarz. Average to obtain $I-S_2\succeq\gamma Q$ on the centered slice space, including mixtures with coarse observables.

The restrictions to one slice, the full coarse orbit algebra and all orientations matter. A gauge-invariant observable living exclusively on a different slice is exterior-measurable for every update. If only the eight $xy$ colours are used, a $z$ winding on a line of odd transverse coordinate never changes. Its trace has positive conditional variance given the skeleton at finite coupling by strict positivity relative to Haar. Both examples defeat an extrapolation outside the given domain.
