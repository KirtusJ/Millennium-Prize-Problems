# A same-skeleton test at two matched bare couplings

The complete fine space includes functions multiplied by a coarse event. In particular, an inequality for every slice function implies the same inequality on almost every coarse orbit for a given test. To test this implication in four dimensions, the two copies must use a common coarse sample drawn from the original Wilson marginal. Simply multiplying two Wilson actions with a shared skeleton generally reweights that marginal by an extra conditional partition function. The [compact disk](disk.md) gives an example where a rare orbit matters.

For a one-slice real test $f$ on a finite periodic torus, the numerator $J=\operatorname{avg}_k J_k(f)$ is half the mean squared difference of two independent colour refreshes of the same exterior, averaged over all 24 colours. At each pair propose its two links independently from their five-staple Wilson laws and accept with probability $\exp(\beta(\Re U_p-1))$ for their common plaquette. Removing the partner from the staple eliminates only that face. The pair density and the stationary colour variance are therefore exact, subject to ordinary floating-point and sampling errors.

For the denominator start two independent heat-bath evolutions from the *same* full equilibrium configuration, freezing all individual skeleton edges on the marked slice. After $m$ systematic sweeps measure $D_m=\mathbb E|f(U')-f(U'')|^2/2$. If $K$ is the conditional sweep on each skeleton fibre, stationarity and conditional independence give
$$D_m=\|f\|^2-\|K^m f\|^2\ \uparrow\ \|f-\mathbb E[f\mid\mathcal C]\|^2.$$
Each constituent heat bath preserves its conditional law. $K$ is an $L^2$ contraction. On a finite torus at fixed coupling, a full sweep has a positive lower density relative to product Haar on all free links, uniformly in the starting point, so the limit follows from minorization. For invariant tests the raw skeleton predictor equals the coarse-orbit predictor. Thus $J/D_m$ is an **upper** test for the best conditional Poincaré constant whenever $D_m>0$. A large value cannot establish a gap. Turning finite samples into a rigorous refutation would require sampling and preparation error bounds.

The two center-neutral tests were the spatial $xy$ plaquette sum from PR #25 and the squared half-trace of a straight $z$ winding at transverse coordinates $(1,1)$. Four independently seeded streams at each size used the matched bare couplings, periodic time extent $2N$, alternating hot and cold starts, 192 discarded cycles and 96 readings separated by three cycles. Each cycle is a heat bath followed by a microcanonical reflection sweep. Replica updates use independent pseudorandom draws and leave the reference trajectory intact. The pilot uses a finite periodic time preparation. It does not measure rare coarse gates or establish vacuum convergence.

Parentheses give paired delete-one-stream standard errors. Population monotonicity in $m$ need not hold for the two noisy estimated means. The sizes have different temporal extents and two sizes cannot establish a refinement trend. In particular neither trial has isolated an anomalously slow four-dimensional sector. Phase-conditioned replicas, longer temporal preparations and growing operator families would be required to probe that possibility.

At $\beta=0$ the sum uses scale one. The Haar predictions for $(J,D_m)$ are $(7/96,1/4)$ and $(1/48,1/16)$, respectively, for $m\ge1$. Distinct face traces are orthogonal; a refreshed colour erases a trace if it touches any of its links. Seven incident faces per selected pair give the fraction $7/24$ averaged over the slice. Eight of the 24 colours touch the chosen winding and its squared half-trace has Haar variance $1/16$. In the initial four-stream Haar run the mean plaquette numerator was $.074322\pm.000377$, above $7/96$ by 3.7 estimated standard errors. A separate four-stream control with 384 samples per stream gave $.072566\pm.000205$. The exact target is $.0729167$. Both runs are deposited.
