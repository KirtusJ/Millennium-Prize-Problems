# Center signs and minimal cubical surfaces

Start in a finite free box in $\mathbb Z^4$. At the small couplings used here Dobrushin uniqueness identifies its local thermodynamic limits with those of the periodic-space transfer vacuum. The total one-link influence is at most $18\tanh\beta<1$: a link belongs to six plaquettes and each has three other links. One first takes the infinite-volume state at each fixed set of regulator parameters and integer contour sizes. All estimates below are independent of the size of the surrounding box. [Wilson influence calculation](../strong-coupling/)

Choose a measurable representative of $SU(2)/\{\pm I\}$ on each positive link and write each link as $z_e V_e$ with $z_e\in\{\pm1\}$. Under reference Haar the signs are independent fair coins given the quotient variables. The Wilson tilt makes their conditional weight proportional to $\prod_f e^{J_f z_{\partial f}}$ with $J_f=\beta\operatorname{Tr} V_f/2\in[-\beta,\beta]$; the sign for inverse traversal equals the forward sign. The signs of the $J_f$ can disagree.

Expand every positive weight exactly as
$$e^{J s}=e^{-|J|}\big[1+(e^{2|J|}-1)1_{s=\operatorname{sgn}J}\big].$$
At $J=0$ the choice of sign has no effect. Label a plaquette active if the second term was chosen. Given the signs, activations are independent, and each probability is either zero or $1-e^{-2|J_f|}\le p$. Thus any specified set of $h$ faces is simultaneously active with probability at most $p^h$, including after averaging over signs and representatives. Given the active set and representatives, signs are uniform on the compatible affine binary space. A contour sign character averages to zero unless its incidence vector lies in the binary row span of the active face boundaries. This follows by pairing solutions by a nullspace vector with odd pairing against the contour. In the nonzero case some set of active faces has the required boundary over $\mathbb F_2$. The absolute trace of the representative loop is at most two, or four for the product of two traces.

Partition a filling into components using face adjacency by common vertex. Different components have disjoint vertex sets. Each component boundary is a cycle, so each simple contour belongs in full to one component. Discard the components with empty boundary. A filling for one loop now has one component; for two vertex-disjoint loops it has at most two. Anchor each required component on a fixed edge of its boundary loop. There are at most six possible incident faces. In four dimensions a vertex touches 24 plaquettes and a face has four vertices, giving a face adjacency degree at most 96. The number of connected sets of $h$ faces containing one prescribed face is at most $H^{h-1}$ with $H=384$. To encode a set, choose its deterministic ordered search tree rooted at that face. Its plane tree shape has at most $4^{h-1}$ possibilities (balanced parenthesis walk), and every child edge can be specified by one of 96 neighbor labels. The encoding recovers the set.

Summing over component sizes for two roots, the crude count at total size $h\ge1$ is at most $(6+36h)H^h\le42(h+1)H^h$. If every filling has at least $d$ faces, the required activation probability is at most $42\sum_{h\ge d}(2Hp)^h\le84(2Hp)^d$ since $h+1\le 2^h$ and $Hp\le1/4$. This upper bound also covers the single contour. It makes no assumption of independent physical plaquettes.

## The minimum filling

A single planar rectangle has minimum area $A$. Projection to its plane takes a finite mod-two filling to the unique finite planar filling, and cannot increase the face count. For the pair, project off the unused fourth coordinate, and clamp each remaining integer coordinate to the rectangular prism between the loops. These coordinate maps send each elementary cell to one cell of the same dimension or collapse it. Mod-two boundary commutes with the induced cellular map; opposite faces of collapsed cells cancel. Projection therefore cannot increase the minimum. The prism is also embedded in the original lattice, giving equality.

More generally for identical planar loops bounding a finite cell set $Q$, the minimum number of faces at separation $k\ge1$ is
$$\min_{S\text{ finite}}\{2|Q\mathbin\triangle S|+k|\partial S|\}.$$
For any projected filling, cut at heights $j+1/2$ for $0\le j<k$. The vertical faces intersect each midplane in a planar edge cycle: at every vertex its degree is even because the original filling has no boundary on the vertical edge it cuts. Each such cycle bounds a unique finite binary cell set $S_j$. Write $S_{-1}=S_k=Q$. At level $j$ the horizontal faces are exactly $S_{j-1}\triangle S_j$, by the boundary relation and uniqueness of a finite planar filling. The resulting cost is the sum of these horizontal distances plus $\sum_j|\partial S_j|$. Pick a slice of least perimeter. The triangle inequality from $Q$ to it and back charges twice its symmetric difference with $Q$, and every vertical slice charges at least its perimeter. Conversely repeat any fixed $S$ at all levels and use $Q\triangle S$ as both caps. This attains the displayed cost. For this general statement one clamps only the height, or first uses a bounding spatial rectangle that contains all the data.

For $Q$ an $n\times m$ rectangle we may work in the prism, hence $S\subset Q$. If $z=|S|$, each occupied row and column contributes at least two boundary edges in its respective direction, and their counts are at least $z/n$ and $z/m$. Hence $|\partial S|\ge (P/A)z$. Every cost is at least $2(A-z)+kPz/A$, a convex combination of $2A$ and $kP$. The empty and full slices realize both endpoints. For a nonrectangular loop the general minimization matters: a $5\times5$ square with a four-cell straight protrusion at separation two has disk cost 58 and wall cost 56, but removing just the protrusion gives cost 48.
