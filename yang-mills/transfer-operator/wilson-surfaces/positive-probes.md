# Relative mixing for every positive insertion, including the charged heat trace

Even a bound $|\operatorname{Cov}(F_0,F_k)|\le c\,3^{-k}\operatorname{Var}(F_0)$ can become ineffective after division by a very small squared mean. For a nonnegative insertion the original Wilson local conditional densities give a bound directly in units of that mean.

Consider isotropic $0<\alpha=18\tanh\beta<1$, in a free finite box or its unique infinite-volume limit. Let $I$ be any finite set of spatial links on time zero, and $B$ a finite set of spatial links at time $k\ge2$. For $f$ and $g$ functions of those two sets, respectively, and $f,g\ge0$ with finite positive means, put
$$D_k=18 |I| e^{24\beta}\alpha^{k-1}/(1-\alpha).$$
Then
$$ e^{-D_k}\le\frac{\mathbb E[f g]}{\mathbb E[f]\mathbb E[g]}\le e^{D_k}.$$
The size of $B$ is unrestricted. For signed or complex tests the same argument bounds the absolute covariance by $(e^{D_k}-1)\mathbb E|f|\,\mathbb E|g|$ (with conjugation as appropriate). Cancellation in a signed mean can make this much larger than a bound in units of the means themselves.

To prove the relative estimate, order the links of $I$. Pin the same previously exposed coordinates in two systems and change the boundary values at $B$. For any unexposed link $i$, the density of its value $v$ relative to Haar, conditional on the previous values and $B$, is the expectation of its full one-link conditional density $h_i(v)$ in that pinned system. As a function of the current configuration $h_i$ depends only on its at most 18 incident neighbors. For fixed $v$, it lies in $[e^{-12\beta},e^{12\beta}]$ since the total one-link exponent has absolute value at most $6\beta$.

Use the Dobrushin comparison theorem with the single-link influence matrix $C$, row sums at most $\alpha$. Pinning identical coordinates deletes possible influence paths. Changing a collection of pinned boundary values produces direct variation at most $\sum_{j\in B} C_{ij}$ by changing them one by one. The comparison thus bounds the change in the expectation of a test $h$ by its single-link oscillations times sums of products along paths ending at $B$. The sum over all paths of length $r$ from a given link is at most $\alpha^r$. Each plaquette adjacency changes the time of the anchor link by at most one, so every path from a neighbor of $i$ to $B$ has length at least $k-1$. It follows that the absolute change in the conditional density at $v$ is at most $18e^{12\beta}\alpha^{k-1}/(1-\alpha)$. Its uniform lower bound turns this into a log-density change at most $18e^{24\beta}\alpha^{k-1}/(1-\alpha)$. This comparison applies for every fixed choice of the previously exposed values and for every $v$. [Rebeschini and van Handel, Corollary 2.6](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~rvan/dobrushin130819.pdf) · [Wilson single-link influences](../strong-coupling/)

Multiply the successive conditional densities along $I$. For any two values of $B$ their ratio for the entire $I$ configuration lies in $[e^{-D_k},e^{D_k}]$. Integrating the second density over the marginal distribution on $B$ compares the joint law on $I,B$ to the product of its marginals in that same pointwise relative range. Integrate the nonnegative product $fg$. For a signed product, subtract the product measure and take absolute values. At finite coupling all local densities are positive. Passage through free finite boxes first holds for continuous tests on the compact finite product, then as a comparison of measures and hence for all nonnegative measurable tests.

For the charged heat trace $F_s=\operatorname{Tr} e^{-sA_U}$ in a Dirichlet spatial box of fixed physical size, only $O(b^{-3})$ links occur. Take $k b\to T>0$, and fix a coupling with $\alpha<1$ or vary it under a common bound $\alpha\le\alpha_0<1$. Then
$$
 \frac{\mathbb E[F_s(0)F_s(k)]}{(\mathbb E F_s)^2}-1\longrightarrow 0.
$$
The conclusion is uniform in the spatial volume surrounding the insertion. It holds even though the equal-time second moment of the unit-mean trace diverges along these trajectories. This resolves the mean-normalized heat-trace question in the high-temperature regime of the **interacting** measure. With growing inverse coupling this influence bound ceases to be summable.
