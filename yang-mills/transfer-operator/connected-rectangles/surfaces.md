# A nontrivial temporal cut and the first connected coefficient

Start with free boundaries in a finite four-dimensional box containing both loops. Give every plaquette its own variable $a_f$, with factor $\exp(a_f\chi(U_f)/2)$ and $\chi=\operatorname{Tr}$ in the defining representation. The normalized connected reading is analytic near zero. Two independent restrictions apply to each nonzero Taylor monomial.

1. Flipping the sign of a single link flips exactly the incident face variables and gives the boundary sign of the two observed loops. This holds for the joint expectation and for the product of the means. Therefore the faces of odd multiplicity have binary boundary equal to the pair of contours.
2. In **every intervening time slab** the spatial edges underlying the selected temporal faces contain a cycle. To see this, set all face variables outside the monomial support to zero. With the boundary spatial links held fixed, integrate the temporal vertex links in that slab. On a forest, integrating at a leaf makes its incident Wilson factor a scalar independent of both boundaries, by left and right invariance and inversion of Haar. Remove leaves until there are no edges. The resulting constant kernel separates all earlier and later observables, so their connected reading is identically zero for arbitrary retained couplings. Analytic coefficients on that support therefore vanish. On the open cubic spatial graph a cycle costs at least four **distinct** temporal faces. Repetitions on an isolated edge do not transmit a signal.

Conditional on both ends of a slab, its temporal links are the variables of this group integral. Spatial action weights can be assigned to their respective sides. Open time precludes a second path around a seam. Gauge fixing a periodic time history without keeping the residual holonomy would change the argument. [Original transfer and seam](../periodic-time/)

## The minimum with these constraints

Project away the unused spatial coordinate and clamp to the $n\times m$ prism between the loops. Projection and clamping induce a chain map over $\mathbb F_2$ and cannot increase the number of faces. Cut the projected odd chain halfway across each time slab. Its planar edge cycle is the boundary of a unique finite cell set $S_j\subseteq Q$, where $Q$ is the rectangle. Horizontal faces at successive levels account for at least the sum of the Hamming distances along the sequence $Q,S_1,\ldots,S_k,Q$. These are exact distances for the projected chain. Taking the boundary past a clamped time end simply includes faces originally outside the time interval in this lower bound. [Cubical slicing argument](../wilson-surfaces/center-surfaces.md)

Choose a slab with the fewest **actual** temporal face occurrences among these $k$ slabs. Its count $t$ bounds the total temporal cost below by $kt$. Its section $S$ satisfies $|\partial S|\le t$. In the preceding horizontal bound, spatial faces anywhere in the box count as horizontal. Temporal faces outside the retained time interval collapse when time is clamped. Thus the horizontal and temporal charges do not double count. If $S$ is empty, horizontal faces cost at least $2A$, and the forest test still gives $t\ge4$. This exceeds the unit-tube competitor.

If $S$ occupies $a$ columns and $d$ rows, then $|S|\le ad$, $|\partial S|\ge2(a+d)$ and the total cost is at least
$$
 f(a,d)=2(A-ad)+2k(a+d),\qquad 1\le a\le n,\quad 1\le d\le m.
$$
This is bilinear, hence is the bilinear interpolation of its four corner values. The mixed corners exceed $f(1,1)$ by $2(k-1)(n-1)$ and $2(k-1)(m-1)$. Since $k,n,m>1$, the only minimizing corners are the unit section or the full section, or both when their costs tie. No interior or other boundary point can minimize, since its interpolation gives positive weight to a strictly larger corner.

Equality for the full section uses no horizontal faces and exactly $P$ occurrences per slab. The prescribed odd boundary therefore propagates unchanged at its original spatial position. Every vertical face is used once. For a unit section equality requires exactly four occurrences per slab, so the actual support in every slab is an elementary cycle used once. No section can project to the empty set, since going to empty and back already costs $2A$ horizontally. Thus each projects to one cell. Reaching the initial and final sections costs $A-1$ each. The budget leaves no horizontal faces to change cells in between. The cells in all slabs agree. The two caps must consist of the $A-1$ other cells at the two time ends. No other horizontal face can be used, folded by the clamp, or cancelled under projection.

These equality statements also fix the lift to four dimensions. A four-edge cycle projecting to a unit square has all its edges in the two loop directions with the prescribed coordinates there. At the first lower boundary plane a cycle translated to a different unused spatial coordinate would require a cap on the missing cell to cancel it in that plane. No such horizontal face is available. Equivalently, without faces in the unused direction the boundary condition holds independently on each parallel plane. Interior time levels contain no horizontal faces, forcing the lift through every slab to remain at the same location. In the full-wall case the absence of horizontal faces already fixes each actual cut by the original contour. Beyond the two measured time levels the equality budget leaves no temporal faces. The parity boundary then forces all horizontal caps to lie on the two measured levels, even when an enlarged box was initially clamped. Both candidate surfaces attain their costs in the original lattice.

## The coefficient of each surface

All minimizing multisets use distinct faces. In a given minimizing support no nonempty subset has zero binary boundary. At the top or bottom a cap filling a cross-section cycle would require the missing cell, and at interior levels the cross-section is constant. On the full walls the same argument uses the entire rectangle. Any subset filling just one observed contour is also impossible: its projection to the loop plane would have to contain every cell of $Q$, including the missing cell. Consequently the normalization denominator, and the product of the one-loop means, contribute nothing to this square-free monomial. One can see this by using the unnormalized expression $N_{01}Z-N_0N_1$, where $N$ denotes a Haar numerator. At its lowest nonzero total degree its coefficient is the same as that of the normalized covariance since $Z(0)=1$.

The full walls give coefficient $4^{-Pk}$ from fundamental Haar orthogonality. For the unit tube, start from its small square on a cap and grow a rectangle to $Q$ one cell at a time. Extend one row or column strip at a time, so each added cell shares a connected boundary arc with the region built so far, and its outer arc contains fresh links. In reverse order, integrating the fresh arc uses $\int U_{ab}\overline U_{cd}=\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}/2$. The product of its independent links is Haar. Pair the big boundary trace with the cell's normalized trace, reversing orientation if necessary since $SU(2)$ traces are real. The contraction leaves the smaller boundary trace times $1/4$. This removes $A-1$ faces on each end, including for a corner or edge opening. What remains is a four-sided cylinder of $4k$ faces. Its product of oriented Haar contractions gives $4^{-4k}$ (each link paired once, Euler characteristic zero). The total coefficient per opening is $4^{-[2(A-1)+4k]}$. Distinct openings give distinct monomials with the same positive sign. The Taylor factorial leaves no additional factor for distinct faces.
