# Relative transfer bounds along explicit joint trajectories

Throughout, $G$, the faithful representation and $\eta>0$ are fixed. Constants may depend on them. Let $N\ge2$, choose the coordinate tree reducing one nonzero coordinate at a time toward the root, and use the Lebesgue embeddings in the [kernel proof](kernel.md). Write $T_g,T_*$ for the embedded compact and Gaussian transfers on the anchored space. Both commute with simultaneous conjugation. For all sufficiently large $N$ and $gN^{18}$ sufficiently small,
$$
 \frac{\|T_g-T_*\|}{\lambda_*}
       \le C g N^{18}+e^{-cN^5},\qquad
 \lambda_*=\|T_*\|=\exp[-(D/2)\textstyle\sum_j\theta_j].
$$
Here the sum counts each spatial mode once. In particular the estimate compares errors to a vacuum eigenvalue which can be exponentially small in the number of links. The powers are sufficient bounds chosen for a direct proof.

**Global confinement in chord coordinates.** In the coordinate tree, the root-to-vertex path takes axes in increasing order. To reduce the fundamental loop of a positive chord in direction $i$, commute that last step backwards past the later axes of the tail path. This uses at most $2N$ distinct unit squares, each with directions $(i,j)$, $j>i$. The result of the swaps is exactly the root-to-head path; cancellation completes the contraction. All swaps remain inside the box. For any one of these squares the tail coordinate along $i$ is fixed by its base, and $i$ is the smaller of its two directions. At most $M^2$ possible chord tails remain. Thus any square belongs to at most $M^2$ such fillings. With bi-invariant distance, each square move costs at most the plaquette distance to identity, including the parallel transport back to the root. Sum over fillings, apply Cauchy–Schwarz to each, and count each face with its multiplicity to get
$$
 \sum_f \operatorname{dist}(U_f,1)^2
    \le 2N M^2\sum_p\operatorname{dist}(U_p,1)^2\le C N^3 V(U).
$$
The linear counterpart bounds $|dx|^2$ below by $cN^{-3}|x|^2$, either by the same signed square sums or by differentiating along tree-fixed exponentials. This estimate prevents large nearly flat configurations on the entire compact integration domain.

**Tail cost.** In minimal normal coordinates, single-link Haar density $J$ is bounded above uniformly, has $J(0)>0$, and satisfies $J(X)/J(0)=1+O(|X|^2)$ near zero. For clarity, one can take the domain of minimizing vectors in the Lie algebra, where the exponential is one-to-one off cut loci. It lies in the fixed ball of radius $\operatorname{diam}G$. Domain boundaries have zero Riemannian volume; local change of variables and zero extension suffice. The one-link normalization satisfies $Z_t=g^D J(0)(2\pi\eta)^{D/2}(1+O(g^2))$. Taylor expansion on a small fixed ball gives the error by integrating $O(g^2)(|z|^2+|z|^4)$ against a weaker Gaussian after $X=gz$; compactness and the global quadratic bound control the complement.

Before the tree integral, both rescaled integrands for $T_g,T_*$ have magnitude bounded by
$$
 C^{|E|}\exp[-cN^{-3}(|x|^2+|y|^2)-c|z|^2].
$$
Here $z$ is the collection of tree noises; all three norms are Euclidean norms on the whole corresponding arrays. The $g^{-D}$ upper bound per chord density cancels against endpoint Jacobians, and on tree edges the rescaled density has a Gaussian upper bound. The constants for each link cost at most $C^{|E|}$. In the linear integrand this bound follows before integrating the tree Gaussian, so no inverse covariance determinant needs a separate bound. Let $R=N^4$. Restrict $|x|,|y|,|z|\le R$ in the two integrals. By integration in $z$ followed by the $L^2(dx\,dy)$ bound, the omitted kernels have Hilbert–Schmidt norms at most $\exp(CN^3\log N-cN^5)$. Indeed all dimensions are $O(N^3)$, and reserving half the Gaussian exponent outside those balls costs at least $cN^{-3}R^2$. Fixed polynomial weights in endpoint norms can be included by reserving another fixed fraction of the exponential. At fixed $N$ the same majorant proves Hilbert–Schmidt convergence by dominated convergence.

**Central comparison.** On the retained region $gNR\to0$. A product of at most $l\le 6N+4$ exponentials of signed factors $gX$, $|X|\le R$, has logarithm differing from $g\sum X$ by at most $C(g l R)^2$. This follows by multiplying the convergent exponential series in the faithful matrix representation, bounding their quadratic remainder by $C(\sum g|X|)^2$, then using the logarithm series. The spatial and temporal action exponents thus differ from their linearized quadratic forms by at most $C g(lR)^3$ per face or chord. The spatial face on the tree-fixed initial or final slice has at most four factors. The kinetic chord word includes two tree paths and the two chord endpoints. The domain cutoffs are absent in this central region. Multiplying the Jacobians and one-link constants adds $O(N^3 g^2(1+R^2))$ to the log ratio. With $O(N^3)$ factors the total log difference is bounded by $C gN^{18}$, tending to zero on both trajectories. Therefore the absolute error *after integration in the retained tree noises* is at most $C gN^{18} T_*(x,y)$ on retained endpoints. For arbitrary $L^2$ vectors bound the integral of their product against this error by the bilinear form of $T_*$ on their absolute values. Its norm costs only $\lambda_*$, preserving relative precision. Finally $\lambda_*\ge e^{-C N^3}$ since $\omega_j\le\sqrt{12}$ and $m=O(N^3)$. Absorbing the tail cost proves the estimate.

**Energies and vacuum vectors.** Set $\epsilon=CgN^{18}+e^{-cN^5}$. Vacuum-normalized transfers differ by $O(\epsilon)$ on the full embedded space. They are contractions. The anchored Gaussian vacuum is separated by $1-e^{-\theta_1}\ge c/N$ from its nearest excited eigenvalue, even before enforcing full gauge invariance. The vacuum projections and positively phased unit vectors consequently differ by $O(N\epsilon)$, using the resolvent circle at half this separation or the eigenvector equation. The vacuum is physical for both transfers. On physical states min–max gives $O(\epsilon)$ absolute error for **every ordered normalized eigenvalue**. Ratios near one hence have the same absolute error in their step energies. Since $\theta_1\sim\eta\sqrt2\pi/M$, the first physical gap is $2\sqrt2\pi/(Mb)\,(1+o(1))$ for both trajectories. The graph has $N$ cells and $N+1$ vertices per axis; the physical cell extent $Nb$ and the mode length $Mb$ differ by one spacing. At fixed $Mb=R_0$ the same reasoning controls every fixed-index physical level via the finite-cavity oscillator formula. This controls the ground and excited levels of the original discrete-time compact regulator with $a=\eta b$.

**Endpoints and powers.** A centered physical plaquette divided by $g^2$ grows at most quadratically in $|x|$ by bi-invariance and the four-edge word. On $|x|\le R$ it differs from the quadratic curvature by at most $CgR^3$ before centering, and both norms as multipliers there are at most $C R^2$. The bounds also apply to the bent observables, by expanding their words of at most eight factors. The positive vacuum equation and the kernel tail bound give $\|\mathbf1_{|x|>R}\Omega_g\|+\|\mathbf1_{|x|>R}\Omega_*\|\le e^{-cN^5}$, also with fixed polynomial weights. On the entire compact chart the face distances are at most the summed chord distances, so all these deficits divided by $g^2$ are bounded by $C|x|^2$ there, independently of $g$. These facts give the same central comparisons for vacuum means and tested vectors with errors at most $C(R^2 N\epsilon+gR^3)$ and negligible tails. In a two-point reading at integer lag $k$, telescoping the normalized transfer powers costs at most $C k\epsilon$. Thus for two centered plaquette readings with the additional $b^{-4}$ per endpoint, the total error is at most
$$
 C b^{-8}\big[R^4(N+k)\epsilon+gR^5\big]+o(1)
$$
for the spacings used here; the tail term decays faster than every inverse power of $N$. On $b=N^{-1/2}$ and $k=O(1/b)$ the two-point error tends to zero whenever $gN^{39}\to0$. For the gap alone $gN^{19}\to0$ suffices.

For any fixed number of strictly time-separated insertions, $g=e^{-N}$ beats every fixed polynomial accumulated by central cutoffs and telescoping. To justify removing the cutoffs, reserve one transfer step next to each insertion (there are eventually at least two between successive times). A multiplier supported in $|x|>R$ followed by a normalized transfer has norm bounded by its weighted Hilbert–Schmidt tail divided by the top eigenvalue. For compact and Gaussian observables the global quadratic bound gives the weighted tail directly. Each full multiplier followed by a step has norm at most its central bound plus the tail. Vacuum endpoints obey the same estimates. Centering uses the respective exact means. On $b=N^{-1/2}$ the central norm costs at most $C N^{10}$ including the physical normalization of each insertion. In particular $gN^{19+10m}\to0$ suffices for a fixed moment of order $m$; any superalgebraic decrease in $g$ works for every fixed order on one trajectory. No estimate here covers the logarithmically running bare coupling expected on a nontrivial asymptotically free trajectory.
