# Which cavity levels and which plaquette readings survive Gauss law?

For one interval of $N$ edges and $M=N+1$ vertices, the forward difference has squared singular values $\ell_j=4\sin^2(\pi j/(2M))$, $0\le j<M$. On vertices the normalized vectors are $f_0=M^{-1/2}$ and $f_j(n)=\sqrt{2/M}\cos((n+1/2)\pi j/M)$ for $j>0$. Their normalized differences on edges are $s_j(n)=-\sqrt{2/M}\sin((n+1)\pi j/M)$. These are orthonormal bases by the finite trigonometric sums. There is no edge vector for $j=0$.

In the cubical product complex choose $j=(j_1,j_2,j_3)$ and let $l$ be its number of nonzero entries. A one-cochain slot can lie only along one of the $l$ active axes. In the tensor product of the displayed bases the coboundary is exterior multiplication by $(\sqrt{\ell_{j_1}},\sqrt{\ell_{j_2}},\sqrt{\ell_{j_3}})$. On the one-cochains its curl squared is $|v|^2I-vv^*$ for this vector $v$. Removing gradients leaves $l-1$ modes when $l\ge2$ and none otherwise. Each retained mode has
$$
  \omega_j^2=\sum_i\ell_{j_i},\qquad \theta_j=2\operatorname{arsinh}(\eta\omega_j/2).
$$
The minimum is $\omega_1^2=2\ell_1$, realized in three ways at permutations of $(1,1,0)$. The next distinct value is $\omega_2^2=3\ell_1$, realized by the two transverse slots at $(1,1,1)$. When index two exists, $\ell_2/\ell_1=4\cos^2(\pi/(2M))\ge3$, so no other vector intervenes. These formulas include one elementary cube with squared frequencies $4,4,4,6,6$. Directly setting the tree kinetic covariance to identity incorrectly gives a first squared frequency about $0.486$ on this cube. The ratio for an oscillator occupation pattern is $\exp(-\sum_j n_j\theta_j)$ with multiplicities from its spatial modes and color tensors. Keep only invariant tensors in the bosonic Fock space on the spatial modes tensored with $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$. This specifies every finite level, including accidental equal sums. The adjoint has no fixed vector and has a unique invariant bilinear form up to scalar, which is symmetric, because the complexified Lie algebra is simple. Thus no one-quantum state survives. At two quanta in the lowest three spatial modes the independent singlets are the symmetric pairs of modes contracted with the color metric. There are six. The next shell contains one lowest and one next-spatial mode, with multiplicity $3\cdot2=6$. Its step energy $\theta_1+\theta_2$ is smaller than $3\theta_1$, since $\omega_2/\omega_1=\sqrt{3/2}$ and $\operatorname{arsinh}$ is increasing and concave on the positive half-axis. At fixed box the rest of the physical spectrum stays separated from both clusters. On an abelian group a single quantum is already invariant and the first-gap factor of two would be incorrect.

One can also read all limiting multiplicities from the Haar average of $\prod_j\det(I-e^{-k\theta_j}\operatorname{Ad}(h))^{-1}$, with one factor per spatial mode. This is the trace of the $k$-step vacuum-normalized Gaussian transfer on the singlet subspace: expand each symmetric-power generating function and average the character. For integer $k\ge2$ it is also the limit of the corresponding compact physical traces at fixed box, since Hilbert–Schmidt convergence gives trace-norm convergence of the squares and operator-norm convergence of the additional factors.

## Six independent physical probes

At a common vertex choose one elementary face in each coordinate plane, based and oriented there. Put $F_p=2(1-\tau(U_p))/g^2$. For two perpendicular faces use $F_{pq}=[\tau(U_pU_q^{-1})-\tau(U_pU_q)]/(2g^2)$. They are ordinary bounded gauge-invariant spatial functions at each cutoff. In anchored coordinates they tend to $|X_p|^2$ and $\langle X_p,X_q\rangle$ where $X_p=(dx)_p$ is the linear curvature. Center the readings in the true vacuum. Products of normalized plaquette traces alone would first give quartic terms for different faces.

For a mode $(1,1,0)$ its curvature has only the 12-component. Its coefficient at any 12-face is proportional to $s_1(x_1)s_1(x_2)f_0(x_3)$ and is nonzero there. Permuting the axes gives the other two lowest modes, each on its own orientation. Thus the three squared probes project onto the three diagonal color singlets, while the three bent probes project onto the other three pairs. All six projections are nonzero and mutually orthogonal in the limiting first cluster. The Gaussian vacuum subtraction removes the scalar constant; normalizing a quadratic form applied to the vacuum picks the two creation operators with factors $1/\sqrt{2\nu_j}$. The rest of each reading may have higher-frequency components. At fixed $N$ the six projected vectors for the actual compact cluster are independent for sufficiently small $g$ by continuity of their Gram determinant. For the unbounded rescaled tests in the proof, normal-coordinate growth is at most quadratic. Gaussian tail bounds from the vacuum equation justify norm convergence of the tested vectors. The distinction can be checked by A4's positive exterior-power thermal tests, which do not require choosing the six probes.
