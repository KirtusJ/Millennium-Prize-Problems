# Faithful Wilson characters on compact connected groups

The time-block argument extends to each nontrivial compact connected Lie group $G$ with a chosen faithful finite-dimensional unitary representation $\pi$. This covers the compact connected simple groups, including their quotient forms when the representation is faithful on the chosen quotient. The small magnetic-coupling interval and the required temporal fineness in this note depend on $(G,\pi)$. The SU(2) estimates in the [main proof](README.md) give numerical constants for the defining representation.

Take $\phi=\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}\pi/\dim\pi$ and $V=1-\phi$. Equip $G$ with the bi-invariant metric given at the identity by $\mathrm{Hess}_1 V$. This fixes the one-link Brownian clock. Use Haar probability, $h=a/\kappa$, and the normalized Wilson density $w_h=Z_h^{-1}\exp(-V/h)$. There are $T\ge1$ and $h_0\in(0,1]$, depending only on $(G,\pi)$, with the following consequence. When $a/\kappa\le h_0$ and $\kappa\lambda\le1/[120(T+1)]$, the full gauge-invariant Wilson log gap and its continuous-time Hamiltonian counterpart are at least
$$
\frac{\log 3}{(T+1)\kappa}.
$$
The estimate is uniform in finite spatial side $L\ge2$. Fixed bounded local vacuum correlations have a common limit along every joint removal of time spacing and spatial volume at fixed spatial mesh and couplings. We give an additional sufficient condition below for a nonzero finite-energy plaquette correlation. This result concerns a lattice theory with continuous physical time. It leaves the spatial continuum Yang–Mills problem open.

## A heat component in one actual Wilson step

The Hessian metric is positive definite because $-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(d\pi(X)^2)/\dim\pi>0$ for $X\ne0$. The only global minimum of $V$ is the identity: a unitary matrix with normalized real trace one is the identity, and $\pi$ is faithful. Let $r$ denote distance to the identity, and $D=\dim G$. Taylor expansion locally and compactness away from the identity give constants $0<c_1\le c_2$ with $c_1r^2\le V\le c_2r^2$. Normal-coordinate integration gives $Z_h\asymp h^{D/2}$ as $h\downarrow0$. In particular, for constants independent of small $h$,
$$
c h^{-D/2}e^{-c_2r^2/h}\le w_h(x)\le C h^{-D/2}.
$$

Write $p_t$ for the heat density of $e^{t\Delta}$ in this metric. The Gaussian upper bound at small times on the compact group is $p_t(x)\le A t^{-D/2}\exp(-r^2/(B t))$. The metric has nonnegative Ricci curvature and small metric balls have volume comparable to $t^{D/2}$ at radius $\sqrt t$. Choose $u>0$ with $1/(Bu)\ge c_2$. The preceding inequalities then give $w_h\ge\theta p_{uh}$ for some fixed $0<\theta<1$ and every sufficiently small $h$. Thus one Wilson step is an exact mixture $w_h=\theta p_{uh}+(1-\theta)v_h$ with a symmetric probability density $v_h$ satisfying $\|v_h\|_\infty\le C' h^{-D/2}$. [Li and Yau, parabolic kernel estimate, Theorem 3.1 with zero potential](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02399203)

## From one step to a uniformly mixed block

Choose $m=\lceil T/h\rceil$. Heat kernels are central and convolution by probability measures preserves a uniform density bound. Expanding the $m$th convolution power of the mixture gives a binomial number $J$ of heat steps of parameter $\theta$. Conditional on $J=j$, the heat part has time $juh$, with the other $m-j$ factors having law $v_h$. Since $G$ is compact and connected, $\|p_t-1\|_\infty\to0$ as $t\to\infty$. For example, convolve on both sides with a fixed positive-time smooth heat kernel and use the first positive Laplace eigenvalue on the mean-zero $L^2$ subspace. Choose $T$ so that the error is at most $1/20$ for all $t\ge u\theta T/2$.

On $J\ge m\theta/2$ the resulting conditional density is then within $1/20$ of one. Otherwise at least one residual density occurs, so the supremum of the conditional density is at most $C'h^{-D/2}$ even for $J=0$. The exceptional probability obeys
$$
\Pr[J<m\theta/2]\le2^{m\theta/2}(1-\theta/2)^m\le
\exp[-m\theta(1-\log 2)/2].
$$
This is Markov's inequality applied to $2^{-J}$. Exponential decay in $1/h$ beats the polynomial supremum. Shrinking $h_0$ if necessary gives **$\|w_h^{*m}-1\|_\infty\le1/10$**. This controls all representation labels together for the actual Wilson step. It uses neither a truncation to low modes nor an equality between a one-step Wilson density and a heat kernel.

The discrete bridge construction in the main proof now applies with $s=ma\le(T+1)\kappa$. The magnetic log weight still has range at most $2\lambda s$. At $p=\lambda s\le1/120$, the same face incidence gives $q_0\le2/3$ and $q_3\le44/45$. Hence the block has normalized excited norm at most $1/3$ and the Wilson gap has the asserted physical floor. Positivity as an operator follows from the exponential series of the normalized real character, a positive sum of characters of tensor powers of $\pi\oplus\pi^*$. The spatial half factors preserve positivity. The strictly positive kernel fixes the vacuum and its phase. ## Fixed-box Hamiltonian identification

Let $N$ be the number of spatial links in the fixed box and $A=-\sum_e\Delta_e$. At the identity, $V(\exp X)=|X|^2/2+O(|X|^4)$ and $V$ is invariant under inversion. In exponential coordinates normalized by the metric, the covariance of $X/\sqrt h$ converges to the identity by Gaussian domination and rescaling. Odd first moments in a symmetric chart vanish, and the complement of the chart is exponentially small. Taylor expansion of a smooth test function therefore gives $(I-K_a)/a\to A/(2\kappa)$ on the smooth product core. For the full one-step transfer, the two half potentials add $W=\lambda\sum_p(1-\phi_p)$. The contraction product formula gives strong convergence of the unnormalized evolutions at fixed physical time to $e^{-tH}$ with $H=A/(2\kappa)+W$, also along time arguments tending to a positive limit. [Finite-volume transfer argument and product formula](../hamiltonian-time/README.md)

To compare the vacua, the all-links heat choice in the product Wilson kernel has probability $\theta^N$. The remaining mixture consists of symmetric Markov contractions. In quadratic form order,
$$
(I-K_a)/a\ge \theta^N(I-e^{-uh A})/a .
$$
Also $\|(I-T_a)/a-(I-K_a)/a\|\le\|W\|_\infty$, by expanding the two half factors. The lowest defect eigenvalues are nonnegative and bounded above by testing the constant vector. For a normalized lowest vector, the squared norm on $A>\Lambda$ is thus bounded by $C a/[\theta^N(1-e^{-uh\Lambda})]$. At fixed $N$, taking $a\downarrow0$ and then $\Lambda\to\infty$ gives zero. The low eigenspaces of $A$ are finite dimensional, hence the vacuum family is norm-precompact along sequences tending to zero. Testing the defect eigen-equation on the core identifies every limit with the positive normalized ground state of $H$ and the limiting eigenvalue with its ground energy. In particular the vacuum normalization of the powers converges. The coefficient $\theta^N$ is used here only at fixed $N$.

Use this convergence first in a fixed box at block times $nma\to nT\kappa$. The covariance estimate at all $n$ and density of centered multipliers give the Hamiltonian gap (even $\log 3/(T\kappa)$ in this limit). Next the spatial boundary estimate with $q_0\le2/3$ transfers local expectations between boxes uniformly in $a$. Taking a fixed large comparison box, removing its time cutoff, and then increasing that box yields the same thermodynamic local limit along every joint path. This argument provides qualitative convergence for the general group. The local $O(a^{1/3})$ rate uses the separate SU(2) resolvent estimates. [Spatial comparison](local-limit.md)

## A local excitation for each fixed group

Let $\ell_G>0$ be the first positive eigenvalue of $-\Delta$ on one link, $v=\operatorname{Var}_{\rm Haar}(\phi)>0$, and $D_\phi=\sup_G |\nabla\phi|^2$. These are fixed once $(G,\pi)$ is chosen. On a face let $F=\phi(U_p)$. In the Hamiltonian vacuum the sum of all link kinetic energies is at most the constant-vector trial energy, at most $6\lambda L^3$. Translation and cubic symmetry make the expectations equal on the $3L^3$ links. For the one-link constant projection $P_e$ this gives $\langle I-P_e\rangle\le4z/\ell_G$. On the four distinct links of the face, $I-\prod_e P_e\le\sum_e(I-P_e)$; their reduced state's trace distance to the product constant pure state is therefore at most $d=4\sqrt{z/\ell_G}$. Equivalently, project the full vector onto that local pure state, normalize, and use contractivity of trace distance under marginalization. At $d\ge1$ the distance bound is automatic.

The product Haar law of the holonomy is Haar. Since $|F|\le1$, its first two moments differ from their Haar reference by at most $2d$ each, and the squares of the means differ by at most $4d$. Hence $\operatorname{Var}_{\Omega}(F)\ge v-6d\ge v/2$ for $z\le\ell_G v^2/2304$. The ground-state Dirichlet identity gives the centered spectral first moment at most $2D_\phi/\kappa$, since each of the four link derivatives is a translated or inverted character derivative in a bi-invariant metric. Jensen's inequality then bounds the connected Hamiltonian correlation below by $(v/2)\exp[-4D_\phi t/(\kappa v)]$. This lower bound passes to the common local limit. Together with the transfer floor, it gives finite and positive physical energies detected by the plaquette at fixed mesh and sufficiently small $z$ for every group in this note. [Dirichlet identity for bounded multipliers](../../observable-normalization/hamiltonian-variance/README.md)

Faithfulness matters for the one-link continuum identification. For example, the SO(3) defining representation pulled back to SU(2) has a central kernel. The pulled-back temporal density is invariant under $U\mapsto-U$ and annihilates every odd SU(2) label at each step. The walk on SU(2) then cannot converge on those modes to the identity as $a\downarrow0$. On SO(3) itself the representation is faithful. [SO(3) character and lifted odd-mode checks](execution.md)

## Open time reflection and the split Wilson measure

On an open slab with free ends, reflect halfway between time slices zero and one. Before gauge fixing a crossing face has holonomy $U_{x,0,i}h_{x+i,0}U_{x,1,i}^{-1}h_{x,0}^{-1}$ with temporal links $h$. Their graph at each fixed spatial vertex is a chain. Starting at one end and defining the vertex rotations successively sets every $h$ to the identity. At fixed temporal links the resulting change of each spatial link is left and right multiplication by fixed elements. Product Haar is unchanged. The original normalized temporal Haar integrals then contribute one for gauge-invariant insertions supported strictly within the two halves. In the fixed coordinates reflection exchanges matching spatial links at slices zero and one. The crossing action is exactly
$$
\sum_e (g/\dim\pi)\operatorname{ReTr}[\pi(U_{e,0})^*\pi(U_{e,1})].
$$
Explicitly, let $l,r$ be the two vertex rotations on the left slice, propagated on the right slice to $lh_x,rh_y$. Then $(lUr^{-1})[(lh_x)V(rh_y)^{-1}]^{-1}=l(Uh_yV^{-1}h_x^{-1})l^{-1}$ in any group. Conjugation and reversal of the trace pairing preserve its real part. [Checked group identity](BlockedTime.lean) The remaining factors, including the temporal-end spatial half weights, occur in reflected pairs on identical product-Haar halves. Additive constants in the Wilson action cancel in the finite positive normalization. This verifies the cross-plane hypothesis of G4's matrix-feature Gram expansion for this geometry. Temporal circles can carry a residual holonomy. A site reflection through shared boundary variables uses a different disintegration. In the blocked representation this statement applies to pulled-back original observables; an arbitrary function of the auxiliary random seeds need not represent a Wilson observable.
