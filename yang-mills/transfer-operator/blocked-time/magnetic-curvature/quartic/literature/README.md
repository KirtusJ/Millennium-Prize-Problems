## A check that resolves spatial momentum

I read equations (26)–(28) on the journal page image because the text extraction garbles the fractions. Write $t_i=2\cos(p_i/2)$, $\hat p_i=2\sin(p_i/2)$ and label each face by its normal $i$. The order-$u^4$ coefficient of Münster’s SU(2) effective Hamiltonian is $-(50/3-|\hat p|^2)I-2A$, where $A_{ii}=0$ and $A_{ij}=t_it_j$ for $i\ne j$. The source uses connected two-plaquette correlations and isolates the part of their logarithm linear in the time separation, a direct precedent for our time-slope extraction. [Münster, §2 and eq. (28), journal pp. 71–72](https://www-library.desy.de/preparch/desy/postpr/1985/desy85-007.pdf#page=6)

On the project side, two contributions carry momentum at this order. The shared-edge quadratic response has coefficient $-D(p)$ after $b=g$ and expansion in $u$, since $J=u^2/2+O(u^4)$ and $b^2=16u^2+O(u^4)$. Its diagonal is $2\sum_{j\ne i}\cos p_j$. The six-face quartic contribution has coefficient $-C(p)$, since its two time weights both tend to one and $b^4=256u^4+O(u^6)$. Its opposite-face diagonal is $2\cos p_i$. Both off-diagonals are $t_it_j$, from the four half-step displacements. The equal-time repeated-face contribution at this order is local: the single-face term in the static pressure is a sum of independent source functions, and its Hessian is diagonal at a single spatial face. Thus $D+C=(6-|\hat p|^2)I+2A$. The remaining scalar coefficient is $-32/3$: the momentum-independent piece of the quadratic response contributes $-28/3$, and the static repeated-face quartic term contributes $256(-1/192)=-4/3$. This recovers the published matrix entry by entry. For example its lowest fourth-order coefficient changes from $-98/3$ at zero momentum to $-62/3$ at $(\pi,0,0)$. This tests the relative weights of motion within a face plane and motion transverse to it. Separating both mechanisms at general unequal couplings requires additional information. ## Anisotropy and the Hamiltonian endpoint

- **An established expansion procedure.** Billó, Caselle, D’Adda and Panzeri retain the full temporal Wilson coupling in SU(2) and expand through $\beta_s^2$. On p. 7 they identify a spatial adjoint plaquette and a pair of fundamental plaquettes as the two contributions at this order. They integrate out spatial links to obtain a finite-temperature Polyakov-loop effective action. This establishes precedent for the expansion and the character bookkeeping. A vacuum-subtracted, zero-temperature magnetic-plaquette gap at order $\beta_s^4$ has different insertions and time boundaries. [Primary paper, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-lat/9601020#page=7)
- **Hamiltonian coefficients.** The 1986 linked-cluster article in *Nuclear Physics B* 270, 536–552 reports a series for the total scalar gap of Hamiltonian SU(2) in three spatial dimensions. I could verify the publisher’s description but could not inspect its numerical coefficient table. To make a future comparison reproducible, in B4’s fixed-mesh Hamiltonian use $W=\kappa H/2$, $x=\kappa\ell/4$. Up to a constant, $W=\sum_e C_2(e)-x\sum_p\operatorname{Tr}U_p$ with $C_2(1/2)=3/4$. Our gap then starts at $3-71x^2/105$. The cube contributes $-73x^4/27$ to the next term. The historical total fourth-order coefficient would include the repeated faces as well. Neither number is attributed here to the inaccessible table. [1986 Hamiltonian series](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(86)90567-5) · [Project Hamiltonian convention](../../../README.md)
- **Regime of spectral proofs.** Schor reports isolated one-particle states in the full energy-momentum spectrum of the strongly coupled lattice pure-gauge theory. Karsch’s anisotropic Wilson calculation distinguishes $a_s$ from $a_t$ and derives coupling matching in the weak-coupling regime. Together these specify two separate requirements for a continuum argument: persistence of spectral control under spatial refinement and calibration of the physical slab thickness on that trajectory. [Schor (1984)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(84)90289-X) · [Karsch (1982), §1](https://noah.nrw/ubbihs/download/pdf/5114815)

## Independently calibrated physical comparisons

Sommer defines a reference distance from the static force by $R_0^2 F(R_0)=1.65$. A spectral quantity on an isotropic lattice can thus be compared as $(am)(R_0/a)$ without defining the lattice spacing by the unknown gap. Athenodorou and Teper use an independent string-tension scale in their SU(2) simulations. Their continuum extrapolations give $M_{0^{++}}/\sqrt\sigma=3.781(23)$ and $M_{2^{++}}/\sqrt\sigma=5.349(20)$ (Table 34). Table 23 separately gives $5.343(30)$ in $E^+$ and $5.353(23)$ in $T_2^+$, illustrating the additional evidence used in a continuum spin-two assignment. A single lattice $E^{++}$ branch only tests part of that assignment. These measurements supply numerical targets with uncertainties. Jaffe and Witten require existence of a nontrivial theory on $\mathbb R^4$ and spectral exclusion on that theory for every compact simple gauge group. A bound uniform through refinement in independent physical units, with the continuum field construction, remains outstanding. [Sommer, definition of $R_0$](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-lat/9310022) · [Athenodorou–Teper, Tables 23, 33–34](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00364#page=84) · [Jaffe–Witten, §4](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf#page=6)
