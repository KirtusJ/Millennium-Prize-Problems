# Removing the time cutoff of the SU(2) Wilson regulator

Director B4 · November 22, 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

Keep the spatial lattice spacing fixed. Let $a$ be the time step and take the temporal and spatial Wilson couplings to be $\beta_t=\kappa/a$ and $\beta_s=a\lambda$, with $\kappa>0$ and $\lambda\ge0$. At fixed finite spatial volume the Wilson transfer and its vacuum correlations converge to those of the electric-plus-plaquette Hamiltonian below. For a sufficiently small, volume-independent $z=\kappa\lambda$, the interacting Hamiltonian has a gap uniform in the spatial volume. An elementary plaquette has positive spectral weight at finite energies. The ground-state plaquette action at large $z$ also admits explicit two-sided bounds, where the gap remains undetermined. | Regime at fixed spatial spacing | Result |
|---|---|
| $\lambda=0$, spatial side $L\ge2$ | The exact first gauge-invariant excitation has energy $3\min(L,4)/(2\kappa)$. Short winding loops matter at $L=2,3$. |
| $0<z\le1/384$ | The centered unnormalized plaquette has variance at least $5/9$ and energy per unit variance at most $12/\kappa$, uniformly in $L$. |

| $z\ge36$ | Writing $\phi_p=\operatorname{ReTr}U_p/2$, the true vacuum satisfies $3/64\le\sqrt z\,\langle1-\phi_p\rangle\le3$ at every spatial volume. |

## Transfer, Hamiltonian and vacuum

Let $E$ be the positively oriented edges of a periodic spatial cube of side $L\ge2$ and $V=\sum_p(1-\phi_p)$. On $L^2(SU(2)^E)$ let $A_e=-\Delta_{S^3,e}$ for the round unit-quaternion metric. The generators here are $i\sigma_1,i\sigma_2,i\sigma_3$. The physical Hilbert space consists of functions invariant under independent gauge rotations at the vertices.

$$
H_0=\frac1{2\kappa}\sum_{e\in E} A_e,\qquad H=H_0+\lambda V .
$$

In temporal gauge use the normalized one-link convolution with kernel proportional to $\exp[(\kappa/a)\operatorname{Tr}U/2]$. Let $K_a$ be its edgewise product and $M_a=\exp(-a\lambda V/2)$. The physical one-step transfer is $T_a=M_aK_aM_a$ on invariant vectors. Integrating the time links effects the gauge projection. Normalization by its leading eigenvalue removes any scalar convention for the Wilson action. $M_a$ and $K_a$ are contractions. We can use their gauge-equivariant extension to the full product space, where both the discrete transfer kernel and the continuous-time heat kernel with bounded real potential improve positivity. Each ground state is unique and can be chosen strictly positive. Gauge transformations preserve this choice, so the unrestricted ground state belongs to the physical Hilbert space. [Lüscher’s transfer construction](https://bib-pubdb1.desy.de/record/396349/files/7611148.pdf) · [Kogut and Susskind](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.11.395)

For Peter–Weyl label $n=2j=0,1,\ldots$, the link convolution eigenvalue at $g=\kappa/a$ is $r_n(g)=I_{n+1}(g)/I_1(g)$. In this metric the corresponding Laplace eigenvalue is $n(n+2)$. At each **fixed** $n$, the large-$g$ expansion gives $r_n(g)=1-n(n+2)/(2g)+O_n(g^{-2})$. On finite sums of product matrix entries we therefore have $(I-K_a)/a\to H_0$. These functions form an operator core. Adding the bounded smooth potential preserves the core. Since $(I-M_a)/a\to\lambda V/2$ in operator norm and $K_a\to I$ strongly, $(I-T_a)/a\to H$ there. The contraction product formula gives $T_a^{\lfloor t/a\rfloor}\to e^{-tH}$ strongly for fixed $t\ge0$ (use uniformity on bounded time intervals). [Kirtus Justus, E2 §§1, 8](https://github.com/KirtusJ/From-Prephysical-Hypotheses-to-Physical-Models/blob/aca29276aa180af3c81ccc9f3aa06b4e2ae4d404/edition/PAPERS/E2_Compact_Group_Transfer_Spectra_and_Ultraviolet_Operator_Limits/source/01_editable_Compact_Group_Transfer_Operators.md) · [Bessel asymptotics, DLMF §10.40](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.40) · [Chernoff product formula](https://web.ma.utexas.edu/mp_arc/c/20/20-110.pdf)

Vacuum convergence needs an additional compactness argument. Write $B_a=(I-T_a)/a$ and $D_a=(I-K_a)/a$. The estimate $\|B_a-D_a\|\le\lambda\|V\|_\infty$ follows by expanding $K_a-M_aK_aM_a$ and using $1-e^{-x}\le x$. A normalized lowest vector of $B_a$ has bounded energy as $a\downarrow0$ by testing on the constant, hence bounded $D_a$ expectation. Outside the finite-dimensional joint cutoff $n_e\le J$ for every edge, $D_a\ge [1-r_{J+1}(\kappa/a)]/a$ because the positive Bessel factors decrease with order. At fixed $J$ this lower bound tends to $(J+1)(J+3)/(2\kappa)$. Sending $J$ to infinity gives norm-precompactness along every sequence $a\downarrow0$; there is no uniform-in-order expansion in this argument. [Bessel order monotonicity, DLMF §10.37](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.37)

Along a convergent subsequence, test the eigenvector equation on the core. The limiting normalized vector is an $H$ eigenvector at the limiting energy. The variational upper bound from testing $B_a$ on each core vector gives a limiting energy at most $\inf\sigma(H)$. Positivity fixes the phase and uniqueness identifies the limit for the whole family. Thus the largest transfer eigenvalue is $1-aE_0+o(a)$ at fixed $L$. Bounded time-slice observables and the normalized transfer powers now converge to their vacuum correlations for $H-E_0$. This justifies using the true vacuum on both sides of the time limit. All statements about sending $L$ to infinity below concern the Hamiltonian after this step. The [joint time-cutoff estimate](../joint-cutoff/README.md) gives an explicit sufficient temporal spacing when the volume and couplings vary together.

## Exact free electric spectrum on the physical space

For $\lambda=0$ the eigenvalue on edge labels $(n_e)$ is $\prod_e r_{n_e}$. At a vertex incident to exactly one nonzero representation, integrating the local gauge transformation annihilates the component: a single nontrivial irreducible representation or its dual has no invariant vector. The support of any nonconstant physical component has no vertex of degree one. A nonempty such finite support contains a cycle, counting parallel links as distinct edges.

The girth of the cubic spatial torus is $g_L=\min(L,4)$. Winding in one coordinate gives a length-$L$ cycle, and an elementary face gives a length-four cycle. For $L=2$ the winding cycle uses the two distinct parallel links. At $L\ge3$ there are no parallel links. A three-step circuit either winds, requiring $L\le3$, or lifts to an odd circuit on the bipartite cubic lattice, which is impossible. Consequently every excited transfer eigenvalue is at most $r_1^{g_L}$. The fundamental character around a shortest cycle attains this value because every active edge occurs once and lies in representation $n=1$. This determines the *full* physical first excitation. The one-link gap alone would give the wrong threshold even at infinite side length. The free Hamiltonian limit is $3g_L/(2\kappa)$. For one fixed plaquette the unnormalized trace has Haar variance one and autocorrelation $e^{-6t/\kappa}$ even when a shorter winding loop exists. [Finite-geometry controls](readback.txt)

### An explicit spatial refinement that fails a rotation test

At $\lambda=0$ and $L\ge3$, fix the $xy$ orientation and let $F_x$ be its unnormalized plaquette trace. Distinct such traces are orthogonal under product Haar: a link appearing only in one face integrates to zero. All are electric eigenvectors of energy $6/\kappa$. Thus their exact centered two-point function is $\mathbf1_{x=y}\,e^{-6|t|/\kappa}$. For a compactly supported smooth spatial test $f$, use $Y_b(f)=b^{3/2}\sum_x f(bx)F_x$ at mesh $b$. With physical boxes growing to contain the supports and $b\downarrow0$, the covariance of $Y_b(f)$ and $Y_b(g)$ converges to
$$e^{-6|t|/\kappa}\int_{\mathbb R^3} f(x)g(x)\,dx.$$
This follows by a Riemann sum. The result has a finite nonzero decay rate and survives physical time separation. Its spacetime difference kernel is supported on the time axis. A Euclidean rotation exchanging $z$ and $t$ leaves the $xy$ plane fixed and sends this support to the $z$ axis, so the covariance fails that rotation test for a geometrically identified $xy$ plaquette. This counterexample concerns the explicitly uncoupled electric model. It isolates the spatial and rotational information still needed even when a positive temporal gap and a local finite-energy witness coexist.

## A volume-independent gap with a magnetic interaction

We apply Yarotsky’s Theorem 1 to the unrestricted product, then restrict to the invariant subspace containing its unique vacuum. Group the three outgoing links at each spatial vertex $x$ into one site with Hilbert space $L^2(SU(2)^3)$. Let $A_x$ be their summed positive Laplacian. Its kernel is spanned by the product constant, and on its orthogonal complement $A_x\ge3$. Use the range $S=\{0,e_1,e_2,e_3\}$. All three plaquettes anchored at $x$ act on sites in $x+S$. Up to a scalar shift, $(8\kappa/3)H$ is the sum of

$$
h_x=\tfrac13\sum_{y\in x+S}A_y,\qquad
v_x=-\tfrac{8\kappa\lambda}{3}\sum_{i<j}\phi_{x,ij}.
$$

Each site belongs to four translated sets, so the electric coefficients sum exactly. On each whole block $h_x$ is diagonal in a product spectral partition, has a one-dimensional constant ground space, and costs at least one on its complement. Merely extending $A_x$ by the identity would leave a neighboring excitation at zero block energy and violate hypothesis (1). Each $v_x$ is a bounded self-adjoint local perturbation with $\|v_x\|\le8z$, uniformly in the volume. The periodic-cube theorem permits infinite-dimensional site Hilbert spaces and unbounded classical terms. If $8z$ is within its bounded-perturbation allowance, the unrestricted gap is at least $3\gamma/(8\kappa)$ with $\gamma>0$ independent of $L$. Restriction to the gauge-invariant sector preserves this lower bound. The theorem also supplies a thermodynamic limit of the equal-time ground-state expectations on fixed local observables. We do not assign a numerical value to its perturbative threshold or gap constant. [Yarotsky, hypotheses (1)–(2) and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0412040)

For a fixed compact connected simple gauge group $G$ the same Hamiltonian argument works with any fixed bi-invariant Riemannian metric, three outgoing $L^2(G)$ links per site, and a bounded real normalized plaquette character. Write $d_G>0$ for the first positive one-link Laplace eigenvalue. Then $h_x=d_G^{-1}\sum_{y\in x+S}A_y$, the physical rescaling is $8\kappa/d_G$, and the local perturbation norm is at most $24z/d_G$ for a normalized character of absolute value at most one. This extension concerns the continuous-time, fixed-mesh Hamiltonian. The Bessel matching and the explicit observable constants here use SU(2).

## Spatial refinement

Neither the fixed-mesh gap nor its positive plaquette correlator identifies a nontrivial Euclidean-invariant theory on four-dimensional continuum space. For example, the bare tree-level matching with spatial spacing $b$ and Wilson normalization $\beta$ reads $\kappa=\beta b$, $\lambda=\beta/b$, so $z=\beta^2$. A sequence with $\beta\to\infty$ eventually leaves the small-$z$ gap theorem. The large-$z$ estimates below control a local energy and a local observable; the low-energy spectrum of the full vacuum complement remains to be bounded in a common physical clock on that trajectory.
