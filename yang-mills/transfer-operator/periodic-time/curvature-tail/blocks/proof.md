# From the compact link tuple to a Gaussian rotation orbit

## The exact orbit at finite coupling

Orient edges positively and choose a spanning tree in $\Gamma$ with root $o$. Its $m=|E|-|V|+1$ chords define fundamental loops by following the tree from $o$ to each end of a chord. A change of vertex frames can put the tree links at identity. The remaining freedom conjugates all chord holonomies simultaneously. Conversely two tuples with the same simultaneous orbit are related by vertex gauges. At fixed exterior, the compact interior measure is invariant under an arbitrary gauge at every vertex of $\Gamma$, including those attached to unobserved interior links. Thus, given the orbit and the exterior, the link tuple has the unique invariant probability on that orbit under $\mathrm{SU}(2)^{|V|}$. The identical kernel reconstructs its unconditional law. Projection to the orbit and lifting by this common kernel give equality of the two joint-versus-product TV coefficients, by contraction in both directions. For a tree the orbit space is a point, so even a conditional measurement of all its links has product Haar law. [Maximal-tree gauge background](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/files/30278302/POST-PEER-REVIEW-PUBLISHERS.PDF)

On the chords use principal scaled logarithms, assigning an arbitrary value on the null cut locus. They identify the simultaneous group orbit with a simultaneous $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ orbit of $m$ three-vectors in the chart; this map and its inverse are measurable on their support. The compact link-orbit quotient is compact metrizable. The vector-orbit quotient is generally noncompact and standard Borel since the rotation group is compact and acts continuously on a finite-dimensional Euclidean space. Using separate conjugations at different chords would discard physical relative color information.

## Fixed-radius conditional density

Fix a tree on the shell. Inside, extend the central tree to a spanning tree of the strict interior and attach it to one shell vertex by one edge. This pins one edge per interior vertex and leaves all central chords free. The box-conditional kernel for the orbit observable depends only on the shell, by the finite-link Gibbs specification. Gauge fixing in this forest preserves the exterior and eliminates product Haar integrals on its pinned edges. In this gauge the selected free links are precisely the chord holonomies up to one common rotation. Let $b$ be the unpinned shell logarithms scaled by $\sqrt\beta$.

For clarity, Z4's compact-shell conditional-density argument extends to this choice of forest. With trivial shell data the sole zero of the action is the identity in this gauge: flatness on the contractible box is a pure gauge, the connected shell fixes its boundary values, and the pinned paths determine every interior value. The linearized curl $D$ on unpinned interior edges has trivial kernel by the same potential argument. These are the two forest-dependent inputs to L3. Specifically the least eigenvalue of $D^*D$ is positive at this fixed radius. On a sufficiently small link chart the quadratic expansion gives $\beta S\ge c_R|\phi|^2-C_R|b|^2$ for the scaled free logarithms $\phi$. Outside that chart, compactness and uniqueness give a positive action minimum at trivial shell, retained with half its value uniformly for bounded $b$ and large coupling. Since $|\phi|^2\le n_R\pi^2\beta$, this region also admits a Gaussian dominating bound. The Haar chart factors and the action converge uniformly on compacts to their Gaussian counterparts. Integrating the tails and normalizing therefore gives total variation convergence uniformly on compact $b$. No growing-dimension estimate is used. [Z4's detailed chart inequalities](../../../../z4/attempt/reviews/a8-BL34/L3_L4.md)

Write $S$ for shell curl, $J$ for extraction of the $m$ chord edges from the free logarithms. The Gaussian integral with energy $|D\phi+Sb|^2/2$ gives, for these selected coordinates,
$$
 M(b)=-J(D^*D)^{-1}D^*Sb,\qquad K_R=J(D^*D)^{-1}J^* .
$$
The covariance is $K_R\otimes I_3$, positive definite since $J$ selects distinct free variables. The Gaussian mean is an $m$-tuple of colors. Pushing the conditional density forward first by $J$, then to its single rotation orbit, contracts TV. All fixed shell logarithms are uniformly tight by the plaquette moment bound and finite loop fillings. The expected conditional TV error therefore tends to zero uniformly over state choices.

## The mean distribution and the exterior limit

Choose fixed finite two-chain fillings $f_j$ of the central fundamental loops. The unconditional scaled, jointly rooted loop logarithms converge at every fixed $R$ to a centered Gaussian tuple with one-color covariance $K_{ij}=\langle f_i,\Pi f_j\rangle$, where $\Pi$ is the infinite-lattice exact-form projection. Indeed each fixed loop factors as a finite product of elementary face lassos. Their angles are $O_{\mathbb P}(\beta^{-1/2})$, and the scaled log of their product differs from the signed sum by $o_{\mathbb P}(1)$. Fixed path replacements cost $o_{\mathbb P}(1)$ as well. The even-periodic tangent law gives the joint centered Gaussian face field. Fundamental loop basepaths cancel in the linearized sum. Fillings with the same boundary give the same projected field because their difference pairs to zero with every compact curl. The rotated tuple in the box gauge has the same unconditional law: it has the same orbit and an invariant joint law under a common rotation at the shell root. Invariant laws on the same compact group orbit use its unique invariant probability. [Periodic tangent law](../../../../central-quotients/tangent-curvature/periodic-limit/spectral.md) · [Change of root](../review.md)

Equivalently the unaveraged box-gauge chart can be sampled by first retaining tree-gauged boundary data and then sampling the free interior variables. Its marginal is invariant under common rotation, since rotating the original shell at its root preserves its law and the kernel is equivariant. Apply the compact-density comparison to this chart before quotienting. For every subsequential weak limit of the tight means $M(b)$, convolution with $N(0,K_R\otimes I_3)$ gives $N(0,K\otimes I_3)$. Division of characteristic functions proves that the mean limit is Gaussian of covariance $V_R\otimes I_3$, $V_R=K-K_R\ge0$, possibly singular. In particular $K$ is strictly positive definite for $m>0$.

Addition of a Gaussian with the fixed nonsingular covariance is continuous in the mean in total variation. Orbit projection preserves this continuity. Tight weak convergence of the mean laws gives norm convergence of their mixtures, by finite continuous approximation on each compact set. The triangle inequality now gives the fixed-radius limit
$$
 a_\Gamma(R)=\mathbb E_M d_{\rm TV}\big(\mathcal L([M+Z]\mid M),\mathcal L([M+Z])\big),
$$
with $M\sim N(0,V_R\otimes I_3)$, $Z\sim N(0,K_R\otimes I_3)$ independent; brackets denote the simultaneous orbit. This compares conditional laws on standard Borel spaces and does not require total variation convergence of the exterior links themselves. The argument applies to every sequence of eligible states. [Single-loop mixture argument](../wilson/proof.md)

For all large $R$ the fixed fillings are supported well inside the cube. A finite conditional Gaussian fluctuation is the curl of an interior potential with zero shell data. Its sum on each filling is the corresponding chord fluctuation by telescoping around the fundamental loop. Hence $(V_R)_{ij}=\langle f_i,(\Pi-\Pi_R)f_j\rangle$, where $\Pi_R$ projects onto curls supported by interior edge variations. For any fixed face near the origin the diagonal projection deficit is $O(R^{-4})$ by cutting off the lattice Green potential. Cauchy–Schwarz and the finite sizes of the fillings give $\|V_R\|=O_\Gamma(R^{-4})$. The channel estimate below yields $a_\Gamma(R)=O_\Gamma(R^{-4})$. If $\Gamma$ contains a plaquette, restricting to its four edges bounds the block dependence below by the single-plaquette dependence of order $R^{-4}$ (a fixed displacement only changes the enclosing radius by a constant, by sandwiching translated cubes). Finally $a_{\beta,\mu}(\Gamma,R)$ is nonincreasing in $R$ because links are discarded from the exterior. Choosing a large fixed radius and then controlling the finitely many smaller enclosing radii proves absolute convergence uniformly in radii and states, and vanishing along every joint divergence.
