# Review of the Gaussian specification and its tail

Use original periodic pure SU(2) Wilson cubes of side $2^k$. Take a periodic limit at each fixed $\beta$ first. Next extract a finite-dimensional weak-coupling limit of the rooted, scaled logarithms of positively oriented plaquettes. The thermodynamic exponential bound provides uniform moments of every fixed order. The review below concerns this limiting random field $F$. [Finite-link argument, L3 and L4](../../../z4/attempt/reviews/a8-BL34/L3_L4.md) · [Target, §4](../../../z4/attempt/reviews/a6-Bfull/PROOF_B_v2.md)

## Which data may be conditioned on

L4(c) uses the shell base $b$ and paths outside the varied links to each exterior face. Interior gauge fixing can set its forest to one while keeping $b$ fixed. Thus the lasso colors at $b$ are legitimate tests, even when an individual test depends on the color frame. In the limit the shell data in that frame are the fixed linear inverse applied to the shell curvatures. The argument uses this linear inversion and the finite-box Laplace comparison.

The last paragraph of L4(c) assumes that a path from the original root $o$ to $b$ uses only exterior links. If $o$ lies strictly inside the box, its first step already has an interior endpoint. Normal centered exhaustions encounter this for every sufficiently large box. One can transfer the **joint law** without making that measurability assumption. For any finite list of interior, shell and exterior faces use paths from $o$ through $b$, with common bridge $V$. Every lasso in the list is the same simultaneous rotation $\operatorname{Ad}(V)$ of the corresponding lasso at $b$. For $o\ne b$, independent Haar gauge transformations at the two vertices turn the bridge into $g_o Vg_b^{-1}$ and the list at $b$ into $\operatorname{Ad}(g_b)F^{(b)}$. Conditional on the old configuration and $g_b$, the new bridge is Haar. The resulting joint law is the product of Haar bridge law and the law of the whole list at $b$. That list is invariant under common rotations. Consequently the two rooted lists have the same law. If the roots coincide, there is nothing to transport. Replacing the paths through $b$ with the original fixed paths changes every scaled lasso by $o_{\mathbb P}(1)$ for a fixed finite list, by L4(a)(i). Apply this equality of limiting laws to both sides of the bounded cylinder identity, including its shell arguments. A monotone-class argument gives the original-frame conditional law. This uses L4(a)(iii) and does not require independence of the bridge from the interior links.

## The finite projections and the reverse limit

For the centered cube $Q_R=[-R,R]^4$ let $I_R$ be links with an interior endpoint, $P_R$ the faces touching $I_R$, and $\mathcal G_R=\sigma(F_q:q\notin P_R)$. Use $V_R=d\mathbb R^{I_R}$ for the finite-dimensional space of curls of edge tests supported on $I_R$, extended by zero on all other edges. Pinning the temporal forest leaves that curl range intact. Indeed a vertex potential vanishing on the shell can be solved along each tree toward its shell vertex so that subtracting its gradient removes all pinned values. The covariance of the conditional Gaussian in L4 is the orthogonal projection $\Pi_R$ onto $V_R$ in one color. In pinned coordinates with injective curl matrix $D$, this is $D(D^*D)^{-1}D^*$. The conditional mean is stationary for the quadratic energy under all these variations, giving $d^*h^R=0$ on $I_R$. Every finite edge test lies in some $I_R$. The nested projections thus converge strongly to $\Pi$, projection onto the closure of all compact curls.

Use a fixed exhaustion and complete the sigma fields up to null sets. For each face, $h^R_p=\mathbb E[F_p\mid\mathcal G_R]$ converges in $L^2$ to $h_p=\mathbb E[F_p\mid\mathcal G_\infty]$, with $\mathcal G_\infty=\bigcap_R\mathcal G_R$. This is the reverse conditional-expectation theorem. Norm convergence also follows from decreasing orthogonal projections in $L^2$. For a finite colored test $v$ and bounded tail variable $Y$, the conditional characteristic function gives
$$
 \mathbb E\,Y e^{i(F-h^R)(v)}
     =\mathbb E Y\,\exp[-\langle v,\Pi_R v\rangle/2].
$$
Here the projection acts diagonally on the three colors. Eventually the test lies in $P_R$. The exponential on the left is Lipschitz in its real argument; strong projection convergence handles the right. Thus $\xi=F-h$ is a joint centered Gaussian with covariance $\Pi$ in each of three independent colors, independent of the whole curvature tail. Bounded cylinder tests determine this joint independence. [Fremlin, Measure Theory, §275K](https://www1.essex.ac.uk/maths/people/fremlin/chap27.pdf) · [Sheffield, Gaussian free fields and Hilbert-space projections](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0312099)

Closure gives $dh=\mathbb E[dF\mid\mathcal G_\infty]=0$, and the finite stencil for $d^*h$ vanishes in the limit by the preceding Euler equation. Hence $h$ is componentwise lattice harmonic. For one color and oriented face type set $H_x=h_{x,ij,c}$. Its raw second moment is constant in $x$ because $\mathbb E H_x^2=\mathbb E F_{x,ij,c}^2-\Pi_{pp}$ by independence. The first term is stationary and the second is translation invariant. Harmonicity and the equality of second moments give
$$
 \sum_{|e|=1}\mathbb E|H_{x+e}-H_x|^2
 =\sum_{|e|=1}\mathbb E H_{x+e}^2-8\mathbb E H_x^2=0.
$$
Every summand is nonnegative. Countability gives a single almost-sure event on which each of the 18 components is constant. This argument uses neither ergodicity nor a classification of infinite-volume Gibbs states.

Even before reflection, the curvature tail equals $\sigma(h)$ up to null events. Indeed $h$ is tail measurable, and independence implies that for every bounded cylinder $f(F)$ its conditional tail expectation is the function of $h$ obtained by integrating $f(\xi+h)$ over $\xi$. This extends to bounded functions of the entire curvature field by a monotone class. Apply it to an indicator in the curvature tail itself.

## Reflection removes the constant

C4’s odd-reflection test also works directly at second order here, since Z4 controls sixth moments. Let $q_n$ be the positive $ij$ face at $n e_i$, based via its straight path from the origin on the site reflection plane $x_i=0$. At fixed $\beta,n$ use the continuous bounded test given by a color component of the rooted $\sqrt\beta\,\operatorname{Im}U_{q_n}$. Reflection sends it to the negative of that component of the positive $ij$ face at $(-n-1)e_i$. At the reflected square the inverse edge moving its basepoint cancels the last step of the straight transporter. Site-plane positivity therefore bounds the expectation of the product of the two positively oriented components above by zero. Z4's pure-gauge square proves positivity for real half-tests before taking gauge invariants. Gauge averaging, if used, is applied to the whole product. [Reflected word identity](../../../central-quotients/tangent-curvature/reflection.md) · [Menotti and Pelissetto on site-plane positivity](https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/458456)

For $\alpha$ the class angle, $\sqrt\beta|\alpha-\sin\alpha|\le(\sqrt\beta\alpha)^3/(6\beta)$. Hence the test agrees with the scaled logarithm in $L^2$ up to $O(\beta^{-1})$. In the weak-coupling tangent law, uniform integrability of the product gives
$$
   0\ge\Pi_{q_n,q_{-n-1}}+\mathbb E H_{ij,c}^2 .
$$
Only now send $n\to\infty$. The Fourier symbol of $\Pi$ is bounded on the compact Brillouin torus, hence its Fourier coefficient tends to zero at this separation. It follows that $H_{ij,c}=0$ almost surely. This applies in all four reflection directions and to all components. The curvature tail in the limiting law is therefore trivial.

For time-translates of a spatial plaquette the centered covariance of $|F_p|^2/2$ is $(3/2)G(n)^2$ by Wick contraction; the variance is $(3/2)G(0)^2=3/8$. Thus along the dyadic periodic thermodynamic states the **fixed-lag normalized action covariance converges to $(G(n)/G(0))^2$**, uniformly in the choice of state at each coupling. To see uniformity, extract a tangent subsequence of any proposed violating sequence; all such limits give the displayed moments. This conclusion combines Z4's conditional law and moment bound with C4's reflection geometry, without invoking a Ward identity.

Two scope qualifications apply to Z4's summary. The pressure bounds carry a finite-size remainder proportional to $(\log\beta)/L$ before the fixed-coupling thermodynamic limit; the constant 60.2 in the displayed argument is for the resulting limit states. Also, gap closure as $\beta\to\infty$ alone allows a strictly positive gap at every finite coupling. The massless weak-coupling phase of four-dimensional compact U(1) has a separate proof. The same distinction applies when using it as a discriminator.
