## Bond reflection and the two gauge averages

Write $P_+$ and $P_-$ for the Haar averages over vertex gauges in the two disjoint halves, and $\theta$ for bond reflection. For a complex test $F$ of links internal to the positive half, integrating a gauge transformation of the **whole** original configuration gives
$$
\mathbb E[\overline{\theta F}F]
=\mathbb E[\overline{\theta F}\,P_+F]
=\mathbb E[\overline{\theta(P_+F)}\,P_+F].
$$
The first average can move seam links, but the measure is invariant and the negative test uses none of them. The second equality uses the negative vertex group, which fixes $P_+F$, and $P_-\theta=\theta P_+$. This order lets both tests survive the later change of gauge. Each crossing link on either seam can now be set to the identity. At side at least four their endpoint pairs are disjoint. For fixed seam data this is translation of the remaining link variables on the left and right by fixed group elements, preserving product Haar measure. Each crossing plaquette then pairs two reflected tangential links through $\exp(\beta\,\mathrm{ReTr}(UV^*)/N)$ times a constant. Its positive feature expansion is the exponential series of the real matrix inner product. The tensor product over both seams yields a squared norm. For the forward time shift this gives $0\le T\le I$ on the site-reflection space, including its previously exhibited null eigenvectors. Menotti and Pelissetto treat site reflection and discuss the earlier bond-reflection construction. The periodic two-seam calculation here uses the pure Wilson law. [Menotti–Pelissetto, 1987](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01221251)

I checked the order of averaging by independent integer summation on a periodic three-site spatial ring with four time slices and group $\mathbb Z_2$. Give a positive plaquette weight 3 and a negative plaquette weight 1. For two rings $x,y$ and three temporal signs $u$, the strip weight is $w(x,y,u)=3^{\#\{i:x_i y_i u_i u_{i+1}=1\}}$. Put $D(x,y)=w(x,y,\mathbf1)$, $K(x,y)=\sum_u w(x,y,u)$ and let $P$ be the unnormalized sum over the eight vertex gauges on a ring. Then $K=DP$ and $PDP=8K$. For a test on a half-torus put $v(x,y)=\sum_u w(x,y,u)F(x,y,u)$. The direct two-seam sum is $v^\top(K\otimes K)v$. Replacing $v$ by $(P\otimes P)v$ in the fixed-seam sum and dividing by 64 reproduces it. The open-edge sign gave zero. Fixing both seams and skipping the averages gave 537,395,200. The direct sum for a gauge-invariant plaquette sign was 17,246,978,048. These are unnormalized count checks on the auxiliary lattice.

## Each plaquette occurs once

For a face in directions $i,j$, a site cut through coordinate $r$ maps an in-plane unit interval starting at $t$ to one starting at $2r-1-t$. A transverse bond cut through $r-\tfrac12$ acts on the face’s single transverse site by exactly the same index rule. No transverse copy touches a cut link. Each reflected word has the identical real trace. For each axis of even length $L$, an already-filled cross-section can therefore be copied along contiguous runs. An off-center cut of a run of length $2j$ gives parts of lengths $j-1,j+1$ and their reflections give runs of lengths $2j-2,2j+2$, including the full wrap. Together with the single-layer Cauchy–Schwarz step this yields $Z_1^L\le Z_L$ in A4’s notation. Applying the four inequalities successively gives one copy at each of the $\prod L_i$ anchors even when the four sides differ. The numerator cancels the action of this entire orientation. With a transverse forest of length $L_h$, the remaining chain exponent is $3(1-1/L_h)V$ and the trial-measure exponent is $(3+1/L_h)V$. This gives A4’s finite-volume seam term and, at fixed coupling followed by the thermodynamic limit, $\mathbb E e^{Y_p}\le6^{9/2}$ for fundamental SU(2). Uniform integrability obtained this way for $e^{\theta Y_p}$ applies at $\theta<1$.

## A mixed-moment consequence

Take the real normalized site-scalar fields in Z4’s reduction, with $Z_i>0$, spacing $a\downarrow0$ and H3(b) at the orders specified below. Split $k$ insertions into two nonempty groups of sizes $l$ and $k-l$. All centers within either group have pairwise Euclidean distance at least $\delta>0$. In one coordinate the entire second group lies later than the first by a gap $D\ge d>0$, measured between the nearest layers. Let $S'$ be the same mixed moment after moving the entire second group one step farther along this coordinate. There is a constant depending only on $k,d,\delta$ and the moment bounds such that $|S'-S|\le C a$ for all sufficiently small $a$.

To see this, use that coordinate as time. Put $p=\lfloor D/(4a)\rfloor$, $n=D/a-2p$. Reflect the first group into the positive half and translate the two groups separately to place their nearest respective field centers at layer $p$. Let $v,w$ be the two resulting half-space vectors, keeping all transverse coordinates. For small $a$, $p\ge1$, so even the fields’ one-link buffers lie in the closed half. By reflection symmetry of each site scalar and translation invariance,
$$
S=\langle v,T^n w\rangle,\qquad S'=\langle v,T^{n+1} w\rangle .
$$
Each squared norm is a reflected, normalized moment, of order $2l$ or $2(k-l)$. All center separations in it are at least $\min(\delta,2pa)$, bounded below by $\min(\delta,d/4)$ for small $a$. H3(b) at these two orders bounds the norms independently of the coupling. For $0\le x\le1$, $(n+1)(1-x)x^n\le(1-x)\sum_{j=0}^n x^j\le1$. Thus
$$
|S'-S|\le \|v\|\,\|w\|/(n+1)\le 2a\,\|v\|\,\|w\|/d.
$$
The same argument applies in every coordinate by lattice symmetry. Its hypothesis is an empty interval in the direction of displacement. A pair of sites separated transversely at equal height need not provide such an interval for that displacement. H3(b) at the actual clock, unrestricted H3(c), rotation invariance of the limit and a nonzero continuum mass remain open.
