# The exceptional normal leg and its transverse distribution

## Review of the starting estimate

C4’s normal one-leg calculation at a964c45 clears. With $I_j=\int x^j b(x)\,dx$ for $j=0,2$, the transverse integral is $2I_0I_2$. In the longitudinal integral the mean of $(2\sin(x/2))^{14}$ is 3432. Including the factor $1/2$ from the curvature amplitude, both signs and $\int_H^\infty x^{-9}dx=H^{-8}/8$ gives $b_N=3432 I_0I_2/[4(2\pi)^3]=43186/(315\pi)$. Smooth compact tests of $P_3/H$ periodize on the interval of width $2\pi R/H$. Their Fourier coefficients have uniformly bounded absolute sum. For integer shifts larger than $R$, summing the rapidly decreasing transform gives $O_K(H^{-K})$ for every fixed $K$. The remaining shifts cost $O(R^{7/2}/\sqrt\beta)$ in total at each lag. Both errors are $o(H^{-8})$ under the stated hypothesis. The scalar normal mass converges after multiplication by $H^8$ to $L_s b_N$, where $L_s=12s^2v/(1+4sv)^{5/2}$. This independently checks the scale and Fourier cutoff requested in C4's review. [Starting theorem](../../../../central-quotients/tangent-curvature/periodic-limit/directional-tail/README.md)

## Free law at two momentum scales

Let $W_R$ be the one-color curvature measure in block units, $v_R$ its mass, and $W$ its continuum-block limit. Its normal-tail density for $x=|p_3|$ is bounded by
$$
 C x^{-9}(q_1^2+q_2^2)(1+|q_1|)^{-8}(1+|q_2|)^{-8},\qquad q=(p_1,p_2),\quad x\ge1.
$$
This follows from the lattice symbol and $|2R\sin(p_i/(2R))|\asymp |p_i|$ on the representative cube. At $x=Hy$, $y$ bounded away from zero and infinity, and $q$ in a fixed compact set, the leading scaled density separates into $y^{-9}$, the oscillating factor $(2\sin(Hy/2))^{14}$, and $(q_1^2+q_2^2)b(q_1)b(q_2)$. More precisely the polarization factor times $x$ tends uniformly on such sets to $(q_1^2+q_2^2)/2$; the lattice denominator corrections are $1+o(1)$. Period averaging replaces the oscillation by 3432, even against a continuous joint test in $(y,q)$. The displayed majorant controls both transverse truncation and the region $y>M$. The one-leg energy divided by $H$ tends to $y$ on these compacts since $H/R\to0$. Each sign contributes half. Thus under $|p_3|>H$ one leg has limiting law $(Y,\sigma Y,Q)$ in energy, normal momentum and unscaled transverse momentum, with independent $Y,\sigma,Q$.

At determinant order $j$ sum $N=2j$ independent draws from $W_R/v_R$. For fixed $N$, a normal exceedance of the sum requires a leg of normal magnitude at least $H/N$, even after folding. The probability of two such legs above $\delta H$ is $O_{N,\delta}(H^{-16})$. To keep the *unscaled* transverse law of the spectators, impose instead a fixed cutoff $M$ on the full momenta of the other legs. The portion with a designated leg above $H/N$ and another of norm above $M$ costs at most $C_N H^{-8}\Pr_{W_R/v_R}(\|p\|_\infty>M)$ by independence. This is negligible after first taking the scaled limit and then $M\to\infty$. The transverse momentum of the designated leg can also be truncated by the displayed integrable majorant. On the retained events the spectator normal momenta shift the threshold by at most $NM$, their energies divided by $H$ vanish, and their transverse components are added in full. Replacing $H\pm NM$ by $H$ costs $o(H^{-8})$ at fixed $M$ by the continuous Pareto tail. Normal folding changes event or sign with probability $O_N(R^{-8})$; transverse folding disappears on the retained bounded transverse configurations. The unconditional spectator laws converge to $W/v$ and are independent of the designated draw. These steps give the $N$-fold choice of exceptional leg and preserve the ordinary spectator convolution. [Related scalar conditional large-deviation result, Armendáriz–Loulakis](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.1516)

The conditional order weights are proportional to $2j w_j$, where $w_j=(1+2sv)^{-3}(3/2)_j z^j/j!$. A union bound at $H/N$ dominates the rescaled order mass by $C N^9$ times its uniformly geometric weight (up to a polynomial), allowing summation in the limit. Substituting $j=k+1$ yields the negative binomial law in the statement. The normal mark $(Y,\sigma)$ has the same law at every order, giving its independence from $T$. If $\phi$ is the characteristic function of the transverse marginal of $W/v$, the conditional limit has
$$
 \widehat{\mathcal L(T)}(\xi)
   =\widehat{\mathcal L(Q)}(\xi)\,\phi(\xi)
       \left[\frac{1-z}{1-z\phi(\xi)^2}\right]^{5/2}.
$$
The marginal is symmetric, so $\phi$ is real. Fourier inversion writes the transform of $b$ as $2\pi$ times the centered eightfold unit-box spline (with an immaterial reflection). The transform of $x^2 b(x)$ is its negative second derivative. Both are supported on $[-4,4]$, proving the claimed compact Fourier support in two dimensions.

## Wilson transfer retaining the transverse scale

Use products $f(P_1,P_2)g(P_3/H)$ with $f$ Schwartz of compact Fourier support and $g$ smooth of compact support away from zero. On the representative momentum cube replace $f$ by its $2\pi R$-periodization. Its uniform alias error is $O_{f,K}(R^{-K})$ for every fixed $K$, including near a seam, since all noncentral translates have at least one coordinate of size $\pi R$ or more. Its Fourier coefficients have uniformly bounded absolute sum by the lattice Riemann-sum bound and vanish at shifts of norm greater than $L_f R$. Periodize $g$ with period $2\pi R/H$, retaining its Fourier coefficients at shifts of length at most $R$ as in C4’s proof. The product therefore uses translations of length at most $(L_f+1)R$ (enlarge $L_f$ for the $\ell^1$ convention); its retained coefficient norm is uniformly bounded. The total error against either bounded-mass measure from periodization and truncation, plus the Wilson comparison error, is
$$
 O_{f,g,K}(R^{-K}+H^{-K})+O_{s,f,g}(R^{7/2}/\sqrt\beta)=o(H^{-8})
$$
for $K>8$. This applies also to lags $m\lfloor R/H\rfloor$ for each fixed nonnegative integer $m$ by the time-uniform comparison. Thus polynomial tests of $x=\exp(-\lfloor R/H\rfloor E)$ include the equal-time mass. [True-translation comparison](../../../../observable-normalization/transfer-increments/spatial-packet/strip/rectangles.md)

These tests determine the vague limit with normal variable bounded away from zero, transverse variable in $\mathbb R^2$ and $x\in[0,1]$: band-limited Schwartz functions approximate transverse compact smooth tests uniformly by cutting off their Fourier transforms. The approximation error is multiplied by the rescaled mass on the normal support of $g$, which is bounded by the normal-tail estimate. Products and polynomial tests then approximate continuous compactly supported tests there. Restricting to $|P_3|/H>1$ introduces a boundary of zero limiting mass. More explicitly, vague convergence on this open event already identifies the full candidate measure there. Its mass equals C4's separately established limit of the scalar normal tail, including both signs and all orders. Vague convergence with convergence of total masses gives tightness and weak convergence on the event: even transversely escaping mass would be counted in that scalar total. The free limit has $x=e^{-Y}>0$ almost surely; $\lfloor R/H\rfloor/(R/H)\to1$ returns the energy coordinate. This proves the conditional Wilson assertion. Using the generic sixth-power maximum-norm bound alone would leave an error of order $H^2$ in this last tightness argument.
