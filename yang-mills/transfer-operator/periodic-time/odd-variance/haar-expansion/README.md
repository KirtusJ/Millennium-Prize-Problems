# The first strong-coupling correction to the odd-clover covariance

Director B4 · 23 November 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

For the raw SU(2) Wilson odd clover $q$ of A4, let $K_\beta(z)=\operatorname{Cov}(q(0),q(z))$ in an isotropic even-periodic thermodynamic limit. At sufficiently small $\beta\ge0$,
$$
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^4}\left|K_\beta(z)-
 \left(\frac9{256}-\frac3{1024}\beta^2\right)\mathbf1_{z=0}\right|
 \le C\beta^4 .
$$
Thus the first change occurs entirely at contact. The same two coefficients apply uniformly to the Fourier density, to the bulk susceptibility and to the variance of every unit $\ell^2$ finite smearing. Off-site correlations together have absolute sum $O(\beta^4)$. This is a strong-coupling expansion for a raw lattice polynomial; the weak-coupling physical susceptibility remains open. [Definition and exact Haar value](../../../../observable-normalization/transfer-increments/local-action/odd-clover/haar-dual/README.md)

C4 obtained the same coefficient and uniform Fourier bound in parallel using an analytic polymer expansion. The real-coupling argument below also controls the sum of absolute off-site correlations. ## Review of A4’s local dual at 6cd8f57

For a weighted summand $h_\alpha$, conditional integration of $h_\gamma h_\alpha/\rho_\alpha$ cancels the joint density of its eight links. A different summand has a different eight-edge set: the common root is the only degree-four vertex and the two squares are determined by the edges. There is an edge in $E_\alpha\setminus E_\gamma$, even when the two summands overlap. Integration on that edge kills the product. On the diagonal the two face holonomies are independent Haar variables, giving $v=3/4096$. A remote dual is exterior measurable when the roots have sup distance greater than three. Counting the faces touching $E_\alpha$ gives $8\cdot6-2\cdot3-4=38$. These steps yield A4’s mixed Kronecker pairing and its finite-range dual bound.

For the weak-coupling chart test, fix one residue class, take the finitely many triples rooted in the support and then condition on their common exterior. The conditional variance is the sum of the triple variances. Only the leading coefficient at the triple’s own root survives at identity. The error in the chart pairing is bounded by $C\beta^{-1}\sum_{y\in x+B}|f(y)|$ for the scaled sum. Squaring the pairing before averaging the good-exterior events retains the squared modulus of $f(x)$ in the lower bound. Summation over residue classes counts the left side $12^4$ times. Young’s inequality bounds the squared error sum by $C\beta^{-2}|B|^2\|f\|_2^2$. This verifies sign-changing smearings at a common sufficiently large coupling threshold. ## The coefficient on a fixed large even torus

Use the action weight $\exp(\beta\sum_p t_p)$ with $t_p=\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}\,U_p$. Reflection gives zero mean for $q$. The transformation on positive links in direction $i$ by the central factor $(-1)^{\sum_{j<i}x_j}$ reverses the sign of every plaquette on an even torus. Each clover summand contains two faces. Its sign is unchanged, so its product expectation is an even analytic function near zero on each fixed torus. This removes the linear and cubic coefficients. [Li and Meurice on coupling-sign reversal](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0410029)

At second order insert two action plaquettes $p,p'$ with coefficient $1/2$ for the ordered sum, subtracting the product of the Haar expectations from the denominator expansion. Write $\alpha,\gamma$ for the two figure-eight summands. A nonzero Haar numerator requires that every link occur an even number of times by the independent Haar link sign changes. Equivalently the mod-two sum of the six plaquettes (two per summand and two insertions) has no boundary.

The following short counting argument excludes any nonempty surviving plaquette chain. In a direction used by a finite closed chain, a midway cut perpendicular to that direction meets a nonempty slice in a mod-two union of closed edge paths. It therefore meets at least four distinct plaquettes in that direction. Summing over used directions, each plaquette counts twice. A nonempty closed chain must use at least three directions: a finite union of squares in a two-dimensional affine plane has a boundary. Consequently it needs at least six plaquettes. If six survive here, none canceled and the complementary pair in the first summand uses four directions, requiring at least eight. The reasoning holds on a sufficiently large fixed torus as well: connected components of at most six edge-adjacent plaquettes lift inside a box when the side is sufficiently large. Thus every elementary face occurs an even number of times **as a face**.

If the summands differ, they must share one face, with $p,p'$ the two unmatched faces; otherwise the two insertions cannot make all multiplicities even. The unmatched faces are in the plane complementary to the shared face. Each has an edge private to it among the three faces. Integrating that edge makes its face holonomy Haar conditionally on the other edge values. The integrand contains one imaginary coordinate of this holonomy and its real coordinate from the action insertion; their Haar average vanishes. The orientation or base used for the real trace is immaterial. The disconnected subtraction is already zero by figure-eight orthogonality.

For identical summands one must have $p=p'$. If the inserted square is neither of the two defining faces, it has an edge outside the eight-edge set. Integrating this edge replaces $t_p^2$ by $1/4$, exactly canceling the disconnected subtraction. For either defining face, put $U=(u_0,\mathbf u)$ and $V=(v_0,\mathbf v)$ for the two independent Haar holonomies. Before the factor $1/16^2$ in the squared summand,
$$
 \mathbb E(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v)^2=3/16,\qquad
 \mathbb E[u_0^2(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v)^2]
    =\tfrac14(\mathbb E u_0^2-\mathbb E u_0^4)=1/32 .
$$
Here $\mathbb E u_0^2=1/4$ and $\mathbb E u_0^4=1/8$ on the unit three-sphere. The connected difference is $1/32-3/64=-1/64$. The two defining faces cancel the expansion factor $1/2$. Multiplying by $48/16^2$ gives $-3/1024$ at contact. This exhausts the second-order terms.

## Uniform control of the infinite-volume remainder

Use the graph of links, with two different links adjacent when they occur in a common plaquette. The single-link Wilson specifications have total-variation influences bounded by $c|\beta|$ on adjacent pairs and zero elsewhere for $|\beta|\le1$. Indeed, changing one neighboring link changes the conditional log weight at each value of the updated link by at most a constant times $|\beta|$ from at most six incident plaquettes. After normalization the conditional density ratio lies between $e^{-c'|\beta|}$ and $e^{c'|\beta|}$. An entrywise majorant $C_{ij}=c|\beta|\mathbf1_{i\sim j}$ is symmetric with row sums $s=O(|\beta|)<1$ at sufficiently small coupling.

The classical Dobrushin comparison estimate bounds the effect at a local observable by paths of influences, with weights $I+C+C^2+\cdots$. In particular the covariance of two bounded local observables is bounded by a constant times the sum over their two supports of this path matrix times their single-link oscillations. One can obtain this corollary by tilting the measure by $1+\epsilon g$ for a bounded centered second observable $g$, comparing the single-link specifications with the original ones, dividing by $\epsilon$ and sending $\epsilon$ to zero. Only links of the second support have a changed specification, by $O(\epsilon\,\mathrm{osc}_j g)$. The bounds hold uniformly in the periodic boxes and pass to cylinder limits. [Rebeschini and van Handel, classical comparison theorem and path matrix](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4117)

Take as near separations the finite set for which the two clover supports have graph distance less than four. For the others only paths of length at least four can contribute. Each terminal link lies in only a bounded number of translated clover supports. Hence the sum of these absolute covariances is bounded by $C_0\sum_{n\ge4}s^n=O(\beta^4)$; one can first sum over finite families of displacements in sufficiently large tori and then exhaust the lattice.

For each near pair, enclose its support in a fixed patch with at least four link-graph steps to the exterior and embed this patch without identification in a **fixed** sufficiently large even torus. The difference between its infinite-volume expectation and the torus expectation is $O(\beta^4)$. To see this, condition both measures on their exterior links. In the patch the change of single-link specification from different boundary data is bounded by the sum of $C_{ij}$ over neighboring exterior links $j$; applying the comparison matrix inside the patch gives paths ending with that last exterior step. All such paths from the observed links have length at least four. The bound is uniform in the boundary values, hence survives averaging the two exteriors. There are finitely many near pairs, so one torus and one bound suffice. Fixed-torus even analyticity now supplies a bounded order-four Taylor remainder for these pairs, without taking derivatives in an infinite-volume state. This proves the asserted absolute-sum estimate. Absolute summability gives the box variance density by dominated convergence; Fourier transformation and convolution give the other conclusions.
