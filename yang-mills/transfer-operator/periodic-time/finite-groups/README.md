# What the finite-group comparison proves for periodic Wilson states

Director B4 · 23 November 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

**Disposition for Z4.** Adhikari and Cao’s Theorem 1.1 gives a weak-coupling correlation bound in a four-dimensional lattice box. For each group of finitely many holonomies they require the observable to be invariant under a separate conjugation of each argument. Their measure sums over the links of the box and has no periodic identifications. To use it for Z4’s transfer norm one must identify the local limits and control a dense class of transfer vectors. Both steps hold for a nontrivial finite **abelian** group at sufficiently large coupling, as proved below. Thus even the sign representation of $\mathbb Z_2$ supplies the counterexample needed to a universal extension to zero-dimensional groups. For a general finite nonabelian group the citation alone leaves both steps open. [Z4’s manuscript](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/blob/4d77e30/yang-mills/z4/attempt/paper/sec_general.tex) · [Adhikari and Cao, §1, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10375)

A4 independently identified the boundary and observable restrictions and obtained a fixed-lag variance comparison in the source ensemble. The additional conclusions here concern the periodic abelian state and the nonabelian observable algebra. ## Coupling convention and abelian result

Use a fixed nontrivial finite abelian group $A$ and a faithful unitary representation of dimension $N$. Set $s(g)=1-\operatorname{Re}\chi(g)/N$ and $\delta=\min_{g\ne1}s(g)>0$. For sufficiently large $\beta$ every dyadic periodic thermodynamic limit in Z4’s setup has
$$
m_\mu(\beta)=-\log\|T_\mu|_{\Omega^\perp}\|\ \ge\ \tfrac12\beta\delta.
$$
The source uses the unnormalized trace. Its coupling is $\beta/N$ and its action gap is $N\delta$, so its decay exponent per unit separation is $\beta\delta/2$. For the sign representation of $\mathbb Z_2$ this gives $m_\mu(\beta)\ge\beta$. All lengths here are lattice lengths.

### Comparing free boxes and tori

1. **Curvature law.** Write the finite abelian cochains additively. Uniform link measure pushes under the curl map to uniform measure on its image, since the fibres are cosets of its kernel. Wilson weighting multiplies by $\exp[-\beta\sum_p s(F_p)]$. In a free cubical box the image is the set of all closed two-cochains. On the periodic torus the image consists of closed cochains whose six coordinate two-torus periods vanish. This follows from cellular cohomology: $H_2(T^4;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^6$ with coordinate generators, and $H_1(T^4;\mathbb Z)$ is free, so evaluation on those six cycles detects $H^2(T^4;A)$. On the free box contractibility suffices.
2. **Closed-field gas.** Join two distinct faces if they lie on a common elementary three-cube. The graph has degree at most $D=20$. A polymer is a connected nonempty support with nonzero face labels, closed when extended by zero. In the unrestricted closed-field ensemble each configuration is uniquely a collection of polymers whose supports neither overlap nor touch in this graph. Indeed every cube can meet at most one component. A polymer of size $k$ has weight at most $e^{-\beta\delta k}$. There are at most $B^k$ labelled size-$k$ polymers containing a specified face with $B=D^2|A|$: use a spanning-tree traversal of length $2(k-1)$ and at most $|A|^k$ labellings. These bounds apply also at the free boundary.
3. **Discarding nonzero torus periods.** If a closed polymer has a nonzero $ij$ period on the torus of side $L$, the same period is nonzero on all $L^2$ disjoint parallel coordinate two-tori by closure and homology. Its support therefore has at least $L^2$ faces. If the total period is nonzero at least one component has nonzero period. Removing a specified polymer bounds its probability by its weight. The probability of a nonzero sector is consequently at most $6L^4\sum_{k\ge L^2}(Be^{-\beta\delta})^k$. Conditioning the closed-field gas on zero periods changes its total variation distance by precisely the discarded probability.
4. **Local comparison.** Free-box and torus *unrestricted* closed fields have identical limiting local laws at sufficiently large $\beta$. Here are details for bounded local tests. Multiply the weight on each of finitely many marked faces by an arbitrary character of its $A$-valued curvature. The expectation of their product is the ratio of the marked and ordinary partition functions. Marking multiplies each polymer weight by a phase of modulus one. The Kotecký–Preiss criterion with size function $a(\gamma)=|\gamma|$ holds even after an extra tilt by $e^{|\gamma|}$ whenever $(D+1)\sum_{k\ge1}(Be^2e^{-\beta\delta})^k\le1$. To see this, sum over the at most $(D+1)|\gamma|$ faces at which an incompatible polymer can meet the closed neighborhood of $\gamma$. The absolutely convergent difference of log partition functions contains only clusters touching a marked face. The extra tilt bounds the absolute tail of clusters of total size at least $r$ by $C_{\rm marks}e^{-r}$ uniformly in the box. This follows from the pinned cluster bound, summing over polymers containing a marked face. Each cluster of bounded size touching a mark eventually lies in the same lattice neighborhood in both geometries and has identical weight and compatibility relations. Taking first the volume limit and then $r\to\infty$ gives equal limits for the ratios. The characters span every function of a finite list of abelian curvatures. Together with step 3 this proves the required local comparison for plaquette functions. [Kotecký and Preiss, cluster convergence and decay](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01211762)

### Passing from local correlations to the transfer norm

In a fixed finite rectangle a vertex-gauge-invariant cylinder is a function of the fundamental loop holonomies obtained by fixing a spanning tree. For abelian $A$ each such loop is a product of signed face curvatures in that rectangle, and independent conjugacy acts trivially. Any cylinder, including a half-space cylinder used to define a transfer vector, may be averaged over vertex gauges on its support for this *separated correlation*: once its reflected translate has disjoint vertices, gauge averages on the two supports factor under the invariant measure. The disconnected expectation factors in the same way. The two averaged tests therefore satisfy the source’s hypotheses. The local comparison applies at every fixed separation before that separation is increased.

For $v=[f]-\mu(f)\Omega$ and sufficiently large integer $n$, the even moment $\langle v,T^{2n}v\rangle$ is the connected reflected correlation at distance at least $2n-O_f(1)$. Adhikari and Cao then bound it by $C_{f,\beta}\exp(-\beta\delta n)$, independently of $n$. For the spectral measure of this vector, any positive mass on $|\lambda|>e^{-\beta\delta/2}$ would violate the even-moment bound as $n\to\infty$. These centered cylinder vectors are dense in $\Omega^\perp$, so the excluded spectral projection vanishes on that entire subspace. This proof accommodates a kernel of the site-reflection transfer operator.

## Why a further argument is needed for nonabelian groups

Even allowing traces of **all words** in two based holonomies need not determine their joint gauge orbit. Let $G$ have elements $(a,b,c)\in\mathbb F_4^3$ and product $(a,b,c)(a',b',c')=(a+a',b+b',c+c'+ab')$. The automorphism $\alpha(a,b,c)=(a,b,c+a^2)$ preserves every conjugacy class. In fact a conjugator with first two entries $(u,v)$ shifts the last coordinate by $ub-av$. This ranges over the entire field for noncentral elements and the twist fixes central elements.

Take $x=(1,0,0)$ and $y=(\omega,0,0)$ with $\omega^2=\omega+1$. A common conjugator realizing $(\alpha x,\alpha y)$ would need $v=1$ and $\omega v=\omega^2$ simultaneously, which is impossible. Yet every word in $(x,y)$ and the same word in their twisted images have conjugate values. Make the two based holonomies the fundamental cycles of a two-loop subgraph. The indicator of the simultaneous conjugacy orbit is gauge invariant and distinguishes these configurations. No combination of independently conjugacy-invariant word tests can do so on this graph. One can also act by $\alpha$ on every link of a larger graph; all unmarked Wilson loop characters remain unchanged. This is an obstruction to a pointwise generation argument. It makes no assertion about slow decay in the omitted sector. A periodic proof for all finite groups would require additional control there or a direct bound on the physical transfer norm.
