# The first spatial splitting and its volume bound

Give each spatial plaquette its own coupling $b_f$. Write $a(b)=\int_{SU(2)}e^{b\chi/2}dg=2I_1(b)/b$, defined at zero by continuation. Differentiation of the finite partition function gives $\Gamma_{pq}=4\partial_p\partial_q\log Z$. A single independent Haar plaquette contributes $v(b)=4(\log a)''=1-b^2/8+5b^4/384+O(b^6)$ on the diagonal. To find the first interaction, divide each Wilson factor by $a(b_f)$ and expand the product of $1+h_f$ with $h_f=e^{b_f\chi_f/2}/a(b_f)-1$. If a chosen edge lies in exactly one chosen face, integrating that link kills the term. This follows from bi-invariance of Haar even when every other edge is held fixed.

Any nonempty connected set of at most six distinct squares surviving this test in the cubic lattice is the boundary of a unit cube. To prove this, take a vertex maximizing the sum of its three coordinates. Incident edges there use only the three negative directions. Their link graph has minimum degree two, since every occupied edge must occur twice. The graph must therefore be the whole triangle. The three corner faces are forced. They leave six edges to be covered, grouped in three pairs by the neighbor of the maximal vertex at distance one. Two parallel exposed edges are diagonally displaced and cannot be opposite sides of a single square. Two exposed edges meeting at a distance-two vertex already span a corner face. Each remaining square therefore covers at most two exposed edges. A square covering two must complete one of the three pairs at a distance-one vertex, and that pair determines its square. With at most three squares left, precisely the three missing cube faces are forced. The same argument works for connected sets of at most six squares on $L\ge8$ tori. In each axis at most six unit intervals occur, so one can cut an unused interval and lift the connected support to the cubic lattice. The [finite corner calculation](GirthShell.lean) enumerates all covers of the exposed edges given the possible incidence masks.

Thus through total degree six the normalized partition is one plus one multilinear term per elementary cube. On its oriented boundary a fundamental trace occurs once per face. Each shared link appears once with each orientation. Schur orthogonality identifies the two endpoint indices separately and divides the weight by two. This leaves one free binary color per vertex, producing $2^{8-12}$. With the six $b_f/2$ insertions the contribution to $\log Z$ is
$$
  4\prod_{f\subset\partial c}(b_f/4).
$$
Lower-degree interactions are absent, so taking the logarithm preserves this first term. Two different derivatives from one cube give $b^4/256$ after multiplying by four and setting all couplings to $b$. Two derivatives on the same face give zero for this term.

At common coupling the degree-five covariance coefficient also vanishes for $L\ge8$. By multilinearity it is a sum of Haar joint cumulants of seven plaquette traces, with two marked entries. A disconnected dependency graph has zero joint cumulant by link independence. Each connected support of at most seven squares fits in a cut-open box by the unused-interval argument. On the lifted box multiply each link in direction $j$ by the central sign $(-1)^{\sum_{i<j}x_i}$. Every relevant plaquette changes sign and Haar is unchanged. Each cumulant of odd total order is its own negative. This reasoning allows repeated marked or unmarked faces, including $p=q$.

For a volume-uniform operator statement one must bound a row of the remainder. On $|b|<\rho=1/1024$ the linked local estimate gives $|\Gamma_{pq}|\le8e^8$ on every torus. By Cauchy's inequality the absolute degree-$n$ coefficient is at most $8e^8\rho^{-n}$. It vanishes unless $p,q$ belong to a connected dependency graph formed by at most $n+2$ faces counting both marks. This follows by taking mixed derivatives of the log moment-generating function of the Haar traces. Their independence across disjoint sets of links factors the generating function. Hence contributing $q$ are within $n+1$ face adjacencies of $p$. Their number is at most a constant times $(n+2)^3$, uniformly on cubic tori, by lifting a chain and bounding its coordinate displacement. The same bound covers the diagonal and the independent-face function $v$. The summed absolute tail from degree six is $O(|b|^6)$ for $|b|\le\rho/2$. Symmetry and the row-sum bound give the same operator norm error. [Holomorphic bound and proof](../connected-rectangles/analyticity.md)

Let $C$ be the **unsigned** cube-by-face incidence, $C_{cf}=1$ on the six boundary faces. Every periodic face belongs to two cubes. Distinct faces occur together in at most one cube. The previous calculation proves
$$
 \Gamma=vI+z_s^4 A+O(\beta_s^6),\qquad A=C^*C-2I .
$$
The diagonal of $A$ is zero and each row has ten ones. On cube space $CC^*=6I+J$, where $J$ sums over the six nearest neighbors. With one face per direction based at each site, the Fourier row of $C$ has entries $1+e^{ip_j}$ up to phase conventions. Consequently $A$ has eigenvalue $4+2\sum_j\cos p_j$ along the conjugate incidence row and eigenvalue $-2$ on its kernel. At $(\pi,\pi,\pi)$ the row vanishes. The latter value then has multiplicity three. This is an exact statement for $A$. For the interacting covariance it specifies the three bands to the stated norm error. In particular $\|\Gamma\|=v+10z_s^4+O(\beta_s^6)$ uniformly; the proof only needs the zero-momentum test and the upper norm bound.
