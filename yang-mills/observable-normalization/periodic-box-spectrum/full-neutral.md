# Ordered neutral levels on every fixed periodic box

Director A4 · 23 November 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

**Result submitted for independent review.** For each fixed periodic rectangular SU(2) Wilson box with all three side lengths at least two, the ordered full gauge-invariant, center-neutral energies satisfy, at each fixed index counting multiplicity,
$$ E_j^{\rm neutral}=E_{*,N}+\beta^{-1/3}n^{-1/3}e_j+o_N(\beta^{-1/3}),\qquad n=N_1N_2N_3 . $$
Here $E_{*,N}$, $e_j$, and the probability-normalized isotropic time step have the conventions of the [central-patch calculation](README.md). On a cube the first neutral excitation gap thus has coefficient $(e_1-e_0)/N$. This is a weak-coupling limit at fixed regulator size. In particular it gives no simultaneous limit with $N$ increasing and no four-dimensional continuum mass gap.

## Independent readback of B4’s two-site argument

At [B4’s revision `acfcd44`](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/blob/acfcd44/yang-mills/observable-normalization/second-box/intermediate-shell/README.md), convolution of the three constant links has a nonnegative product Fourier multiplier. Deleting all but link $j$ increases that multiplier and hence its quadratic form. The magnetic half-weight stays on both sides of this comparison. For the two-site product with the hard transfer $G$, the inequality $D\otimes G\le gD\otimes I$ uses positivity of $D$ and $0\le G\le gI$. It holds on entangled inputs and survives compression by an arbitrary sector indicator. To compare magnetic multipliers pointwise, one must then take the absolute value of the test on each frozen fiber: pointwise ordering alone is not an ordering of operators with different end factors.

On the slow shell $|c_i|\ge\kappa r$ the pair $i,j$ gives $S(c)\ge(\kappa r)^2|P_{c_i^\perp}c_j|^2$. After $c_j=sy/\sqrt8$ each perpendicular half-weight is $e^{-m y^2/4}$ with $m=\kappa^2r^2/4$. The two perpendicular coordinates give the factor $e^{-2\operatorname{arsinh}(\kappa r/4)}$. Its linear deficit coefficient is $\kappa/2$, in agreement with an independent real-space quadrature. The hard vacuum factor is retained exactly. Below, at general side lengths, replacing $G$ by its norm too early in the *linear interaction* would lose a term of order $r$.

The passage to ordered levels also checks: neutrality makes the eight localized inner components translates of a single component, so requiring orthogonality to the first $j$ patch modes costs at most $j$ constraints on the original neutral vector. The squared cutoff partition has bounded overlap in the angular and dyadic variables. Its one full-kernel localization error is $O(s^2/r_0^2)$, independent of the number of shells. These checks support B4’s conclusion for the full two-site neutral box with C4’s exterior input. The following two arguments remove the period-two restrictions.

## 1. Fixed exterior: a strict minimum from winding walks

Use dimensionless quaternion angles. On the full compact link space let $F$ concatenate plaquette quaternions minus the identity, so $S=|F|^2/2$. For a closed set $A$ the compactness and affine Gaussian comparison in [C4’s exterior estimate](../../central-quotients/tangent-curvature/periodic-limit/two-site-exterior/README.md) give, on any fixed box,
$$ \limsup\|1_A T 1_A\|\le\max_{x\in A,F(x)=0}e^{-E(x)},\qquad
 E(x)=\tfrac12\sum_j\omega(\sigma_j(DF_x)^2) . $$
The singular values here are in quaternion angles with electric variance $s^2$; the estimate is zero for an empty flat set. To check the extension in dimension, localize the full link form on normal balls of radius $\rho=\beta^{-3/8}$. The squared partition costs $O_N(s^2/\rho^2)$ by the Wilson jump second moment. Each magnetic end is dominated by its affine model via $|F|^2\ge(1-\epsilon)|b+Dz|^2-C_N\rho^4/\epsilon$ with $\epsilon=\beta^{-1/4}$. The exponent error is $O_N(\beta^{-1/4})$. The flattened kinetic kernel is bounded by $1+O_N(\rho^2+s^2)$ times a normalized Gaussian with variance $s^2/(1-O_N(\rho^2))$. Translating the component of $b$ in the range of $D$ leaves separated oscillators, with the remaining component of $b$ decreasing the norm. This uses no lower singular-value estimate, so rank changes do not affect uniformity on the compact fixed box. Balls near a nonflat limit have exponentially small norm since $S$ there is bounded below. Taking a convergent subsequence of worst ball centers proves the claim.

Every flat SU(2) connection with periodic links is determined up to site gauge by a commuting triple of cycle holonomies, since parallel transport on the universal cover is path independent. Use diagonal commuting $N_i$th roots as constant links. Let $\theta_i$ be their adjoint angles per link. The three color charges have momentum shifts $0,\theta,-\theta$. At each such momentum the linear plaquette map is the covariant lattice curl and its square has two transverse eigenvalues $m(k)=2\sum_i(1-\cos k_i)$ and a longitudinal zero (at $m=0$ all vanish). This gives one $\omega(m)$ per momentum per charge in $E$. Thus strict minimization on the flat locus reduces to
$$ \sum_p \omega(m(p+\theta))\ge\sum_p\omega(m(p)),\qquad p_i=2\pi k_i/N_i . $$

Indeed $\omega'(x)=x^{-1/2}(x+4)^{-1/2}$ for $x>0$. Each factor is a Laplace transform of a positive density on $(0,\infty)$ by the gamma integral. Convolving the two densities and integrating in $x$ gives $\omega(x)=\int_0^\infty(1-e^{-t x})\,d\nu(t)$ with strictly positive density on every open interval. This identity also holds at zero. For $t>0$, expand
$$ e^{-tm(k)}=e^{-6t}\exp\!\left[t\sum_i(e^{\mathrm i k_i}+e^{-\mathrm i k_i})\right]. $$
Its absolutely convergent Fourier coefficients are positive sums of nearest-neighbor walk weights. Averaging over grid momenta retains precisely the walks whose net displacement has components $N_i w_i$ for integers $w_i$. Pair each walk with its reversal. The difference of the heat traces (central minus shifted) is a sum of nonnegative terms proportional to $1-\cos(\sum_i N_iw_i\theta_i)$. For each $i$ there is a positive-weight straight walk of $N_i$ steps along that axis. The difference is strictly positive unless **every** $N_i\theta_i$ is a multiple of $2\pi$. These conditions also suffice for equality by grid relabeling. Integrating the heat traces with $\nu$ gives the displayed inequality with the same strictness; the sums are finite and each integrated $1-e^{-t m}$ is finite. Equality means each cycle holonomy is central in SU(2). There are exactly eight such flat gauge orbits. On the complement of any fixed open neighborhood of their union the compact flat locus therefore supplies a norm deficit $\eta_N>0$ below $g=e^{-E_{*,N}}$ for all large $\beta$.

## 2. Intermediate radii: retain the hard vacuum until the linear term cancels

Near each central orbit use the additive coordinates and notation of the patch calculation. Let $t=(|c|^2+|v|^2)^{1/2}$. On a shell $r/2\le t\le2r$ restrict further to a sector where $|v|$ or one $|c_i|$ is at least $\kappa r$ with fixed $0<\kappa<1/4$. Take $c_0h|\log h|\le r\le\delta_N$ for a fixed $c_0>0$. The hard sector satisfies $S(c+v)\ge a_N|v|^2$ and its compression norm is at most $\exp[-a_N\kappa^2r^2/s^2]$, uniformly below $g/2$ for small $h$.

For a slow sector put $U=\{|c|\le2r,\ |c_i|\ge\kappa r\}$, and let $M(c)=1_U e^{-S(c)/(2s^2)}$, preserving the exact slow magnetic action. The following comparator acts on $L^2(U)$ times **all** hard coordinates. Write $D=M C_s M$ where $C_s$ is the normalized Gaussian of slow variance $s^2/n$. Its leading kernel is $D(c,c')G$; add
$$ -\tfrac12 D(c,c')[b_c G+G b_{c'}]. $$
Here $b_c=z^T DM_0[c] z/2$ is linear in the unscaled $c$. On inputs supported in the original shell sector the comparator form differs from the exact transfer form by at most $C_N(r^2+s)\|f\|^2$. This follows by applying the electric estimate on $t\le2r$ and expanding the magnetic factors there as in the patch calculation, keeping $S(c)$ exactly. The half-weight remainder after the linear insertion is $O_N(r^2+s)$ uniformly in $z$; the product of two linear insertions costs $O_N(r^2)$. No approximation outside the retained shell is asserted. Enlarging the comparator domain is legitimate because we only use its upper form bound on supported inputs. In particular one must not compress the hard vacuum projector by a $v$-dependent angular cutoff and treat it as a projection.

On this enlarged space project onto the full hard vacuum ($P$) and its complement ($Q$). The $P$ block is precisely $gD$ because each endpoint insertion has zero hard-vacuum expectation. The mixed norm is $O_N(r)$, and the $Q$ block has upper bound $g-d+O_N(r)$ with fixed $d>0$. For $D$ use the positive convolution comparison from the two-site readback, freezing $c_i$ and retaining a distinct $c_j$. Uniformly for small $r$,
$$ S(c)=2n\sum_{a<b}\operatorname{sinc}^2|c_a|\operatorname{sinc}^2|c_b|\,|c_a\times c_b|^2
 \ge (n/8)(\kappa r)^2 |P_{c_i^\perp}c_j|^2 . $$
Thus after $c_j=s y/\sqrt n$ the two perpendicular oscillators have the same conservative $m=\kappa^2r^2/4$ as above. Taking absolute values within each fiber gives $\|D\|\le e^{-2\operatorname{arsinh}(\kappa r/4)}\le1-b r$ for a fixed $b>0$ on a small interval. Free parallel motion has norm one.

Using $2kxy\le 4k^2x^2/d+dy^2/4$ on the off-diagonal block with $k=O_N(r)$ now bounds the whole comparator by $g(1-br)+O_N(r^2)$ after choosing $r$ small enough that the $Q$ upper bound lies below this. Restoring the form error, shrinking $\delta_N$ and using $s/r\le\sqrt h/(c_0|\log h|)$ gives a uniform sector deficit $\|1_{\rm sector}T1_{\rm sector}\|\le g(1-b' r)$ at each fixed box. This is where the positive complementary gap converts the genuine order-$r$ interaction into an order-$r^2$ cost. Estimating that interaction by its norm without the two blocks would not give this inference.

## 3. Counting the full ordered neutral levels

Use B4’s square partition on each centrally translated chart. The inner cutoff is supported at $t\le2r_0$ with $r_0=h|\log h|/8$, inside both faces of the growing rectangle. Dyadic radial and four angular cutoffs cover the shells out to a fixed small radius; a single exterior cutoff is supported away from a smaller fixed neighborhood of the eight orbits. The vector of cutoffs can be chosen globally Lipschitz with constant $C_N/r_0$ using bounded overlap and the fixed compact based chart. 

**Cutoff details (B4, addressing Z4's read-back).** Take Euclidean norms induced by the sum of squared link-coordinate norms on the hard slice, and $|c|^2=\sum_i|c_i|^2$ on the constant coefficients. Thus both $|c|$ and $|v|$ are at most $t$. If the rectangle is instead implemented with other fixed norms, take $B_N\ge1$ bounding both rectangle norms by $B_N$ times these Euclidean norms and use $r_0=h|\log h|/(8B_N)$. This changes only the fixed positive constant at the first shell. In either convention $r_0/h\to\infty$ and $s^2/r_0^2=o_N(h)$.

For the Lipschitz estimate, the pinned site action is free: fixing a configuration and the pinned site forces all site rotations to be the identity by propagation along edges. In the central orbit chart the derivative of the orbit-and-slice map at the identity is $(a,w)\mapsto d_b a+w$, an isomorphism by the orthogonal slice decomposition. Translation along the compact based orbit gives the same invertibility everywhere on it. The saturated chart constructed in the patch argument therefore has inverse slice coordinate with bounded derivative on a smaller closed tube (a finite cover suffices). Pull back the radial and angular cutoffs there. In a shell of radius $r$ the angular functions depend smoothly on the unit slice direction with derivative $O_N(1/r)$; the radial bump derivatives obey the same bound. After normalization to a squared partition, bounded overlap and a uniform positive lower bound on the pre-normalization vector norm give an $\ell^2$ derivative bound $C_N/r_0$. Choose the outer transition inside the tube, making the cutoff vector constant (only the exterior component survives) on a collar before its edge. The eight tubes are disjoint and the cutoffs can be center-translated. The derivative bound now holds on the full link manifold; integration along a shortest product-metric geodesic gives the global Lipschitz estimate, including pairs in different charts. [Z4's two questions](../../z4/attempt/reviews/j1-box/report.md)

The full normalized Wilson jump second moment is $O_N(s^2)$. The symmetric kernel identity with $\frac12\sum_k|\chi_k(x)-\chi_k(y)|^2$, followed by the two Schur bounds, costs $O_N(s^2/r_0^2)=o_N(h)$, including for sign-changing inputs. Use the based and globally invariant cutoffs with the eight center families permuted by center flips.

In the neutral space inner components of this isometric localization are translates. Orthogonality of one inner component to the first $j$ eigenvectors of the larger single rectangle costs at most $j$ linear constraints and bounds the sum of its eight localized forms by the rectangle’s $j$th transfer eigenvalue times their total squared norm. The exterior lies a fixed distance below $g$ in transfer norm and the shell tops lie at least order $r_0\gg h$ below $g$. For fixed $j$ these are smaller than the $j$th rectangle eigenvalue. Summing and using min–max gives the global $j$th transfer eigenvalue bounded above by the patch value plus $o_N(h)$. The center-symmetrized patch trials give the reverse bound. The ordered energy law follows since $g$ is fixed and positive. The same estimates alone give no side-length-uniform error or independently calibrated physical unit.
