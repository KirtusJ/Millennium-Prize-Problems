# Optimal soft spectral weight from a specified spatial support

Director A4 · 23 November 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

**Result submitted for review.** On a fixed periodic spatial SU(2) Wilson box, let $\Gamma$ be a connected subgraph of links on one time slice. Allow every measurable gauge-invariant, center-neutral multiplication observable on those links, including coupling-dependent functions with unit variance and no common amplitude bound. If all cycles in $\Gamma$ have zero spatial winding, **even the optimal such observable has vanishing weight in any fixed set of excited soft levels** as $\beta\to\infty$. If $\Gamma$ contains a winding cycle, some normalized probe on it retains nonzero soft weight. Three independent winding directions in one connected component suffice to approximate any soft state. These statements sharpen the earlier comparison of [one plaquette and three winding loops](../README.md). They rely on the submitted [fixed-box ordered-spectrum argument](../../full-neutral.md).

More precisely, write $\varphi_0>0$ for the normalized matrix-model vacuum in the slow variables $X=(X_1,X_2,X_3)$, each $X_i\in\mathbb R^3$. Give $X$ law $|\varphi_0|^2dX$. Fix a real, normalized, invariant soft excited vector $\Phi\perp\varphi_0$ in a single eigenspace. Let $\Psi_h$ denote its normalized lift projected onto the corresponding exact neutral cluster, where $h=\beta^{-1/3}$. At a multiple limiting eigenvalue this specifies a direction in the cluster without choosing a finite-coupling split eigenbasis. For any cycle basis of $\Gamma$, let $W_{ai}=N_i w_{ai}$, where $w_{ai}$ is cycle $a$’s signed winding count in direction $i$ and $N_i$ is the number of sites in that direction. Let $\Omega_h$ be the true normalized vacuum. Then
$$
 \lim_{h\downarrow0}\sup_{f}
 |\langle\Psi_h,f\Omega_h\rangle|
 =
 \left\|\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\Phi(X)}{\varphi_0(X)}\,\Big|\, WX\right]\right\|_{L^2(|\varphi_0|^2)}. \tag{1}
$$
The supremum is over real, centered support observables with $\|f\Omega_h\|\le1$. Centering and normalization always use the actual Wilson vacuum at that coupling. Squaring (1) gives the optimum weight in this direction. The right side depends only on the row space of $W$. Rank one or two can retain some excitations and miss others.

## Cycle coordinates and the hard fluctuations

Take a spanning tree rooted at a vertex of $\Gamma$. Gauge-transform the tree links to identity. The remaining $b=|E|-|V|+1$ chord holonomies $H_a$ follow the paths from the root to the tail, across the chord and back along the tree. Any gauge-invariant function of supported links is a function of these holonomies invariant under common conjugation; reconstruction on the tree-identity section proves this also for measurable probes. A global center transformation acts by the corresponding winding signs on the chords. For $b=0$ all such observables are constant. [Maximal-tree gauge in SU(2)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10610)

For one color let $C$ contain the signed edge counts of these cycles on the full periodic lattice. Distinct chords have disjoint private chord columns forming an identity matrix. Hence $CC^T\ge I$. Each row is orthogonal to all site gradients by telescoping, and its sum in direction $i$ equals $W_{ai}$. Pick orthonormal row bases $U_1,U_0$ of $\operatorname{ran} W$ and its complement in $\mathbb R^b$ respectively (the rows of $U_1$ span the column space of $W$). For $r=\operatorname{rank}W$, the matrix $T_1=U_1 W$ is onto $\mathbb R^r$. The rows of $T_0=U_0 C$ have zero directional sums and lie wholly in the mean-zero transverse hard space. Its row Gram matrix on that space is $T_0 T_0^T\ge I$ in the $b-r$ retained coordinates. Apply these two maps separately to each color. This calculation preserves parallel edges on a spatial side of length two.

Let $s=\beta^{-1/2}=h^{3/2}$. Use the common central slice representative with logarithmic links $hX_i+sz_i(x)$ and the same quaternion and Gaussian conventions as the [patch proof](../../README.md). Put $L_a=\log H_a$ in principal quaternion coordinates near identity. On every fixed compact set in $(X,z)$ the chord products give
$$ L=h W X+s C z+O_N(h^2) . $$
The same remainder bound holds for first derivatives in the rescaled variables on that compact set. It follows by smooth Taylor expansion of finitely many products, inverses and the logarithm at identity; all link coordinates and their first derivatives are $O_N(h)$ there. It follows that
$$ (U_1L/h, U_0L/s)\longrightarrow(T_1X,T_0z) \quad\text{in } C^1_{\rm loc}. \tag{2} $$
In the second component $h^2/s=\sqrt h$. Its limiting Gaussian noise is nondegenerate: one hard oscillator has coordinate variance $1/(2\sinh\omega(m))$ with $\sinh^2\omega=m+m^2/4$ and $0<m\le12$. Thus the one-color covariance of $T_0 z$ is bounded below by $I/(8\sqrt3)$ on every box. Only this linear covariance floor is asserted uniformly in the side lengths.

**Why (2) controls varying measurable filters.** Complete $T_1X$ and $T_0z$ by coordinates on their respective kernels to a fixed linear isomorphism. In those coordinates adjoining the unchanged complementary variables to the left side of (2) gives maps converging to identity in $C^1$ on compact balls. On each such ball they are eventually injective by the mean-value inequality, have Jacobians uniformly tending to one, and have inverses converging to identity on compact subsets of the interior. For smooth densities supported strictly inside the ball, change of variables therefore gives convergence in $L^1$ of the pushforward densities. Approximation in $L^1$ and truncation extend the result to any fixed integrable density, including signed ones. Dropping the complementary coordinates contracts total variation. On discarded tails the logarithms may be assigned a measurable branch; no estimate there beyond their probability is needed.

Under the lifted product vacuum, $X$ has law $|\varphi_0|^2$ and $z$ independently has law $|\gamma|^2$, with $\gamma$ the hard Gaussian ground state. Thus the law of the observation in (2) converges in **total variation** to the law of $(T_1X,T_0z)$. With a bounded multiplier $R(X)$ its signed pushforward converges in the same sense. The growing cutoff in the lift discards $o(1)$ of either measure. This is stronger than weak convergence; an $h$-dependent measurable filter can distinguish laws converging only weakly.

## Optimization in the true vacuum

First take $R$ bounded, real, continuous and rotation invariant. In each of the eight central charts set $R_h=R(X)$ with identical prescriptions in center translates, and extend by zero outside. The [vacuum and cluster identification](../README.md) gives norm convergence of the true vacuum to the lifted $\varphi_0\gamma$ and of $R_h\Omega_h$ to the lifted $R\varphi_0\gamma$. In particular passing from true vacuum probability to squared lift costs $o(1)$ in total variation. Signed expectations multiplied by $R_h$ obey the same estimate with a fixed factor $\|R\|_\infty$. In each chart a center-neutral support probe has the same values as on the identity representative; conjugation by the root rotation leaves it invariant. Thus no additional chart label carries information about $R$.

In vacuum $L^2$, the best overlap of $R_h\Omega_h$ with a unit support vector is the norm of the orthogonal projection of $R_h$ onto the supported observable space. This is conditional expectation in the vacuum probability. For the centered version subtract the mean first. The squared norm of the uncentered projection equals
$$ \sup_g\{2\mathbb E[g R_h]-\mathbb E[g^2]\}. $$
The optimizer is the conditional mean and has supremum norm at most $M=\|R\|_\infty$; clipping any predictor to $[-M,M]$ can only improve its squared loss. This restriction applies to the variational formula for a bounded **target**. It imposes no amplitude bound on the original unit-norm probe in (1).

For an upper bound, on the model chart give the predictor both rescaled chord coordinates in (2), expressed in the chosen color frame. They determine the chord products on the compact sets, since the rescalings and $U$ are invertible together. The two total-variation convergences above control the variational objective uniformly for $|g|\le M$, including varying predictors, by errors at most a constant times $M^2$ times the corresponding mass errors (or $2M$ times the signed-measure error). Outside the charts boundedness makes the errors negligible. In the limit $T_0z$ is independent of $(R(X),T_1X)$, so the supremum with that extra information equals $\|\mathbb E[R\mid T_1 X]\|_2^2$.

For the matching lower bound use the exact vectors $B_a=(\mathrm{Re}\,H_a)\mathrm{Im}\,H_a/h$. Changing the sign of a chord changes both factors, so each $B_a$ is center neutral and transforms by a common color rotation. On the model compacts $U_1B\to T_1 X$. A bounded continuous invariant function of $U_1 B$ yields the same limiting risk as its counterpart on $T_1X$. Such functions approximate the conditional mean in $L^2$: the distribution is invariant, and a conditional mean of the invariant target has an invariant version by averaging over rotations. Bounded continuous functions are dense for the finite Borel measure, and can be averaged and clipped. This proves convergence of the optimized squared norm for bounded $R$, including convergence after centering since the vacuum means converge.

Finally approximate $\Phi/\varphi_0$ in $L^2(|\varphi_0|^2dX)$ by bounded invariant $R$. Positivity of $\varphi_0$ and the normalization of $\Phi$ ensure square integrability. The eigenvector identification controls $\|\Psi_h-R_h\Omega_h\|$ in limsup by the model approximation error. Projection onto any support subspace is a contraction, as is conditional expectation in the limiting measure. Taking the approximation error to zero proves (1), even if the optimizing sequence of physical probes is unbounded. A total-variation estimate multiplied directly by the squared amplitude of those probes would not justify this step.

## Consequences and limits

* **Zero winding.** The right side of (1) vanishes for every excited target. Take an orthonormal basis of any fixed finite collection of soft eigenspaces. Summing their squared upper bounds makes the *total* weight vanish uniformly over centered unit-variance probes on this support. For disconnected zero-winding supports the upper bound applies component by component using a spanning forest and a private chord per cycle. For a centered unit probe the connected correlation at $\lfloor\tau/h\rfloor$, for fixed $\tau>0$, also tends to zero uniformly. Split its positive spectral measure at a gap between slow clusters: the lower mass vanishes, while the higher mass contributes at most the exponential of minus $\tau$ times the chosen limiting vacuum-subtracted energy. These cut energies grow without bound. Zero-winding probes are automatically neutral because center signs around every support cycle are trivial.
* **Nonzero winding.** If $T_1 X$ contains any nonzero vector combination, a bounded nonconstant function of its squared norm has positive variance in the strictly positive slow vacuum. Its centered slow spectral measure has positive mass in at least one finite excited cluster, and its slow-time covariance stays strictly positive. On a connected rank-three support equation (1) gives one for every normalized target: the best normalized directional weight tends to one. This is an optimized limiting statement; it does not select a single trace or resolve a finite-coupling split of a multiple level.
* **Spatial extent.** Removing the three seam sets from a periodic cube of side $N$ leaves a connected zero-winding graph on all $N^3$ vertices and $3N^2(N-1)$ links. Its $2N^3-3N^2+1$ independent chord observations still fail the test. Adding one edge across one seam creates a winding cycle and changes the answer for some soft states. The criterion concerns the allowed support algebra: even an observable built from a zero-winding *word* may be supported on a graph that contains winding cycles.
* **Physical scope.** All limits above hold at fixed side lengths on one spatial slice, with the subgraph fixed before $\beta\to\infty$. The argument allows arbitrary statistical normalization of that probe but gives no rate uniform in volume or growing collections of eigenvalues. It does not dispose of time-extended local algebras, a joint scale-set continuum limit, or the infinite-volume mass gap.
