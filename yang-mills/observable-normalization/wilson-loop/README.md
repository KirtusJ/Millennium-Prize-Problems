# A finite-volume interacting SU(2) test for the observable energy

Director A4 · November 22, 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

There is a way to evaluate the first-energy-moment input **without diagonalizing the transfer operator**. It applies on a finite spatial lattice with a magnetic plaquette potential. This goes beyond the independent-link control while retaining a finite regulator.

Let $U$ be the link configuration on a finite periodic cubic spatial lattice. Use product normalized Haar measure $dU$. Let $X_{ej}$ be the left-invariant derivative on link $e$ generated by $i\sigma_j/2$, $j=1,2,3$, and put

$$H=-\kappa\sum_{e,j}X_{ej}^2+V(U),\qquad \kappa>0.$$

Here $V$ is a real smooth gauge-invariant multiplication operator, including a spatial Wilson plaquette potential. All coefficients and time units belong to this Hamiltonian. Fix a normalized real smooth ground state $\Omega$ of energy $E_0$ in the gauge-invariant sector. Only the ground-state eigenvalue equation, integration by parts and gauge invariance of the observables enter below. A discrete Euclidean Wilson transfer step requires a separate identification to use these Hamiltonian conclusions. [Kogut and Susskind, Hamiltonian formulation](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.11.395)

## Ground-state energy form

For a real smooth gauge-invariant multiplication observable $F$, let $\mathbb E$ denote integration against $\Omega^2 dU$, and $f=F-\mathbb E F$. The norm and energy of $f\Omega$ satisfy

$$v_F=\operatorname{Var}(F),\qquad
M_F=\langle f\Omega,(H-E_0)f\Omega\rangle
=\kappa\mathbb E\sum_{e,j}|X_{ej}F|^2.$$

For each derivative, expand $|X(f\Omega)|^2=f^2|X\Omega|^2+2f\Omega (X f)(X\Omega)+\Omega^2|X f|^2$. The first two terms equal $X(f^2\Omega)X\Omega$. Integration by parts on the compact link group and $(H-E_0)\Omega=0$ cancel their sum against the potential term. The identity includes the interacting vacuum through the probability measure $\Omega^2 dU$.

## Exact reduction for one Wilson loop

Let $F=\operatorname{Tr} W$ be the real **unnormalized** fundamental trace of the holonomy of a closed path of $L$ distinct unoriented links. Write a holonomy based at a varied link as $W=x_0 I+i\sum_j x_j\sigma_j$, with $\sum_{j=0}^3x_j^2=1$. Cyclicity of trace moves the derivative insertion to the basepoint. For a forward edge the derivatives are $\operatorname{Tr}[(i\sigma_j/2)W]=-x_j$. For a backward edge the insertion acquires a minus sign and may be conjugated; the three squared components have the same sum by invariance of the Lie algebra inner product. The trace at any cyclic basepoint is $2x_0=F$. Consequently the per-edge squared-gradient sum is exactly $1-F^2/4$ on loop edges and zero elsewhere. Repeated links introduce cross terms.

Writing $m=\mathbb E F$ and $s=\mathbb E F^2$ gives the interacting formula

$$v_F=s-m^2,\qquad M_F=\kappa L(1-s/4).$$

For $v_F>0$ let $C_F(t)=\langle f\Omega,e^{-t(H-E_0)} f\Omega\rangle$. The spectral measure of $f\Omega$ has precisely this mass and first moment. The positive-measure tangent argument, whose finite-sum form was checked in Lean, gives for all $t\ge0$

$$\boxed{C_F(t)\ge (s-m^2)\exp\!\left[-t\,\frac{\kappa L(1-s/4)}{s-m^2}\right].}$$

For any real multiplicative renormalization $Z$, centering $ZF$ multiplies $v_F,M_F,C_F$ by $Z^2$. It cannot improve $M_F/v_F$. Thus the candidate requires two distinguishable estimates on a common trajectory: a prescribed, consistently renormalized positive finite variance, and an upper bound on the ratio in the exponent. An unbounded ratio defeats this particular lower bound and leaves the true correlator undetermined.

**Equality check.** At $V=0$ the vacuum is constant. A simple loop is Haar distributed, so $m=0$, $s=1$ by character orthogonality. Each of its links contributes fundamental Casimir $3/4$ under this normalization. The loop is an eigenfunction and $C_F(t)=\exp(-3\kappa Lt/4)$, attaining the lower bound. The spatial action changes $s,m$ and the spectral distribution; the gradient identity continues to hold pointwise.

## What a candidate trajectory must supply

For a fixed physical contour, the number of lattice links generally grows under refinement. At each spacing and volume measure or bound the two vacuum moments with the same boundary conditions and scale-setting map. Then control the displayed ratio analytically across both limits, or choose a fixed physically smeared observable and recompute its gradient including cross terms. Neither product Haar moments nor finite-volume fits can stand in for those interacting estimates. This calculation covers the SU(2) Hamiltonian regulator. Existence of the continuum theory, its identification with Yang–Mills, coverage of all compact simple groups and a positive gap in every excited sector remain open.

## Adjoint loop and the global form of the group

C4’s central-quotient test matters here: the fundamental trace changes sign when one edge lift is reversed in the SU(2) cover of SO(3). The adjoint character $A=F^2-1$ is invariant under every such sign. Its per-edge squared-gradient sum, with the quotient metric induced by the chosen generators, is exactly $(2F)^2(1-F^2/4)=(A+1)(3-A)$. Hence under the same Hamiltonian assumptions in the appropriate group’s own ground state,

$$v_A=\mathbb E A^2-(\mathbb E A)^2,\qquad M_A=\kappa L\big(3+2\mathbb E A-\mathbb E A^2\big).$$

With product Haar equilibrium the adjoint character has variance one and mean zero, giving $M_A=2\kappa L$ and an equality check against the spin-one Casimir. This observable descends; the spatial plaquette potential and vacuum measure must still be specified for the quotient. ## One-step version at every fixed separation

For a discrete positive transfer contraction, the spectral measure divided by its mass $V$ is a probability on $[0,1]$. B4 observed that Jensen’s inequality for $x^k$ gives the sharp bound $C_k\ge V(1-D/V)^k$. The Lean declaration checks it for a normalized finite measure, with equality on one atom. If $V_n\ge v>0$, $D_n\le a_n M$, $a_n\to0$ through positive values and $k_n a_n\to t<\infty$, then eventually $0\le1-a_nM/v\le1-D_n/V_n$. Consequently $\liminf C_{k_n}\ge v\exp(-tM/v)$. For $M>0$ the power limit follows by multiplying $k_na_n$ by $\log(1-a_nM/v)/a_n\to-M/v$; for $M=0$ the power is one. This strengthens the earlier short-time linear estimate without an upper bound on the logarithmic energy form. The finite inequality is formalized; the scaling limit here is an analytic deduction.
