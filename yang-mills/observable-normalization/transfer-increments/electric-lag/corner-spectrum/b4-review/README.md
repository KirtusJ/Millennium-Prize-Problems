## A coupling where the full spectral projection matters

Write $q=I_2(g)/I_1(g)$ and $a=I_3(g)/I_1(g)$. A simple square of side $7k$ in the fundamental representation has eigenvalue $q^{28k}$. A simple square of side $3k$ in the adjoint representation has eigenvalue $a^{12k}$. Thus the same coupling solving $a^3=q^7$ produces a coincidence for every positive integer $k$. The two spin networks are orthogonal. One can use disjoint supports in the allowed box.

There is a root in $(1.44014227,1.44014228)$. The check bounds the sign of $I_3^3 I_1^4-I_2^7$ by rational arithmetic at both endpoints. For each Bessel function it sums the defining positive series through index 12, then bounds the remaining tail by the last term times $r/(1-r)$, where $r=(g/2)^2/[13(13+n)]$ for $I_n$. Continuity gives existence. The weaker bracket $(1,2)$ also has an independent integer check in the native worker. Why does this coincidence leave the asserted moments intact? For the entire spectral projection $P$ at the square eigenvalue $r_0=q^{4\ell}$, apply $P$ **after** multiplying a reached simple loop by one plaquette. With no shared edge the product has eigenvalue $r_0q^4$. With one shared edge the singlet and triplet pieces have multipliers relative to $r_0$ of $q^2$ and $q^2a$. On a shared two-edge path the two edges carry the same intermediate spin: their contracted fundamental matrices depend on the product along the path. Decomposing the product of the two fundamental representations therefore gives the simultaneous singlet and the simultaneous triplet. Their relative multipliers are $1$ and $a^2$. Since $0<q,a<1$, only the singlet survives. Integrating the path by Schur orthogonality gives half the detoured trace, or $1/4$ after the magnetic normalization. This exhausts the product even at the exhibited crossing. In the energy derivative $B=-PAP$ each allowed move has amplitude $-1/4$. [Shared-path decomposition](../../bends/README.md)

## How many steps fit on a finite square?

Index the cells inward from each corner. After $d$ steps, the removed cells form four Young diagrams of sizes $m_c$ with $\sum_c m_c\le d$. To prove the induction up to the $j$th step, take $d\le j-1$ and $\ell\ge j+1$. A diagram of size $m_c$ and every in-square cell sharing an edge with it fit in its corner box of side $m_c+1$. Include the original corner cell when the diagram is empty. Any two corners differ in at least one coordinate direction. In that direction their box widths sum to at most $d+2\le\ell$, so the two boxes share no cell. No box reaches the opposite side since $m_c+1\le j<\ell$.

For any interior test cell the boundary edges count the neighbors with opposite occupancy, treating the exterior of the square as empty. Off the boxes, only the original straight sides can contribute, with at most one edge per cell. Inside one box only that corner’s removed diagram and the original sides incident to that corner can contribute. In the inward coordinate quadrant, a retained cell can have missing neighbors only on its two decreasing-coordinate sides. It has both precisely when adding the cell preserves the downward-closed diagram. A removed cell can have retained neighbors only on its two increasing-coordinate sides. It has both precisely when the cell is removable. Thus all two-edge contacts are adjacent and toggle a single legal cell. A planar exterior face and a face normal to the plane have at most one edge on the boundary.

At depth $j$, the unpadded boxes at any two corners have total width at most $j\le\ell-1$ in a separating direction. A full cell separates them. Each nonempty diagram is connected and contains its own original corner, so the removed set recovers its four diagrams uniquely. The boundary determines the filled set by fixing the exterior as empty. This gives the same weighted four-diagram walks used by A4. Their commutator $DU-UD=4I$ gives $\|B^j f\|^2=(2j-1)!!/4^j$. [Stanley, *Differential Posets*](https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/pubs/pubfiles/77.pdf) · [A4’s moment argument](../README.md)

There is a concrete obstruction to continuing this *local move proof* one step further. Remove $\ell-1$ cells in order along the top row starting at the left corner. Every removal is a legal two-edge move. The last occupied top-row cell then has three exposed edges. Its projection requires a different calculation. A direct occupancy enumeration at sides 2 through 9 checked the moment at every depth through $\ell-1$ and reached that obstruction in every case. For side 9, depth 8 it found 3,274 distinct boundaries.

## A stronger bound on slopes carrying square weight

Let $z_n$ be the largest zero of the probabilists’ Hermite polynomial $\mathrm{He}_n$. Under the same conditions, the support of the square’s slope measure reaches at least $z_{j+1}/2$ in the positive direction and $-z_{j+1}/2$ in the negative direction. To see this, use the polynomial basis $h_n(s)=\mathrm{He}_n(2s)/\sqrt{n!}$ for $0\le n\le j$. The established even moments through $2j$ make this basis orthonormal together with A4’s exact sign symmetry, which makes all odd moments zero. The compression of multiplication by $s$ in this basis therefore has zero diagonal and off-diagonal $\sqrt n/2$ between indices $n-1$ and $n$. Its entries use moments at most $2j+1$, including the vanishing last odd moment. The determinant recurrence gives its extreme eigenvalues $\pm z_{j+1}/2$. The variational bounds apply inside the square’s own spectral measure, hence the extreme branches have positive square weight. For side four one may use $j=3$, giving $\sqrt{3+\sqrt6}/2$ versus $\sqrt5/2$ from the ratio of the sixth and fourth moments. The argument gives no lower bound on the overlap of an individual branch.
