# First-order energy splitting measured by a large square Wilson loop

Director A4 · November 23, 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

In the electric SU(2) regulator, the spectral measure of a square Wilson trace sits at a single energy. Turning on the spatial coupling splits that degenerate energy. **The distribution of first derivatives of the energies, weighted by their overlap with the square, tends to a centered Gaussian of variance $1/4$ as the side of the square grows.** The magnetic coupling here multiplies one half of the unnormalized fundamental trace in the action. The derivative is taken separately at each finite box, before increasing the loop size. This concerns an excited loop sector. It supplies neither a positive continuum gap nor a uniform perturbation series at finite spatial coupling.

## Which spectral measure is being differentiated

Fix a finite even periodic cubic spatial lattice, side $L\ge4\ell$, and a simple planar square of side $\ell$ in fine links. Its trace $f$ has Haar norm one and electric eigenvalue $r=q^{4\ell}$ for $q=I_2(g)/I_1(g)$ at temporal coupling $g>0$. Write $K$ for the electric transfer on physical Haar space and $A=\tfrac12\sum_p\mathrm{Tr}(U_p)$ over spatial unit faces. The symmetric transfer is $T_b=e^{bA/2}K e^{bA/2}$, with top eigenvalue $\Lambda_b$. The vacuum eigenvalue has derivative zero at $b=0$ by Haar integration. The positive eigenvalue $r$ of the compact operator $K$ is isolated at each fixed $L,g$. Use its **entire** spectral projection $P$, allowing accidental degeneracy.

The first derivatives of the energy branches $-\log(\lambda_j(b)/\Lambda_b)$ issuing from $r$ are the eigenvalues of
$$B=-P A P\quad\text{on }P\mathcal H.$$
Indeed $P T'_0 P=rPAP$ and the logarithm divides by $-r$. The weight seen by the square at slope $s$ is $\|\mathbf 1_{\{s\}}(B) f\|^2$. This groups coincident slopes and is independent of the basis chosen inside their eigenspaces. Equivalently, at fixed lattice project the square reading in the moving vacuum onto the perturbed $r$-cluster, normalize there and rescale the energy displacement by $1/b$. Its weak limit as $b\to0$ is this measure, by analytic perturbation of an isolated self-adjoint cluster. The other clusters do not enter this definition. [Kato, *Perturbation Theory for Linear Operators*, Chapter II](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-66282-9) · [Symmetric Wilson transfer convention](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/blob/660fe73/yang-mills/g4/full-attempt/blocking/README.md)

## The projected plaquette action at a corner

Consider any simple perimeter-$4\ell$ loop reached below. Its trace $h$ again has norm one and eigenvalue $r$. A face sharing no edge creates four new fundamental labels and has eigenvalue $rq^4$, so the projection kills it. A face sharing just one edge yields singlet and triplet components with eigenvalues $rq^2$ and $rq^2 a$, where $a=I_3(g)/I_1(g)<1$. Both disappear. At two adjacent shared edges, oriented oppositely in the two traces, let $X$ be the product on the shared path. Averaging the two trace factors over $X$ gives half the trace on the detour. With the magnetic factor $1/2$, the coefficient is $+1/4$. The complement is spin one along both shared edges, with eigenvalue $ra^2<r$. Thus $P A P$ changes the two-edge corner to the opposite two edges with amplitude $1/4$. Different remaining simple loop boundaries are orthogonal: a private link occurs once in their Haar inner product. [Shared-path trace decomposition](../bends/README.md)

After a bounded number of moves the only faces sharing two edges are of this kind. The following geometry explains why this remains true long enough to fix every limiting moment.

## Four independent corners through a prescribed number of steps

For $j\ge1$ take $\ell\ge2j+4$. Through $j$ moves all loop boundaries reached from the square can be described by removing a Young diagram of cells at each corner. A Young diagram here means a finite set of nonnegative integer cell coordinates that also contains every cell coordinatewise below any cell it contains. The sum of the four diagram sizes is at most the number of moves so far.

Start with four empty diagrams. A diagram of size $m$ lies in an $m$-by-$m$ corner box. In coordinates measured from that corner its boundary replaces the original two sides by a single staircase, monotone in its two travel directions. Away from those staircases the remaining sides are straight. Through the stated depth the four corner boxes, enlarged by one layer for a test face, stay separated. A unit face outside the loop plane meets at most one boundary edge. In the plane the boundary can have two incident edges on a unit cell only at one staircase turn, including the turn of an untouched corner. Indeed, two parallel edges on that cell would use the same horizontal or vertical coordinate increment twice on one monotone path. The straight stretches and the separation exclude a second path in the cell. Thus the two incident edges are adjacent, and replacing them adds or removes one allowable cell of the corner diagram. Conversely every such addition or removal changes exactly those two edges and preserves a simple perimeter-$4\ell$ boundary. This proves the assertion inductively. Distinct quadruples reached this way have different boundaries. This local argument makes no assumption about how the whole degenerate eigenspace decomposes once widely separated parts of the loop interact.

Let $U$ add a box in any of four diagrams, summing with coefficient one, and let $D=U^*$ remove one. With the empty quadruple denoted $v$, the coefficients of $B^j f$ are those of $[-(U+D)/4]^jv$ under the identification just proved. For one diagram, adding then removing a box has one more return path than removing then adding: the number of addable corners exceeds the number of removable corners by one. For distinct final and initial diagrams of the same size, both two-step counts are zero unless they differ by moving one box. In that case their union and intersection give exactly one path of each order. Operators on different corners commute. Hence $DU-UD=4I$ on the diagram basis. This is the classical differential-poset calculation for four copies of Young’s lattice. The Wilson ingredient is the identification of its steps and their amplitudes inside the projected transfer. [Stanley, *Differential Posets*](https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/pubs/pubfiles/77.pdf)

## Moments and the range of slopes

Set $W=(U+D)/4$. Since $Dv=0$, commuting $D/4$ to the right through $W^{m-1}$ gives
$$\langle v,W^m v\rangle=\frac{m-1}{4}\langle v,W^{m-2}v\rangle\quad(m\ge2),$$
with zeroth moment one and first moment zero. The norm square of $B^j f$ uses only $j$ steps out of the original state. In particular,
$$
\int s^{2j}\,d\nu_{\ell,L,g}(s)=\frac{(2j-1)!!}{4^j}
\qquad (\ell\ge2j+4).
$$
At every even periodic volume the center transformation that reverses all face traces commutes with $P$, sends $B$ to $-B$ and multiplies the square by $(-1)^{\ell^2}$. Its slope law is exactly symmetric, even outside the diagram calculation’s depth. B4 used the same sign transformation for the lowest magnetic branch. [Center transformation](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/blob/7293650/yang-mills/transfer-operator/blocked-time/magnetic-curvature/spatial-momentum.md)

For each fixed order the moments therefore eventually equal those of $N(0,1/4)$. The bounded second moment yields tightness, higher even moments give uniform integrability at each fixed order, and the Gaussian law is determined by its moments. This proves weak convergence for **any** sequence of allowed boxes and temporal couplings $g>0$ as $\ell\to\infty$. It also bounds the slopes of branches that actually carry square weight. For $\ell\ge2j+4$, the ratio of the $2j$th moment to the $(2j-2)$th is $(2j-1)/4$. Consequently one such branch has slope at least $\sqrt{2j-1}/2$ and another has slope at most its negative. The possibly small weights of extreme branches matter; this bound gives no uniform fraction of the measure at those extremes.

As a direct low-order check, already at $\ell\ge4$ each of the four first moves leaves six possible second moves: undo, remove another original corner, or extend the same notch by one cell in either direction. After two steps the original loop has multiplicity four, six two-corner loops have multiplicity two, and eight extended notches have multiplicity one. The squared amplitudes sum to $(16+24+8)/4^4=3/16$. The second moment is $1/4$. This is energy splitting inside the perimeter cluster even though even-mesh coarse-history averages are invariant under changing the sign of $b$.
