This gives Cauchy–Schwarz for the bounded measurable tests used by the chessboard step, including indicators. ## Bond reflection on a periodic lattice

Use the half-integer reflection $x_j\mapsto1-x_j$. Each half contains $L_j/2$ vertex layers. All links between the halves cross one of two seams. At even $L_j\ge4$ those links are disjoint pairs of vertices and form a forest. Take an arbitrary bounded complex positive-half test $F$ using only links with both endpoints in that half. In its reflected pairing, average $F$ over vertex gauges in the positive half. Gauge invariance of the measure and disjointness of the two vertex supports leave the pairing unchanged. The negative-half test can be averaged independently. Reflection identifies these averages, including complex conjugation. Thus it suffices to prove positivity for gauge-invariant half tests.

Fix all seam-crossing links to identity with the corrected forest transformation. Pure half actions and their reflected counterparts are the same. Each plaquette crossing a seam now has Wilson factor, up to a positive constant,

$$K(U,V)=\exp\!\left(\beta\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(UV^*)/N\right),$$

where $U,V$ are the matched tangent links inside the two halves. The argument holds for any fixed finite-dimensional unitary representation. The exponent is a real Euclidean scalar product on matrix entries. The exponential power series is a positive kernel by its symmetric tensor powers. The finite product of these kernels over tangent links on **both** seams is positive, by tensoring their feature vectors. The two reflected interior measures are identical. The pairing is therefore the squared norm of the integrated feature vector against the half weight and test. The kernels are bounded at fixed volume, justifying the integrations. This proves bond reflection positivity also for the original gauge-variant tests, by the two independent half averages. Local continuous pairings pass to the periodic thermodynamic limit.
