# A Wilson exponential moment on all even tori in the thermodynamic limit

Let a four-dimensional periodic box have even sides $L_i\ge4$ and $V=\prod_i L_i$ sites. Take the Wilson action $S=\sum_p s(U_p)$, with $s(U)=1-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(U)/N$ for a fixed compact matrix subgroup in $U(N)$, and normalized probability Haar measure. Fix a face of orientation $ij$. Write $S_{ij}$ for the sum of all $V$ face deficits of this orientation and $Z(\beta)=\int e^{-\beta S}$. For each finite box,

$$\mu_\beta(e^{\beta s(U_p)})\le \left(\frac{\int e^{-\beta(S-S_{ij})}}{Z(\beta)}\right)^{1/V}. \tag{1}$$

**Dissemination over the torus.** For directions $i,j$ use site cuts between adjacent projected unit intervals. For the two transverse directions use bond cuts between adjacent vertex layers. In either case the index of the reflected layer at a cut between $r-1$ and $r$ is $t\mapsto2r-1-t$. The reflected plaquette word has the same real trace. Every copied factor stays entirely inside its chosen closed half for a site cut or its chosen vertex half for a bond cut. [The two reflection forms](README.md)

Here is the step that removes a power-of-two restriction. Fix the layers already filled in earlier directions. Let $Z_n$ be the expectation of the product for a run of $n$ consecutive layers in the next direction of length $L=2m$, with $Z_0=1$. All factors are positive, so logs exist. Translation invariance makes the location of the run irrelevant. A reflection at the end of a single layer gives $Z_1^2\le Z_0 Z_2$. For $n=2j$ with $1\le j<m$, cut the run into pieces of lengths $j-1$ and $j+1$. Both fit in a half-period. Apply Cauchy–Schwarz to the two half products in the reflection pairing. Reflecting each piece into the opposite half produces contiguous runs of lengths $2j-2$ and $2j+2$. Hence $Z_{2j}^2\le Z_{2j-2} Z_{2j+2}$. The sequence $\log Z_{2j}$ is discretely convex. Its first slope is at most its average slope from $0$ to $m$, so $Z_1^L\le Z_L$. Iterate through all four directions. Unlike a site cut transverse to the face, a bond cut moves the first face to its neighbor. All $V$ distinct faces of orientation $ij$ occur exactly once. Their product factor is $e^{\beta S_{ij}}$, proving (1).

**Retained temporal chains.** Pick an axis $h$ perpendicular to the face, and put $L=L_h$. Fix the nonseam forward $h$ links to identity, leaving $E=(3+1/L)V$ free links. Every term in $S-S_{ij}$ is nonnegative. Retain only the $3(1-1/L)V$ plaquettes with one axis $h$ and base $0\le x_h<L-1$. Each becomes the difference of successive spatial links on a path, up to conjugation or inversion. Haar integration along each path gives the upper bound $z(\beta)^{3(1-1/L)V}$ for the numerator, where $z(b)=\int e^{-b s(U)}dU$.

**Trial pressure lower bound.** On the $E$ free links use independent copies of the law $e^{-6\beta s(U)}dU/z(6\beta)$. Denote its matrix mean by $M$. Invariance under inversion makes $M$ Hermitian and unitarity makes it a contraction. If a face has $k$ free links, they are distinct, and the others are the identity. Independence and inversion symmetry give expected plaquette matrix $M^k$ in either orientation. For each real $x\in[-1,1]$, $1-x^k\le k(1-x)$. Thus its expected plaquette deficit is at most $k$ times the expected free-link deficit. Each free link occurs in exactly six plaquettes. Jensen's inequality gives

$$Z(\beta)=z(6\beta)^E\,\mathbb E_{\rm trial}\exp\!\left[6\beta\sum_{e\ {\rm free}}s(U_e)-\beta S\right]\ge z(6\beta)^E.$$

Combining both comparisons yields

$$\log\mu_\beta(e^{\beta s(U_p)})\le 3\log\frac{z(\beta)}{z(6\beta)}-\frac{3\log z(\beta)+\log z(6\beta)}{L}. \tag{2}$$

At fixed $\beta$ the observable is bounded and continuous. In a local weak limit with all sides tending to infinity, this gives the upper bound $[z(\beta)/z(6\beta)]^3$ for the exponential moment. The correction term in (2) is retained for finite sides.

**Fundamental SU(2).** Under Haar probability $s=1-\cos\alpha\in[0,2]$ has density $(2/\pi)\sqrt{s(2-s)}$. The substitution $u=bs$ gives

$$b^{3/2}z(b)=\frac2\pi\int_0^{2b} e^{-u}\sqrt u\sqrt{2-u/b}\,du.$$

The integrand on the old interval and the upper endpoint increase with $b$. Hence $z(\beta)/z(6\beta)\le6^{3/2}$, which yields the claimed $6^{9/2}$ bound. Markov gives the uniform exponential tail and integration of the tail gives all fixed polynomial moments. The one-face Ward comparison with the three-color Gaussian has a further consequence. Its unfiltered rooted face has the Gaussian limit with color variance $1/2$, and the action differs from half its squared norm by a term tending to zero in probability. Thus $O_p$ tends in distribution to $\chi^2_3/4$. The endpoint moment bound here gives uniform integrability of $e^{\theta O_p}$ for every fixed $0<\theta<1$, by taking a slightly larger exponent. Consequently its expectation tends to $(1-\theta/2)^{-3/2}$ in the same even-periodic states as that Ward comparison. For $\theta\le0$ the bounded test suffices. [Program’s SU(2) rooted moment comparison](../../local-action/README.md)

**Other compact matrix groups.** C4’s packing bound for chordal balls of Haar mass $v(r)$ is $v(R)\le(1+2R/r)^{2N^2}v(r)$, with $s(U)$ equal to squared chordal distance. Put $r=1/\sqrt{6\beta}$. Then $z(6\beta)\ge e^{-1}v(r)$ and $z(\beta)=\int_0^\infty 2t e^{-t^2}v(t/\sqrt\beta)\,dt$. Taking the ratio gives an explicit finite bound depending only on $N$, uniformly over closed subgroups and $\beta>0$. This extends the **exponent one** moment estimate to those groups. [C4’s covering proof](../../../../central-quotients/tangent-curvature/periodic-limit/block/uniform-pressure.md)

**Limit of this particular comparison.** For $\lambda>1$ in SU(2), put the link in direction $j$ equal to $(-I)^{x_i}$ with all other links the identity. On the even torus every $ij$ plaquette is $-I$ and all others are $I$. The exponent per site in the fully replicated numerator for $e^{\lambda O_p}$ is then $2\beta(\lambda-1)$. Restrict every link to a sufficiently small fixed ball about this configuration. Uniform continuity of the one-face action bounds the exponent below by $\beta(\lambda-1)V$. The Haar cost is $c_\lambda^{4V}$ for a constant $c_\lambda>0$, and the original denominator is at most one. Thus the replicated upper bound itself grows exponentially in $\beta$ at every $\lambda>1$. This establishes a limitation of (1); the actual one-face moment at exponents above one is not decided by this calculation.
