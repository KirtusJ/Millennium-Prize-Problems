## The exact transfer zero mode

Use Z4’s $\mathcal A_+$ of all bounded continuous cylinder functions of links at times $t\ge0$, with site-reflection inner product $\langle[F],[G]\rangle=\mu(\overline{\Theta F}G)$. Let $F=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}U_{(0,\mathbf0),1}$ for an open spatial link at time zero. Its marginal is Haar by independent gauge rotations at its endpoints. Thus $\mu(F)=0$ and $\|[F]\|^2=\mu(F^2)=1/4$ by the four-coordinate symmetry of the unit quaternion. For any $G\in\mathcal A_+$ the center gauge transformation at just one endpoint of the translated link, at time one, fixes $\Theta G$ and negates $\tau F$. Gauge invariance gives $\langle[G],T[F]\rangle=0$. Density yields $T[F]=0$.

The kernel has a simple relation to the physical gap. Average gauge transformations on the boundary time slice with product Haar measure. They act unitarily on the reflected Hilbert space since they commute with reflection. Their average $P_0$ is an orthogonal projection. On cylinder representatives only finitely many vertices enter. A translated positive-half representative has every endpoint at time at least one, so $P_0T=T$. By self-adjointness $TP_0=T$ as well. Gauge transformations at interior positive-time vertices already act as the identity on classes: they fix every reflected half-test and preserve the measure. Averaging over the finitely many such vertices of a cylinder, and then over its boundary vertices, gives a gauge-invariant representative of the projected class. Thus the physical closed subspace is the range of $P_0$, contains the vacuum, and $T$ is zero on its orthogonal complement. Removing these boundary gauge modes preserves the norm of $T$ on the vacuum complement. This argument makes no assumption about injectivity within the physical infinite-volume space.

The abstract statement of R′ postulates injectivity. On the full Wilson space use the observable’s positive spectral part instead: for $0\le T\le1$ and $n>0$, $\int_{[0,1]}u^n\,d\nu=\int_{(0,1]}u^n\,d\nu$. If that last measure is nonzero, the normalized positive-time ratios in R′ make sense. A zero atom must be included for the original norm at $n=0$. For a self-adjoint site transfer one can likewise work at even times with $T^2$. The spatial plaquette and buffered 24-face scalar have nonzero separated correlators at weak coupling. Lüscher’s positivity theorem concerns physical gauge-invariant states. ## The forest and the extension of L3

The stated gauge action is $U^g_{xy}=g_xU_{xy}g_y^{-1}$. Along a tree edge traversed from a treated vertex the update is $g_y=g_xU_{xy}$, using the inverse edge value on a backward traversal. The draft’s formula $U_{yx}^{-1}g_x=U_{xy}g_x$ reverses the order. With consecutive links $\mathbf i,\mathbf j$ and root gauge one it leaves the second transformed link equal to $-1$. The corrected recursion depends only on the tree links, and left and right Haar invariance on the remaining links prove the same integration identity.

Write $L=2N$ for the number of intervals along the side of Z4’s box. The [Dirichlet Hessian calculation](spectrum.md) identifies the bottom temporal modes responsible for the $L^{-3}$ eigenvalue. The [global rectangle estimate](coercivity.md) bounds even large nonabelian links throughout their chart with polynomial dependence on box size. These yield the following extension of L3 for the entire conditional vector of gauge-fixed interior link coordinates.

* With identity shell and $L=O(\beta^\eta)$, every $\eta<1/21$ gives convergence in total variation to the Dirichlet Gaussian.
* With an actual Wilson shell in a shortest-path shell tree, the [averaged comparison](mean-shell.md) gives convergence in mean conditional total variation for $L=o(\beta^{1/20})$, uniformly over Z4’s dyadic periodic-limit states. The Gaussian has the mean appropriate to that same realized shell.

The bounds are sufficient powers. The conditional distribution on a box has a fixed shell and differs from G4’s compact periodic-box partition function, which retains global holonomies. The previously obtained $m\le C\beta^{-1/7}$ puts the actual correlation length, if finite, beyond these windows. A subsequent [comparison using temporal Wilson lines](temporal-lines/README.md) improves the full thermal-shell window to $L=o(\beta^{1/16})$ and identifies a spatial Gaussian free field in two interiors conditioned on the same shell. It gives a lower bound of order $L/\beta$ on the single-line conditional variance.
