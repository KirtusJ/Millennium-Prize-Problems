# A growing-scale limit for the hypercubic Wilson action density

Director A4 · November 23, 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

Use any thermodynamic limit $\mu$ of even periodic isotropic four-cubes at each fixed coupling in the original pure $SU(2)$ Wilson theory. Let $Y_p=\beta(1-\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}U_p)$. At a site $z$ average over the four incident bases of each of the six plaquette orientations and sum over orientations,
$$
 A(z)=\tfrac14\sum_{p\ni z}Y_p,\qquad A^\circ=A-\mu A .
$$
This is the centered field from Z4’s example of 24 plaquette traces up to the factor $-\beta/4$. Keeping all four faces per orientation makes it exactly scalar under signed coordinate permutations about a site.

Let $r\to\infty$ be integer, $\mathcal Z_r^2=\mu[A^\circ(0)A^\circ(r e_0)]$, with the positive square root whenever the covariance is positive. For fixed $k\ge2$, if $r^{4k}\beta^{-1/8}\to0$, then $\mathcal Z_r\sim 3/(\pi^2r^4)$. The $k$-point functions of $A^\circ/\mathcal Z_r$ on the mesh $r^{-1}\mathbb Z^4$ converge uniformly on compact sets of distinct Euclidean points, including equal-time configurations, and as distributions on Schwartz tests flat on all coincidence diagonals. All limits are uniform over the state choice.

One choice, $r=\lfloor\log(2+\beta)\rfloor$, gives every fixed order along the same trajectory. The limit is the scalar Wick square of three independent free Maxwell curvatures, with the two-point normalization $|x-y|^{-8}$. Its two-point mass-squared measure is $\pi^2 s^2\,ds/192$ for the propagator convention below. Thus this local observable retains weight at arbitrarily small strictly positive masses on the chosen scale. The Wilson gap in these units satisfies $rm(\beta,\mu)\to0$. Regularity and rotations after normalization at the actual correlation length, fixed-coupling gap positivity, and the interacting continuum theory in the Clay problem remain open. ## Finite-coupling comparison in a four-dimensional patch

Put $L=2\lceil\beta^{1/4}/2\rceil$. For any fixed number $q$ of unit face/color tests with arbitrary orientations and sites in a four-dimensional cube of radius $C_0L$ about the root, all products of their common-root curvature components differ from their lattice Gaussian moments by at most $C_{q,C_0}\beta^{-1/8}$. Coinciding faces are allowed. The Gaussian has covariance $P$, the exact two-cochain projection, in three independent colors. We give the changes to the [single-orientation polynomial comparison](../README.md).

1. Fix one coordinate order for **all** roots and all tests. For each face choose a spatial axis $i$ in its plane. Use the degree-three filter $(I-A_{L,i})^3$ from the single-orientation proof along that axis. Its coefficient mass, primitive mass, spatial radius and residual estimates apply to every orientation. All four coordinates of the original support now lie in an $O(L)$ patch.
2. To control an interval average beginning at a site $b$ in that patch, first transport each of its faces by the root path to $b$ followed by the straight segment of length $s$ along $i$, $0\le s<L$. In this frame the vector average has $L^2(\mu)$ norm at most $C/\sqrt L$. Pair successive faces and re-root on the midpoint site plane for each pair of blocks. Under reflection in axis $i$, a face at normal coordinate $x$ becomes the inverse positive face at $2m-x-1$ after straight transport to the midpoint $m$. This also holds when $i$ is the *second* direction of the positively oriented face, by cancellation of the same base-change edge. Real componentwise site positivity gives a nonpositive mean dot product for different pair sums.
3. Compare these straight paths with the fixed ordered paths. Move the appended segment past each later coordinate block. Each exchange costs $C\sqrt{s|b_j|/\beta}$ in $L^2(\mu)$ by the rectangle bound. There are at most three exchanges, and $|b|_1=O(L)$, also for starting points of the translated intervals in $A_{L,i}^2$ and $A_{L,i}^3$. The whole discrepancy costs $C L/\sqrt\beta$ in transporter $L^2$. For restoring a factor in a product, multiply the discrepancy in the adjoint action by the curvature norm and the other $q-1$ observed factors. Their product has bounded $L^2$ by Hölder, the exponential one-face action bound and bounded coefficient masses. Consequently the path correction costs $C_q L/\sqrt\beta$ **in the product expectation**. This avoids requiring an $L^4$ rectangle estimate. The straight vector average itself costs $C_qL^{-1/2}$ against that product by Cauchy–Schwarz. The free restoration costs $C_qL^{-1/2}$ by the $\ell^2$ contraction of $P$. 4. Apply the compact Haar variation to one filtered prefactor $g$ and the product of the other filtered components. For $h=d^*S_Mg$, $k'=dS_Mg$ the half-weighted norm sum is $O(L^{3/2})$ and $\|h\|_\infty=O(1)$, by the order-one and order-four walk differences in the parent proof. A time-oriented face can now induce a nonzero time component of $h$. **Every** root path seen by a differentiated test, including its time trunk, has length $O(L)$. Its commutator therefore still costs $O_q(L/\sqrt\beta)$. The signed direct insertion, action score and coexact cube error cost $O_q(L^{3/2}/\sqrt\beta)$ against the full remaining product, by the same $L^2$ prefix bound and Hölder. The truncation residual vanishes at fixed $L$, centers and coupling. This yields the Gaussian integration-by-parts recursion with a uniform error for every fixed degree $q$. The induction uses only bounded covariance coefficients and finitely many lower moments. Restoring the filtered factors costs a total $O_q(L^{-1/2}+L/\sqrt\beta)$. Our choice of $L$ proves the assertion. [Haar derivative and cube terms](../../../../central-quotients/tangent-curvature/periodic-limit/rate/ward.md)

The identity $2Y_p=|F_p|^2+Y_p^2/\beta$ and the uniform action moments bound the error of replacing each action by $|F_p|^2/2$ in any fixed product by $O_k(\beta^{-1})$. Center each lattice Gaussian quadratic by its own mean. Expanding the actual and Gaussian centered products and applying the comparison through degree $2k$ gives
$$
 \left|\mu\prod_{i=1}^k A^\circ(z_i)-\mathbb E_G\prod_{i=1}^k A_G^\circ(z_i)\right|
 \le C_{k,C_0}\beta^{-1/8}
$$
uniformly in the patch, where $A_G^\circ$ uses the identical 24-face stencil. This step applies directly to the Wilson trace action. ## Free cross-kernel and the continuum scalar

Index the six positive face orientations by $\alpha=(i,j)$, $i<j$. At nonzero Euclidean separation $x$ set $I_x=1-2xx^\mathsf{T}/|x|^2$ and $Q_x=\bigwedge^2 I_x$. In the forward-difference convention the lattice covariance obeys
$$
 P_{\alpha\gamma}(z)=\frac{(Q_z)_{\alpha\gamma}}{\pi^2|z|^4}+O((1+|z|)^{-5})
 \quad (|z|\ge1),\qquad |P_{\alpha\gamma}(z)|\le C(1+|z|)^{-4}.
$$
Indeed $P=d\Delta^{-1}d^*$ on two-forms, with symbol made of products of two first differences divided by $4\sum_i\sin^2(p_i/2)$. Its order-zero principal symbol uses $ip_i$ for each forward difference and $|p|^2$ for the denominator. Its inverse transform off the origin comes from the two signed derivatives of $1/(4\pi^2|x|^2)$ and gives the displayed exterior square. For the remainder, on a smooth annulus of momentum size $u$ the order-$j$ derivatives after subtracting the principal symbol are $O_j(u^{1-j})$ by Taylor expansion. Integration by parts on its rescaling bounds its coefficient by $C_Nu^5(1+u|z|)^{-N}$. Sum dyadic annuli with $N>5$. The periodic part away from zero is smooth. Cutting off the homogeneous continuum principal symbol changes its transform away from the origin by a rapidly decaying term at large $|z|$, since all sufficiently high derivatives of its high-frequency part are integrable. Bounded lattice covariance covers small $z$. This proof permits arbitrary directions and the bounded base offsets in the clover. [Free scalar and Maxwell tensor conventions](https://cds.cern.ch/record/617903/files/0305200.pdf)

At distinct continuum sites the unnormalized limiting field is $\mathcal A=\tfrac12\sum_{a=1}^3\sum_{\alpha=1}^6 :F_{a,\alpha}^2:$, with the above Maxwell cross-kernel. In the lattice Wick calculation internal pairings at each quadratic insertion cancel by centering. Each remaining diagram has $k$ edges and every vertex has degree two. Four adjacent bases per orientation have combined weight one. At positions obtained by rounding $rx_i$, every inter-vertex kernel multiplied by $r^4$ equals its continuum value plus $O_{K,k}(1/r)$ on any compact separated set $K$. There are finitely many contractions for fixed $k$. The Wilson error in the same scaling is $O_k(r^{4k}\beta^{-1/8})$. For $k=2$ the twice-contracted numerator is $\tfrac32\|Q_x\|_F^2/(\pi^4|x|^8)=9/(\pi^4|x|^8)$. This proves the normalization; the relative error at separation $re_0$ is $O(1/r+r^8\beta^{-1/8})$. Thus the normalized limiting field is $(\pi^2/3)\mathcal A$. For the trace sum used in Theorem S the normalized field has the opposite sign, with the same limiting scalar moments since all odd ones vanish.

In particular the difference between any normalized $k$-point reading and its value at a simultaneously rotated, nearest-mesh configuration is $O_{K,k}(r^{-1}+r^{4k}\beta^{-1/8})$, uniformly in the rotation in $O(4)$. On the logarithmic trajectory the error is $O_{K,k}(1/r)$. The limiting separated kernel is smooth. For distinct configurations on the mesh their distance is at least $1/r$; together with the uniform error this gives a Lipschitz bound on compact separated sets there, for each fixed $k$. These are rotation and regularity tests at spacing $1/r$. The hypotheses of Theorem S at spacing $m(\beta,\mu)$ remain unverified.

## Which distributions converge

Let $f\in{}^0\mathcal S(\mathbb R^{4k})$, meaning that $f$ and all its derivatives vanish on every coincidence diagonal. Pair lattice functions with $r^{-4k}$ times the grid sum. Inside a cube with $\max_i |z_i|_1\le L/2$ the uniform Wilson-to-Gaussian error times $r^{4k}$ goes to zero, and the mesh sum of $|f|$ with cell volumes is bounded. Outside the cube, both unscaled centered moments are bounded by $C_k$ by Hölder. The mesh tail of $|f|$ is $O_{f,N}((L/r)^{-N})$ for every fixed $N$. This absorbs $r^{4k}$ since $r^{4k}=o(\beta^{1/8})$ and $r\le\beta^{1/(32k)}$ eventually. Translation of the averaging stencils enlarges the patch by only a fixed multiple of $L$ in its estimates.

For the Gaussian lattice sum, distinct mesh sites at $x_i,x_j$ have each scaled edge bounded by $C|x_i-x_j|^{-4}$ even with the bounded clover offsets, using the global $P$ bound. Put $\rho_i=\min_{j\ne i}|x_i-x_j|$. Splitting each edge bound between its two endpoints gives a product majorant $C^k\prod_i\max(1,\rho_i^{-4})$ for each graph. Summing colors, orientations and slot pairings changes the constant to at most $C^k k!$ since $(2k-1)!!\le2^k k!$. This bound also holds for the limiting hierarchy. Flatness of $f$ gives arbitrary powers of the smallest distance at coincidences with Schwartz decay at infinity, by Taylor's formula at a closest diagonal. The contribution with smallest distance $\le\delta$ is then $O_{f,k}(\delta)$ uniformly in the mesh; coincident grid points contribute zero. On compact sets with separations at least $\delta$ the Gaussian sums converge by the cross-kernel estimate and Riemann summation. This gives distributional convergence for the stated tests. The normalization is a scalar factor already shown to converge after rescaling. Correlations at contact have not been assigned by this argument. ## Fourth cumulant and mass spectrum

The Hodge star on real Euclidean two-forms anticommutes with $Q_x$ because $I_x$ reverses orientation. Each trace of an odd cycle therefore vanishes. Every graph with an odd total number of scalar vertices has such a cycle. For four distinct points let $d_{ij}=|x_i-x_j|$ and $Q_{ij}=Q_{x_i-x_j}$. The connected four-point function of the normalized limiting scalar is
$$
 \kappa_4=\frac1{27}\sum_{(i j k l)}
 \frac{\operatorname{tr}(Q_{ij}Q_{jk}Q_{kl}Q_{li})}
      {(d_{ij}d_{jk}d_{kl}d_{li})^4},
$$
where the sum runs over the three unoriented cycles through the four labeled points. Each cycle has 16 slot assignments, three color choices, and a factor $2^{-4}$ from the vertices. For a collinear configuration the product is the identity on six two-forms, giving $2/9$ times the sum of the three inverse distance products. This nonzero connected four-point function occurs in an explicitly free Maxwell composite. [Prior fourth-order control](../../../../spectral-support/electric-slab/odd-readings.md)

For the normalized two-point function use the four-dimensional Euclidean propagator $\Delta_E(x;\sqrt s)=\sqrt s\,K_1(\sqrt s |x|)/(4\pi^2|x|)$. At every $x\ne0$,
$$
 |x|^{-8}=\int_0^\infty \Delta_E(x;\sqrt s)\,\frac{\pi^2}{192}s^2\,ds.
$$
On substituting $y=\sqrt s |x|$, the remaining integral is $\int_0^\infty y^6K_1(y)dy=2^5\Gamma(4)\Gamma(3)=384$. This gives the positive Källén–Lehmann mass-squared measure of the normalized scalar, including its absolute coefficient, modulo Euclidean contact terms invisible at positive time. A nonnegative smooth positive-time smearing of nonzero integral also has a centered time-translate correlation asymptotic to a positive constant times $t^{-8}$. A positive gap in this scalar sector would bound it by a constant times $e^{-\Delta t}$. This identifies a gapless observable sector on the chosen trajectory. ## Bounds on the actual gap-clock normalization

Write $C_\mu(n)=\mu[A^\circ(0)A^\circ(ne_0)]$. The clover extends one time step on either side of its center. Translate its center one step into the positive half, giving a half-lattice vector $v$ for the centered observable. Reflection about the site plane maps that clover to the one centered a step on the opposite side. Consequently $C_\mu(n)=\langle v,T^{n-2}v\rangle$ for $n\ge2$. Site and link reflection for the Wilson measure give a positive contractive transfer. This representation includes the entire centered vector and allows more than one vacuum. [Wilson transfer construction](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01614090) · [Periodic-state transfer conventions](../../../../central-quotients/tangent-curvature/periodic-limit/spectral.md)

Choose $r=\lfloor c\beta^{1/64}\rfloor$ with $c>0$ sufficiently small. At both $n=r,2r$ the finite-coupling comparison above has absolute error at most $C\beta^{-1/8}$. The free clover covariance is $9/(\pi^4 n^8)+O(n^{-9})$. Thus $C_\mu(r)$ and $C_\mu(2r)$ are each bounded between two positive multiples of $r^{-8}$ uniformly over the states. More precisely, for any fixed $\epsilon>0$ one can choose $c$ and then the coupling threshold so that $C_\mu(2r)/C_\mu(r)\ge 2^{-8}e^{-\epsilon}$. Positivity at $2r$ gives positive spectral measure on $(0,1]$, hence positive correlations at every later time, including for continuous spectrum.

Spectral Hölder gives convexity of $\log C_\mu$ for real lags above two, and positivity of the contractive transfer gives monotonicity. For every integer $N\ge2r$ the secant slope on $[r,2r]$ bounds the slope on $[r,N]$ from below, yielding
$$
 C_\mu(r)\left(\frac{C_\mu(2r)}{C_\mu(r)}\right)^{(N-r)/r}
 \le C_\mu(N)\le C_\mu(2r).
$$
When the lattice gap $m(\beta,\mu)$ is positive, the actual clock is $N=2\lfloor \xi/2\rfloor$ with $\xi=1/m$. The seventh-root upper bound on $m$ gives $N\ge2r$ at all sufficiently large couplings. For constants $a,b,B>0$ independent of the state, the resulting normalization obeys
$$
 a\beta^{-1/8}\exp[-b\,\xi\beta^{-1/64}]\le
      \mathcal Z_N^2=C_\mu(N)\le B\beta^{-1/8}.
$$
For the unscaled sum of 24 half-traces both bounds are multiplied by $16/\beta^2$. This proves strict positivity for this clock covariance conditional on a positive gap, and gives a decaying upper bound. The exponential factor leaves the ratio between half and one correlation length undetermined.
