# Boosting the spectral measure

Assume a symmetric Hermitian conserved rank-two field, translation invariance, an invariant vacuum, and a positive matrix-valued spectral measure supported in the closed forward cone. Use its usual Lorentz-equivariant mass-orbit disintegration. At $s=p^2>0$ the conditional tensor form is transported from the rest frame with invariant shell measure $d^3k/(2E)$. At $s=0$ away from the origin use the open null orbit with measure $d^3k/(2|k|)$. The following argument uses the resulting equivariant representatives of the conditional forms. It does not evaluate an arbitrary measurable density at a privileged point. For timelike masses this is also the standard transverse spin-zero and spin-two spectral representation. [Karateev, *Two-point functions and bootstrap applications in quantum field theories*, §§3.1–3.2, especially Eq. (3.49)](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP02(2022)186)

**Timelike shells.** Represent the positive form by Gram vectors $v^{\mu\nu}$. With rest momentum $(\sqrt s,0)$, conservation in both tensor slots gives $v^{0\nu}=0$. Boost in the direction $n=\mathbf k/|\mathbf k|$. The time-time component becomes $(|\mathbf k|^2/s)v^{nn}$ up to the unitary transport of the fiber. Its squared norm is $|\mathbf k|^4\|v^{nn}\|^2/s^2$. Rest-frame rotations make $\|v^{nn}\|^2$ independent of $n$. If this norm is zero, polarization of the symmetric spatial tensor shows all its rest-frame components are zero. Positive spectral sectors cannot cancel each other.

Equivalently, write the two positive rest-frame weights as $c_0(ds)$ on the scalar projector and $c_2(ds)$ on the traceless symmetric projector, normalized as orthogonal projectors on symmetric three-by-three matrices. The covariant transverse tensor $P^{\mu\nu}=p^\mu p^\nu/s-\eta^{\mu\nu}$ has $P^{00}=|\mathbf k|^2/s$. The $00;00$ contractions of the two projectors are $(P^{00})^2/3$ and $2(P^{00})^2/3$. Hence $d\alpha=(dc_0+2dc_2)/(3s^2)$ in this normalization. Compact bands bounded away from zero avoid any singular convention at the light cone.

**Null shell.** Start with a reference momentum $(1,n)$ in fixed units. Conservation gives $v^{0\nu}=v^{n\nu}$; symmetry then gives $v^{00}=v^{0n}=v^{nn}$. A boost of rapidity $y$ in direction $n$ sends the momentum to $e^y(1,n)$ and multiplies the time-time amplitude by $(\cosh y+\sinh y)^2=e^{2y}$ up to unitary transport. Rotational invariance fixes the reference norm over all directions. The resulting squared norm is $a_0|\mathbf k|^4$, $a_0\ge0$. This uses neither division by $s$ nor an extrapolation from massive particles. Momentum zero is a different orbit and is removed by the detector. No classification of massless helicities is needed for this component.

**Equivalent first-moment test.** In any fixed compact timelike band, or on the null shell, multiplication by $E$ cancels the denominator $2E$ in the raw shell law. Its first moment is half the band coefficient times $\|\Delta\varphi\|_2^2$. For $\varphi(Lx)$ this norm squared scales as $L$, while $\|\nabla\varphi(L\,\cdot)\|_2^2$ scales as $L^{-1}$, with the gradient taken after dilation. If the filtered lattice measures converge vaguely on $(0,\infty)$ for each test to the corresponding candidate law, the raw positive-energy first-moment budget passes as an inequality. Test against $E\chi_j(E)/(1-e^{-s_0 E})^2$ for fixed $s_0>0$, with nonnegative continuous compact energy cutoffs increasing to one on $(0,\infty)$. Then take monotone convergence. This reasoning permits escape of part of the lattice first moment. The positive-time triple proof in the [main note](README.md) avoids the vague convergence assumption entirely.

**Two fields giving different answers.** For a free scalar field $\Phi$, the improvement tensor $(\partial^\mu\partial^\nu-\eta^{\mu\nu}\Box)\Phi$ is symmetric and identically conserved. Its $00$ component is $\Delta\Phi$. It gives exactly the quartic spectral weight at nonzero or zero mass. It is a control on the transformation law and need not generate translations. For a free massive Proca field $A^\mu$ of mass one the positive polarization sum is $P^{\mu\nu}$. Its conserved time component instead has shell weight $|\mathbf k|^2$. Its first moment is $\|\nabla\varphi\|_2^2/2$ in the same Fourier convention. Thus an argument based only on first-moment compactness could not have excluded all nonzero covariant fields.

The [control script](check_shells.py) transports explicit four-tensors, checks conservation in the moving frame, and integrates a compact smooth radial test in the momentum annulus used in the proof. At dilation 64 the massive improvement tensor and the null improvement tensor give annular triples about 1.09544 and 1.09553, respectively. The massive vector gives about 0.00010188. Those numbers depend on the Fourier convention and the chosen test. The different dilation powers are fixed by the tensor and shell measure.
