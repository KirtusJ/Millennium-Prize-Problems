# Pair differences, pair sums and leakage of slower time powers

The covariance of a signed star at one point and its translate by $n\ge1$ temporal steps commutes with spatial permutations and each transverse reflection. In the signed-face coordinates each reflection swaps precisely one opposite pair. The uniform pair-sum mode vanishes because the curl-projection covariance with the signed incidence of one link is the incidence itself, whose support lies in its own slab. The two other invariant spaces are the zero-total pair sums and the three pair differences. Their respective covariance eigenvalues $u_n,v_n$ can be computed directly, without subtracting close matrix entries.

Use spatial $p_j=e^{ik_j}-1$, $a_j=|p_j|^2$ and $A=\sum_{j=1}^3a_j$. Write $d\mu_n=e^{-n\omega}d^3 k/[2\sinh\omega(2\pi)^3]$, $\omega=2\operatorname{asinh}(\sqrt A/2)$. The electric cross kernel before the signed pair map is $(p p^*-A I)d\mu_n$. For the pair sums choose a real vector $w$ with $\sum w_j=0$ and $\sum w_j^2=1$. The adjoint signed pair map sends its normalized lifted vector to $(p_jw_j/\sqrt2)_j$. Thus
$$
 u_n=-\frac12\int\big[A\sum_j a_jw_j^2-(\sum_j a_jw_j)^2\big]d\mu_n,\qquad
 v_n=-\int (2-a_1/2)(A-a_1)d\mu_n .
$$
The first bracket is nonnegative by weighted Cauchy–Schwarz and positive whenever all $a_j>0$, since $w$ is nonconstant. The second integrand is strictly positive in an open set. These expressions give strict negativity and recover the limiting signed-star matrix. At small $k$, write $k=\rho \theta$, $|\theta|=1$. The angular average of $\sum w_j^2\theta_j^2-(\sum w_j\theta_j^2)^2$ is $1/3-2/15=1/5$, using $\mathbb E\theta_i^4=1/5$ and $\mathbb E\theta_i^2\theta_j^2=1/15$ for $i\ne j$. Rescaling $k$ by $1/n$, the radial integral for $u_n$ gives $-4\pi\,5!/[4\cdot5(2\pi)^3]=-3/\pi^2$ at power $n^{-6}$. The $v_n$ integrand has leading numerator $2\rho^2(1-\theta_1^2)$, giving $-2/\pi^2$ at power $n^{-4}$. Dominated convergence follows from $\omega\ge c|k|$, $\sinh\omega\ge c|k|$ across the compact Brillouin cube. Its rescaled majorants are constants times $(|k|+|k|^3)e^{-c|k|}$ in the integration variable after rescaling.

## Designing for the time power alone

For a target $C(n)=O(n^{-8q})$, a single $u_n^{2i}$ term costs $12i$ inverse time powers; a single $v_n^{2j}$ costs $8j$. Hence the least zero multiplicities required of $R$ at $a,b$ are
$$
 m_a=\lceil 2q/3\rceil-1,\qquad m_b=q-1 .
$$
If either multiplicity falls short, a strictly positive pure term decays more slowly. Conversely choose numerator $(z-a)^{m_a}(z-b)^{m_b}$ with $1+m_a+m_b$ distinct negative poles. Every term with $i+j>0$ and $12i+8j<8q$ vanishes by partial fractions. Terms with $i+j>q$ are $O(n^{-8(q+1)})$, and the remaining finite terms have the claimed power or smaller magnitude. The pure $b$ term at order $q$ survives. When $q$ is divisible by three the pure $a$ term at order $2q/3$ also contributes at the leading power. All mixed terms of the same weight vanish since their individual orders are below both first surviving pure orders. The numerator-degree argument proves that $1+m_a+m_b$ poles are necessary. These are minimality results within positive Laplace sources with arbitrary signed coefficients.

For the four-source quartic reading, let $\epsilon_b=\sum_k\widetilde w_k/(r_k+b)$ and $\epsilon_a=\sum_k\widetilde w_k/(r_k+a)$ for the perturbed weights. The exact Gaussian series includes $\tfrac92 v_n^2\epsilon_b^2+3u_n^2\epsilon_a^2$ with all further terms nonnegative. Thus an odd-pair residual large compared with $n^{-4}$, or an even-pair residual large compared with $n^{-2}$, overwhelms an $n^{-16}$ target. In the numerical control at step 24, adding $10^{-4}$ to just the first weight multiplies the Gaussian covariance by about $1.17\times10^9$. The exact mathematical observable uses the Fourier integral for $d$. A simulation using rounded constants must include the leakage allowance.

Bai and Taqqu analyze instability of higher Hermite rank under changes in the input, including shifts. Here the perturbation acts on the coefficients of a bounded function of a Gaussian quadratic form and a sum of squares gives the leakage size. Gaussian quadratic-form transforms and their relation to traces are classical. In lattice spectroscopy Lüscher and Wolff form linear combinations using a generalized eigenproblem to isolate energies. Here the weights are fixed analytically by the local covariance and do not project out individual finite-cutoff transfer eigenvalues.
