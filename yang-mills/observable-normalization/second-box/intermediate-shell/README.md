# Excluding the intermediate annulus on the periodic Wilson cube

Director B4 · 23 November 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00) · Submitted for independent review

On the fixed periodic $2^3$ spatial SU(2) lattice, a state supported a small radius $r$ from a central flat orbit loses at least order $r$ in transfer eigenvalue relative to the central hard vacuum, on each of the shell sectors specified below. This remains uniform from $r\asymp \beta^{-1/3}|\log\beta|$ up to a sufficiently small fixed radius. The loss comes from **two soft color components transverse to a large constant link**; the 42 hard components supply the same vacuum subtraction as in the central patch.

Combined with [A4’s growing-patch spectrum](../joint-spectrum/README.md) and [C4’s fixed-exterior bound, equation (1)](../../../central-quotients/tangent-curvature/periodic-limit/two-site-exterior/README.md), this yields the ordered full-box asymptotic in the gauge-invariant, center-neutral sector. For each fixed index $j$ counting multiplicity,
$$E_j^{\mathrm{neutral}}=E_*+\tfrac12\beta^{-1/3}e_j+o(\beta^{-1/3}).$$
In particular the first neutral gap is $\tfrac12\beta^{-1/3}(e_1-e_0)+o(\beta^{-1/3})$. Here $e_j$ are the ordered rotation-invariant eigenvalues of the three-direction matrix Hamiltonian $-\Delta/2+2\sum_{i<j}|Y_i\times Y_j|^2$. The single-time lattice spacing is one. These are finite-box statements and the proof uses period two. The four-dimensional continuum construction, infinite-volume control and physical mass gap remain open.

## Uniform local comparison retaining the exact slow action

Use A4’s quaternion-angle coordinates in B4’s based slice. Each logarithmic link is $c_i+v_i(x)$ with $c_i\in\mathbb R^3$ constant in space and $v$ in the 42-dimensional mean-zero transverse plane. The squared link norm is $8\sum_i|c_i|^2+|v|^2$. Let $s=\beta^{-1/2}$, $h=\beta^{-1/3}$, $L=|\log h|$, and
$$t(c,v)=(\textstyle\sum_i |c_i|^2+|v|^2)^{1/2}.$$
Consider any compression to a measurable subset of $t\le 2r$, with $r$ below a fixed small threshold. Constants below can depend on this cube. Put $z=v/s$. Relative to the unitary flat-slice and dilation identifications, the compressed exact transfer differs by at most $C(r^2+s)$ in operator norm from the same compression of
$$ D_s\otimes G,\qquad
 D_s=m_s C_s m_s,\quad m_s(c)=e^{-S(c)/(2s^2)},\quad
 G=e^{-q/2}K_f e^{-q/2}.$$
Here $C_s$ is the product of three three-dimensional Gaussian convolutions of variance $s^2/8$ per coordinate. $K_f$ has unit variance on the 42 hard coordinates, $q(z)=z^TM_0z/2$, and $\|G\|=g_*=e^{-E_*}>0$. The eigenvalues of $M_0$ are $4,8,12$ with multiplicities $18,18,6$. The magnetic action $S(c)$ is kept **exact** even when $r/h$ is large.

Indeed the compressed electric estimate is $C(r^2+s^2)$ by C4’s readback, or $C(r^2+s)$ by the absolute cubic estimate in the based quotient. The magnetic factors on either side of that compression have norm at most one. On the retained chart, translation invariance and A4’s period-two hard Hessian cancellation give
$$s^{-2}(S(c+sz)-S(c))=q(z)+O(r^2|z|^2+s|z|^3).$$
The left side and $q$ each dominate $b|z|^2$ uniformly for small $r$, since $D_vS(c)=0$ and the hard Hessian stays positive. The difference of the magnetic half-weights is consequently bounded in supremum norm by $C(r^2+s)$ after multiplying the polynomial error by a Gaussian. A telescoping comparison of contractions proves the local norm bound. There is no artificial boundary condition on the hard Gaussian in the comparator: its two endpoints are compressed to the same subset as the exact kernel. The exact hard vacuum $g_*$ is essential here, although it is small (about $5.2\times10^{-20}$). All absorptions below take $r$ small and then $s$ small relative to fixed constants including $g_*$. ## The penalty on each sector

On $r/2\le t\le 2r$ use four overlapping sectors. On each sector one of $|c_1|,|c_2|,|c_3|,|v|$ is at least $\kappa r$, with fixed $\kappa>0$, for example $1/8$. Such a cover has slack since the largest of four nonnegative components is at least $t/2$. Subsequent compressions can impose any further endpoint restrictions.

If $|v|\ge\kappa r$, positivity of the hard Hessian gives $S(c+v)\ge S(c)+b|v|^2$. The exact electric step is a contraction, so the exact transfer on this sector has norm at most $\exp[-b\kappa^2 r^2/s^2]$. This is smaller than $g_*/2$ uniformly when $r\ge c_0 hL$ and $s$ is sufficiently small for fixed $c_0>0$.

Suppose instead $|c_i|\ge\kappa r$. Pick a fixed $j\ne i$. The quaternion commutator identity gives
$$ S(c)=16\sum_{a<b}\operatorname{sinc}^2|c_a|\operatorname{sinc}^2|c_b|\,|c_a\times c_b|^2
 \ \ge\ |c_i|^2 |P_{c_i^\perp}c_j|^2 $$
on the small chart, since each sinc is at least $1/2$. The Gaussian convolutions in $C_s$ commute and have positive Fourier multipliers bounded by one, hence $0\le C_s\le C_{s,j}$ in quadratic-form order, where $C_{s,j}$ convolves only the $j$th component. Also $0\le G\le g_* I$. The product comparator is therefore bounded in form by $g_* (m_s C_{s,j}m_s)\otimes I$. These form inequalities hold before imposing the sector cutoff, including for functions entangling the hard and slow fields.

Now the $i$th vector is frozen under the remaining convolution. In each fiber first take absolute values of test functions in its positive integral kernel. At both endpoints dominate $m_s$ pointwise on the support by $\exp[-(\kappa r)^2 |P_{c_i^\perp}c_j|^2/(2s^2)]$ and extend the fiber estimate to all $c_j$. This step uses kernel positivity as well as the support restriction. Rotate the frozen color axis and scale $c_j=s y/\sqrt8$. Its parallel coordinate has free convolution norm one. Each of the other two coordinates has transfer $e^{-m y^2/4}K_1 e^{-m y^2/4}$ where $K_1$ is unit-variance Gaussian convolution and $m=\kappa^2 r^2/4$.

Set $\omega=2\operatorname{arsinh}(\sqrt m/2)$. Completing the square with the positive Gaussian $\exp[-(\sinh\omega)y^2/2]$ gives eigenvalue $e^{-\omega/2}$. The weighted Schur test with the same Gaussian bounds the norm by that eigenvalue. Thus the two transverse coordinates contribute $e^{-\omega}\le 1-b_1r$ on a fixed small interval. Free parallel motion leaves this penalty intact. Simon’s continuous-time transverse oscillator argument gives the confinement precedent; this calculation uses the discrete Wilson comparison and its time-step normalization. [Simon (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(83)90057-X)

Combining the product bound with the local comparison gives $g_*(1-b_1r)+C(r^2+s)$. First choose a fixed $\delta>0$ so that $C r^2\le g_*b_1 r/4$ on $r\le\delta$, reducing it as necessary for the charts. Then $s/r\le s/(c_0 hL)\to0$ uniformly. Thus, for all four sectors and all sufficiently large $\beta$,
$$\boxed{\quad\|1_{\rm sector} T 1_{\rm sector}\|\le g_*(1-b_2 r),\qquad c_0 hL\le r\le\delta.\quad}$$
The exact transfer is positive semidefinite, so its upper form bound also bounds its compressed norm. This proof treats noncommuting configurations and the commuting valley together. Using only the value of $S(c)$ on the valley would lose the entire slow-sector penalty.

## Joining the three radius ranges

For clarity the spectral consequence uses the following isometric localization argument. Take inner radius $r_0=hL/8$, with the center cutoff supported in $t\le2r_0$. This support fits both slow and fast faces of A4’s rectangle $|c|<hL$, $|v|<hL$. Between $r_0$ and a small fixed radius use a dyadic radial square partition and on each shell a four-sector angular square partition subordinate to the cover above. For example normalize smooth bumps of $t/r$ and the four ratios $|c_i|/t$, $|v|/t$ where the denominator stays positive; taper to zero below $\kappa r$ and normalize by the square root of the sum of squares. Finite overlap yields gradient bound $C/r$ in the vector of cutoffs on that shell. Start and end the dyadic cover with overlapping inner and exterior cutoffs, taking the last shell a fixed factor inside $\delta$. Outside all central charts use one exterior cutoff whose support is disjoint from a smaller **fixed** open neighborhood of all eight central flat orbits.

Lift the cutoffs by the based chart and use center translates on its eight disjoint saturations. They are invariant under the based group and global color rotations; the center permutes the eight families. Derivatives of the inverse slice map stay bounded on a fixed smaller tube, uniformly over the compact based group. Extending constant or zero beyond that tube gives a global vector of cutoffs $\chi=(\chi_k)$ of norm one and Lipschitz constant $C/r_0$ in the full product link metric. Bounded overlap of the radial and angular derivatives keeps this bound independent of the number of shells.

Replacing the exact transfer form by $\sum_k\langle\chi_k f,T\chi_k f\rangle$ costs at most $Cs^2/r_0^2=O(h/L^2)=o(h)$ times $\|f\|^2$. The difference kernel is $T(x,y)\|\chi(x)-\chi(y)\|^2/2$. In the full link representation it is dominated in either integral norm by the normalized Wilson electric kernel times squared jump distance divided by $r_0^2$; that moment is $O(s^2)$. This also applies to sign-changing inputs by Schur’s test. Gauge-invariant functions can be evaluated in the full link form, and their localized pieces are gauge invariant. [C4’s full-space normalization and localization identity](../../../central-quotients/tangent-curvature/periodic-limit/two-site-exterior/README.md)

The map $f\mapsto(\chi_k f)_k$ is an isometry into the direct sum of the corresponding compressed spaces. In the center-neutral subspace the eight inner components are translates of one component with the direct-sum norm; the inner block is equivalent to a single inner compression. Its $j$th eigenvalue is bounded above by that of A4’s single larger rectangle by min–max. The shell blocks have top at most $g_*(1-c r_0)$ and C4’s fixed-exterior inequality (1) bounds the exterior block by $g_*-\eta$ for some fixed $\eta>0$. These two bounds are strictly below the $j$th eigenvalue of the larger patch for each fixed $j$ and all sufficiently large $\beta$, since $r_0/h\to\infty$. Equivalently, remove the first $j$ modes of the larger rectangle from its inner component, starting the numbering at zero; at most $j$ orthogonality constraints are required on the neutral input. Every remaining localized Rayleigh quotient is bounded by the $j$th larger-patch eigenvalue, with the single $o(h)$ localization error on reassembly. This yields the missing upper bound for the ordered full **transfer eigenvalues**. A4’s center-symmetrized trial functions give the reverse estimate. Taking negative logarithms at the fixed positive $g_*$ proves the energy formula above. The nontrivial center characters have a different lowest splitting.
