## A slice retaining the residual symmetry

Use $T_j=-i\sigma_j/2$, so $[a,b]=a\times b$ and the adjoint angle of $\exp a$ is $|a|$. There are 24 directed links. Norms and Lebesgue measures in their logarithms use the **sum**, without volume averaging, of squared Euclidean link norms. Set $(d\xi)_{xy}=\xi_x-\xi_y$. Pin the site rotation at a root to the identity and call the restricted incidence matrix $d_b$. Its 21 columns are independent by connectivity. The slice $S=\ker d^*=\ker d_b^*$ has dimension 51, with nine constant and 42 nonconstant coordinates.

For $A\in S$ small, set $\Phi(g,A)_{xy}=g_x\exp(A_{xy})g_y^{-1}$ for the based group $SU(2)^7$. At the identity the derivative in logarithmic link coordinates sends $(t,v)$ to $d_b t+v$, an isomorphism. The inverse function theorem supplies a slice neighborhood. After shrinking it, two slice points in the neighborhood cannot be based-equivalent: a transformation carrying one near-identity configuration to another has all neighboring site rotations close, and pinning one site bounds every rotation along a path. Thus it lies in the uniqueness neighborhood. Translating by the based group extends this chart to the saturation; the based action is free. The remaining full-gauge condition is simultaneous SU(2) conjugation. One can work in its invariant $L^2$ subspace through the singular orbit at the origin. Multiplication by any fixed flat central link configuration transports the statement to its center patch.

## Slice measure, including the constant-mode coefficient

Define $B_A t$ by differentiating $\log\Phi(\exp t,A)$ at $t=0$, initially for all site rotations. On a link $a=A_{xy}$ the derivative is
$$ (B_A t)_{xy}=H(a)(t_x-t_y)-\tfrac12\operatorname{ad}_a(t_x+t_y),\qquad
H(a)=\tfrac12\operatorname{ad}_a\coth(\tfrac12\operatorname{ad}_a). $$
The value at zero eigenvalue is one. This follows by differentiating the two endpoint exponentials, or by summing the left and right inverse exponential derivatives. With $r=|a|$, $H$ has parallel eigenvalue one and perpendicular eigenvalue $(r/2)\cot(r/2)$. Write $B_{A,b}$ for the pinned columns and $j(a)=\operatorname{sinc}^2(r/2)$ for the Haar Jacobian normalized at zero, with $\operatorname{sinc}u=\sin(u)/u$. On the slice the density relative to its value at the origin is
$$J(A)=\prod_e j(A_e)\;\frac{\det(d_b^*B_{A,b})}{\det(d_b^*d_b)}.$$
Indeed the 51 slice columns in the logarithmic chart are an orthonormal basis of $S$, so the other columns contribute only their projection onto $\operatorname{ran}d_b$ to the volume determinant. Its sign is positive near zero. Conjugation invariance gives $J(A)=1+O(\|A\|^2)$: there is no nonzero rotation-invariant linear functional on copies of the adjoint color representation.

For constant links $A_{x,i}=c_i$, the normalized determinant can equivalently be computed with mean-zero site parameters and divergence in the mean-zero codomain. The two changes of basis cancel in the ratio: subtracting a constant from a gauge parameter alters $B_A t$ by a global conjugation tangent, which lies in $S$. The period-two directions give zero divergence to each endpoint-sum term at constant $c$. On the nonzero Walsh mode with support $I\subset\{1,2,3\}$ the normalized matrix is $|I|^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}H(c_i)$. This holds for arbitrary small triples, including noncommuting ones. Thus
$$\log J(c)=-\frac{19}{18}\sum_i |c_i|^2+O(|c|^4).$$
Eight link Haar factors per direction contribute $-8/12$. In the orbit determinant $\operatorname{tr}(H(c_i)-1)=-|c_i|^2/6+O(|c_i|^4)$, weighted by $\sum_{I\ni i}1/|I|=7/3$. The two contributions are $-2/3$ and $-7/18$. Only even orders occur along constant triples in this formula. The mean-zero representation also shows translation invariance on the slice. For nonconstant $y\in S$ of zero spatial average in each direction, the derivative of $J$ at any constant $c$ vanishes on $y$ by translation averaging. Consequently $J(c+y)=J(c)+O(\|y\|^2)$ uniformly near zero. For $c=O(\beta^{-1/3})$, $y=O(\beta^{-1/2})$ the leading density correction is quadratic in $c$.

## Operator comparison on the shrinking ball

Put $s=\beta^{-1/2}$, $h=\beta^{-1/3}$ and $P_R=1_{\{\|A\|\le Rh\}}$ in $S$. Identify the based-invariant space locally with flat $L^2(S,dA)$ by multiplying by the square root of the slice density (including its constant value). Denote the resulting compressed electric kernel by $P_R K_s P_R$, and write
$$G_s(v)=(8\pi s^2)^{-51/2}\exp[-|v|^2/(8s^2)].$$
Then, with $G_s*$ denoting convolution,
$$\|P_R(K_s-G_s*)P_R\|_{2\to2}\le C_R(s+h^2).$$

1. **Normalization and exact integral.** Write $F(A,B,t)=\sum_e(1-\operatorname{ReTr}[\exp(-A_e)\Phi(\exp t,B)_e]/2)$ in based group logarithms. The exact kernel integrates $e^{-F/s^2}$ over normalized based Haar and multiplies by the square roots of the two slice densities and the product electric normalization. If the one-link probability Haar density at zero is $c_H$, then one-link normalization for $e^{-(1-\operatorname{ReTr}U/2)/s^2}$ is $c_H(8\pi s^2)^{3/2}(1+O(s^2))$. This follows from $1-\cos(r/2)=r^2/8+O(r^4)$, the Haar formula and Gaussian integration, with an exponentially small complement. In based logarithms the site Haar density is $c_H^7(1+O(|t|^2))$ and the quotient density at zero is $c_H^{17}\sqrt{\det(d_b^*d_b)}$. The constants cancel. The resulting prefactor for $dt$ is $(8\pi s^2)^{-72/2}\sqrt{\det(d_b^*d_b)}$ times $1+O_R(s^2+h^2+|t|^2)$ on the small chart.

2. **Uniform quadratic approximation in the integral.** For $v=B-A$, smooth Taylor expansion in $(v,t)$ about $(0,0)$ with $B$ fixed gives
$$8F=Q_B(v,t)+O((|v|+|t|)^3),\qquad
Q_B=\|v+B_{B,b}t\|_{g_B}^2.$$
Here $g_B$ is the link product of the bi-invariant metric in normal exponential coordinates, in the normalization $g_0=I$. The factor eight follows from the trace convention. The quadratic expression follows by pulling this metric back along the two endpoint maps at their common point. In particular $g_B=I+O(\|B\|^2)$. On $S\oplus\mathbb R^{21}$, both $Q_B$ and $8F$ dominate $c(|v|^2+|t|^2)$ in a fixed small neighborhood by transversality at the origin. Outside a fixed based-identity neighborhood the exact cost is uniformly positive for $A,B$ sufficiently small, by compactness and freeness at zero.

   In the integral, the difference of the exact and quadratic exponentials is bounded by $C(|v|+|t|)^3s^{-2}\exp[-c(|v|^2+|t|^2)/s^2]$. This uses the mean-value inequality with the smaller of the two exponents. Extending the quadratic integral to all $t$ costs an exponentially small tail. After the $t$ integration and the 72-dimensional prefactor, the pointwise error is bounded by $Cs\,s^{-51}(1+|v/s|^3)e^{-c'|v/s|^2}$, plus an exponentially small term with at most polynomial prefactor. This estimate also absorbs the $|t|^2$ and $s^2$ prefactor errors. The $h^2$ part of the prefactor contributes $O_R(h^2)$ in either kernel integral norm.

3. **The orbit tilt.** Let $L=B_{B,b}$ and $M=L^*g_B L$. Completing the square gives the exact integral of the quadratic approximation. Its determinant factor relative to the flat result is $\sqrt{\det(d_b^*d_b)/\det M}=1+O(\|B\|^2)$. Indeed the smooth scalar determinant is invariant under simultaneous color rotations, hence has zero linear term at the origin. The minimized form at $v\in S$ is
$$v^*g_Bv-(L^*g_Bv)^*M^{-1}(L^*g_Bv)=|v|^2+O(\|B\|^2)|v|^2.$$
Here $d_b^*v=0$, $L=d_b+O(\|B\|)$ and $M^{-1}$ is uniformly bounded. Although the orbit tangent tilts to first order, the exponent correction after integration is quadratic. At $B=0$ the Gaussian integration in 21 variables cancels the Gram determinant and leaves exactly $G_s(v)$. At $\|B\|\le Rh$ the difference is bounded by $C_Rh^2s^{-51}(1+|v/s|^2)e^{-c|v/s|^2}$. These bounds integrate in either endpoint uniformly. Schur’s test gives the asserted operator norm, including points at the boundary because both operators are compressed by the same indicator.

For $M_\beta(A)=\exp[-V_\beta(A)/2]$ with the nonnegative spatial Wilson action, multiplication commutes with the cutoff and has norm at most one. Consequently the same error applies to the two **compressed** magnetic sandwiches with the same exact $M_\beta$. Both commute with residual global conjugation. The based kernel itself does not integrate the last global rotation. On invariant functions this remaining group projection is the identity. A kernel between two specified full gauge orbits can have different pointwise asymptotics. The chart coordinates use an orthonormal link norm. For a constant value repeated at eight sites the induced norm is $\sqrt8$ times the per-direction color norm; the slow kinetic coefficient must carry this volume factor.

Here the exact minimized quadratic form in step 3 retains that metric. The norm estimate permits discarding its correction to resolve order $h$ on a bounded patch.
