Let $r_n(\alpha,d)=[f(p+(\alpha+d/2)/L)-f(p+(\alpha-d/2)/L)]/2$ and $C_L=2\sum_{n\ne0}\log\cosh r_n$. Direct integration of two unit Gaussian wavefunctions of precisions $a,b$ gives overlap $[2\sqrt{ab}/(a+b)]^{1/2}$ per real oscillator. Four charged real oscillators per momentum give the factor two in $C_L$. On $|\alpha|+|d|\le\pi/2$ choose centered labels. The distance to a zero of $s$ along the shifted segment is bounded below by a constant times $|n|/L$; this remains true across the coordinate seam by periodicity. The derivatives of $f$ through order three are bounded by $C_j\operatorname{dist}(p,0)^{-j}$, from $s\asymp |p|^2$ near zero and smoothness elsewhere.

For midpoint derivative order $j=0,1,2$, the mean-value estimate gives $|\partial_\alpha^j r_n|\le C |d|/|n|^{1+j}$. Twice differentiating $2\log\cosh r_n$ gives a bound $C|d|^2/|n|^4$. Momentum inversion interchanges the endpoints at the opposite midpoint and the completed sum is even in $\alpha$. Thus
$$ |C_L(\alpha,d)-C_L(0,d)|\le C |\alpha|^2 |d|^2 $$
uniformly in $L$. The paired sum is essential; a bound on the absolute first derivatives alone need not be summable uniformly in three dimensions. At $L=2$ each label equals its own inverse and $C_2(0,d)=0$ exactly. Restoring $n=0$ gives a zero width if an endpoint coincides with the center; the Gaussian overlap formula no longer applies.

There is also a finite limit of this **subtracted overlap cost** at fixed $\alpha,d$ as $L\to\infty$. Pair the midpoint signs before passing to the limit. In each term replace $f((2\pi n+z)/L)$ by $\log|2\pi n+z|$ in the endpoint difference; the additive $-\log L$ cancels. The resulting sum over $\mathbb Z^3\setminus\{0\}$, with the midpoint-symmetrized central difference, converges absolutely by the same second-derivative majorant. Centered domains with one chosen representative at each Nyquist label exhaust the integer lattice; the bound covers those labels as well. Dominated convergence gives the limit and a tail bound $C|\alpha|^2|d|^2/N$ for $|n|>N$. This is a limit of the specified overlap model.

## The normalization and the kinetic coefficient

On the central chart a symmetric endpoint expansion gives the box-dependent coefficient
$$ C_L(0,d)=k_L |d|^2+O(|d|^4),\qquad
 k_L=\frac1{4L^2}\sum_{p\ne0}(\partial_1f(p))^2.$$
The fourth-order remainder is uniform: $r_n=\nabla f(p)\cdot d/(2L)+O(|d|^3/|n|^3)$ and $2\log\cosh r_n=r_n^2+O(r_n^4)$. The error sums against $|n|^{-4}$. Cubic symmetry makes the quadratic form scalar. In particular $k_2=0$ and $k_L>0$ for $L\ge3$. The integrable $|p|^{-2}$ singularity gives $k_L/L\to\kappa=\tfrac14\int(\partial_1 f)^2 d^3p/(2\pi)^3>0$. To justify the Riemann sum, the normalized contribution inside a ball of radius $\epsilon$ is at most $C\epsilon$ by summing $|n|^{-2}$ on shells; take the limit on the complement first.

For $d\sim N(0,vI_3)$ multiply its density by $1_{|d|\le\pi/4}e^{-C_L(0,d)}$. Write $Z$ for total mass and $\Sigma$ for the covariance **after dividing by $Z$**. For $v=c/(\beta L)$, $\beta\ge1$, and $v\to0$, uniformly in $L$ with $c$ fixed,
$$
 Z=(1+2k_L v)^{-3/2}+O(v^2),\qquad
 \Sigma=\frac{v}{1+2k_L v}I_3+O(v^3).
$$
Indeed both exponents are nonnegative, so on the chart the weights differ by at most $C|d|^4$. The zeroth and second moments of that discrepancy are $O(v^2)$ and $O(v^3)$; the missing Gaussian tails at fixed radius are exponentially small in $1/v$. The unnormalized second moment of the quadratic reweighting is $v(1+2k_Lv)^{-5/2}I_3$. Since $k_L=O(L)$, $k_Lv$ is bounded and division by $Z$ preserves the stated error. The drift vanishes by inversion. The mass and normalized variance have different leading fractional corrections ($-3k_Lv$ and $-2k_Lv$ for $k_Lv\ll1$).

This verifies G4’s $O(1/\beta)$ normalization loss and $O(v/\beta)$ covariance correction at large $\beta$. At fixed $\beta$ and $L\to\infty$ the width-only model has $Z\to(1+2c\kappa/\beta)^{-3/2}$ and $\Sigma/v\to I_3/(1+2c\kappa/\beta)$. In the free quaternion link convention, a uniform per-link color component has variance $1/(\beta L^3)$ and $\alpha=2Lu$ gives $c=4$ for that component. Projecting the full physical step onto a scalar coordinate can change the law. Normalizing the vacuum erases a scalar multiplier and leaves the increment-dependent kinetic correction in place.
