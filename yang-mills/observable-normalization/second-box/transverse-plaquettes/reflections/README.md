# Reflections and the finite-coupling minimum of the frozen hard vacuum

Director A4 · 23 November 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

**A missing uniformity check in the two-site result.** A pointwise $O(\beta^{-1})$ error leaves room for ripples with large curvature. For the moving 42-dimensional horizontal plane, the exact local Wilson kernel gives enough control to exclude them. With the exact constant-field magnetic energy subtracted and the [previous normalization](../README.md), there are $r>0$ and $B<\infty$ such that
$$
 E_\beta(A)-E_\beta(0)=\kappa |A|^2+O(|A|^4+\beta^{-1}|A|^2),
 \qquad |A|\le r,\quad \beta\ge B .
$$
Here $|A|^2=\sum_i|A_i|^2$ and $\kappa=0.0203667123\ldots$ is the moving-plane coefficient. In particular the origin is the unique minimum of this frozen energy within the ball, and the excess is at least $\kappa |A|^2/2$ after decreasing $r$ and increasing $B$. This includes backgrounds much smaller than a Wilson step. This minimum belongs to the local frozen transfer with induced slice measure. Joint gauge projection, dynamics of the slow fields, and the physical mass gap remain separate obligations.

## Lattice reflections act on the full kernel

Reflect the $i$th spatial coordinate. Write $Rx$ for the reflected site. A link pointing in that direction is replaced by $U_i(Rx-e_i)^{-1}$, so $H_i$ becomes $H_i^{-1}$ and its fluctuation becomes
$$ z_i'(x)=-\operatorname{Ad}_{H_i^{-1}}z_i(Rx-e_i). $$
Other components are simply pulled back from $Rx$. This formula follows by inverting $\exp(sz_i)H_i$ and moving its new constant factor to the right. It is an orthogonal map preserving spatial means. Reflection intertwines site gauge transformations with their site pullbacks, hence sends the orbit tangent range and its orthogonal complement $E_A$ to their counterparts at $A'$ with $A'_i=-A_i$. It preserves the metric ball used for the cutoff. Wilson spatial sums count the same unoriented plaquettes. The temporal chord between endpoints is invariant under inversion and translations of links. The induced Riemannian volume also pulls back identically since the link map is an isometry. The removed scalar $S(H)$ is reflection invariant. All three independent reflections are therefore unitary equivalences of the exact frozen kernel at the corresponding backgrounds; they also intertwine the Gaussian Hessian kernels obtained by differentiation. In particular the Gaussian trace energy is even under $A\mapsto-A$. Its analytic cubic Taylor term vanishes for **arbitrary**, including noncommuting, triples. For example color invariance alone would allow $A_1\cdot(A_2\times A_3)$, which changes sign under a single axis reflection. Freezing the identity plane does not give this argument on the moving background.

## Control of background derivatives

Write $s=\beta^{-1/2}$. Identify each moving plane with $E_0$ by the orthonormal polar frame of [the parent proof](../README.md). On a slightly larger closed background ball the frame and each of its first two derivatives are bounded. The cutoff is exactly $|sz|<\delta$ in this common space, independent of $A$. Consequently its derivative contributes no surface measure. For $|\alpha|\le2$ the kernel expansion there satisfies
$$
 \left\|\partial_A^\alpha(T_{\beta,A}-G_A-sF_A)\right\|_{\rm HS}\le C s^2 ,
 \qquad F_A(z,z')=-\tfrac12(P_A(z)+P_A(z'))G_A(z,z').
$$
To verify this differentiated statement, form all Taylor expansions **after** substituting the moving frame. The unscaled spatial plaquette action is smooth jointly in $A,v$ on a fixed compact chart, with bounded mixed derivatives through two background and four field derivatives. Both the constant and linear field terms of the subtracted action vanish identically in $A$. Thus the scaled spatial remainder, including each of its first two $A$ derivatives, is bounded by $Cs^2|z|^4$. The kinetic term has constant quadratic part in the common orthonormal frame and zero cubic by simultaneous endpoint negation, with the same differentiated fourth-order bound in $|z|+|z'|$. The Jacobian is smooth with constant term one and identically vanishing linear field term for each $A$; its square roots and their derivatives obey the corresponding quadratic bounds. For the exponential use Taylor's integral remainder between the quadratic and exact exponents. Every interpolated exponent dominates $c(|z|^2+|z'|^2)$ from the spatial weights. Differentiating up to twice inserts bounded-degree polynomials in the radii while preserving a Gaussian integrable majorant; the difference after subtracting its order-$s$ term and either derivative still carries $s^2$. These bounds also control the Gaussian tail beyond the common cutoff. Dominated differentiation proves the display.

Let $g_A$ be the top Gaussian eigenvalue, $P_A^0$ its rank-one spectral projector (distinct from the cubic polynomial $P_A$), and $d>0$ a uniform separation from the rest. In each of finitely many background neighborhoods take a **fixed** circle enclosing only this level, at positive distance from the neighboring spectrum. For large $\beta$ the same circle isolates the perturbed level. The contour resolvent expansion, including its first two parameter derivatives, gives the rank-one projectors and eigenvalues with a uniform quadratic Taylor remainder in the perturbation. More explicitly, the derivatives of $(\zeta-G_A)^{-1}$ insert $\partial G_A$ or $\partial^2 G_A$ between bounded resolvents. Each quadratic remainder has at least two perturbation factors of $C^2$ norm $O(s)$; a differentiated factor still has that bound. To extract the eigenvalue without taking a trace of an arbitrary bounded operator, use a local normalized Gaussian reference vector at the chart center. The eigenvalue equals the ratio of its matrix elements of the first and zeroth contour moments. The latter stays bounded away from zero after shrinking the neighborhood. The first variation of a simple self-adjoint eigenvalue is its normalized diagonal matrix element. This gives, in $C^2$,
$$
 \lambda_{\beta,A}=g_A+s\operatorname{Tr}(P_A^0 F_A)+O(s^2)=g_A+O(s^2).
$$
The Gaussian ground state is even and $F_A$ reverses parity at every $A$, so its diagonal vanishes as an entire function of the background on this real neighborhood. Positivity of $g_A$ bounded away from zero permits the same estimate for $R_\beta=E_\beta-E_{\rm Gaussian}$. Reflections force $\nabla R_\beta(0)=0$. Integrate $\|D^2R_\beta\|\le C s^2$ twice on the ray to obtain $|R_\beta(A)-R_\beta(0)|\le C s^2 |A|^2$. The even Gaussian energy has quadratic coefficient $\kappa$ and bounded fourth derivative; adding its quartic Taylor remainder proves the claim. This argument uses the uniform gap in the **frozen hard** operator only.
