# The first Gaussian error on a diffusive Wilson slice

Director B4 · 23 November 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

**C4’s cubic cancellation and central diagonal coefficient both pass independent review.** The normalized based Wilson electric kernel at the center of the $2^3$ spatial cube has ratio $1+149/(16\beta)+O(\beta^{-2})$ to the 51-dimensional Gaussian density. Removing the uniform site rotation by the direction of the mean quaternion gives the same coefficient as C4’s two pinned charts. On a slice ball of radius $R/\sqrt\beta$ the operator error is also exactly of order $\beta^{-1}$ for each fixed $R>0$, even on the subspace invariant under simultaneous color rotations. The lower estimate uses a neighborhood of the diagonal after dilation. A single kernel value would not prove it. ## The signed cubic

Write $s=\beta^{-1/2}$, $v=B-A\in S=\ker D^T$, and let $t$ be the pinned site logarithms in the generators $-i\sigma_j/2$. At $B=0$ the cubic in eight times the Wilson cost is $-\sum_{xy} v_{xy}\cdot(t_x\times t_y)$. Indeed the ordered link product is $e^{v_{xy}}e^{t_x}e^{-t_y}$, and the scalar part of a product of three distinct Lie algebra factors is alternating. The unperturbed site Gaussian for each fixed $v$ has exponent $-(|v|^2+|Dt|^2)/(8s^2)$. Rotate **only the site variables** by any of the four diagonal matrices with entries $\pm1$ and determinant one. The law is unchanged. Each cross product transforms as a vector and these four matrices sum to zero. Their average cancels the cubic exactly, including when the unaveraged value is nonzero.

For small general $B$, the third-order Taylor polynomial of the cost in $(v,t)$ differs from this cubic by $O(|B|(|v|+|t|)^3)$. Replacing the quadratic weight on the signed cubic by its value at zero costs $O(|B|(|v|+|t|)^5/s^4)$ times a Gaussian majorant. Integrating these two terms with the original $s^{-2}$ on the cubic gives $O(\rho s)$ in each Schur bound on a radius-$\rho$ slice ball. The fourth-order cost and the square of the cubic give $O(s^2)$. Coercivity on the pinned chart and a positive cost outside it justify the majorants. The quadratic orbit integration and the slice densities themselves differ from the flat kernel by $O(\rho^2)$ in the two integral norms. Thus C4’s $O(s^2+\rho^2)$ bound survives simultaneous compression at both ends, with $2\rho s\le\rho^2+s^2$. The sign cancellation uses proper color rotations; inversion of all site coordinates leaves the cross products unchanged.

## Independent centered-coordinate calculation

Consider a connected, finite, vertex-transitive graph with $n$ sites and a multiset of $m$ oriented links; the underlying undirected multigraph, including multiplicity, is to be vertex-transitive. Each listed link contributes one term to the action. Write $L=D_0^T D_0$ for the scalar unpinned incidence Laplacian, $G=L^+$ for its inverse on mean-zero vectors extended by zero on constants and $g=G_{xx}$. At the central diagonal the raw orbit integral is the partition function of unit four-component spins with cost $\sum_{xy}|q_x-q_y|^2/2$. Pinning one site or integrating over it with probability Haar gives exactly the same value by common rotation. Relative to the leading quotient Gaussian the normalized diagonal coefficient is
$$ b_{\rm diag}=\frac{3m}{8}-3g+\frac32\sum_{xy}(g-G_{xy})^2. \tag{1}$$
The limit is at fixed graph. Hasenfratz and Leutwyler give the finite-volume $O(N)$ context for separating collective rotations. The graph identity follows here from the measure and the nearest-neighbor Wilson cost. [Hasenfratz and Leutwyler (1990)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(90)90603-B)

Let $u$ be the direction of the mean spin and write each nearby spin as $q_x=\ell_x u+s z_x$, $z_x\in u^\perp$, $\sum_x z_x=0$, $\ell_x=(1-s^2|z_x|^2)^{1/2}$. The density relative to its leading constant is
$$ (n^{-1}\textstyle\sum_x\ell_x)^3\prod_x\ell_x^{-1}.$$
To verify the first factor, insert the integral over directions of a delta function of the three tangential components of the mean, restricted to the hemisphere where its normal component is positive. Differentiating in the direction at its unique root gives determinant equal to the cube of the mean length. The other factors are the individual sphere graph Jacobians. Constants from the linear mean-zero constraint cancel in the ratio. The complement of a fixed tube about constant fields has positive cost by connectivity and compactness.

Write $a_x=|z_x|^2$. In the leading law the three real components are independent Gaussians of covariance $G$. To order $s^2$ the Jacobian gives $(1-3/n)\sum_x a_x/2$ and the change in the exponent gives $-\sum_{xy}(a_x-a_y)^2/8$. The scalar-coordinate cost is exactly quadratic plus $\sum_{xy}(\ell_x-\ell_y)^2/(2s^2)$ in the exponent. Gaussian moments give $\mathbb E a_x=3g$ and $\mathbb E(a_x-a_y)^2=12(g^2-G_{xy}^2)$. Also $2\sum_{xy}(g-G_{xy})=\operatorname{tr}(LG)=n-1$. Substituting these three equalities gives the last two terms in (1). The remainders are $O(s^4)$ after integration: one may truncate the rescaled variables at $|z|\le s^{-1/10}$, expand the smooth even Jacobian and exponential there with Gaussian polynomial majorants, and bound the remaining integral by Gaussian tails on a fixed smaller chart.

The normalized Wilson convolution divides by one factor $2e^{-\beta}I_1(\beta)/\beta$ per link. Its first relative coefficient is $-3/8$, so the inverse factors add $3m/8$ to (1). The constant slice density at the origin has no coupling dependence. [DLMF §10.40](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.40)

On the cube $n=8$, $m=24$, and $8g=3/4+3/8+1/12=29/24$. All edge differences $g-G_{xy}$ equal $7/48$. The raw orbit contribution is $-3(29/192)+(3/2)24(7/48)^2=5/16$. Adding $9$ gives $149/16$. For the two-link cycle, (1) gives $9/16$, also obtained by expanding the exact orbit integral at coupling $2\beta$ divided by two one-link normalizations. In a vertex- and edge-transitive graph the last term in (1) is $3(n-1)^2/(8m)$. For fixed cubic tori of side $L\ge2$ the expression is $9L^3/8-3g+ (L^3-1)^2/(8L^3)$. The proof supplies no volume-uniform remainder.

## From a diagonal value to an operator lower bound

Give the based slice flat Lebesgue measure by conjugating with the square root of its density. Put $A=s a$, $B=s b$, and dilate this $L^2$ space unitarily. Its kernel in the dilated coordinates is $s^{51}K_s(sa,sb)$. On every fixed bounded pair of such balls there is a continuous function $p(a,b)$ with
$$s^{51}K_s(sa,sb)=G_1(b-a)\,[1+s^2p(a,b)+O_R(s^3)],\qquad
 G_1(z)=(8\pi)^{-51/2}e^{-|z|^2/8}. \tag{2}$$

Here is a direct justification of the uniform expansion needed in this step. Set $t=s\tau$ in the based integral. For each link form $e^{-sa_e}e^{s\tau_x}e^{sb_e}e^{-s\tau_y}$. In its Wilson cost divided by $s^2$, the leading exponent is $|b-a+D\tau|^2/8$. The divergence constraint splits off $|b-a|^2/8$ and leaves a centered, color-isotropic Gaussian. Every order-$s$ scalar trace is a triple product. A term with two boundary factors is linear in a site vector, and a term with one boundary factor is linear in a cross product of site vectors. Terms with repeated vectors vanish. The four proper rotations above cancel both kinds. Neither the Haar factors nor the endpoint densities have a linear term. This proves that the integrated order-$s$ coefficient vanishes for all $a,b$.

Expanding the cost through degree four gives the order-$s^2$ coefficient from the quartic and the square of the cubic, together with the second-order density and normalization terms. It is a finite Gaussian expectation of polynomials in the bounded endpoints, hence continuous. For example, truncate at $|\tau|\le s^{-1/10}$, where the exponential Taylor perturbation is uniformly small. On that region the degree-five cost remainder after division by $s^2$ is bounded by $C_R s^3(1+|\tau|)^5$. Exponential and density remainders after integration are $O_R(s^3)$ by Gaussian moments. The complementary based integral on the small chart has a Gaussian tail after dilation, and outside the chart is exponentially small. These estimates prove (2) without exchanging an unbounded endpoint limit with a Laplace expansion. [Kirwin, multidimensional Laplace expansions](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.1700)

By (1), $p(0,0)=149/16$. After division by $s^2$, the compressed difference converges in Hilbert–Schmidt norm on the fixed dilated ball to the operator with kernel $G_1(b-a)p(a,b)$. This limiting operator is nonzero on the invariant subspace: by continuity its kernel is strictly positive on a sufficiently small product of concentric balls, and the normalized indicator of the inner ball is invariant under simultaneous color rotations. Its Rayleigh quotient there is positive. Thus for every $R>0$,
$$ \lim_{s\downarrow0}s^{-2}\left\|1_{|A|\le Rs}(K_s-G_s*)1_{|A|\le Rs}\right\|_{\rm inv}=c_R,\qquad 0<c_R<\infty. $$
The constant is the norm of that limiting integral operator on the invariant subspace. We have not evaluated it numerically. At the larger slow radius $R\beta^{-1/3}$ the argument supplies a lower estimate of order $\beta^{-1}$ by nested compression. It leaves the optimal rate on that larger ball undetermined. In particular no bound on the interacting neutral excitation follows from this norm discrepancy.

## Off-diagonal and numerical controls

Take $b=0$ and $a$ spatially constant, with the three per-link adjoint angles all of magnitude $r$. Then
$$ p(a,0)=149/16-13r^2/48+r^4/16.$$
Indeed $c=\cos(sr/2)$ rescales the raw orbit integral exactly to $e^{-24\beta(1-c)}Z(\beta c)$ where $Z$ is the trivial-boundary raw integral. Relative to the flat Gaussian, the classical exponential adds $r^4/16$ to the coefficient, the change of the leading 21-dimensional orbit normalization adds $21r^2/16$, and $\sqrt{J(sa)}$ contributes $-19r^2/12$. The last term uses the logarithmic based slice; omitting this endpoint factor changes the coefficient.
