## The uniform frozen-link estimate

Use $k=2$ or $3$ spatial links and probability Haar measure on $SU(2)^k$. Let $K$ be the product of the normalized central Wilson convolutions, $V=2\beta\sum_{i<j}|\mathbf u_i\times\mathbf u_j|^2$, $T=e^{-V/2}Ke^{-V/2}$, and $h=\beta^{-1/3}$. Simultaneous conjugation implements gauge symmetry. Choose a frozen link $i$, write $r=|\mathbf u_i|$, and walk another link $j$. The nearly commuting set is a tube about a great circle whose axis is fixed by $i$.

Here are the points at which uniformity could have failed in A4’s argument. For $r\ge h$ the block length $m=\lceil8/r\rceil$ satisfies $8\le mr\le9$, and $d^2=m/\beta\le9\beta^{-2/3}$. All late steps $m/2\le t<m$ are covered by the Wilson density and displacement bounds $Cd^{-3}$ and $8d^2$. In an ambient ball of radius $Rd$ about **any** starting point, the tube of perpendicular radius $\epsilon d$ has Haar volume at most $CRd(\epsilon d)^2$. In coordinates given by perpendicular displacement and circle phase, the Haar Jacobian is constant and the phase map is uniformly Lipschitz on the tube. If the intersection is nonempty, take any intersection point to bound its phase diameter. Adding the probability outside the ball gives $CR\epsilon^2+8/R^2$. Choose $R$ before $\epsilon$. These choices work for all axes and starting points.

Consequently each late time has probability at least $1/2$ of $mV_{ij}\ge 2\epsilon^2(mr)^2$. The expected fraction of such visits is bounded below. Concavity of $1-e^{-az}$ on $0\le z\le1$ turns this into a uniform deficit in the killed block without a temporal independence assumption. Telescoping the positive sum of its first $m$ survival functions and applying the symmetric-kernel square identity yields $I-K_j+V_{ij}\ge c/m\ge c'r$ on this fiber. The argument applies to every fiber test function. Commutativity and positivity of the kinetic factors give $I-K\ge I-K_j$ on the full space. ## Exponential localization in the Wilson transfer

Write $Q(u)=\langle u,(1-T)u\rangle$ and $v=e^{-V/2}u$. Fix a link $i$. Its distance to the nearer center in the round metric is $\theta_i=\arccos|\operatorname{Re}U_i|$. Set $S_i=\{\theta_i>\pi/6\}$. On $S_i$, $r>1/2$, so the fiber bound and positivity on the remaining fibers give $Q(u)\ge c\|1_{S_i}v\|^2$, using $e^V-1\ge V$. Also $Q(u)\ge\|u\|^2-\|v\|^2$. A positive linear combination gives, uniformly for large $\beta$,
$$Q(u)\ge b\|1_{S_i}u\|^2,\qquad b>0.$$
This is a form estimate produced by transverse kinetic energy. The bare potential vanishes along a commuting path between the centers.

The strictly positive symmetric kernel has a unique normalized positive top eigenfunction $F$. Gauge and center symmetries fix it. Write $E=1-\lambda_0=O(h)$ by the vacuum trial bound. For any bounded real multiplier $w$, its eigenvalue equation gives the exact identity
$$Q(wF)-E\|wF\|^2=\tfrac12\int T(du,dv)F(u)F(v)(w(u)-w(v))^2,$$
where the symmetric kernel measure includes Haar in its first variable. Use $w=\exp[\alpha\sqrt\beta(\theta_i-\pi/6)_+]$. Its log is $\alpha\sqrt\beta$-Lipschitz in the $i$th link. Writing $G=wF$, the integrand after the factor $1/2$ becomes $G(u)G(v)(\cosh(\log w(u)-\log w(v))-1)$. Bound the product by the arithmetic mean of the two squares and use $T\le K$ pointwise. For one Wilson jump of geodesic length $s$, uniformly for $0<\alpha\le1$,
$$\mathbb E[\cosh(\alpha\sqrt\beta s)-1]\le C\alpha^2.$$
Indeed its radial density is proportional to $\sin^2 s\,e^{-\beta(1-\cos s)}$ on $[0,\pi]$. The normalizing integral is at least $c\beta^{-3/2}$ by integrating to $\beta^{-1/2}$. Using $1-\cos s\ge2s^2/\pi^2$ and $\sin s\le s$, the rescaled density is bounded by $Ct^2e^{-2t^2/\pi^2}$ for $t=\sqrt\beta s$. Now $\cosh(\alpha t)-1\le\alpha^2 t^2 e^t$. The same bound on the density gives $\Pr(s>\pi/6)\le C e^{-c\beta}$.

Combining the two form estimates gives $b\|1_{S_i}G\|^2\le(E+C\alpha^2)\|G\|^2$. Choose $\alpha$ small and then $\beta$ large so the right coefficient is at most $b/2$. Since $w=1$ off $S_i$, $\|G\|^2\le2$. In particular the vacuum probability of $\theta_i\ge\pi/3$ is $O(e^{-c\sqrt\beta})$. This argument works directly with the compact Wilson kernel. Klein, Léonard and Rosenberger develop the corresponding exponential-weight method for symmetric jump forms in Euclidean space. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.3918)

## Trial states in every center character

For a nonempty subset $J$ of links, multiply $F$ by $w_J=\prod_{i\in J}\operatorname{sign}(\operatorname{Re} U_i)$. The result has unit norm, is gauge invariant, and transforms in the center character $J$. The null equators can be assigned arbitrarily. The exact bounded-transfer identity accepts this discontinuous multiplier. If the sign changes, at least one indicated link crosses its equator. Either that jump has length greater than $\pi/6$, or both endpoints lie at distance at least $\pi/3$ from the nearer center. Apply $2F(u)F(v)\le F(u)^2+F(v)^2$ in the identity. Kernel symmetry, the uniform long-jump estimate, the vacuum band probability and the union over at most three links give
$$0<\lambda_0-\lambda_{0,J}\le Q(w_J F)-E\le C e^{-c\sqrt\beta}.$$
Strictness follows from compactness and simplicity of the positive top eigenvalue. Since $\lambda_0\to1$, the same upper order holds for $\log(\lambda_0/\lambda_{0,J})$. Thus the unrestricted gauge-invariant first gap also has this exponential upper bound. The center-even excitation and the lowest electric-flux excitation answer different spectral questions.
