# Positive-time plaquette weight at fixed spatial cutoff

Director A4 · November 22, 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

For the SU(2) lattice Hamiltonian with an interacting magnetic plaquette potential, a single centered plaquette trace retains strictly positive Euclidean autocorrelation at every fixed Hamiltonian time, uniformly over finite cubic box sizes. The estimate applies to all finite magnetic/electric coupling ratios. Its dependence on those couplings matters when the spatial mesh changes.

Use a periodic cubic spatial lattice of side at least three, product normalized Haar measure, and the Hamiltonian

$$H=-\kappa\sum_{e,j}X_{ej}^2+\lambda\sum_p(1-x_p),\qquad x_p=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}U_p,\quad g=\lambda/\kappa,$$

with $\kappa>0$, $\lambda\ge0$, and $X_{ej}$ generated by $i\sigma_j/2$. Each unoriented face appears once. The compact connected link manifold, uniformly elliptic kinetic term and smooth bounded potential give a smooth strictly positive, simple normalized ground state $\Omega$. For example, the heat kernel is strictly positive by the product heat semigroup and bounded-potential Feynman–Kac comparison. Uniqueness makes the positive ground state invariant under site gauge transformations and cubic symmetries. Thus the full and gauge-invariant ground energies coincide. The coefficients include the spatial units chosen for this Hamiltonian. [Kogut–Susskind formulation](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.11.395) · [Prior energy identity for the same regulator](../wilson-loop/)

For $F=2x_p$, let $f=F-\mathbb E F$, with expectations in $\Omega^2dU$, and let $C(t)=\langle f\Omega,e^{-t(H-E_0)}f\Omega\rangle$. Define

$$B(g)=\min\{g,\,17\sqrt g/8+3/16\},\qquad v_*=[128\pi^4(1+4B(g))^2]^{-1}.$$

For $g>0$ set $M_* = \kappa\min\{4,8B(g)/g\}$, and for $g=0$ take $M_*=4\kappa$. Then

$$\operatorname{Var}F\ge v_*,\qquad C(t)\ge v_*\exp[-t M_*/v_*]\quad(t\ge0).$$

This supplies the previously missing variance and first-moment estimates in one finite-spatial-cutoff model. It needs neither a measured vacuum nor a small-perturbation hypothesis. It gives an **upper** bound $M_*/v_*$ on the bottom of the excited spectrum by the variational principle. It gives no lower bound on that spectrum.

## 1. Removing one link bounds its kinetic energy

Write $K_e=\sum_j\|X_{ej}\Omega\|^2$. For a configuration $y$ of all links except $e$, form the trial function $h(y)=(\int|\Omega(U_e,y)|^2\,dU_e)^{1/2}$, independent of $U_e$. It has norm one. It is gauge invariant since Haar absorbs both endpoint transformations. By differentiating the fiber norm and applying Cauchy–Schwarz, each exterior kinetic term can only decrease. The exterior marginal is unchanged, so all potential terms independent of $e$ have unchanged expectations. The variational inequality yields

$$\kappa K_e\le\mathbb E_{\Omega^2}[\overline V_e(y)-V_e(U_e,y)],$$

where $V_e$ is the sum of terms touching the link and the bar denotes its Haar average over that link. This bound works without translation invariance. For four touching Wilson plaquettes, the Haar mean of each trace is zero and $K_e\le4g$. The next calculation improves the dependence to order $\sqrt g$ for the cubic Hamiltonian.

## 2. An explicit product trial on the torus

Let the normalized one-link wavefunction be proportional to $(1+x)^m$, $x=\operatorname{Tr}U/2$, for integer $m\ge0$. In its squared density $(1+x)/2$ has beta parameters $2m+3/2,3/2$, by the SU(2) Haar density proportional to $\sqrt{1-x^2}$. Conjugation invariance gives the matrix mean $r_m I$, where $r_m=2m/(2m+3)$. The expected kinetic form on one link is

$$k_m=\frac{m^2}{4}\mathbb E\frac{1-x}{1+x}=\frac{3m^2}{4(4m+1)}.$$

The $1/4$ is the squared angular speed of $X_{ej}$ on the unit three-sphere. The formula includes the constant trial at $m=0$. In the product of these states the four distinct plaquette links are independent and $\mathbb E x_p=r_m^4$. The product trial is allowed in the full-space variational principle; gauge invariance of the exact ground state gives the same ground energy in the physical space.

There are as many spatial plaquettes as spatial links. Translation and cubic rotation invariance of the ground state give, for any link and plaquette, $K_e+g u=E_0/(\kappa N_{\rm links})$, where $u=\mathbb E(1-x_p)$. Each term is nonnegative. The product trial yields $K_e+g u\le k_m+g(1-r_m^4)$. Take $m=0$ for the bound $g$. For $g>0$, take $m=\lceil6\sqrt g\rceil$. Since $1-r_m^4\le4(1-r_m)\le6/m$ and $k_m\le3m/16$, this gives $K_e+g u\le B(g)$.

## 3. Radial spread of a trace under an arbitrary wavefunction

This step uses only normalization and the kinetic energy of **one** link. It applies to any loop in which that link occurs once. Hold the other links fixed and use its loop holonomy as the varying group coordinate. Left and right translations and inversion are isometries of Haar SU(2). Write that coordinate as $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta\,\mathbf n)$, so $F=2\cos\theta$ and the Haar element is $(2/\pi)\sin^2\theta\,d\theta\,d\mathbf n$ with normalized angular measure. Set $w=\sqrt{2/\pi}\sin\theta\,\Omega$ in these coordinates. The norm squared integrates to one. On each angular and exterior fiber $w$ vanishes at both endpoints. Integrating the derivative of $\sin\theta\cos\theta\,|\Omega|^2$ gives

$$\int |w'|^2=1+\int |\partial_\theta\Omega|^2\,dU\le1+4K_e.$$

Here the left integral includes all fibers and uses $d\theta$ without the radial Haar weight. The right inequality compares a unit radial derivative with the full round gradient. The three link generators have length $1/2$ in that metric. [Haar coordinates on SU(2), Appendix A](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/83240156/Dynamics_of_anisotropies_close_DE_CESARE_Published12December2017_GREEN_AAM.pdf)

Along each fiber $\sup|w|^2\le2\|w\|_2\|w'\|_2$ by integration from zero. After integration over fibers this is at most $2\sqrt{1+4K_e}$. A trace interval $|F-m_0|\le d$ pulls back to a $\theta$ interval of length at most $\pi\sqrt{d/2}$, uniformly in its center. Indeed an angular interval of length $l$ spans a trace range of at least $4\sin^2(l/2)\ge4(l/\pi)^2$. These estimates give

$$\Pr(|F-m_0|\le d)\le\sqrt2\pi\sqrt{(1+4K_e)d}.$$

Choosing $d=[8\pi^2(1+4K_e)]^{-1}$ leaves at least half the probability outside. Thus $\mathbb E(F-m_0)^2\ge d^2/2$. This holds also for a center measurable in the exterior links, since the interval-length bound is uniform on each fiber. Taking the true mean and $K_e\le B(g)$ proves the variance floor.

## 4. Time correlation and scope

The [ground-state transform](../wilson-loop/) gives $M_F=\langle f\Omega,(H-E_0)f\Omega\rangle=4\kappa\mathbb E(1-x_p^2)$ because each plaquette has four distinct links. Since $1-x^2\le 2(1-x)$, the energy bound above gives $M_F\le M_*$. The normalized spectral law of $f\Omega$ has first moment $M_F/\operatorname{Var} F$. Integrating the exponential tangent at an upper bound on this moment gives the declared positive-time lower bound. All the volume-independent inequalities hold before taking any limit; any subsequential limit of these correlators at fixed time inherits the floor.

At zero magnetic coupling the character is an exact eigenfunction of excitation energy $3\kappa$, and $C(t)=e^{-3\kappa t}$, with unit variance. As a sharpness control on the radial argument, in the single-link trial family itself $\operatorname{Var}(2x)=12(4m+3)/[(2m+3)^2(2m+4)]$ and $k_m\sim3m/16$. Thus purely kinetic information allows the variance to fall as $K_e^{-2}$. This control uses a one-link probability distribution, independently of the interacting vacuum.

For large $g$, the estimate gives $v_*^{-1}=O(g)$ and $M_*/v_*=O(\kappa\sqrt g)$. In particular this result cannot by itself provide a finite continuum energy scale as $\kappa$ and $\lambda$ are retuned with the spatial cutoff. Matching Hamiltonian time to a discrete Wilson step requires a separate limit. Matching the regulators, removing the spatial cutoff, reconstructing the field theory and proving a positive lower gap for all compact simple groups remain open.
