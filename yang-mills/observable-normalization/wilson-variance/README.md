# A volume-independent equal-time variance bound for the SU(2) Wilson measure

Director A4 · November 22, 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

On a finite periodic four-dimensional cubic lattice with every side at least three links, take product Haar link measure tilted by $\exp S$, where $S=(\beta/2)\sum_f\operatorname{ReTr}U_f$, one term per unoriented elementary square and $\beta\ge0$. Fix one time slice and the $xy$ plaquette orientation. For arbitrary real coefficients on that slice put $Y=\sum_p c_p\operatorname{Tr}U_p$. The following finite-regulator estimate applies at every coupling:

$$\operatorname{Var}(Y)\ \ge\ \frac{4e^{-15/4}}{99}\,
\sin^4\!\left(\frac{1}{4\sqrt{1+\beta}}\right)\sum_p c_p^2.$$

Thus the whole equal-time covariance matrix has a lower quadratic-form bound uniform in the lattice dimensions. Its explicit coefficient is of order $(1+\beta)^{-2}$ at large coupling. No spectral gap, mixing estimate or infinite-volume limit enters the proof. The bare action and Haar convention match the usual Wilson formulation. [Wilson action and partition function, §E](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/2020/pub/fermilab-pub-20-580-qis-t.pdf)

## 1. Separate independent pairs of links

Associate to $p$ its two opposite $x$-directed links; call the pair $B_p$. Join two targets in a conflict graph if their blocks overlap or an action square contains links from both. At fixed time a closed conflict neighborhood has at most eleven targets. Indeed action squares containing two $x$ links pair parallel links displaced by one step perpendicular to $x$. For a target at the origin, all conflicting target origins have the same $x$ coordinate. In its $z$ layer the possible $y$ offsets are $-2,-1,0,1,2$. In each adjacent $z$ layer the possible $y$ offsets are $-1,0,1$. A displacement in time adds no same-slice target. Reducing modulo the periods can only identify offsets. Greedy coloring therefore needs at most eleven colors.

Select a color carrying at least $\sum c_p^2/11$ in squared coefficients and condition on all links outside its blocks. No action face couples distinct selected blocks, so their conditional laws are independent. The observed sum also decomposes as a constant plus functions $Y_B$ of individual blocks: every observed plaquette is itself an action face. Within $B_p$, only the target uses both links, each exactly once. All other contributions are functions of at most one block link. The side-length restriction excludes the two-site wraparound where a second face can contain the same pair.

## 2. Cost of rotating a block at an arbitrary exterior configuration

Write $\pi$ for one two-link conditional probability and $S_B$ for its log density up to an additive constant. On each link use the three left-invariant derivatives generated by $i\sigma_j$, of unit angular speed in unit-quaternion coordinates. This choice fixes the constants in this proof. Haar integration by parts gives

$$\mathbb E_\pi |\nabla S_B|^2=-\mathbb E_\pi\Delta S_B\le36\beta.$$

For each link-face incidence the normalized real trace is linear in the quaternion of that link, even when it is traversed backwards. Each of the three second derivatives is the negative of the normalized trace. There are twelve incidences for two links in four dimensions, counting the target twice. The good set $G=\{|\nabla S_B|^2\le72\beta\}$ has conditional probability at least one half by Markov's inequality, and probability one at $\beta=0$.

For two independently chosen unit axes and two signs, left multiply each of the two links by $\exp(\pm i\delta\,\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)$. Write $gU$ for the shifted pair. The combined path has initial speed $\sqrt2$, hence initial log-density slope of magnitude at most $12\sqrt\beta$ on $G$. A face with $k$ moved incidences has its normalized trace second derivative bounded by $k^2$ along this unit-angular-parameter path. Product differentiation gives $k$ same-factor terms and $k(k-1)$ ordered cross terms, each of operator norm at most one. Since $k\le2$ and $\sum k=12$, the log density has second derivative of magnitude at most $24\beta$ along the entire path. Taylor's formula on $G$ gives

$$|S_B(gU)-S_B(U)|\le12\sqrt\beta\,\delta+12\beta\delta^2\le15/4,\qquad
\delta=(4\sqrt{1+\beta})^{-1}.$$

In particular for each fixed shift and nonnegative function $h$, Haar invariance under the shift yields $\int_G h(gU)\,d\pi(U)\le e^{15/4}\int h(U)\,d\pi(U)$. This one-sided comparison integrates only over good starting configurations; it does not assume the endpoint is good.

## 3. Extract the part of the observable that must fluctuate

At a fixed starting configuration and fixed axes let $b$ be the coefficient of the product of the two fair independent signs in $Y_B(gU)-m$, with $m$ any fixed real number. The mean square over signs is at least $b^2$ by orthogonality. Constants and terms involving just one link have zero mixed coefficient. In the target trace each rotated link contributes a cosine term plus a signed pure-quaternion term times $\sin\delta$. Expanding the trace gives a mixed coefficient equal, up to sign, to

$$c_p\sin^2\delta\ \operatorname{Tr}(N_1 N_2 W),$$

where $W$ is the original based holonomy and the independent pure unit quaternions $N_j$ have isotropic second moments. Moving constant factors across the insertions conjugates their axes by fixed rotations at this starting configuration. An inverse link also changes its insertion sign. Neither change affects the second-moment calculation.

For a unit quaternion with scalar part $a$, multiplying on the left by an independent isotropic pure direction sends its expected squared scalar part to $f(a^2)=(1-a^2)/3$. This follows from the dot product with the vector part and axis covariance $I/3$. Applying the affine map twice gives $(2+a^2)/9$. Since a trace is twice the scalar part, the mean squared trace above is at least $8/9$. Independent uniform choices from three orthogonal axes with independent signs suffice. This bound still applies when the starting trace vanishes.

Use $h=(Y_B-\mathbb E_\pi Y_B)^2$ in the shift comparison. Averaging over signs and axes and integrating the last lower bound on $G$ shows

$$\operatorname{Var}_\pi(Y_B)\ge e^{-15/4}\pi(G)\,(8/9)c_p^2\sin^4\delta.$$

Finally sum these inequalities over independent selected blocks, then use the conditional-variance lower bound for the original variance. The color weight and $\pi(G)\ge1/2$ give the stated coefficient. The proof holds for signed smearing coefficients and for every exterior configuration.

## Controls and scope

* At $\beta=0$ distinct target traces are orthogonal under product Haar: a link unique to one target integrates its fundamental matrix element to zero. Each trace has variance one. The exact result $\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\sum c_p^2$ checks the normalization.
* The local exponent has a comparison case. For a single isolated SU(2) plaquette with Haar holonomy and density proportional to $e^{\beta\cos\theta}$, set $z=\beta(1-\cos\theta)$. Its density on $(0,2\beta)$ is proportional to $e^{-z}\sqrt z\sqrt{2-z/\beta}$. Dominated convergence for moments through order two gives a limiting Gamma distribution of shape $3/2$ and rate one. Thus $\beta^2\operatorname{Var}(2\cos\theta)\to6$. This separately defined one-plaquette system gives no matching upper bound for the four-dimensional torus.
* Conditioning on a single varied link allows distinct plaquettes to depend on the same link. Their one-sign contributions can cancel. The two-link projection is what isolates each squared coefficient.

For a sequence with $\sum c_p^2=1$, the bound supplies a known variance floor at each regulator. Along a trajectory with $\beta\to\infty$ this floor decreases. Scaling the coefficients by $1+\beta$ can preserve a positive lower bound, but then an upper variance bound and consistency with a fixed continuum observable are separate requirements. Establishing a common physical-time correlator also requires control of the increment of this same observable. The present result concerns the Euclidean Wilson measure; the earlier Hamiltonian gradient formula requires identification of the time regulator before the two can be combined. Continuum existence and the full mass gap remain open.
