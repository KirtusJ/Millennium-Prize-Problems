# Microscopic curvature of the four-dimensional Wilson vacuum

Director C4 · November 23, 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time · Yang–Mills existence and mass gap

For the original isotropic $SU(2)$ Wilson law, this calculation identifies the weak-coupling joint law of any fixed finite set of plaquettes. In each finite periodic spatial box take the infinite-time transfer vacuum. Then let both the inverse bare coupling $\beta$ and the side in lattice cells $L$ tend to infinity, with no restriction on their relative rates. Orient each face, transport its holonomy $U_p$ into a common root frame along fixed lattice paths, and put $F_p^\beta=\sqrt\beta\,\operatorname{Im}U_p$. The limiting field is centered Gaussian with covariance the orthogonal projection onto the closure of exact lattice two-cochains, independently in three colors. All fixed joint polynomial moments converge.

For one face $Y_p=\beta(1-\operatorname{tr}U_p/2)$ converges to $\chi^2_3/4$ with limiting mean $3/4$ and variance $3/8$. For a spatial face observed through the transfer, the limiting connected action spectral measure in lattice units has mass asymptotic to $3\epsilon^8/(2\pi^4\,8!)$ below $\epsilon$. Every fixed small positive lattice-energy window has positive limiting mass. The positive gap of the finite regulator consequently tends to zero **in lattice units**. On a trajectory with lattice spacing $b\downarrow0$, this probe's raw connected weight below any fixed *physical* energy also tends to zero. The two conclusions involve different windows.

For the action and the two bounded observables, connected correlations at any fixed positive physical time tend to zero even after normalization by the variance or by the square of the mean. Their nonzero microscopic correlations have the lattice clock held fixed first. No rate for the physical-time limit is established here. The construction of an interacting continuum field with nonzero positive-time weight and a positive mass gap remains open.
