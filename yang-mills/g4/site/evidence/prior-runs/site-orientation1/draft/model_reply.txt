### Finite-lattice positivity

- **Even extent \(2m\).** Condition on all tangent links at slices \(0\) and \(m\). Site reflection fixes these links pointwise. Every remaining link belongs to one half, and every plaquette off the boundary slices lies in one half, possibly using conditioned links. Splitting the boundary action equally leaves two identical real integrals, whose product is a square. A real bounded half-test may depend on the shared tangent links and on the color frame. Gauge invariance of the test is unnecessary.
- **Odd extent \(2m+1\).** Condition on the tangent links at slice zero. The normal links across the far cut are independent Haar variables before weighting. Each is indexed by a transverse vertex, and their integration averages the gauge action on one endpoint slice. With the Wilson \(SU(2)\) coupling \(\beta\ge0\), the kinetic operator \(K\) is positive on the full slice space. Each power \((q\cdot q')^r\) is a positive kernel by the tensor-power inner product, and the exponential has nonnegative coefficients. Simultaneous endpoint gauge rotations preserve the kernel, so \(K\) commutes with the orthogonal Haar projection \(\Pi\). The resulting quadratic form is \(\langle \Pi Mh,K\Pi Mh\rangle\ge0\) for the real half-integral \(h\). In particular, \(h\) may be gauge dependent even with the slice-zero links held fixed. This argument requires the test to leave the far-cut normal links out of its support.

### Rooted reversal and the limit

The orientation sign is exact for the straight axis paths. Let \(a=-(n+1)e_i\), \(b=-ne_i\), and let \(E\) be the link from \(a\) to \(b\). If \(P_a\) is the positive plaquette word based at \(a\), the reflected word based at \(b\) is \(E^{-1}P_a^{-1}E\). If \(R_b\) transports from the root to \(b\), then \(R_a=R_bE^{-1}\) is the straight transporter to \(a\). Thus the reflected rooted word is \((R_aP_aR_a^{-1})^{-1}\), and its imaginary part changes sign exactly. The source’s separate small-conjugation step is redundant for these paths. For other prescribed fixed root paths, the required comparison follows if fixed contractible loop holonomies tend to identity in probability and the scaled plaquette vectors are tight. The supplied rooting argument derives both from the stated uniform plaquette-deficit bound. The comparison uses one common root frame.

At fixed \(n,t\), the inequality passes first to a compact-link vacuum cylinder limit and then to a joint rooted curvature limit by bounded continuity. If that limit has the stated decomposition \(F=G+H\), with the given Gaussian covariance and \(H\) independent of \(G\), the bounded Fourier multiplier gives vanishing Gaussian cross-covariance as \(n\to\infty\).
