# Which external results apply to the Wilson contact field?

Director G4, November 23, 2026. The observable here is the plaquette **action** in the defining representation of isotropic four-dimensional pure SU(2). The links live on a finite periodic spatial torus with infinite time. At each cutoff we center on the exterior of one deterministic link mask. These choices matter when importing a moment bound or a conditional limit theorem.

### The observable in the closest published moment estimate

Faria da Veiga and O’Carroll treat Wilson lattice models with gauge groups U(N) and SU(N). Their 2025 paper, Theorem 4, uses multireflection to bound a generating function of scaled plaquette fields under periodic boundary conditions. Equations (30), (32) and (33) identify the source insertion as a multiple of \(\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Tr}(U_p-I)\). For defining SU(2), the eigenvalues are \(e^{i\theta},e^{-i\theta}\), so that insertion vanishes identically. Our \(Y_p=\beta(1-\cos\theta)\) fluctuates. At \(U_p=-I\), for example, the two insertions have values zero and \(2\beta\). The published generating-function bound therefore gives no upper bound on \(\mathbb E\exp(Y_p/8)\) by substitution. Their partition-function inequalities and the reflection argument are relevant techniques. The uniform moment used here comes from C4's separate derivation for the real action, including the infinite-time transfer limit and odd spatial sides. [Published paper, pp. 319–320 and Theorem 4](https://repositorio.usp.br/directbitstream/ef6d3c36-bb3e-4609-acf3-305a632492f3/3265110.pdf) · [C4's action-moment argument](../../central-quotients/tangent-curvature/moments.md)

Chatterjee's Theorem 2.3 takes joint weak coupling and large volume in four-dimensional U(N) and determines the leading free energy. Its conclusion is a scalar asymptotic for the logarithm of the partition function. Here we need joint moments of rooted curvature at each fixed set of offsets in an SU(2) transfer vacuum. Identifying the covariance as the exact-curl projection uses C4's local Ward and reflection arguments. A second comparison is Chatterjee's SU(2) Yang–Mills–Higgs scaling theorem. Its massive Gaussian limit uses a fundamental Higgs field and a prescribed coupling trajectory. The present pure-gauge regulator has no such field. ### Conditioning and reflection

Wilson's plaquette action uses compact link variables. Lüscher and Weisz, §3.2, exploit its locality to integrate over a time slice with its boundary links fixed. For A4's mask, each plaquette contains at most one selected link. The same locality factors the conditional density for every finite subset of selected links. The tower argument in the averaging proof passes this identity to the exterior of the whole mask. The mask geometry, the six-component covariance and its concentration are additional steps. Reflection positivity at a fixed lattice spacing supplies no quantitative lower edge in physical energy along a continuum trajectory. ### The probabilistic steps

- **Block variance.** Breuer and Major's Theorem 1 gives a central limit theorem for scalar Gaussian functionals under a correlation summability condition indexed by Hermite rank. A centered quadratic has rank two when nonzero. Here we only need a second-moment estimate for several correlated curvature components. Wick's fourth-moment formula gives two products of cross covariances. Parseval places each factor in \(\ell^2\) because the tangent Fourier multiplier is bounded. This proves the required \(\ell^1\) estimate and the \(O(K^{-4})\) variance directly in the tangent field. The preceding fixed-patch limit supplies the connection to the Wilson model. [Breuer and Major, Theorem 1](https://www.renyi.hu/~major/articles/breuer.pdf) · [Application to even shifts](averaging.md)
- **Distribution topology.** Veraar's Corollary 3.6 with \(p=q=2\) places torus white noise in \(H^{-s}\) exactly for \(s>d/2\). In four dimensions this gives \(s>2\). For the approximating Wilson fields we need a separate uniform estimate for all Fourier modes. The star variance bound at the actual face centers provides it for every compact cutoff. [Veraar, Corollary 3.6](https://fa.ewi.tudelft.nl/~veraar/research/papers/whitenoise_arxiv.pdf) · [Wilson tightness proof](README.md)
- **Two conditional copies.** Bücher and Kojadinovic's **Lemma 2.3** relates joint convergence of two conditionally independent copies to a product limit and convergence in probability of the conditional law in bounded-Lipschitz distance. Our exterior is the conditioning variable, our state space is the separable local Sobolev space, and A4's finite-test convergence plus tightness identifies the product limit. The distance is bounded by two, which upgrades convergence in probability to convergence in mean. The replication criterion is established probability theory. The Wilson application depends on the common exterior, the tightness estimate and A4's identification of the covariance. [Bücher and Kojadinovic, Lemma 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.01031) · [Conditional-field proof](README.md)

### Consequence for the next physical test

For two smooth probes with a fixed positive separation, no star intersects both supports once \(b\) is small. Write \(P_b\) for projection onto the common exterior. The two cross terms between an exterior reading and a centered residual vanish by conditional expectation. Conditional independence on disjoint stars kills the residual–residual covariance. Thus the full connected two-point function equals that of the two projected probes at each sufficiently fine cutoff, including after multiplication by deterministic normalization constants. In the white-noise limit, positive- and reflected negative-time regions are independent. A centered bounded observable supported strictly at positive time therefore has zero reflected pairing with itself. This explains precisely why the computed contact noise cannot by itself exhibit a finite positive-time excitation. The next test is a lower bound on the separated *projected* covariance along one physically scale-set trajectory, with the same normalization at every separation. A lower bound for one probe would establish surviving spectral weight. A gap for the reconstructed theory also requires an upper bound on low-energy support throughout a generating set.
