# Proposed construction and where it breaks

**Target.** Fix a compact simple gauge group $G$ and a faithful finite-dimensional unitary representation. The goal is a nontrivial quantum Yang–Mills theory on $\mathbb R^4$ with a vacuum and a physical Hamiltonian whose spectrum on the orthocomplement of the vacuum starts at a positive finite energy. The estimates may depend on the group and the representation. The construction must identify the local gauge-invariant fields and their short-distance behavior, as well as satisfy axiomatic quantum field theory requirements. [Jaffe and Witten, pp. 5–6](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf)

### From a regulator to a candidate theory

1. Use the Wilson probability weight for $s(U)=1-\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Tr}\rho(U)/\dim\rho$ on a periodic spatial cube. At finite spatial cutoff and volume take the vacuum from the time transfer. Work on gauge-invariant slice functions and use positive, even time translations if required. Normalize the largest eigenvalue to one. For a symmetric transfer with positive top vector $\Omega$, the vacuum Markov kernel acts as $Kf=\Omega^{-1}T(\Omega f)$. Its invariant probability is $\Omega^2$ times slice Haar. The magnetic weight changes this probability. [Lüscher’s transfer construction](https://bib-pubdb1.desy.de/record/396349) · [Osterwalder and Seiler](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(78)90039-8)
2. Prescribe a scale in physical units before measuring the gap. One possible prescription chooses a fixed group-dependent value of $t^2\langle E(t)\rangle$ under gradient flow, and assigns the corresponding smoothing length $\sqrt{8t}$ the value one. Then $a=1/\sqrt{8t_{\rm lat}}$. A positive stable crossing, its infinite-volume independence, and $t_{\rm lat}\to\infty$ along the proposed bare trajectory are obligations. Gradient flow supplies a scale observable. For the reconstruction proof the fields must be local in Euclidean time, so a flowed observable alone cannot serve as the generating field family. [Lüscher’s scale prescription](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.4518)
3. For every sufficiently small $a$ and large physical spatial box, try to show $\|K^{n(a)}\|_{L^2_0}\le q_G<1$, where $n(a)$ is even and $a n(a)\to s>0$. Constants are uniform along the joint limit at fixed $s$. A first proof couples all possible initial boundaries by requiring $K^{n(a)}(x,dy)\ge\epsilon_G\nu(dy)$ almost everywhere. When this holds, write $K^n=\epsilon_G\nu+(1-\epsilon_G)R$. For centered $f$, $\operatorname{Var}_\pi K^nf=(1-\epsilon_G)^2\operatorname{Var}_\pi Rf\le(1-\epsilon_G)^2 \pi R(f^2)\le(1-\epsilon_G)\pi(f^2)$. Thus $q_G=\sqrt{1-\epsilon_G}$ suffices. The [electric Wilson calculation](electric.md) breaks the proposed volume-uniform premise. The [spatial repair](resolutions.md) gives the replacement inequality and its failure tests.
4. To take a continuum limit, construct and renormalize a separating family of local gauge-invariant composite fields. Obtain distributional convergence of all joint smeared products along compatible subsequences as $a\downarrow0$, $aL\uparrow\infty$. Prove uniform distribution bounds including an OS linear-growth condition of the required order, permutation symmetry, full Euclidean covariance, time reflection positivity and clustering. Finite Gram forms preserve positivity in a common limit. Discrete rotations, pointwise bounds away from diagonals, and convergence of the single plaquette leave distinct obligations here. In particular the growth of the order-dependent constants and the treatment of contact terms matter. The original OS II reconstruction uses E0′ and E1–E4. Time clustering can be derived from step 3 for centered word states once the convergence estimates apply, followed by rotation covariance for spatial directions. [Osterwalder and Schrader II, §IV.1](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/communications-in-mathematical-physics/volume-42/issue-3/Axioms-for-Euclidean-Greens-functions-II-with-an-Appendix-by/cmp/1103899050.pdf) · [G4 on complex reflection positivity](../complex/README.md)
5. The conditional spectral conclusion is rigorous. Let $F,G$ be centered words smeared strictly inside the positive half-space, on the dense OS word core. At finite cutoff, place the slice cut at the reflection plane. Contracting the links on the outer sides gives boundary vectors, or equivalently half-space vectors in the transfer representation. The centered inner product with a translation by $n$ is bounded by $q_G\|F\|\|G\|$ if step 3 holds on the physical vacuum sector. Use step 4 for the two reflected norms and the translated pairing. Translation of a smooth test by $a n\to s$ converges in its test topology, and the uniform distribution seminorm bounds justify this moving test. Density then yields $\|\exp(-sH)|_{\Omega^\perp}\|\le q_G$ and $\inf\sigma(H|_{\Omega^\perp})\ge-\log(q_G)/s$. At least one centered reflected norm must converge to a strictly positive value at separated supports. In that case its nonzero spectral measure has weight at some finite energy. This excludes a vacuum-only limit. The inequalities persist under subsequences. Existence and identification of even one full theory still require step 4 and the field identification below.

### Field identification

A free neutral scalar of mass $m>0$ is an adverse control even if one insists on a scalar observable of engineering dimension four. Work in its already constructed free-field theory and take the local Wick polynomial $O=:\!\phi^4\!:$ with its vacuum subtraction. At distinct Euclidean points its two-point function is $24C_m(x)^4$, where $C_m(x)\sim 1/(4\pi^2|x|^2)$ at short distance. Its connected three-point function is $1728 C_{12}^2 C_{23}^2 C_{31}^2$. Wick’s rule gives connected four-point contributions as well. This produces a positive-mass theory, a positive-norm local observable, an $|x|^{-8}$ leading power and non-Gaussian observable correlations in an underlying free theory. These formulas are for noncoincident insertions or disjoint smearings. Defining time-ordered distributions on their diagonals requires a renormalization prescription. [Wick’s original theorem](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.80.268) · [Jaffe and Witten on local operators](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf)

An actual Yang–Mills construction owes a group-specific correspondence between its regulator composites and the gauge-invariant local quantum fields. This includes the stress tensor, Ward identities with consistent contact terms, and the short-distance operator products and logarithms of asymptotic freedom in the chosen normalization. Recovering one perturbative coefficient or detecting a connected cumulant would test part of that claim. It would leave identification and the full hierarchy of Schwinger functions open. Bałaban’s small-field analysis supplies an actual four-dimensional renormalization step with effective interactions, an input quite different from a nonperturbative bound through the mass-generating scale. [Bałaban’s four-dimensional small-field calculation](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01215223) · [Official statement](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf)

### Order of limits

The electric calculation sends the spatial side to infinity at fixed positive temporal coupling. The magnetic calculation first differentiates the spatial coupling at zero with temporal coupling and side fixed. The checkerboard example takes continuous time and shrinks the spatial spacing at fixed positive mass. The prize construction needs isotropic weak bare coupling, diverging physical spatial volume, and continuum limits of smeared local fields before drawing conclusions about their ultraviolet behavior. None of the three calculations licenses exchanging these limits. [Magnetic calculation](magnetic.md) · [Wilson’s lattice regulator](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.10.2445)
