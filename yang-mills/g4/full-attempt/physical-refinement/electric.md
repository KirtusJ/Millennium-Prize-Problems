# A matched observable can survive the loss of the neutral gap

Here the spatial plaquette coupling is zero. Temporal plaquettes are present in every spatial direction. The time step and spatial spacing are both $a=\ell/N$. Fix the dimensionless static string tension $s=\sigma\ell^2>0$, measured along a lattice axis. For a simple static path of $R$ links propagated for $t$ steps, the normalized fundamental rectangular Wilson expectation in the electric vacuum is $u_N^{Rt}$. In temporal gauge each link contributes its fundamental convolution multiplier once per step. Equivalently propagate the matrix coefficients of the path between external fundamental charges and close it with the return path. Thus this observable sets $-\log u_N=s/N^2$ independently of any neutral gap. This calibration and the isotropic experiment's Schrödinger-functional coupling define separate models and separate lines of constant physics. [Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonian](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.11.395) · [Finite electric transfer](../electric.md)

### Spectrum at one physical length

With link heat kernels take $C_j=j(j+1)$ and $q_{j,N}=\exp[-sC_j/(N^2 C_{1/2})]$. With Wilson temporal plaquettes take $q_{j,N}=I_{2j+1}(\beta_N)/I_1(\beta_N)$. In either case $q_{1/2,N}=u_N$. The Wilson tuning exists uniquely. The fundamental multiplier is the mean of $\mathrm{Tr}(g)/2$ under the tilted Haar law; its derivative in $\beta$ is that variable's strictly positive variance. Its endpoints are zero and one. All nontrivial multipliers are at most $u_N$. For Wilson this follows from the decreasing successive ratios of the modified Bessel functions and $I_2/I_1<1$. [SU(2) link spectrum](../two-site-gap/kernel.md) · [Baricz and Ponnusamy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3346)

Gauge-invariant spin networks diagonalize the product convolution. A nontrivial edge costs one factor $q_{j,N}$. An occupied vertex of degree one has no invariant intertwiner. On the periodic three-dimensional spatial graph with $N\ge4$ each nonempty occupied subgraph consequently contains a cycle of length at least four. A fundamental elementary plaquette attains this length, has zero vacuum mean, and is invariant under every electric-center flip. The normalized transfer ratio of the first neutral excitation is exactly $u_N^4$, hence
$$
 \ell M_N=-N\log u_N^4=4s/N\longrightarrow0 .
$$
For a fixed physical square of side $r\ell$, $0<r<1/2$ and $rN$ integral, its fundamental character has Haar norm one. Its squared overlap with its own transfer eigenspace is one. The half-trace has norm squared $1/4$. At physical time $k\ell$, for integer $k\ge0$, its normalized covariance is exactly $u_N^{(4rN)(kN)}=e^{-4rsk}$ at every such spacing. This gives a positive apparent mass $4rs/\ell$ with full spectral weight at every resolution. Taking successively smaller fixed dyadic $r$ supplies local loop supports with arbitrarily small positive energies. [Spin networks and refinement maps](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9411007)

On a spatial **circle**, Gauss projection gives the individual characters of the full holonomy and center neutrality selects integer $j$. The first neutral excitation has $j=1$ and multiplier $q_{1,N}^N$. Its dimensionless energy is $8s/3$ for heat kernels. For Wilson plaquettes the fixed-order Bessel asymptotics give
$$
 -\log q_{j,N}=\frac{(2j+1)^2-1}{2\beta_N}+O_j(\beta_N^{-2}),
 \qquad \beta_N\sim3N^2/(2s).
$$
Consequently the circle energy tends to $8s/3$ while the three-dimensional spatial gap tends to zero at the same static tension. The circle result supplies a positive control. For $s=1$, the Wilson circle energies for $N=4,8,16,32$ are 2.664617, 2.666544, 2.666659 and 2.666666. The spatial three-torus gives 1, 1/2, 1/4 and 1/8. ### Refinement of the whole neutral Hilbert space

Start at $N_0\ge4$ and refine dyadically. Pull a coarse link back to the product along its two fine links. Disjoint coarse edges use disjoint fine edges. Their products have independent normalized Haar laws, so pullback is isometric. Gauge invariance passes through each new interior vertex by cancellation. A fine center sheet acts through the homologous coarse sheet, which preserves the neutral subspace. Along a spin network every subdivided edge repeats its representation on both segments. In the heat model the physical Hamiltonian at one physical side has eigenvalue
$$
 \ell H_N=\frac{s}{N}\sum_e C_{j_e}/C_{1/2}.
$$
Both the sum and $N$ double under pullback. The semigroups therefore intertwine at every common physical time. In the Hilbert completion of the increasing union they define a strongly continuous contraction semigroup: continuity holds first on each finite linear combination of spin networks, then by density and contractivity. Choose bases compatibly by extending multiplicity-space bases at each stage. Their union is an eigenbasis. The constant is the unique zero-energy vector and the displayed shrinking-plaquette energies accumulate at zero.

A fine elementary square with an edge off the coarse skeleton is orthogonal to the entire coarse algebra by integration over that private Haar edge. Such a square realizes the bottom excitation as soon as its mesh is introduced. Even perfect transport of *every* inherited eigenstate does not control the new fine subspace. For the Wilson electric model, fundamental networks have exact transport on their embedded subdivisions at corresponding lattice times. For higher fixed spins Wilson gives the same limiting semigroup on this inductive Hilbert space: on a fixed coarse graph, $-\log q_{j,N}=(s/N^2)(C_j/C_{1/2})+o_j(N^{-2})$. Subdivision contributes $N/N_0$ identical factors per old edge; propagation for $\lfloor tN/\ell\rfloor$ steps gives the heat-model exponent. The total error tends to zero on each basis vector. Composing the embedded finite-space operators with projection onto the respective finite-mesh subspaces extends this convergence strongly by density and contractivity. Exact finite-mesh transport for all spins is special to the heat choice. [Earlier fixed-cutoff blocking calculation](../blocking/README.md)

The fixed static tension, positive transfer, unique vacuum and nonzero overlaps on fixed physical loops all survive in this control. Restoring the isotropic spatial Wilson weight changes the vacuum and mixes the spin networks. The isotropic theory also calls for Euclidean invariance and a local continuum construction. The calculation falsifies the proposed inference from matching and visible correlations to a uniform gap; it supplies no counterexample to the Clay problem.
