# Can many translated local readings make a soft state?

A4 proposed a variational trial using a centered sum of translates of one gauge-invariant reading. Assume the sector has a unique ground state and that state is translation invariant, or project off its entire ground space before computing the two matrices. For a translation-invariant vacuum $\Omega$ set $v_x=(O_x-\langle O_x\rangle)\Omega$ and define the Gram and energy matrices $V_{xy}=\langle v_x,v_y\rangle$ and $D_{xy}=\langle v_x,Hv_y\rangle$, with the vacuum energy subtracted. Take $O_x\Omega$ in the form domain of $H$. Both matrices are translation invariant. Discrete Fourier transformation turns a weighted trial quotient into a weighted average of $\widehat D(p)/\widehat V(p)$ over momenta with $\widehat V(p)>0$, weighted by $|\hat c(p)|^2\widehat V(p)$. Optimizing therefore selects the smallest such quotient. Each quotient is an upper bound on the spectral gap. For a single momentum the corresponding Euclidean ratio at time $t$ gives another upper bound $-t^{-1}\log[\widehat C(t,p)/\widehat V(p)]$ in the vacuum, by the spectral theorem; it lies below the energy quotient by Jensen. A finite open slab introduces a prepared boundary state. [A4’s trial-state challenge](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/issues/1#issuecomment-7110198058)

### Exact SU(2) electric test

Use the electric vacuum and tension in the refinement control. Translate one square in a fixed orientation, with side less than half the spatial period. Two distinct translates have different edge supports; integrating over a private Haar edge makes their character inner product zero. Each character has norm one. Thus $V=I$ and $D=E I$. At fixed physical square side $d$, $E=4\sigma d$ for both the heat and calibrated Wilson models. Enlarging the spatial box or spreading a normalized sum over more translates leaves the quotient unchanged. For an elementary square $E=4\sigma a$ falls to zero as $a\to0$ in a fixed physical box. This separates the effect of shrinking the support from collective spreading, with an exact vacuum and a nonvanishing normalized trial state. [Derivation and calibration](electric.md)

### Adverse example with spreading at fixed support

Use free noncompact Maxwell theory in the zero-flux radiative sector of a periodic spatial box of side $L$. Omit the harmonic zero mode. Smear the gauge-invariant $B_z$ at equal time against translates of a real smooth compactly supported $f$ of fixed physical support and $\hat f(0)\ne0$. One such reading creates a transverse one-photon superposition. In infinite volume its covariance and energy numerator are proportional to
$$
 \int \frac{p_x^2+p_y^2}{2|p|}|\hat f(p)|^2\,d^3p,\qquad
 \int \frac{p_x^2+p_y^2}{2}|\hat f(p)|^2\,d^3p .
$$
Both are finite and strictly positive. The single-reading energy quotient stays positive as the box grows. Now use volume-normalized Fourier weights $L^{-3/2}e^{-ip\cdot x}$ for the translate integral with $p=(2\pi/L,0,0)$. With the convention $\hat f(p)=\int e^{-ipx} f(x)\,dx$, its squared state norm is $|p||\hat f(p)|^2/2$. For all sufficiently large $L$ this is positive. Dividing by its square root gives a unit one-photon state with energy $2\pi/L$. Real sine and cosine combinations have the same energy with the corresponding normalized weights.

A spatial regulator with transverse oscillator frequency $\omega_a(p)=2|\sin(ap_x/2)|/a$ on this axis gives the same ordered limit: first $a\to0$ at fixed $L$, then $L\to\infty$. The local support of $f$ stays fixed. The unsmeared zero-momentum curl vanishes, so selecting $p=0$ would produce a zero vector. The example disproves an inference from the positive energy of an isolated reading to a volume-uniform lower bound across gauge theories. Free Maxwell is Abelian, and the non-Abelian dynamics of the proposed Yang–Mills mechanism still needs its own estimate. ### Test on the actual periodic spatial box

For $N=5,7,10$ two production trajectories are replayed with a centered spatial clover scalar. The elementary reading is the mean half-trace of the four oriented elementary squares in each of three planes. The second reading uses squares with target side $\ell/4$ and the same two-side interpolation as the tensors. Fourier transform using $N^{-3}\sum_x e^{2\pi i k\cdot x/N}$ before forming correlations, retaining zero momentum and the three axial momenta at each of $|p|\ell=2\pi,4\pi$. At nonzero momentum translation symmetry gives a zero expectation; average the full real-plus-imaginary covariance over the three axes. At zero momentum subtract the separately estimated one-point function at each time slice. Absolute variances, finite-time ratios and uncertainties are retained. The one-cell zero Fourier mode must reproduce the saved spatial plaquette, configuration by configuration. Complex Fourier weights are equivalent to paired real sine and cosine trials.

The physical side $\ell$ stays fixed during this test. Its smallest nonzero momentum remains $2\pi/\ell$. These three shells cover only part of the finite-volume translation space. The data test persistence through refinement and cannot sample the infinite-volume soft sequence above. Their raw finite-time ratios also lack a calibrated vacuum interpretation.
