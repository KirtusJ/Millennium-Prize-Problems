# A gapped Wilson transfer that retains whole-slice information

Use an even periodic three-dimensional spatial cube of side $L\ge4$, $V=L^3$. Set the spatial coupling to zero and the temporal Wilson coupling to a fixed $g>0$. On spatial links before gauge projection the vacuum is product Haar and the time transfer $K$ independently convolves each link by $k_g(U)\,dU\propto\exp(g\,\mathrm{Tr}U/2)dU$. This is the electric member of the four-dimensional anisotropic pure $SU(2)$ Wilson family. The physical transfer acts on functions invariant under vertex frames. Let $P_n$ be the joint law of two stationary gauge-orbit slices $n$ time steps apart and $Q$ the product of their marginals. Use total variation with maximum value one. [B4’s transfer and coupling conventions](../../transfer-operator/blocked-time/README.md) · [Lüscher](https://bib-pubdb1.desy.de/record/396349)

Put $q_j=I_{j+1}(g)/I_1(g)$ for spin $j/2$ and $q=q_1$. These are the one-link convolution eigenvalues. Indeed, for class angle $\theta$, Haar density is $2\sin^2\theta/\pi$, and $\chi_j=\sin((j+1)\theta)/\sin\theta$. The cosine integral for $I_j$ and its recurrence give $\int k_g\chi_j=(j+1)q_j$. The coefficients are strictly positive and strictly decreasing with $j$ by Soni’s inequality. Directly, $I_{\nu+1}/I_\nu=(\log(g^{-\nu}I_\nu))'$ is the mean of $\cos\theta$ in the density proportional to $e^{g\cos\theta}\sin^{2\nu}\theta$ for integer $\nu\ge1$, hence is strictly below one. Matrix-coefficient orthogonality gives $\sum_j(j+1)^2q_j^2=\|k_g\|_2^2<\infty$. [Soni’s Bessel inequality](https://doi.org/10.1002/sapm1965441406) · [Peter–Weyl labels and vertex intertwiners, Baez](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9411007)

**Physical norm.** Nontrivial links in a gauge-invariant spin network cannot end in a degree-one vertex, where a single nontrivial irrep has no singlet. Each connected support therefore contains a cycle, of length at least four. Its eigenvalue is the product of its link multipliers. The fundamental plaquette character attains $q^4$, giving the exact centered operator norm $q^{4n}$ at lag $n$. For even $L\ge6$, the eigenspace is exactly the span of the $3V$ fundamental square characters. A four-edge support admitting an invariant is a cycle and every degree-two vertex forces the same representation onto its incident edges, with a one-dimensional intertwiner. Higher labels on that cycle have eigenvalues at most $q_2^4$. All larger supports have eigenvalues at most $q^5$. Thus $\max(q_2^4,q^5)<q^4$ separates this whole cluster from the remaining centered spectrum at lag one. At $L=4$ four-link straight windings also attain it. At $L=3$ the three-link winding would change the answer. This proves a positive volume-independent lattice gap $-4\log q$. [Graph controls](controls.py)

**A lower bound on dependence.** Select $M=V/4$ disjoint $12$-plaquettes based at even first and second coordinates in every third-coordinate layer. For $X,Y$ the two slices let $S_M$ average the $M$ products $\psi_p(X)\psi_p(Y)$, where $\psi_p=\mathrm{Tr}U_p/2$. These products lie in $[-1,1]$ and are independent across the selected plaquettes under each law. This follows before passing to the orbit space because the link pairs are independent and all the tests are gauge invariant. A plaquette holonomy is Haar, so the mean product is $\delta=q^{4n}/4$ under $P_n$ and zero under $Q$. The event $S_M>\delta/2$ and Hoeffding’s inequality give
$$
 1-\|P_n-Q\|_{\rm TV}\le2\exp[-M q^{8n}/128].
$$
Any minorization $K^n(x,\cdot)\ge\epsilon\nu(\cdot)$ forces $\pi\ge\epsilon\nu$ by stationarity. Both $P_n$ and $Q$ then dominate $\epsilon\,\pi\otimes\nu$. Their overlap is at least $\epsilon$. Thus a uniform reset of the entire boundary fails at every fixed $n$ as $L\to\infty$ in this gapped regulator. [Hoeffding, Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830)

**An upper bound and the cutoff.** The density on orbit pairs is the kernel of $K^n$ relative to the physical Haar marginal. For $n\ge1$, the Hilbert–Schmidt expansion gives $1+\chi^2(P_n\|Q)=\mathrm{Tr}\,K^{2n}$ on this invariant space. Bound the sum over labels and intertwiners for a fixed support of $k$ edges by $b_n^k$, where $b_n=\sum_{j\ge1}(j+1)^2q_j^{2n}\le C_g q^{2n}$. This upper bound counts even the unrestricted link matrix entries. A connected $k$-edge set can be encoded by a starting vertex and a $2k$-step traversal of its doubled edges, giving at most $V36^k$ possibilities. Physical multiplicities factor over vertex-disjoint support components. Dropping their incompatibility and exponentiating the sum of connected weights bounds the full trace by
$$
 \exp\!\left[V\,\frac{(36b_n)^4}{1-36b_n}\right]\quad (36b_n<1).
$$
Hence total variation tends to zero if $Vq^{8n}\to0$, since it is at most $\sqrt{\chi^2}/2$. The lower bound gives total variation tending to one if $Vq^{8n}\to\infty$. The transition is centered at $\log V/(8|\log q|)$ with a bounded window at fixed $g$. The one-slab $L^2$ spectral relaxation remains volume independent.

**Finite part at the transition.** If $Vq^{8n}\to\theta<\infty$ as $L\to\infty$, then $\chi^2(P_n\|Q)\to e^{3\theta}-1$. For $L>4$, the admissible four-edge connected supports are exactly $3V$ elementary squares. The degree-two vertices force a common irrep around a square, with one invariant per label. Reversing orientation preserves its $SU(2)$ character. The activity of one square is $\sum_{j\ge1}q_j^{8n}=q^{8n}(1+o(1))$, by strict decrease and summability. All larger connected supports contribute at most $V(36b_n)^5/(1-36b_n)=o(1)$ to the exponential upper bound. For the lower bound, keep $r$ vertex-disjoint fundamental squares. For every fixed $r$ their number is $(3V)^r/r!+O_r(V^{r-1})$ since a chosen square conflicts with a bounded number of others. Summing first to fixed $r$ and then letting it grow gives the matching exponential. This establishes the chi-square profile. The [likelihood calculation](profile.md) uses this second moment to derive the total variation profile $2\Phi(\sqrt{3\theta}/2)-1$.

The same obstruction to a volume-uniform reset has a qualitative, model-independent version. In a spatially mixing stationary massive state, suppose a bounded local observable has a nonzero connected autocorrelation at finite time. Translates of the product at the two times have different means in the joint and independent laws. If their spatial averages have variances tending to zero under both laws, thresholding between their means makes the laws asymptotically singular. The hypothesis on the averages is explicit. The electric Wilson test above supplies it with an exponential bound. This failure of global minorization does not decide physical clustering for the isotropic Wilson model.
