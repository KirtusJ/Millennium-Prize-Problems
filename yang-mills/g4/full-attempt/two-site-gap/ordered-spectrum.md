## Falsifying an extrapolation to physical mass

B4 independently checked the midpoint subtraction and the change of kinetic normalization in G4’s width model. On the two-site box the symmetric central increment costs exactly zero since each hard label has components zero or $\pi$ and the two endpoint widths coincide. At larger sides B4 derived separate first corrections for the total step weight and the normalized variance. The physical gauge transport remains a separate ingredient. [B4’s review](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/pull/18#issuecomment-7109334609)

In isotropic units let $a$ be a physical lattice spacing fixed by an independent observable. If a bound $m_{\rm lat}L\le Ch$ were uniform along a trajectory with $aL\ge d>0$ and $h\to0$, then $0\le m_{\rm lat}/a\le Ch/d\to0$. Thus extending the bare finite-box upper estimate unchanged through refinement would be incompatible with a uniform positive **all-state lattice** gap in physical units. Inferring the continuum spectrum also requires that the relevant spectral weight persists for a class of observables sufficient for reconstruction. Low eigenvalues can be lost in a limit, and aggregate weight can survive even when individual matrix elements vanish. [Lean scale bound and limit](TwoSite.lean) · [Jaffe and Witten](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf)

A positive transfer supplies a concrete independence test. Set $a=e^{-\beta}$ for this example alone and take $m_M=d_0 h/L+M a$ with $d_0>0$, $M\ge0$. Use
$$
 T_M=\tfrac12
 \begin{pmatrix}1+e^{-m_M}&1-e^{-m_M}\\1-e^{-m_M}&1+e^{-m_M}\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its symmetric vacuum and antisymmetric excitation have eigenvalues $1$ and $e^{-m_M}$. In the stationary reversible chain the centered sign observable has time correlation $e^{-m_M|t|}$. This also gives time-reflection positivity by conditioning on the reflection slice, or by positivity of the transfer at a link reflection. At every fixed $L$, changing $M$ is invisible to every algebraic order in $\beta^{-1}$. At $aL$ bounded below and $t=\lfloor t_{\rm phys}/a\rfloor$, the normalized correlation tends to $e^{-M t_{\rm phys}}$. The $M=0$ example retains a time-independent centered state and does not model a unique clustering vacuum. Two **positive** choices of $M$ already give distinct clustering limits with identical fixed-box algebraic data. This is a counterexample to the inference from fixed-box asymptotics alone. The matrices are illustrative and make no claim about the Wilson measure. The physical test would require a scale-setting observable, controlled running between volumes, and nonvanishing continuum correlations. [Lüscher, Weisz, Sommer and Wolff, nonperturbative finite-size scaling in SU(2)](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9207010)
