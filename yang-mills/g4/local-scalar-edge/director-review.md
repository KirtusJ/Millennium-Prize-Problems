# Even Fourier moments and the error in the spatial profile

A4 checked the divided moment and the combined weight of the two unwanted branches. B4 independently recovered the curvature $D=7/768$ from the axial dispersion and checked the three finite-volume normalizations. Their observations sharpen the error in the profile of the scalar reading at separated sites. The common-disk and uniform-band inputs remain those of the [original proof](momentum.md). For identical real ends, self-adjointness, reality and translation invariance imply $C_j(y)=C_j(-y)$ at positive real coupling. Analytic continuation gives the same equality on the disk. Pairing the phases therefore replaces them by cosines, including at self-opposite torus sites. The common exponential tail absorbs $|y|^2$, so the circle bound for each fixed Fourier-moment difference is $C_j|k|^2$. Dividing the difference of the filters by $z^8$ as before shows that the unwanted upper weight is $O(|k|^2+\beta^4)$.

There is also a coupling parity at $k=0$. In a finite open-time box multiply links in direction $\mu$ at $x$ by the center sign $(-1)^{\sum_{\nu<\mu}x_\nu}$. Even spatial periods make this single valued. The product of the signs on every plaquette is $-1$. The scalar trace reading changes sign while its two-end connected covariance is invariant under simultaneous replacement $z\mapsto-z$. The Wilson action's configuration-independent constant cancels in normalized expectations. The uniform holomorphic vacuum limit of the open-time correlations retains the parity, as does division by $(z/4)^{4j}$. The bounded even functions $d_0(0,z),d_1(0,z)$ therefore equal $1+O(\beta^2)$ uniformly, by their values at zero and Cauchy's estimate. Combining the two parities gives simple-top weight $1+O(\beta^2+|k|^2)$ near zero.

Write $a=j\beta^4$. For $a\ge1$, $j\beta^6\le1$ and sufficiently small $\beta$, the absolute error between $q_j(y)/q_j(0)$ and B4’s normalized periodic Gaussian is uniformly $O(a^{-1}+j\beta^6)$. In B4’s summed error bound the weight term now costs $O(\beta^2+|k|^2)e^{-ca|k|^2}$. The $|k|^2$ factor costs $O(a^{-1})$ after summation and normalization, by reserving half the Gaussian exponent and comparing the two grid sums. The dispersion remainder costs $O(\beta^2+a^{-1}+j\beta^6)$ as before. Since $a\ge1$, $\beta^2\le j\beta^6$. This controls absolute error uniformly in the site, including points where the correlation is small. Choosing $j$ of order $\beta^{-5}$ gives error $O(\beta)$ in this fixed-mesh limit.
