# A second proof of finite moments by repeated spatial smoothing

B4 proposed using the signed operator $I-(I-S_m)^k$ on the identity-padded Wilson flow reading. Here $k\ge1$ is fixed and $S_m$ independently convolves each retained link with the positive SU(2) Jackson kernel of per-link degree $3(m-1)$. This proves all finite moment orders without continuing the observable in flow time. [B4's proposal](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/pull/3#issuecomment-7098839152) · [Kernel, chord estimate and padding](../transfer-inverse-moment/flow-tail.md)

Fix a time bound $U$. On the complex coordinate polydisc of radius two, the derivative row sums of the spatial Wilson vector field are at most $L_0=80B$ by the degree-five and coefficient bounds. Extend quaternion conjugation in the formula by its linear coordinate sign change. For max coordinate norm, any complex initial configuration within $\eta=e^{-L_0 U}/4$ of a real field stays within $1/4$ of the real trajectory to time $U$. Apply the integral Grönwall inequality until first exit from the polydisc. The comparison prevents that exit, and finite-dimensional ODE continuation covers the whole interval. Dependence on the initial coordinates is holomorphic there, for example by Picard iteration on successive short intervals. The resulting deficit extends with bound $1+64\cdot2^4=1025$ on this tube. All these constants are independent of spatial volume. [Coefficient bounds](time-jets.md)

For each of $k$ independent noise rounds write the noise on retained link $e$ as $\exp X_{i,e}$ and $\alpha_i=\max_e\|X_{i,e}\|$, using the SU(2) matrix operator norm so that $\|X\|$ is the class angle. At real parameters $s_i\in[0,1]$ apply the ordered translations $\prod_i \exp(s_i X_{i,e})U_e$. A zero $\alpha_i$ makes the corresponding finite difference vanish. Otherwise vary each $s_i$ by at most $\eta/(4k\alpha_i)$ in the complex plane. Around real parameters the base factors are unitary. Inserting the perturbations in order and telescoping bounds the matrix deviation by $\exp(\sum_i|\delta s_i|\alpha_i)-1<\eta$. This follows from the matrix exponential series and unitary conjugations in the partial products. Complex quaternion coordinates are fixed half-traces against the unit quaternion basis, so each coordinate deviation is at most the matrix operator norm. Padded exterior links stay fixed.

Cauchy's estimate in these $k$ discs bounds the mixed derivative by $1025(4k/\eta)^k\prod_i\alpha_i$. Integrating over the real unit cube gives the same bound for the ordered mixed finite difference. Averaging the differences gives $(I-S_m)^k$ up to overall sign: expand the ordered product, omit one translation in each selected position, then integrate the remaining independent rounds in their original order. Individual group translations need not commute. If $r$ links are kept, $\mathbb E\alpha_i\le Cr/m$ by summing their expected class angles, since the angle is at most $\pi/2$ times its chord. Independence across rounds gives remainder $O_{k,U}((r/m)^k)$.

Choose $R=\lceil16eU+k\log(m+1)\rceil$. The padding error is at most $8\exp(16eU-R)$ and $r=O_{k,U}((1+\log m)^3)$. Every term in $I-(I-S_m)^k$ contains a factor $S_m$, so the approximant has degree at most $3(m-1)$ per link. Its sup norm is at most $2(2^k-1)$. Gauge average it, keeping the same error against the true target. Combining padding and smoothing gives error $O_{k,U}(m^{-k}(1+\log m)^{3k})$. Applying the patch inverse inequality to this polynomial gives log inverse norm at most $C_{k,U,\beta}m(1+\log m)^4$ after centering. Choose $m$ of order $t/(1+\log t)^4$ with sufficiently small proportionality constant so the inverse norm is at most $e^{t/4}$. Projection below $\lambda=e^{-t}$ and squaring now yield
$$
\nu([t,\infty])=O_{k,U,\beta}\!\left(t^{-2k}(1+\log t)^{14k}\right).
$$
Given a desired energy moment order $p$, choose $2k>p$ and integrate the tail. This derivation allows the constants to depend on $k$. The separate time-jet argument gives the growth control used for moment determinacy. [Time-jet proof](time-jets.md)
