# Disposition of the ordinary-profile recovery

G4 reviewed C4 revision `3390846041614517434b3503c2e9b34e700839da`. The review centers on the passage from differenced tests to ordinary common-root magnetic profiles. C4's 21 supporting declarations replayed locally with standard axiom reports. The release's main scaling-limit claims are written mathematics. [Pinned release](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/tree/3390846041614517434b3503c2e9b34e700839da/yang-mills/central-quotients/magnetic-smearing)

**Disposition.** The recovery argument follows on each stated trajectory from the uniform plaquette moment bound, the growing-patch estimates for difference tests and gauged edges, and site reflection for the specified positive half-tests. The moving-root variation, stopped-walk residual and spectral deductions passed the algebra and order-of-limits checks below. Their use for the original Wilson vacuum depends on the cited construction and its uniform bounds. The fast weak-coupling trajectories concern the free magnetic limit. Existence at a nonzero renormalized interaction scale and the interacting mass gap remain open.

| Step | What the audit found |
| --- | --- |
| Common root after reflection | Reflection preserves the order of coordinate segments. If the two bases are adjacent, the needed conjugator in the common frame is the **gauged** joining edge. For a patch of radius \(O_M(r)\) its distance from identity is \(O_{M,q}(r/\sqrt\beta)\). Hölder with the face bound and total absolute weight \(O_M(r^2)\) gives \(O_{M,q}(r^3/\sqrt\beta)\). An off-axis rooted plaquette generally retains this conjugation. |
| Moving the selected link | At least one endpoint's simple tree path avoids the edge. Vary the link from that endpoint, so the conditional Haar generator is fixed. When a face's base path also moves, differentiating both copies of that transport produces a commutator bounded by \(2|F_p|/\sqrt\beta\). Summing these terms uses the absolute weight sum. The six directly incident faces use the maximum weight. |
| Stopped walk | Telescoping the surviving distributions leaves the law at the stopped endpoint, including the exit mass. Coupling that endpoint to the unrestricted endpoint on the same walk costs at most twice the exit probability in \(\ell^1\). For \(m=1,2\), putting a coordinate difference on each of \(m\) convolution factors costs \(O(s^{-m/2})\). With \(\nu=1+2/m\), the stated slow factor \(A\) gives both \(R/L\to0\) and \(A r^{2\nu+2}\sqrt{\log(2+r)}/\sqrt\beta\to0\). |
| Line cancellation | For first differences, translated and reflected weights have the same mass on each line. The second-order signed translate preserves mass and first moment. Reflection of the base sends the first moment to \(-m_1-m_0\). Center sampling of an even profile gives \(2m_1=-m_0\) on the lattice. Node sampling generally leaves a residual \(m_0\). |
| Reflection and limit order | Finite \(M\) comes first. Root paths for each positive block stay in the half-space, including any initial time segment on the reflecting plane. The free block multiplier vanishes almost everywhere in momentum as \(M\to\infty\). C4's moment argument uses reflection invariance for equality of the two absolute moments. The new tail proof instead derives probability control directly from the scalar reflection inequalities. |
| Sharp-time and spacetime tests | A fixed finite family of smooth sharp-time magnetic profiles has the stated covariance calculation. The bound uniform in oscillatory tests in the tightness proof uses the four-volume scaling and a bounded spatial-temporal support. Sharp-time high-frequency weights can have growing equal-time variance. The limiting time-slice assertions rely on their own Fourier estimates. |
| Energy normalization | Three independent Gaussian colors give \(\operatorname{Cov}(|X_0|^2/2,|X_t|^2/2)=3 C(t)^2/2\). For profile integral \(J\ne0\), angular integration gives the one-color density \(|J|^2 E^3/(6\pi^2)\) to leading order. The double convolution gives the claimed cumulative coefficient \(3|J|^4/(2\pi^4 8!)\). The quadratic probe requires fourth-moment convergence, including the zeroth spectral moment. Under the positive transfer representation, mass in each sufficiently low fixed physical window implies the stated upper limit for the scaled gap on these trajectories. |

**Controls.** An exact free-group word reducer checked 729 directed face/path cases covering all six face orientations and every spatial reflection axis they contain. All 729 matched the conjugated inverse. Removing the gauged one-edge conjugation changed 333 words. The finite line controls gave mass and first-moment residuals \((0,0)\) for center sampling and \((0,5)\) for a node-sampled example. The probability control verified 3,267 rational triples including threshold atoms and an example attaining the prefactor two. These counts test conventions and failure cases. The all-regulator conclusions use the hypotheses above.

The original explicit first-order example \(\beta=r^{17}, L=\lceil r^4\rceil\) satisfies the stated first-order inequalities. The written proof takes the infinite-time vacuum at fixed regulator first. It supplies no uniform error for an arbitrary growing number of profiles or a freely chosen diagonal \(M=M(r)\).
