# What the spatial-block profile determines at one correlation length

For a positive lattice gap $m$, Z4's condition (C) concerns a centered local reading at $\xi=1/m$. A bounded ratio $C_\beta(\xi/2)/C_\beta(\xi)$ controls the energy distribution after tilting at one correlation length. The results for a block probe give a uniform **absolute** error at every integer lag. For this same observable, uniform absolute accuracy can coexist with either behavior of the ratio. [Z4’s reduction](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/blob/2a05225/yang-mills/z4/attempt/A1_REDUCTION.md) · [Block covariance and error](spectrum.md)

Let $\nu$ be the normalized energy law with Laplace transform $B(t)/B(0)$ from the spatial-block calculation. It has no atom at zero and it gives positive mass to every interval $(0,\epsilon)$. Take $\epsilon\downarrow0$, $q=\nu([0,\epsilon))$,
$$
 D_\epsilon=\epsilon^{-1}+4(1+|\log q|)^2,\qquad e_0=\epsilon/D_\epsilon .
$$
Keep all of $\nu|_{[\epsilon,\infty)}$ in each of two probability laws. In the first, move the removed mass $q$ to energy $e_0$. In the second, put $q e^{-2D_\epsilon}$ at $e_0$ and $q(1-e^{-2D_\epsilon})$ at $\epsilon$. Both have spectral bottom $e_0>0$. At every real time $t\ge0$ the Laplace transform of each differs from that of $\nu$ by at most $q$, since the removed and replacement measures each have mass $q$ and $0\le e^{-tE}\le1$. One can take $q$ below any given positive tolerance, including a tolerance tending to zero along a prescribed sequence.

In block units examine $t=1/e_0$. For the first law the denominator is at least $q/e$ and the half-time numerator is at most $q+e^{-D_\epsilon/2}$. Their ratio stays bounded. For the second, the denominator is at most $q e^{-2D_\epsilon}+e^{-D_\epsilon}\le2e^{-D_\epsilon}$ and the half-time numerator is at least $q(1-e^{-2D_\epsilon})e^{-D_\epsilon/2}$. This ratio diverges since $D_\epsilon/2-|\log q|\to\infty$. The two sequences have the same limiting profile, the same power law of that limiting profile, and the same limiting mass on each fixed positive band. Each individual positive-gap model eventually has exponential decay. The difference comes from the weight of this observable at the smallest energies. Rounding either lag to an adjacent lattice step has the same conclusion when the block lattice size $R\ge1$ and $\epsilon\downarrow0$: the upper bounds on the high tail at either time use its minimum energy $\epsilon$ and the lower bounds use the two displayed atoms.

These laws have positive transfer realizations with a common gap in lattice units. On $\mathbb C\Omega\oplus L^2(\nu|_{[\epsilon,\infty)})\oplus\mathbb C^2$, let the Hamiltonian be zero on the vacuum, multiplication by $x/R$ on the continuous summand, and diagonal with entries $e_0/R,\epsilon/R$ on the last two coordinates. A unit excitation vector is the constant function $1$ on the continuous summand with the square roots of the selected atomic masses as its last two coordinates. The swap $O=|\psi\rangle\langle\Omega|+|\Omega\rangle\langle\psi|$ is a bounded centered one-site observable, and $T=e^{-H}$ produces its required readings. Identify the separable excitation spaces and conjugate $H$ with a unitary carrying each $\psi$ to a single reference vector. This makes the same bounded observable work across both families. Both have unique vacuum and gap $m=e_0/R$. This is an abstract transfer example. Deciding whether a suitable Wilson observable satisfies (C) still requires a Wilson estimate at its actual gap scale.
