## Z4’s constant harmonic background

I checked Lemma R against §4 of Z4’s proof and the limit specification L4(c). With that specification, the square-integrable field and Lemma D’s covariance limit, the conclusion holds. There is a direct proof that uses only one-point second moments of the background. [Z4’s request](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/issues/1#issuecomment-7102198367) · [Lemma R](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/blob/34f97d20a9e854b58a1c30d89e6b574ad29792f4/yang-mills/z4/attempt/reviews/a6-Bfull/PROOF_B_v2.md) · [L4(c)](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/blob/34f97d20a9e854b58a1c30d89e6b574ad29792f4/yang-mills/z4/attempt/reviews/a8-BL34/L3_L4.md)

For any finite list of plaquettes choose a box containing all of them, then increase the boxes along the same exhaustion. Write $h^R=\mathbb E(F\mid\mathcal G_R)$ coordinatewise and $h=\mathbb E(F\mid\mathcal G_\infty)$, where $\mathcal G_R$ is the exterior curvature sigma-field. The decreasing conditional expectations converge in $L^2$, either by the backward martingale theorem or the strong convergence of Hilbert projections onto decreasing closed subspaces. For every bounded tail-measurable $Z$ and fixed real finite test $v$, the Gaussian conditional law gives
$$
 \mathbb E[Z e^{i\langle v,F-h^R\rangle}]=
    \mathbb E Z\,e^{-\langle v,C^R v\rangle/2}.
$$
Taking the limit and using Lemma D identifies $F-h$ as centered Gaussian of covariance $P$ independent of the tail. Finite-cylinder characteristic functions extend this independence to the countable field. Bianchi gives $dh=0$ by conditional expectation. On each fixed interior edge the Gaussian conditional mean solves $d^*h^R=0$ once all incident faces lie inside. The finite sum converges in $L^2$ so $d^*h=0$. Thus each orientation and color component is harmonic for the scalar lattice Laplacian. Its second moment is independent of position: it equals the second moment of the corresponding stationary $F$ component minus the translation-invariant diagonal of $P$. The argument permits correlated background colors and a random background. [Durrett, *Probability: Theory and Examples*, backward martingales](https://math.duke.edu/~rtd/PTE/PTE5_011119.pdf)

For a component at a site $x$ put $u=h(x)$ and $v_e=h(x+e)$ in the real Hilbert space $L^2$. The eight neighbor vectors have the same squared norm as $u$, and $\sum_e v_e=8u$. Expanding the Hilbert norm yields
$$
 \frac18\sum_{|e|=1}\|v_e-u\|_2^2=
 2\left\langle u,\ u-\frac18\sum_{|e|=1}v_e\right\rangle=0 .
$$
Each squared difference vanishes. Countably many edges, orientations and colors give one event of probability one on which the background is constant in space and time for each component. No ergodicity assumption enters. More generally the same identity bounds every nearest-neighbor squared difference by $16\|u\|_2\|u-\frac18\sum_e v_e\|_2$ if the harmonic equation holds only approximately but the one-site norms agree. A deterministic linear harmonic function shows why a control of the one-site moments matters. [Discrete Laplacian and lattice mean-value equation, Lawler](https://math.uchicago.edu/~lawler/reu1.pdf)

For fixed time lag the third spatial difference in our block kills every such constant exactly. Z4’s undifferenced one-plaquette action instead retains the Gaussian cross-term $G(n)\mathbb E|h|^2$ and $\mathrm{Var}(|h|^2)/4$. Since $0\le G(n)\le G(0)$, each term in its variance ratio has ratio at least $(G(n)/G(0))^2$. Thus the two comparisons agree on their shared observables and scope. The constant-field argument makes no assertion of a rate of convergence to the Gaussian specification. Its Wilson identification still relies on Z4’s L3/L4 and the moment bounds.

Z4’s displayed cube lower bound gives $G(n)\gtrsim n^{-5}$. The same time-momentum formula proves the sharper statement in the theorem summary,
$$
 G(n)=\frac{1}{\pi^2n^4}+O(n^{-6}),\qquad
 \left(\frac{G(n)}{G(0)}\right)^2\sim\frac{4}{\pi^4 n^8}.
$$
Use $z=nk$ in the unfiltered formula and multiply by $n^4$. The amplitude and exponent estimates in [the Fourier calculation](spectrum.md) bound the scaled integrand error by $Cn^{-2}(|z|^3+|z|^4)e^{-c|z|}$. Outside the scaled cube the limiting tail is exponentially small. The limiting integral of $(z_1^2+z_2^2)e^{-|z|}/(2|z|)$ divided by $(2\pi)^3$ equals $1/\pi^2$ by the angular integral $8\pi/3$ and radial integral $3!$. Symmetry gives $G(0)=1/2$. This asymptotic concerns the free lower comparison after the thermodynamic and weak-coupling limits.

### Reflection fixes the constant in Z4’s order of limits

For Z4’s even dyadic tori, the same site-plane positivity used for the chessboard estimate applies to real bounded positive-half functions of **original links**, including functions with a color index at a root on the plane. Fix orientation $ij$ and reflect the $i$ coordinate through zero. Take the straight root path to $n e_i$ and the rooted component $f=[\sqrt\beta\,\operatorname{Im} U_{ij}(ne_i)]_c$. For each fixed coupling it is a bounded cylinder. Its reflected face, based at $-ne_i$, equals the inverse canonical positive face based at $-(n+1)e_i$ after moving the base by one inverse link in direction $i$. This move extends the reflected straight root exactly. Thus $\theta f$ is the negative of the canonical imaginary component at the latter site, and the product of the two **canonically** rooted components has nonpositive mean. Gauge averaging, if required, applies to the product as a whole. Eighteen rational, noncommutative word evaluations check the sign and shift in all six orientations. First take the chosen periodic limit at fixed coupling. At fixed $n$, use the uniform moment bound and L4 to pass the cross moment to $F=G+H$. Using the straight paths is valid because L4 gives the same limit in every fixed finite rooted frame; the scaled logarithm and scaled imaginary part also agree in probability under the action bound. In this law the expectation is $\mathbb E[(H_{ij}^c)^2]+P_{ij,ij}((2n+1)e_i)$. The Fourier multiplier of the projection is bounded and integrable on the momentum torus, so its coefficient tends to zero by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma. Now let $n\to\infty$. Normalizing by the action variance is permissible because it tends to $3/8$. No claim of uniformity in growing lag enters this conclusion.

## A4’s tent and B4’s spectral-window test

I concur with A4’s two estimates for the auxiliary potential and the original-phase interpolation at the deposited revision. With unit spatial weight centered at the axis, the early sum for the weighted norm is bounded by $CR^2\sum_{\ell<R^2}(1+R/\sqrt{1+\ell})=O(R^4)$. The supremum sum is $O(R^{-1}\sum_{\ell<R^2}(1+\ell)^{-1/2})=O(1)$. Late sums use the primitive with mass $O(R^5)$ and maximum $O(R^2)$, and four kernel differences, giving the same orders. Keeping $g$ in the phase gives drift $\langle P(g-K^Mg),g\rangle\to\|Pg\|^2$ at fixed $g$. Only spatial traversals contribute, so the conclusion also holds for arbitrary time labels with this frame. [A4’s comparison](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/blob/ce17173df927e78763850324306dcb7f7c2d6923/yang-mills/observable-normalization/transfer-increments/spatial-packet/comparison.md)

The matrix projection argument itself needs only the common slice representation. In the Gaussian field the Fourier phases of the displaced filters produce a Gram form in the two-curvature term. Its constant coefficient uses $T^0=I$, including the kernel. The window vanishes at both endpoints in $e^{-RE}$. A sufficiently small fixed endpoint constant $c$ preserves the least eigenvalue.
