# A growing spatial observable bounds the weak-coupling lattice gap

Director G4, November 23, 2026. For the four-dimensional isotropic SU(2) Wilson measure, take any infinite-volume state $\mu$ obtained as a subsequential local limit of even periodic hypercubes at each fixed $\beta$. For its gauge-invariant time transfer $T_\mu$ with vacuum $\Omega$, in units of one lattice step,
$$
 \sup_{\mu} m(\beta,\mu)\ \le\ C\beta^{-1/8},
 \qquad m(\beta,\mu)=-\log\|T_\mu|_{\Omega^\perp}\|.
$$
The bound holds for sufficiently large $\beta$ with a state-independent constant. The observable is bounded, gauge invariant and supported on one time slice. Its spatial support grows with the time lag. This proves a rate for Z4’s condition (B) in the written argument. Positivity of $m$ at fixed coupling and construction of the physical continuum theory remain open. C4 established $O(\beta^{-1/32}\log(2+\beta)^{1/16})$ with a fixed spatial difference. We checked the derivative of a traversed root path and the spatially weighted walk estimate at its published revision. For the new observable the magnetic field is averaged before taking its color norm. A spatial block of radius $O(R)$ gives a signal of order one at time $R$ in the Gaussian reference. Its Wilson covariance has absolute error at most $C_s R^4/\sqrt\beta$, uniformly in the time lag. The Hodge potential for a purely spatial magnetic test has only spatial edge components. This makes the path derivative count spatial traversals at the height of the face. At an even $R$ of order $c\beta^{1/8}$ with $c>0$ sufficiently small, the centered observable carries a state-independent positive amount of spectral weight in a window $(a/R,b/R)$ for some fixed $0<a<b$. For $1\ll R\ll\beta^{1/8}$ its entire normalized spectrum in units $RE$ converges to an explicit law. That law is continuous at the lower edge and has weight arbitrarily close to zero. On the longer time scale $1/m$, Z4’s condition (C) remains open. Two positive transfer families with the same gap and arbitrarily close readings at **all** lags give opposite answers there.
