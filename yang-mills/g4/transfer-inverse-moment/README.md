# A finite energy window for a spatial Wilson plaquette

Director G4, 23 November 2026. Consider the ordinary elementary magnetic plaquette of four-dimensional isotropic SU(2) Wilson theory after spatial gradient flow. For every fixed coupling $0<\beta<\infty$ and finite lattice flow time $u\ge0$, some of its connected spectral weight lies between two **finite, strictly positive lattice energies**, uniformly in the spatial volume. At $\beta=1$ one can give the same explicit window for the bare plaquette and a short interval of positive flow times.

| Isotropic coupling | Lattice flow time | Transfer interval | Lower bound on absolute spectral weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $1$ | $0$ | $[2^{-43},\sqrt{1-3/800}]$ | $3/400$ |
| $1$ | $0\le u\le1/512$ | same interval | $1/400$ |
| any fixed $\beta>0$ | any fixed finite $u\ge0$ | $[s,b]\subset(0,1)$ given below | $\rho/2$ |

The weight is unnormalized. At each lag $k\ge0$ a row with weight $w$ gives a lower bound $w s^k$ for the connected correlation. The interval describes weight carried by one observable. Spectral weight below its lower **energy** endpoint is allowed. A uniform physical mass gap and the continuum limit remain open. ## Observable and spectral measure

Use a periodic spatial cube of even side $L\ge4$, one transfer step per lattice time unit, and the infinite-time vacuum at each fixed $L$. Write $\operatorname{Re}q=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}q$ on the unit quaternions. The action is $\beta\sum_p(1-\operatorname{Re}U_p)$. On a spatial slice evolve the links by the negative gradient of the sum of spatial plaquette deficits in the product unit round metric. Let $F_u=1-\operatorname{Re}U_p(u)$ for one fixed elementary spatial plaquette, and let $G_u=F_u-\langle F_u\rangle$. With unit vacuum vector $\Omega$ and vacuum-normalized positive transfer $T$, define $\nu_u(D)=\|\mathbf1_D(T)G_u\Omega\|^2$. Thus $C_u(k)=\int\lambda^k\,d\nu_u$ and $\nu_u([0,1])\le1$ by the range $0\le F_u\le2$. Energies in this note are $E=-\log\lambda$, with $E=\infty$ at $\lambda=0$. Lüscher constructs the positive physical transfer. Sakai and Sasaki use spatial flow for glueball spectroscopy. Our estimates use the lattice clock specified here. [Lüscher, transfer matrix](https://bib-pubdb1.desy.de/record/396349/files/7611148.pdf) · [Sakai and Sasaki, §II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.15176) · [Flow conventions](../spatial-flow-variance/README.md)

## How to select the endpoints

1. The two exterior cap events give $C_u(0)-C_u(2)\ge\rho$. An explicit choice is $\rho=(49/32)[q(e^{-16u}/16)e^{-12\beta}]^{m_0}$ where $q(t)$ is the Haar mass of a chord ball of radius $t$ and $m_0=3(2R_0+7)^3$. Choose $R_0\ge0$ such that $\sum_{j>R_0}(16u)^j/j!\le1/32$. The cap argument bounds the error of any predictor using the previous slice. Stationarity and reversibility identify its best squared error with this correlation difference. [Exterior prediction proof](../spatial-flow-variance/README.md)
2. Choose a gauge-invariant polynomial $P$ with $\|F_u-P\|_\infty\le\sqrt{\rho}/4$ by the construction below. Its centered vacuum vector has inverse first transfer moment bounded by $B_*$ uniformly in $L$. Take $B_*\ge1$. [Polynomial approximation](flow-tail.md) · [Inverse-form estimate](inverse-form.md)
3. Put $s=\min(1/2,\rho/(16B_*))$ and $b=\sqrt{1-\rho/4}$. Projection and orthogonal centering contract the norm, so $\nu_u([0,s])^{1/2}\le\sqrt{\rho}/4+\sqrt{sB_*}\le\sqrt{\rho}/2$. The loss $C_u(0)-C_u(2)$ integrates $1-\lambda^2$. The part below $s$ is at most $\rho/4$. The part above $b$ is at most $\rho/4$ since the total mass is at most one. On $[s,b]$ the loss is at most its spectral mass. Thus $\nu_u([s,b])\ge\rho/2$. The endpoints can be chosen uniformly for $\beta$ in a compact subinterval of $(0,\infty)$ and $u$ in a bounded interval by taking the worst cap, patch size and inverse budget in the displayed bounds.

These inequalities pass to any subsequential thermodynamic spectral limit. On $[0,1]$, the moments at integer lags determine the measure. Joint local convergence on finitely many slices gives convergence of these moments by the uniform finite-patch approximation. For the identity-padded flow the finite-volume ODE on a fixed input patch also has a local limit: cut the ODE outside a larger cube, use the bounded boundary forces and integrate the factorial comparison tail to the target, then enlarge the cube. Equivalently compare periodic lifts on the infinite lattice using the same bounded-degree flow inequality. A closed interval retains the finite-volume lower bound under weak convergence. The high-energy tail likewise yields finite energy moments of every order $0<p<2$. For physical spacing $a$, the energy is $E/a$ and fixed physical diffusion time requires $u$ proportional to $a^{-2}$. The constants above can deteriorate in that limit.
