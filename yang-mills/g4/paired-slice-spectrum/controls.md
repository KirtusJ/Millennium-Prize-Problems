# Compact rotor calculation and the matched Wilson records

## A finite-regulator spectrum with certified inputs

The control is the one-loop $SU(2)$ class rotor at $\beta_t=2$ and zero magnetic interaction, with time circumference two. Its state space consists of square-integrable conjugacy-class functions. The characters $\chi_d(\theta)=\sin(d\theta)/\sin\theta$, $d\ge1$, are orthonormal for $(2/\pi)\sin^2\theta\,d\theta$. Temporal Wilson convolution has eigenvalues proportional to $r_d=I_d(2)$, with the same factor for every $d$. In the character integral this follows from $I_{d-1}(2)-I_{d+1}(2)=d I_d(2)$ after the convolution dimension factor. This control exercises compact-group Haar integration; its graph is different from a four-dimensional spatial cube. [Wilson character calculation](../../observable-normalization/replica-gap/electric.md) · [Modified Bessel recurrences](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.29)

Use 32 equal intervals of the class angle $0\le\theta\le\pi$. Each orbit chord diameter is at most $\pi/32$. Define $H_i(d,e)=(2/\pi)\int_{C_i}\sin(d\theta)\sin(e\theta)\,d\theta$. Integrating the squared kernel gives
$$p_{ij}=\frac{\sum_{d,e\ge1}r_dr_eH_i(d,e)H_j(d,e)}{\sum_{d\ge1}r_d^2}.$$
All entries are integrated before taking a square root. The computation cuts off at $d=15$, uses $|H_i|\le1$ and $\sum_{d>D} I_d(2)\le4/(D+1)!$ for $D\ge3$. Indeed the positive series gives $I_d(2)\le e^{1/(d+1)}/d!$. The omitted double sum has absolute value at most $2AS+S^2$ for retained coefficient sum $A$ and omitted sum at most $S$. The Gram integrals reduce to differences of sines on a uniform grid. The program encloses $\pi$ using Machin's arctangent formula, encloses the sine and Bessel series by their tails, and rounds arithmetic outwards on a dyadic grid. [Executable interval computation](rotor.py)

For the numerical certificate choose a unit even vector $u_i=\sqrt{H_i(1,1)}$ and the normalized odd vector $v_i\propto H_i(1,2)/u_i$. The symmetry $\theta\mapsto\pi-\theta$ at both ends makes their cross term vanish for $B$. Write $a=u^TBu$, $b=v^TBv$, $z=2\pi/32$. The vacuum singular value is at least $(1-z^2/2)a$. By the two-dimensional min–max bound and $\|C-B\|_{\rm op}\le z^2/2$, the next is at least $\min(a,b)-z^2/2$. If these lower estimates are $l_1,l_2>0$, Hilbert–Schmidt normalization supplies the upper estimates $\sqrt{1-l_2^2},\sqrt{1-l_1^2}$ respectively. This avoids any floating-point eigenvalue assumption. Only the construction of the test histogram uses the known transfer kernel. The independent spectral answers follow directly from $r_1^2/\sum r_d^2$ and $r_2/r_1$. The interval results in the [main table](README.md) have outward-rounded endpoints. The excitation ratio implies a strictly positive rotor gap in $[-\log(0.503054),-\log(0.403514)]$ in lattice time units.

## Which data the four-dimensional application needs

Z4’s published predeclared rows contain a configuration index, a plaquette summary and slice averages of the flowed magnetic density at multiple flow times. A full-orbit histogram cannot be reconstructed from those columns. There is an exact obstruction to treating a small change in the recorded scalar as a small full-slice chord distance. On a spatial torus insert the center sign $-I$ on one transverse seam of axial links. All spatial plaquette products are unchanged. This flat center transformation preserves the spatial action and the product metric, hence commutes with its gradient flow. Every measured spatial deficit agrees before and after the twist at every flow time. The four-dimensional center symmetry also leaves the ordinary plaquette summary unchanged when extended through time. [Measurement definition](../../z4/fixed-size/MATCHED_MEASUREMENT.md) · [Stored rows](../../z4/reviews/ext-match/predeclared/)

Compare the all-identity slice with that twist. On each of the $L^2$ disjoint axial lines the holonomy is $I$ for the first slice and $-I$ for the second. Telescoping the product of unitaries gives a link chord sum at least two on each line in every relative gauge. The seam representatives attain $2L^2$, hence this is their exact orbit distance. For any energy bin containing a relative neighborhood of the zero reading, continuity gives positive-Haar-measure neighborhoods of both slices with distances arbitrarily close to $2L^2$. This establishes a large essential diameter for that bin. Its probability and the widths of other cells are separate quantities.

For scale, if every chord diameter on the $2L^3+1$ remaining links in a fixed spanning-tree gauge is bounded by $\epsilon$, the uniform vacuum lower multiplier is at least $\operatorname{sech}^2[\beta_t(2L^3+1)\epsilon]$. Retaining a multiplier of $0.99$ is guaranteed by $\epsilon\le\operatorname{atanh}(0.1)/[\beta_t(2L^3+1)]$. This gives about $1.01\times10^{-4}$, $4.08\times10^{-5}$ and $1.16\times10^{-5}$ at the frozen $(L,\beta_t)=(6,2.3),(8,2.4),(12,2.5)$. These are sufficient per-link resolutions for a worst-case certificate. The stored averages have no such guarantee. The new lower bounds would require full-slice data or an independent bound on the oscillation inside the cells actually measured.
