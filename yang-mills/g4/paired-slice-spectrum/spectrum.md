# From an opposite-slice distribution to spectral bounds

Use normalized spatial product Haar $\mu$ (equivalently its pushforward to gauge orbits). Let $K_n(x,y)>0$ be the real symmetric gauge-projected kernel of $T^n$ with the two physical spatial half-weights. Integrating the two complementary slabs of the ordinary period-$2n$ path gives the joint density $U(x,y)^2$ where $U=K_n/\sqrt{Z_{2n}}$, $Z_{2n}=\operatorname{Tr}_{\rm phys}T^{2n}$. The operator $U$ has Hilbert–Schmidt norm one. Its nonnegative eigenvalues $s_i$ are $\lambda_i^n/\sqrt{Z_{2n}}$, so $s_i^2=p_i^{\rm th}$. Positivity of the kernel fixes its pointwise square root; positivity of the physical transfer identifies its singular values with its eigenvalues. The strictly positive continuous kernel has a unique top eigenvector at finite volume. ## The Wilson estimate at a cell boundary

Write $M(x)$ for the positive spatial half-weight. On spatial orbits use
$$d_G(x,x')=\inf_g\sum_e\|x_e-(g x')_e\|_{\rm op},$$
with independent local site transformations in the infimum and the fundamental unitary representation. After taking out $M(x)M(y)$ from the slab integral, each spatial endpoint link enters just one temporal plaquette. Its normalized real trace changes by at most the operator chord of the changed link, by unitary invariance and $|\operatorname{Tr}A|/2\le\|A\|_{\rm op}$. Pointwise comparison of the positive integrands and integration give
$$|\log F(x,y)-\log F(x',y')|\le\beta_t[d_G(x,x')+d_G(y,y')],\qquad F=U/(M(x)M(y)).$$
Endpoint gauge transformations can be chosen independently after the gauge integrations. For $n=1$, first change $x$, then $y$, even though both occur in the same face. For $n>1$ the intermediate spatial weights are positive and unchanged. This accounts for the absence of an explicit factor of $n$ or $\beta_s$ in the oscillation bound.

Take a finite measurable orbit partition, dropping Haar-null cells, and bounds on their essential diameters. Let $w_i=\int_{C_i}M^2d\mu$ and $\phi_i=M1_{C_i}/\sqrt{w_i}$. The $\phi_i$ are orthonormal. Compress $U$ to their span and call its matrix $C$. On the cell pair use the normalized product measure proportional to $M(x)^2M(y)^2$. Then $C_{ij}=\sqrt{w_iw_j}\,\mathbb EF$ and $B_{ij}=\sqrt{w_iw_j}\sqrt{\mathbb EF^2}$. No $w_i$ is needed to compute $B$ from probabilities.

If $0<m\le F\le M_0$, then $\mathbb EF^2\le(m+M_0)\mathbb EF-mM_0$ by the pointwise secant. Completing the square yields $4mM_0\mathbb EF^2\le(m+M_0)^2(\mathbb EF)^2$. The ratio of mean to root mean square is at least $2\sqrt{mM_0}/(m+M_0)$ and at most one. Taking the cell oscillation gives $h_{ij}B_{ij}\le C_{ij}\le B_{ij}$. This is an elementary sharp bound for a random variable of bounded range.

## Recovering the entire ordered law

For any vector split over cells, Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its block bilinear form for $U$ by that for $B$ on the vector of cell norms. Thus $s_1\le\|B\|_{\rm op}$. In the other direction nonnegative symmetric matrices attain their operator norm on a nonnegative Perron vector. Compression and entrywise domination give $\|h\circ B\|_{\rm op}\le\|C\|_{\rm op}\le s_1$.

Let $c_i$ denote the decreasing eigenvalues of the positive compression $C$, padded with zeros. By min–max, $c_i\le s_i$ individually. In particular the sum of the nonnegative differences $s_i^2-c_i^2$ equals $1-\|C\|_F^2\le\ell$. This gives the tail bound beyond the dimension $D$. The cell bounds also give $\|B-C\|_F\le d$. The finite-dimensional singular-value perturbation inequality gives $\sum_i|\sigma_i-c_i|^2\le d^2$. It also follows by expanding $\|B-C\|_F^2$ and applying the trace singular-value inequality to the cross term. This compares singular values even if $B$ has negative eigenvalues. Since the two singular-value vectors have Euclidean norms at most one, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\sum_i|\sigma_i^2-c_i^2|\le2d$. Adding the compression deficit proves the stated total error. In particular
$$ (\sigma_i-d)_+^2\le p_i^{\rm th}\le \min\{1,(\sigma_i+d)^2+\ell\}.$$
For $y=\beta_t\delta$, uniform diameters give $\ell\le\tanh^2 y\le y^2$ and $d\le1-\operatorname{sech}y\le y^2/2$, by integrating $\operatorname{sech}u\,\tanh u\le u$. [Mirsky, singular-value inequalities](https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/11.1.50)

If $L_i,U_i$ enclose $p_i^{\rm th}$, the finite-box gap is at least $\max\{0,\log(L_1/U_2)/(2n)\}$ and at most $\log(U_1/L_2)/(2n)$ when the endpoints are positive. A zero $L_2$ permits an infinite upper bound; the trivial lower bound is zero when $L_1=0$. A zero $U_2$ forces all excited transfer values to vanish. The factor $2n$ converts Gibbs probabilities to one-step energies. Purity and higher replica ratios can also be bounded because $Z_{kN}/Z_N^k=\sum_i(p_i^{\rm th})^k$ and $x^k$ is $k$-Lipschitz on $[0,1]$. No observable-overlap hypothesis enters this spectral comparison.

## Finite data and resolution

With a normalized empirical histogram $\widehat p$ and $\|\sqrt{\widehat p}-\sqrt p\|_F\le\eta$ (entrywise roots), its ordered squared singular values contribute at most another $2\eta$ in summed absolute error by the same singular-value bound. One may use the uniform diameter bounds on $d,\ell$. For unequal diameters and empirical weighted sums, enlarge $\ell$ by $2\eta$ and $d$ by $\eta$ relative to their empirical counterparts: total variation follows from Cauchy–Schwarz on square roots, and multiplication by $1-h_{ij}\le1$ contracts Frobenius norm. For the direct vacuum bracket replace the lower norm by $(\|h\circ\sqrt{\widehat p}\|_{\rm op}-\eta)_+$ and the upper norm by $\|\sqrt{\widehat p}\|_{\rm op}+\eta$. Symmetrization of a table with its transpose preserves normalization and cannot increase Hellinger distance to the symmetric law.

For a fixed partition with $m$ possible ordered pairs and $S$ independent equilibrium histories, one randomly selected opposite pair per history, $\eta^2=[m\log(S+1)+\log(1/\alpha)]/S$ suffices with probability at least $1-\alpha$. The multinomial probability of any particular type $q$ is at most $\exp[-S D(q\|p)]$ by comparing its likelihood at $p$ and at $q$; there are at most $(S+1)^m$ types. Jensen gives $\sum(\sqrt q-\sqrt p)^2\le-2\log\sum\sqrt{qp}\le D(q\|p)$. This conservative certificate can be vacuous for a large partition. Adaptive partitions require independent validation or a simultaneous guarantee. Markov-chain rows and time translates of one history require dependence control before this confidence calculation applies.

On any fixed finite graph the compact orbit space admits finite nets of arbitrarily small diameter, so refinement with exact probabilities recovers the whole ordered law. A spanning-tree gauge reduces the number of variable links to $E-V+1$ on a connected graph; a cover of the remaining compact product induces an orbit cover modulo residual conjugation. Uniform bounds in these statements are in the metric $d_G$. No estimate here keeps the number of required cells or histories small along a continuum limit.
