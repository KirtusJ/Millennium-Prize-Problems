### Two seams and half observables

Let \(A=L_{P-1}(e)\), \(D=L_0(e)\), and \(T_a,T_b\) be the forward wrap links. The physical wrap word is \(AT_bD^{-1}T_a^{-1}\). Its inverse is \(T_aDT_b^{-1}A^{-1}\), whose cyclic rotation is \(DT_b^{-1}A^{-1}T_a\), exactly the split word. At the other seam, the split word is the forward plaquette as written. The internal reflected temporal link \(T_{P-2-j}^{-1}\) also has the stated orientation. These identities hold in the nonabelian case. The sign-cycle example alone cannot check inversion because every sign is its own inverse.

At \(P=2\), the two crossing terms use the same spatial function on each half and independent seam fields under the reference law. The physical forward links \(T_0(v)\) and \(T_1(v)\) give distinct link variables even though their endpoints coincide as unordered pairs. An encoding that stores only one link for that pair and treats the reverse orientation as its inverse would change the model. The two plaquette layers must likewise retain their multiplicities.

The Haar substitution acts only on the seam variables. It leaves \(s\) and \(F\) untouched, so the feature proof requires no gauge invariance of either. It does require identical half reference laws, the same within-half weight in reflected coordinates, and the stated seam factorization. The normalization argument also presumes a nonzero finite half reference measure and an integrable test. A probability reference law and continuous \(F,s\) on compact \(X\) suffice. For the whole finite Gram matrix, the Haar projection can be viewed as integrating each half’s tensor features separately, yielding a positive semidefinite matrix for arbitrary complex coefficients.

The split coordinates retain holonomy because they retain every temporal link, with the indicated inversions. With the product based at time zero, the period-two holonomy is \(T_0(v)T_1(v)=h_{1,v}h_{0,v}^{-1}\). This retention is a property of the full coordinates and measure. The marginal kernel on \((x,y)\) has integrated out the seam variables. Computing a holonomy moment from that marginalization requires an observable-weighted integral, as the script uses.

### Enumeration and spectrum

- Each one-seam average divides by \(16\). Squaring it incorporates the two independent seam layers with denominator \(16^2\). The outer average divides by another \(16^2\) for the spatial pairs. The common factor \(\cosh^8\beta\) then gives the stated scaled partition functions \(1+t^8\) and \((1+t^2)^4\).
- The parity quadratics in the script are scaled by \(\cosh^8\beta\). The corresponding physical expectation in the positive model is \(2t^4/(1+t^8)\). The print labels should say **scaled partition functions** and **scaled parity quadratics** to distinguish these outputs from expectations divided by \(Z\). The Polyakov moments already use the appropriate expectation normalization.
- With the uniform probability measure on the 16 spatial configurations, \(1\) and \(\chi\) are orthonormal. Their eigenvalues are \(\cosh^8\beta(1+t^8)\) and \(2\cosh^8\beta\,t^4\). The unscaled \(16\times16\) kernel matrix has eigenvalues 16 times those values. The operator has rank two for \(\beta\ne0\) and rank one at \(\beta=0\). This example therefore defeats strict positivity on the whole half-function space, including at \(\beta=0.8\).
- Flipping one plaquette coupling in each seam reverses the parity coefficient and gives a negative quadratic on \(\chi\). Flipping one in only one seam gives the constant factor \(1-t^8\) after scaling. The Polyakov numerator vanishes because a simultaneous sign flip of every temporal link on one seam preserves its weight and changes the sign of the insertion. The all-ones model has Polyakov moment \(1\) by construction.

The spectral calculation applies to this finite projected kernel. Any transfer-operator or gap claim would require a separately specified operator and a proof connecting it to the physical time evolution.
