## (A): three routes, three breaks

Each builder was told to commit to a definite argument, tag every step as proved, cited, claimed or missing, run it on the discriminators, and test its first unproved step. The chains are in the reports; in brief:

**Curvature** ([chain](reviews/a3-Acurv/PROOF.md), [independent challenge](reviews/a4-chalcurv/report.md)). Bakry–Émery on SU(2)^E: the Ricci curvature of the group against the Hessian of the action.
- Proved and upheld by the challenger: for gauge-invariant functions only the horizontal part of the curvature matters (the gauge directions drop out); at flat configurations the horizontal curvature equals the Ricci constant exactly. This is the one place non-abelian structure enters: for U(1) the constant is zero.
- Cited: the pointwise criterion holds for β below 1/12 (Shen, Zhu, Zhu). Proved: it cannot hold pointwise above β = 1/2, because one plaquette at −1 has curvature 1 − 2β.
- **The break.** The step meant to carry the route to large β (negative curvature occurs only on rare configurations) was refuted by the builder's own test and the refutation was reproduced by a challenger with separate code: on the periodic lattice the horizontal curvature of *typical* equilibrium configurations is between −1.1 and −2.3 at β = 4, 6, 12, 24, 48, on 3⁴ and 4⁴ (0 of 24 of the challenger's samples above 1/2). The mechanism was measured on a one-loop ensemble without toron fluctuations (one or two seeds, one box size per group): curvature ≈ Ricci − k·β⟨fluctuation²⟩ with k ≈ 2.5 for SU(2), a second-order repulsion of the soft modes, independent of β from 16 to 1024; equilibrium sits at 2.5 times the threshold. For SU(3) the relative deficit is slightly smaller (2.3 against 2.5; one seed) and nowhere near closing.
- **A nuance the challenger found, which the builder's sentence missed.** On the torus at large β the negative modes are the constant (toron) modes. In an *open* 4⁴ box the typical horizontal curvature is strongly positive at β ≥ 12 (+3, +9.6, +23 at β = 12, 24, 48) and of either sign at β = 6. So typical-configuration curvature is positive in the open 4⁴ box at β ≥ 12; the challenger argues, as an extrapolation and not a measurement, that this persists up to side about √β. This does not rescue the route: the criterion is pointwise, the missing step is a mixing condition uniform over all boundary conditions, and the scale √β is nowhere near the correlation length. It is recorded because it is a fact about the measure that a later attempt might use.
- What is left missing is a strong-mixing condition uniform in boxes and boundary conditions, which is (A) in a stronger form. Circular. The steps after it (strong mixing gives a log-Sobolev inequality; a Poincaré inequality gives decay with a rate uniform in the supports) are claimed from the builder's memory of the literature, not read.

**Center vortices** ([chain](reviews/a3-Avort/PROOF.md)). Twisted boundary conditions, the vortex free energy F_L, reflection positivity.
- Proved or cited: 0 < Z⁻ ≤ Z; the Tomboulis–Yaffe / Kovács–Tomboulis inequality bounding Wilson loops by the electric free energy (read in Kovács–Tomboulis, eq. (11), by the builder); hence an area law **if** F_L decays like e^(−σL²) uniformly.
- **Break 1:** that decay at every β is the confinement problem. Monotonicity of F_L in L was claimed with a renormalization-group argument that is exactly the comparison Ito and Seiler object to in Tomboulis's paper; the builder does not close it. Tested: false for Z₂ at β = 0.6 (as it must be: deconfined), exact in two dimensions, undecided for SU(2) at β = 1.8 and 2.4 on 2⁴ to 4⁴ (errors as large as the differences; at β = 2.4 the step from 3 to 4 has the wrong sign at 1.2 standard errors). The inequality was read in Kovács–Tomboulis; Tomboulis–Yaffe, to which they attribute it, was not read.
- **Break 2, independent of the first:** an area law does not give a gap. No theorem goes that way (Chatterjee's goes from a strong gap to the area law), and as an abstract implication it is false: a confining theory tensored with a decoupled massless field has an area law and no gap. The flux free energy controls the sectors of nonzero electric flux, not the neutral sector where glueballs live. The program already has this obstruction (B4: a gap in each of two sectors does not give a gap).
- **No proved or cited step of this chain uses non-abelian structure.** Everything proved holds verbatim for U(1) and Z₂.

**Finite-size criterion** ([chain](reviews/a3-Afs/PROOF.md)). A Dobrushin–Shlosman-type condition at one scale, plus a running coupling z(L, β) = L·(finite-volume gap) claimed to grow with L.
- Proved, given their inputs: a uniform rate of Euclidean decay gives a transfer-matrix gap; uniqueness gives a reflection-positive translation-invariant state.
- Claimed: the criterion itself for link variables (its dependence on boundary conditions and gauge invariance was not checked against a source). Claimed from perturbation theory and a small Monte Carlo: monotonicity of z.
- **Missing, twice:** that z is unbounded in L at every β (confinement again), and any inequality at all connecting a periodic-box number to a condition uniform over exterior configurations, even at strong coupling.
- Measured, and the cleanest picture of the discriminator in this attempt: at β = 2.3 on L³×12, z = 0.56, 2.1, 8.0 for L = 2, 4, 6 in SU(2), against 0.34, 0.55, 0.68 for U(1), where z is expected to be flat (1/(2β) = 0.22). The builder's predicate that the U(1) values lie within a factor of two of that failed; a rotor model fitted afterwards attributes the drift to the finite temperature of the lattice, with residuals of 1.5 to 2.6 standard errors. So: a steep rise for SU(2) and a slow drift for U(1) that is probably thermal; nothing proves either.

**What the three breaks have in common.** Each route reaches, by a different road, a statement that is the infinite-volume gap or confinement at weak coupling under another name: strong mixing uniform in boundary conditions; decay of the vortex free energy; unbounded growth of a finite-volume coupling. In each, the only place the non-abelian group enters with proof is a local fact (the Ricci constant; nowhere; nowhere), and the step that needs it globally is asymptotic freedom used non-perturbatively. That is the content of the problem, and the literature says so (Jaffe and Witten: no idea for a gap uniform in the volume; Chatterjee: the weak-coupling non-abelian area law "remains elusive"). **Attempt 1 adds no idea to it.** What it adds is the list of exactly which statements would suffice, three numerical pictures of the non-abelian/abelian difference, and one refuted mechanism.

**A fourth route, sought on purpose to have a different last step, and broken at its first unproved step** ([report](reviews/a7-wild/report.md)). The idea: write the partition function in its dual, plaquette-character form; if every dual amplitude were nonnegative, SU(2) would be a positive random-surface model and the tools that settle percolation-type questions might apply, with no appeal to a running coupling. For U(1) and Z₂ the amplitudes are indicators. **For SU(2) they are not all nonnegative:** on the periodic 2×1×1 lattice in three dimensions, which embeds in four, one amplitude is −0.003086419753, numerically −1/324 (the rational value is the worker's reading of the float, not proved; 4 of 729 configurations are negative), confirmed by a separate Monte Carlo at −0.002892 ± 0.000237; the director reran that program, bytes identical (register checks K43, failed because the director had not copied a module it imports, and K44, exit 0, in `../pcp/linux-23nov/register.jsonl`). The literature knows this as the sign problem of the dual (found in search snippets, not read). So in the plaquette-character dual the non-abelian group shows up as cancellation, and the positivity tools lose their grip there. The test refutes positivity of that dual only; a resummation over intertwiners, or another representation, is not excluded.

**The wall, stated as a negative result** (the same report, section 3). Six properties are shared, with proof or citation, by SU(2), U(1) and Z₂ in four dimensions: reflection positivity; a strictly positive transfer matrix with a Perron–Frobenius ground state; positive character coefficients of the Wilson weight at every β; a gap and analyticity at small β; a positive gap in every finite volume; a nontrivial center. U(1) has all six and no gap at weak coupling. **So no argument using only these six can prove (A).** The proved, non-perturbative properties that do separate SU(2) from U(1) are few and local: the group is perfect (no homomorphism to U(1)), its Ricci curvature is positive, and its dual amplitudes can be negative. None has a proved global consequence for the gap. Other candidates weighed and where each meets the wall: analyticity in β (proved only at small β; at all β it would exclude a transition U(1) has); the Hamiltonian limit (still the infinite-volume gap); stochastic quantization (a uniform gap of the Langevin generator is the strong mixing of the curvature route); monotonicity in the dimension (no inequality known, and U(1) is gapped in three dimensions and not in four).

## (B): the one link that might be provable

[Chain](reviews/a3-B/PROOF.md). An upper bound on the gap from slow decay at fixed distance: if the normalized correlation of rescaled plaquette energies at fixed separation n has a weak-coupling limit c(n) > 0 with −log c(n)/n → 0, then m(β) → 0. Proved: the spectral bound and the reduction (in Lean). Claimed, in three parts: exponential tails of β(1 − cos α_p) uniform in the volume, by reflection positivity and a chessboard estimate (tested in two dimensions); every weak-coupling limit point of the infinite-volume states is a Gibbs state of the free three-colour Maxwell field (the exchange of the two limits); the classification of those Gibbs states. For a finite group the chain fails at the Gaussian scaling, as it must. If it holds it proves (B) and says nothing about (A): for U(1) it goes through and returns "no gap", correctly.

**Outcome of the challenge** ([report](reviews/a5-chalB/report.md)). Seventeen items stand (two of them only as argument, one checked against a source at abstract level only; the chessboard theorem itself was cited from memory), one defect, one not checked; the challenger found no route that avoids the exchange of limits. What stands, with the challenger's own programs: the spectral bound and reduction; reflection positivity in site planes in all four directions (its own short proof: no plaquette straddles a site plane; the literature found only at abstract level); the chessboard count and the factor 1/8; the partition-function bounds (constant about e⁵¹: finite, useless numerically); the tail bound exactly in two dimensions (888 of 888 cases) and by Monte Carlo on 4⁴ and 6⁴ at β = 4, 8, 16 (24 of 24; the true tail is much thinner than the bound); the estimate that instantons, thick vortices, torons and gauge copies contribute nothing of order one at fixed distance; the free correlation, positive at all 17 separations computed with −log c(n)/n falling toward zero; and the two discriminators (finite groups fail at the Gaussian scaling; U(1) goes through and returns no gap). **Defect:** the gauge fixing in the local-limit step makes the boundary data depend on interior links, so the conditioning is circular as written; the challenger gives the repair (fix the gauge of the boundary shell within the shell). **Not checked:** the classification of the Gibbs states of the free Maxwell field. **Standing only as argument:** the uniform local limit itself.

**Version 2** ([`reviews/a6-Bfull/PROOF_B_v2.md`](reviews/a6-Bfull/PROOF_B_v2.md)), by a second builder told to write it as for a referee. Its own first line: not yet a complete proof. It states the honest theorem, which is narrower than (B) as the attempt put it:

> **Theorem B′ (not yet proved).** For limit points of the periodic states along boxes of side 2^k, and for the vector made from one rescaled plaquette energy: the normalized correlation at every even separation n is, in the weak-coupling limit, at least (G(n)/G(0))² ≥ c₀·n^(−10), G the free field-strength correlation. Hence the supremum of the gap over those states tends to zero.

Written out in full in that file, with nothing cited (no source was read at theorem level; the chessboard estimate is re-proved): reflection positivity in site planes in all four directions; the chessboard estimate for dyadic sides; the free-energy bounds and the uniform tail; nondegeneracy of the Dirichlet Maxwell form; a decomposition of every limit point as a centered free field plus an independent background, which **bypasses the classification of Gibbs states** that the challenger could not check; positivity and decay of G. **Still claimed, two steps:** the Laplace limit of the conditional law uniformly over compact boundary data (an outline; the constants are not written), and the bookkeeping that turns limits of gauge-fixed link variables into a limit curvature field satisfying the Bianchi identity with the right invariances. No step is missing. The discriminators come out right: finite groups fail at the rescaling; U(1) goes through; in two dimensions the free correlation vanishes beyond contact and the chain gives nothing, correctly.

The builder also tried to get the lower bound directly from the conditional law in a large box, without the decomposition, and reports that route as failing. So (B) stands at: a chain with two claimed steps, written only in outline and not checked, unchallenged in this version. ("Routine" was the director's word for them in an earlier draft of this ledger and no report supports it.) It has not been attacked since it was rewritten and the director has not read its proofs line by line. Even if it closes, (B) is an upper bound on the gap: it is the easy direction, it holds for U(1) too, and it brings (A) no nearer.

## (C): reduced, then stuck, and the data lean against the reduced form

[Chain](reviews/a3-C/PROOF.md). Proved (elementary spectral theory, with a free-field test):
- (C) and (C⁺) follow, with one constant, from a one-step inequality **(C′): 1 − C_β(1)/C_β(0) ≤ K′·m(β)**, that is, from the vector having energy of order the gap at time zero.
- (C′) fails in the free field for the lattice-local observables tested, numerically (there C(1)/C(0) tends to a lattice constant), while (C) is true there. So (C′) can only be asked of an observable smeared on the physical scale, such as the slice-flowed density.
- Smearing in Euclidean time does not make (C) automatic: a vector propagated over an unbounded number of correlation lengths satisfies it trivially but is no longer a sharp-time local field.
- Shown by examples, not as a theorem: (C) fails when the weight at the bottom of the spectrum is exponentially small in ξ, and a hole in the spectral measure alone does not break it. And (C) does not exclude a single-atom limit.
- Not checked beyond this: the builder found only lower-bound tools that are blind to the group (log-convexity from reflection positivity, Jensen); no source on correlation inequalities was read.

Missing: (C′) for the slice-flowed magnetic density of SU(2), in infinite volume, uniformly in β.

**A cross-check the director can make at once, measured only.** (B4 has since audited the measurement files: three configurations are repeated in each of the two added fine seeds, from the restart; with them removed the estimates move within their errors, see `../../transfer-operator/findings-audit/`. The numbers below are the original ones.) (C′) predicts that ξ·(1 − C(1)/C(0)) stays bounded as ξ grows. From the three-spacing measurement of `../fixed-size/MATCHED_MEASUREMENT.md` (R at one step over R(0): 0.270, 0.323, 0.385; ξ from the published glueball mass and scale: 0.72, 1.01, 1.40 lattice steps): ξ·(1 − C(1)/C(0)) = 0.53, 0.68, 0.86. It grows, roughly like ξ^0.75, over the factor of two available. These lattices have correlation lengths of about one step, the worst possible place to ask the question, so this is an indication and no more. It points the same way as the open hypothesis H-fin of that file: a flow inside one time slice leaves time sharp, and nothing yet shows that the energy of the flowed vector is of order the gap and not of order the cutoff. In the free field with the slice flow (C′) follows from B3 of `../fixed-size/SPATIAL_FLOW.md`, which is established there only when the smearing is wide against the flow radius and the box is at least about nine flow radii across.

## What attempt 1 leaves for attempt 2

1. **Finish (B)**: write the two claimed steps of version 2 in full, then send the whole to a challenger. It is the only link where the gap between what is claimed and what can be proved looks like work and not like the problem itself. The pieces: reflection positivity in site planes in all directions for the Wilson action, with a locator; the chessboard tail bound, written with its constants; and the exchange of limits, which is where a challenger is most likely to break it.
2. **Do not try (A) again by any route that ends in "the coupling runs".** Three routes ended there. A fourth, chosen to end elsewhere, died on a sign. Before a fifth, use the negative result: an argument for (A) must use a property that U(1) lacks, and the proved ones are local (perfectness, Ricci curvature, signed dual amplitudes).
3. **(C) should be asked of an observable smeared in space-time on the physical scale but supported in a bounded region**, so that both (C′) and the reconstruction have a chance; the slice flow gives up the first, time-evolution gives up the second. A flow in all four directions restricted to a slab away from the reflection plane is the object C4 and G4 are examining from the positivity side; this attempt reaches the same object from the energy side.
4. **(E) needs its own discriminator before anything is built**: the four-dimensional φ⁴ model, whose scaling limit is proved Gaussian. Any argument for an interacting limit must fail there.
