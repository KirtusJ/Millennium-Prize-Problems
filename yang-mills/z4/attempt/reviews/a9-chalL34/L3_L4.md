## 1. Two combinatorial lemmas (PROVED here)

**Lemma A (flat ⇒ pure gauge on the box; any group G).** Let U be a G-connection on the 2-complex of R (all its links and plaquettes) with U_p = 1 for every plaquette. Then U_{xy} = g_x g_y^{−1} for some g. If moreover U = 1 on the shell links and on T_int, then U ≡ 1.

Proof:
- Use the axial tree T_ax: all e₄-links, the e₃-links in {x₄ = −N}, the e₂-links in {x₄ = x₃ = −N}, and the e₁-links in {x₄ = x₃ = x₂ = −N}. This is a spanning tree.
- Gauge U to 1 on T_ax; g is the holonomy from the corner along T_ax.
- Take a non-tree link (x, j). Let k > j be the largest index with x_k > −N. The links (x−e_k, k) and (x−e_k+e_j, k) lie in T_ax: their coordinates above k are all −N. So flatness of the (j,k) plaquette at x − e_k gives U_{x,j} = U_{x−e_k,j}.
- Induct down x_k to −N, then go to the next largest such index. This ends at a tree link, so U ≡ 1 in this gauge, i.e. U is pure gauge.
- For the second claim: U = 1 on shell links gives g_x g_y^{−1} = 1 along shell links. The shell graph is connected (prog1 Q2: rank d₀ = V − 1), so g ≡ g_b on the shell.
- Each tree of T_int is a temporal ray from an interior site to one shell site, so g is constant along it. Hence g ≡ g_b and U ≡ 1. ∎

**Lemma B (shell simple connectivity; any group G).** A connection on the shell 2-complex (links and plaquettes with all sites in ∂R) whose shell plaquettes are all 1 is pure gauge. Consequently π₁ of the shell 2-complex is trivial: take G = π₁ and the tautological connection. In particular each non-tree link of T_s, in T_s-gauge, equals a product of conjugates of at most K_N shell plaquette holonomies.

Proof:
- *Gluing.* Let X = Y ∪ Z (subcomplexes) with flat ⇒ pure gauge on Y and on Z, and Y ∩ Z connected. Then flat ⇒ pure gauge on X.
  Write U = g·g^{−1} on Y and U = h·h^{−1} on Z. On Y ∩ Z, the function h^{−1}g is constant along links, so it equals a constant c. Replace h by hc and glue.
- The faces F_{i,±} = ∂R ∩ {x_i = ±N} are 3-boxes, so Lemma A's first claim applies to each.
- Add them in the order F₁₋, F₂₋, F₃₋, F₄₋, F₁₊, F₂₊, F₃₊, F₄₊. Each new face meets the union so far in a nonempty union of 2-faces of that 3-box which contains the faces {x_j = −N} for all j ≠ i (except at the first steps, where it is a single 2-box or two adjacent 2-boxes). Any two of these faces are adjacent, so the union is connected. ∎

Answer to the brief's question: yes. The shell of a 4-box is a cubical S³ and is simply connected. So shell curvatures determine shell links up to gauge, and no holonomy variables arise. The same proof applies verbatim to the "outer" shell ∂([−N−1,N+1]⁴). Numerical check (over ℝ only, so weaker than π₁): prog1 Q2, H¹ = 0 for N = 1, 2, 3.

Lemma A also gives the **zero set**. With trivial shell data (shell links = 1 in T_s-gauge) and T_int pinned, S_R(U) := Σ_{p∈P_R}(1 − cos α_p) = 0 only at U ≡ 1. Flatness of P_R together with the shell plaquettes (which are trivially flat) is flatness of all of R. **There are no gauge copies and no non-trivial flat connections other than U ≡ 1.**

## 2. L3 (PROVED here)

### (a) Exact conditional density, and the meaning of the shell data

- Every plaquette containing a link of Λ′_R has all its sites in R. Its links are therefore in Λ′_R or are shell links. So by Lemma 1′ (DLR for finite link sets), the conditional law of U_{Λ′_R} given all links ∉ Λ′_R is ∝ e^{−βS_R} dU_{Λ′_R}, and it depends only on the shell links.
- For gauge-invariant w, the conditional expectation Ψ_w(shell) is invariant under all gauge transformations:
  - transformations at interior sites are absorbed by Haar invariance;
  - transformations at shell sites act on its argument.
- So evaluate Ψ_w at shell^g, where g on ∂R is built from shell links along T_s. Hence g is a function of shell links only, and **ψ** (the non-tree shell links, rescaled) **is a function of shell variables only.**
- Inside the integral, apply PROOF_B_v2's forest gauge lemma with the forest T_int. Every tree of T_int touches the shell in exactly one site, at which the gauge is set to 1, so the shell links are unchanged.

Result, with J(x) := (sin|x|/|x|)²/(2π²) the Haar density in the exponential chart (normalised: ∫_{|x|<π} J d³x = 4π·(π/2)/(2π²) = 1):

  K_β(dφ | ψ) = Z_β(ψ)^{−1} exp(−βS_R(φ/√β; ψ/√β)) Π_{l∈Λ″} J(φ_l/√β) 1{|φ_l| < π√β} dφ.

The factors β^{−3n/2} cancel in the ratio, and the set {|x_l| = π} has Haar measure zero. The residual gauge is one global rotation at b, which acts on (φ, ψ) jointly by Ad. "|ψ| ≤ M" is gauge-invariantly implied by max over shell plaquettes of O_q ≤ M′(M, N) (Lemma B and the bi-invariance of α).

### (b) Lower bound on the whole chart

Let λ := the lowest eigenvalue of Q(φ; 0) on Λ″ (one colour). It is > 0 by Lemma H. Measured values: λ = 0.837722 for N = 1 and 0.147122 for N = 2 (prog1 Q1; child and grandchild agree for N = 1).

Fix δ ∈ (0, 1] with 48δ²e^{8δ} ≤ min(λ/16, 1).

*Near region* 𝒩 := {all interior |x_l| ≤ δ}, with β ≥ M²/δ², so that every shell |x_l| ≤ δ as well. For p ∈ P_R we have s_p ≤ 4δ, and:
- By (E1), (E2) and the norms, ‖U_p − 1‖_HS ≥ √2(|dx_p| − ½s_p²e^{s_p}).
- Hence β(1−cos α_p) ≥ ½(a_p − b_p)₊², with a_p = |dφ_p + dψ_p| and b_p = ½√β s_p² e^{s_p} ≤ 2δe^{4δ} s̃_p, where s̃_p = √β s_p.
- By `half_pos_part_sq` and s̃_p² ≤ 4Σ_{i∈p}|φ̃_i|² (φ̃ = φ on the interior, ψ on the shell): β(1−cos α_p) ≥ ¼a_p² − 8δ²e^{8δ}Σ_{i∈p}|φ̃_i|².
- Each link lies in at most 6 plaquettes, so summing over p: βS_R ≥ ¼Q(φ;ψ) − 48δ²e^{8δ}(|φ|²+|ψ|²).
- By `norm_add_sq_ge`, Lemma H, and |dψ_p|² ≤ 4Σ_{i∈p}|ψ_i|²: Q(φ;ψ) ≥ ½λ|φ|² − 24|ψ|².

So on 𝒩: **βS_R ≥ (λ/16)|φ|² − 7|ψ|².**

*Far region* 𝒩^c. The set {U_{Λ″} : max_l α(U_l) ≥ δ} is compact, and S_R(·; shell = 1) is continuous and positive on it (§1, zero set). So η(δ) := its minimum is > 0.

1 − cos is 1-Lipschitz in α, and α is a bi-invariant distance. Therefore |S_R(U;ψ/√β) − S_R(U;1)| ≤ 6Σ_l|ψ_l|/√β ≤ 6√n_s M/√β, where n_s is the number of shell links. For β ≥ (12√n_s M/η)² this gives S_R ≥ η/2. Since |φ|² < nπ²β on the chart, βS_R ≥ (η/(2nπ²))|φ|².

**Bound.** For |ψ| ≤ M and β ≥ β₀(M) := max(M²/δ², 144 n_s M²/η²), on the whole chart:

  βS_R(φ;ψ) ≥ c|φ|² − 7M², with c := min(λ/16, η(δ)/(2nπ²)).

λ is explicit. η(δ) is proved positive by compactness but is **not explicit**. This is the one place where an explicit constant was asked and not delivered.

Measured (child T2, N = 1, 120 cases): inf (βS + 7|ψ|²)/|φ|² ≥ 0.1738 > λ/16 = 0.0524, at β = 10, 100, 1000 and M = 1, 3.

### (c) Uniform Laplace limit

*Pointwise, uniformly on compacts.* Fix |φ| ≤ r and |ψ| ≤ M. By (E3), |√β α_p − |dφ_p+dψ_p|| ≤ s̃_p² e^{s̃_p/√β}/√β. Also 0 ≤ β(α²/2 − (1−cos α)) ≤ βα⁴/24. So

  |βS_R − ½Q(φ;ψ)| ≤ C_N (r+M)³ e^{4(r+M)/√β}/√β + C_N (r+M)⁴/β.

In addition, Π J(φ_l/√β)·(2π²)^n → 1 uniformly, and the indicator equals 1 once β > r²/π².

*Domination.* The integrand is ≤ (2π²)^{−n} e^{7M²} e^{−c|φ|²}, uniformly in |ψ| ≤ M and β ≥ β₀(M).

*Convergence.* For bounded measurable W (possibly also depending on ψ, and on an extra parameter, provided ‖W‖_∞ is uniform), split ∫ = ∫_{|φ|≤r} + ∫_{|φ|>r}:
- the second piece is ≤ ‖W‖_∞ e^{7M²}∫_{|φ|>r}e^{−c|φ|²}, uniformly small for large r;
- the first converges uniformly in |ψ| ≤ M.

Hence sup_{|ψ|≤M} |∫ W f_β − ∫ W f_∞| → 0, and likewise for the normalisations. Z_∞(ψ) = ∫e^{−Q(φ;ψ)/2}dφ is continuous and positive, so it is ≥ z_M > 0 on the compact set {|ψ| ≤ M}. The ratio therefore converges uniformly.

K_∞(·|ψ) is N(m(ψ), Q^{−1}) per colour, with m(ψ) = −Q^{−1}Bψ linear in ψ. Its total-variation modulus is ≤ ½|Q^{1/2}(m(ψ)−m(ψ′))|, so it is equicontinuous in ψ. The error is uniform in ψ, which is stronger than equicontinuity.

### (d) Theorem L3 (PROVED)

For every box R, every M < ∞, and every bounded measurable W(φ, ψ):

  sup_{|ψ|≤M} |K_βW(ψ) − K_∞W(ψ)| → 0 as β → ∞,

where K_∞(·|ψ) ∝ e^{−½Q(φ;ψ)}dφ, with 3 independent colours. In addition, K_β(|φ| > r | ψ) ≤ z_M^{−1}e^{7M²}∫_{|φ|>r}e^{−c|φ|²} + o(1), uniformly in |ψ| ≤ M: the conditional laws are uniformly tight.

## 3. L4 (PROVED here, modulo the standard facts of §6)

### (a) The limit field

For each plaquette p, with corner x_p and a path γ_p from a base point o to x_p, define the lasso field

  F^β_p := √β Ad(U_{γ_p}) log U_p ∈ ℝ³.

Then |F^β_p| = √β α_p **exactly**, and O_p = f_β(|F^β_p|) with f_β(t) := β(1 − cos(t/√β)), where |f_β(t) − t²/2| ≤ t⁴/(24β). Under a gauge transformation, F^β → Ad(g_o)F^β. **No tree is used to define F.**

- **(i) Path independence.** For another path γ′_p, F′_p = Ad(V)F_p with V the holonomy of the closed loop γ′γ^{−1}. By Lemma A or Lemma B (or for any lattice homotopy in ℤ⁴), α(V) ≤ Σ_{q∈Σ} α_q over a fixed finite spanning surface Σ, and this is ≤ (π/√2)Σ_q √(O_q/β) = O_P(β^{−1/2}) by S3a. Also ‖Ad V − I‖ ≤ 2α(V). So F′ − F → 0 in probability: same limit law, and the joint limit law of (F, F′) is carried by the diagonal (Slutsky).
- **(ii) Colour invariance.** A constant gauge transformation g ≡ R gives F → Ad(R)F, and μ is gauge invariant. This is exact at every β.
- **(iii) Base-point independence and translation invariance.** Let V be the holonomy of a path o → b and G := F^{(b)}. Let Ũ := U^g, with g i.i.d. Haar over sites and independent of U, so that Ũ ~ μ. Then V(Ũ) = g_o V g_b^{−1} is Haar and independent of G(Ũ) = Ad(g_b)G(U) ~ G. Therefore law(Ad(V)G) = law(Ad(Haar)G) = law(G) by (ii).
  So the law of F^{(o)} (paths through b) equals the law of F^{(b)}. Translation invariance of μ maps F^{(o)} to F^{(o+a)}, so with (i) the limit law is translation invariant.
- **(iv) Bianchi.** For a 3-cube c, the six face holonomies, each transported to one corner along edges of c, multiply to 1. This is a word identity in the free group on the 12 edges, valid for any connection. By (E2) applied to an exact product 1: |Σ_faces ±x_face| ≤ (s²/2)e^s with s = Σ|x_face|.
  Rescaled, and replacing the edge transports by the fixed paths γ_p at a cost ‖Ad V − I‖·|F| = O_P(β^{−1/2}) by (i):
  |(dF^β)_c| ≤ S_c² e^{S_c/√β}/(2√β) + O_P(β^{−1/2}), with S_c = Σ_{p⊂c}|F^β_p|.
  So dF = 0 a.s. in the limit, with **explicit order β^{−1/2}**. The comparison of F_p computed from two base points is (iii).

### (b) Tightness, joint convergence, moments

- |F^β_p| = √β α_p ≤ (π/√2)√O_p, and μ(O_p > t) ≤ e^{60.2 − t/8} (Corollary S3a). This holds uniformly in β ≥ 1 and in μ ∈ 𝓛_β.
- So every finite family is tight. Prokhorov plus diagonal extraction over the countably many plaquettes give, along a subsequence (β_k, μ_k), the convergence of all finite-dimensional laws to a law μ_∞ of F on (ℝ³)^{plaquettes}.
- Joint convergence of (O_p)_p to (½|F_p|²)_p: for bounded Lipschitz h, |E h(f_β(|F^β|)) − E h(½|F^β|²)| ≤ Lip(h)·sup_{|t|≤r}|f_β(t) − t²/2| + 2‖h‖·P(|F^β| > r) → 0. Then apply the continuous-mapping theorem.
- Moments: sup E O_p^k ≤ ∫ k t^{k−1} min(1, e^{60.2−t/8}) dt < ∞ for every k. So O_p, O_pO_q and O_p² are uniformly integrable (their 2nd moments are bounded by 4th moments of O). Hence Var and Cov converge, which is what §4's inequality needs.

### (c) DLR for the limit

**Statement.** For every box R, under μ_∞:

  the conditional law of F_in := (F_p)_{p∈P_R} given 𝒢_R := σ(F_q : q ∉ P_R) is the law of dA + dL(F_sh),

where:
- F_sh := (F_q) over the shell plaquettes. These are not in P_R, so F_sh is 𝒢_R-measurable.
- L is the linear left inverse of d on 1-forms of the shell that vanish on T_s. d is injective there: its kernel is exact forms dλ with dλ|_{T_s} = 0, so λ is constant and dλ = 0.
- A ~ K_∞(·|L F_sh), the Maxwell–Dirichlet Gaussian on Λ″.

**Test functions.** w_in bounded Lipschitz on (ℝ³)^{P_R}, and w_out bounded Lipschitz of finitely many F_q with q ∉ P_R. By π–λ these determine the conditional law.

**Proof.**
1. *Adapted paths.* Using (i) and (iii), work with base b ∈ ∂R, paths for q ∉ P_R that avoid Λ′_R (the complement of the interior is connected), and paths for p ∈ P_R that lie in R. Write T := T_s ∪ T_int for the box gauge. In box gauge:
   - F^β_out is a function of the links ∉ Λ′_R;
   - F^β_in = F_in(φ, ψ), with |F^β_in − (dφ + dψ)| ≤ C_R(|φ|+|ψ|)²e^{(|φ|+|ψ|)/√β}/√β by (E3) and ‖Ad V − I‖ ≤ 2α(V).
2. *Finite-β DLR.* By Lemma 1′, E[w_in w_out] = E[(K_β w_in∘F_in)(ψ^β)·w_out].
3. *Apply L3.* On {|ψ| ≤ M}, L3 with the uniform tightness and Lip(w_in) gives (K_β w_in∘F_in)(ψ) = Φ(ψ) + o(1) uniformly, where Φ(ψ) := K_∞[w_in(dA + dψ)] is bounded and continuous.
4. *Tail.* Off {|ψ| ≤ M}, the contribution is ≤ 2‖w‖² ε(M). Here ε(M) → 0 uniformly in β and μ, by Lemma B's K_N bound and S3a.
5. *Shell data from curvatures.* ψ^β = L(dψ^β) exactly, and dψ^β = F^β_sh + O_P(β^{−1/2}) by (E3), because the shell transports are near 1 in box gauge. So ψ^β = L F^β_sh + o_P(1).
6. *Pass to the limit.* Φ∘L is bounded and continuous. Weak convergence gives E_∞[w_in w_out] = E_∞[Φ(L F_sh) w_out]. Let M → ∞. ∎

**Link variables versus curvatures (resolved).** At finite β the conditioning is on links modulo gauge. In the limit only the curvatures are conditioned on. Lemma B (shell π₁ = 1) and the injectivity of d on T_s-gauge shell forms show that shell curvatures determine shell links modulo gauge: exactly, for the group (Lemma B), and linearly, in the limit (L). Nothing is lost.

**Colour.** No averaging is needed. With base b the statement holds directly. Moving to base o is a random rotation Ad(V) with V a function of links ∉ Λ′_R. Because K_∞(Ad R·ψ) = Ad R_*K_∞(ψ) (Q is colour-blind), the same computation with w_in(Ad V ·) and w_out(Ad V ·) gives the statement for F^{(o)}. Here L3's uniformity in W covers the parameter V, since Ad V is an isometry.

This yields what PROOF_B_v2 §3 states: the covariance is C^R = D Q^{−1} D^T (deterministic), and the mean is h^R = d(A* + ψ) with A* minimising Q. Lemma R then applies as written.
