## 0. The point that the brief's framing misses (PROVED, elementary)

**Gaussianity is the wrong null hypothesis for composite gauge-invariant observables.** In a free gauge theory the gauge-invariant local fields (tr F², plaquette energies) are *quadratic* in the Gaussian field. A Wick square of a Gaussian is not Gaussian. Concretely, the program's own Theorem B′ observable O_p = β(1 − cos α_p) has, at fixed lattice distance and β → ∞, the limit ½Σ_{a=1}^{3}(F^a_p)² (CLAIMED in THEOREM_B.md, step L4: the limit curvature field is Gaussian; the brief's "it is Gaussian: free Maxwell" is true of the *curvature field* F, not of O_p). If the three colour components are independent with equal variance, O_p is a χ²₃ variable times a constant. Its normalized fourth cumulant is κ₄/κ₂² = 48k/(2k)² = 12/k = **4** (PROVED; recomputed in notes.md step 10). Any test "κ₄ ≠ 0 ⇒ interacting" applied to O_p would declare the free Maxwell limit interacting. So (E-a) and (E-b), as written in the brief, are only meaningful for observables that would be *linear* in the elementary field of the free comparison theory. In Yang–Mills there is no such gauge-invariant observable. This is a defect of the formulations as stated, not of the target.

Corollary (PROVED, elementary): **no statement about one two-point function excludes a GFF.** Any Euclidean-invariant, reflection-positive two-point function is the two-point function of a GFF (take the GFF with that Källén–Lehmann measure). So the short-distance statement (E-d), which concerns one two-point function, does not by itself exclude free theories. It identifies *which* theory, not *whether* it interacts.

## 1. Formulations

**(E-a) Renormalized four-point coupling.** For a scalar local field φ (lattice, centred), χ = Σ_x⟨φ₀φ_x⟩_c, ξ₂ the second-moment length, u₄ = Σ_{x,y,z}⟨φ₀φ_xφ_yφ_z⟩_c, g_R(β) := −u₄/(χ² ξ₂^d). (E-a): liminf_{β→∞}|g_R(β)| > 0. The finite-box version used in my tests is g_R(L) = 3U₄·(L/ξ₂)^d at fixed L/ξ₂, with U₄ = 1 − ⟨M⁴⟩/(3⟨M²⟩²) and M = Σφ.
- Excludes GFFs **only if φ is linear** in the would-be free field. For composite φ (§0) it excludes nothing: a Wick square of a free massive field has g_R ≠ 0 of order one. (PROVED: §0.)
- Status for Ising/φ⁴: in d = 4, g_R → 0 (CITED, Aizenman–Duminil-Copin, §2). In d = 2, 3 it stays positive (CLAIMED: φ⁴₂, φ⁴₃ constructions; the 2D Ising measurements in §3 are consistent).

**(E-b) Non-Gaussianity of a block observable.** For a block average B of physical side ℓξ, κ₄(B)/κ₂(B)² ↛ 0, or failure of Wick's rule ⟨B₁B₂B₃B₄⟩ ≠ Σ_pairings for some four blocks. For linear fields (E-b) ⇔ (E-a)-type statements at the block scale; same defect for composite fields. **Repaired version (E-b′), "failure of the ring rule"**: if O = ½Σ_{a=1}^k φ_a² with φ_a independent copies of one GFF with covariance G, then every connected n-point function of O is the ring sum κ_n(x₁…x_n) = ½k·Σ_{(n−1)! directed cycles} Π G (PROVED by Wick's theorem; checks: n = 2 gives ½kG²; coincident points give the χ²_k cumulants 2^{n−1}(n−1)!k). Since G = (2κ₂/k)^{1/2}, the ring rule is a testable relation between κ₂ and κ₄ of one observable. But for tr F² of free gluons the components F^a_{μν} are correlated through the tensor propagator, which a gauge-invariant two-point function does not determine. So (E-b′) excludes Wick squares of scalar GFFs, not all free algebras. (CLAIMED that no single-observable repair works in general; MISSING a proof.)

**(E-c) Spectral / scattering.** "A second mass" or "a mass ratio" does **not** distinguish: a GFF with ρ = δ_{m₁²} + δ_{m₂²} has two masses in any ratio, including a "bound state" below 2m₁ (PROVED: such ρ is admissible). What does distinguish:
- **(E-c\*)**: the lightest mass m is an isolated eigenvalue (one-particle shell), and the two-particle interaction energy in a periodic box of side L = z/m is nonzero for some z: δ(z) := lim_{β→∞}(E₂(L) − 2m)/m ≠ 0, in some symmetry channel. In the Fock space of any free algebra the Hamiltonian is a sum of one-particle Hamiltonians, so E₂(L) = 2m exactly at zero total momentum (PROVED for Fock spaces, elementary). Hence (E-c\*) excludes every GFF and every free algebra. δ(z) is tied to the scattering length by Lüscher's finite-volume formula, E₂ − 2m = −4πa₀/(mL³)(1 + O(a₀/L)) (CLAIMED from memory: M. Lüscher, Commun. Math. Phys. 105 (1986) 153; not read). Equivalently (E-c\*\*): the Haag–Ruelle S-matrix of the lightest particle is not the identity (CLAIMED standard).
- Hypothesis it uses: an isolated one-particle shell at the bottom of the spectrum. A gap does not give this (the bottom of the spectrum could be continuous).

**(E-d) Short distance, asymptotic freedom.** Operator: the trace of the energy–momentum tensor, Θ = (β(g)/2g)·F^a_{μν}F^a_{μν}, which is renormalization-group invariant (no anomalous dimension). Statement: as x → 0 in physical units,
  x⁸·⟨Θ(x)Θ(0)⟩·(ln(1/(xΛ)))² → C ≠ 0,
with C fixed by one-loop perturbation theory, i.e. by b₀ = 11N/(48π²) = 11/(24π²) for N = 2 and the free correlator ⟨F²(x)F²(0)⟩ = c(N²−1)x⁻⁸. Reason: Θ ∝ ḡ²(x)·F² at leading order, and ḡ²(x) ≈ 1/(2b₀ ln(1/(xΛ))), so the free x⁻⁸ is multiplied by ḡ⁴ ∝ (ln)⁻² (CLAIMED: standard leading-order perturbation theory, not read; the exact constant is not derived here, MISSING). For the non-invariant F^a F^a itself the power of the logarithm depends on the normalisation of the operator. That is why the statement is made for Θ. The distinguishing content is the **log power −2 with coefficient tied to b₀**: a conformal or Gaussian fixed point would give a pure power. But by §0, a GFF with a suitably chosen ρ reproduces it. So (E-d) is a matching condition, and it must be stated together with the same law for ⟨ΘΘΘ⟩ (operator product expansion with ḡ-dependent coefficients) to say anything beyond the two-point function.

**Implications (all PROVED at the level of the argument given, or stated as CLAIMED):**
- (E-c\*) ⇒ "not a free algebra" (PROVED above). (E-a)/(E-b) for a linear field ⇒ not a GFF in that field. Neither implies (E-d). (E-d) implies none of the others (counterexample: a GFF with the matching ρ).
- (E-a) for a composite field does not imply interaction (counterexample: Wick square, §0).
- Weakest formulation that still excludes every GFF and free algebra: **(E-c\*)** among those listed, under the one-particle hypothesis. Without that hypothesis: MISSING. I know of no single-correlation-function criterion.
- **Provable from lattice information?** (E-d) lives at distances ≪ ξ, where ḡ is small. It is the only one in reach of multiscale renormalization-group methods (Balaban-type bounds, §2), conceivably. (E-a), (E-b) and (E-c\*) live at the correlation length. Proving them needs control at that scale, which is the gap problem itself. Today they can only be measured.

## 4. The SU(2) question, stated honestly

- Natural formulation: (E-d) for Θ, the matching condition with asymptotic freedom, **plus** (E-c\*) at the correlation length to exclude free algebras. Measuring (E-c\*) means measuring the two-glueball energy shift in boxes of side a few ξ. Proving it needs control at the correlation length (= the gap problem). Proving (E-d) needs a non-perturbative renormalization-group construction down to scale ξ with the infinite-volume limit: Balaban's bounds stop at finite volume and say nothing at ξ (CLAIMED). That is why no present rigorous method reaches it.
- Numerical: Lüscher, Weisz, Sommer & Wolff, "Computation of the running coupling in the SU(2) Yang–Mills theory", Nucl. Phys. B 389 (1993) 247–264, arXiv:hep-lat/9207010. CITED, abstract read. A Schrödinger-functional coupling, non-perturbatively defined, runs over a range of scales up to q = 20√σ, where α_MS̄ = 0.187 ± 0.005 ± 0.009, and is well described by the two-loop β-function. **Caution (PROVED, elementary):** in a Gaussian gauge theory with action (1/g₀²)∫F² the Schrödinger-functional coupling equals g₀² at every scale. So "a nonzero renormalized coupling" is not the evidence. The evidence is its **running** with slope b₀.
- Cheap rigorous non-result: at fixed lattice distance the β → ∞ limit is the free three-colour Maxwell field (CLAIMED in THEOREM_B.md, L4, with two claimed steps). Hence (E) cannot be asked at fixed lattice distance. It must be asked at separations ∝ ξ (E-a/b/c) or at physical x → 0 *after* the scaling limit (E-d). Gaussianity tests there must be applied to the curvature field, not to O_p (O_p is χ²₃-like with κ₄/κ₂² = 4, §0).
