# A full-space comparison in the quadratic Wilson vacuum

Take the real Gaussian curvature with covariance the orthogonal projection onto the closure of exact lattice two-forms, with unit plaquette inner product. Use either an even periodic spacetime torus of sides at least six, or $N^3\times\mathbb Z$ directly. The infinite cylinder uses the isonormal Gaussian process over the closed range of the lattice coboundary in $\ell^2$; no infinite-dimensional identity-covariance random vector is needed. Three independent colours use an orthogonal direct sum. The measured slice algebra is generated by all additive spatial plaquette circulations. The coarse algebra is generated by coarse additive plaquette circulations on the even grid. Arbitrary square-integrable functions in this slice algebra, including its nonlinear Gaussian chaoses, satisfy the 24-colour conditional inequality with $\gamma=1/2688$. Harmonic link windings and compact non-Abelian holonomies are outside this comparison.

A spatial filling $b$ on a slice defines the linear measurement $W(\Pi b)$ and edge current $c=\partial b$. Here $W$ is the isonormal field, $\Pi$ the curvature projection and $\partial=d^*$. Two fillings with the same boundary give the same measurement since their difference annihilates the range of $d$. For each spatial edge $e$, the local spacetime curvature change $d1_e$ belongs to that range and its pairing with the measurement is $c_e$. Define a colour exterior by retaining the Gaussian space perpendicular to the span of its $d1_e$. This definition fixes all readouts invariant under the selected link variations, without conditioning ill-defined gauge potentials.

### An explicit coarse predictor for every linear current

For $r(x)=2\lfloor x/2\rfloor$ (componentwise), let $a(x)$ go from $r(x)$ to $x$ in increasing coordinate order. For a positively oriented fine edge $e=(x,i)$ with end $y$, the reduced edge $r_\# e$ is zero when $x_i$ is even, and a positive coarse edge when $x_i$ is odd. Lift a coarse edge to its two straight segments by $\iota$. The two positive words $a(x)e$ and $\iota(r_\# e)a(y)$ have the same multiset of directions and endpoint on the periodic cover. Match the latter word from left to right, moving the first matching remaining letter left by adjacent swaps. Each swap encloses a signed unit plaquette. The resulting 2-chain $S_e$ satisfies
$$\partial S_e=a(x)+e-\iota(r_\# e)-a(y).$$
The surface is empty on skeleton edges. Negative edges use the negative of the positive-generator identity. Summing against a closed current cancels the two vertex paths. Moreover $r_\#(\partial b)$ bounds a coarse 2-chain: the boundary of an elementary fine face maps to zero or to a coarse face, by checking whether both spanning base coordinates are odd. Thus the subtracted current truly belongs to the coarse measured algebra, even across seams.

Each selected edge has six incident spacetime plaquettes. The Gram matrix of its pair of variations has diagonal 6 and off-diagonal $\pm1$ from their shared face. ### From currents to every nonlinear slice function

Let $P$ project the one-slice first chaos onto its coarse subspace, and $A_k$ be the compression to the slice first chaos of the exterior first-chaos orthogonal projection. Each $A_k$ is a positive contraction fixing $P$ pointwise, so it reduces $P$ and $Q=I-P$. The first-chaos estimate gives $\operatorname{avg}_k QA_kQ\preceq(1-\gamma)Q$. For order $j$, the compressed exterior conditional expectation acts in the symmetric sector by $A_k^{\otimes j}$. Indeed the normalized Gaussian exponential for $v$ has conditional mean the normalized exponential of its exterior projection, by independence of the orthogonal complement. Expanding in the scalar multiplier of $v$ and polarizing gives the tensor powers. The coarse projection on this chaos is $P^{\otimes j}$.

On an ordered tensor sector with at least one $Q$ slot, keep one such slot and bound all the others by the identity, using positivity. The averaged norm bound on that sector is $1-\gamma$. Each individual colour preserves these sectors. Sum them, restrict to symmetric tensors and sum the orthogonal chaoses, including the constant sector where the defect vanishes. This proves the full conditional Poincaré inequality. The inference uses the Gaussian exponential identity. A uniform first-order estimate for Wilson functions alone would not prove its nonlinear analogue.

At two endpoints separated by at least two steps in both cyclic directions, the two spaces of local variations for a fixed colour are orthogonal. Each is invisible to the opposite slice's spatial plaquette measurements. Decompose the isonormal process into these two independent innovations and their common orthogonal complement. Each endpoint depends only on its own innovation and that exterior, so the stationary two-endpoint argument proves the **joint** defect for arbitrary coarse plus fine functions. The physical-time coarse comparison between two different lattices remains a separate obligation. More explicitly, for the same arbitrary observable at the two translated slices, the lag correlation is the pairing of its two exterior predictions. Taking the real part and applying Cauchy–Schwarz bounds it by the one-slice norm squared minus that colour's defect. Averaging gives the lower bound $\gamma\|Qf\|^2$ for the loss, including any coarse summand in $f$.
