BRIEF p5-fid (micro-review of a paper against its sources). Network: none.

paper/ holds a paper (LaTeX sources and main.pdf; start from main.tex) reporting an end-to-end attempt at the Yang-Mills mass gap that failed, one theorem that held, and several failed routes. src/ holds what it was written from: two ledgers, the statement file of the theorem, the grounding file, and the reports and chains of the workers. The paper was assembled by a director from sections drafted by four writers; none of them saw the others' sections. Summaries are where errors hide, and a paper is the last summary.
 1. FIDELITY. For the front matter (abstract, introduction, "What is not claimed") and the provenance section (written by the director), and for the negative-results sections: check every number, sign, power, count, attribution, status word ("proved", "cited", "measured", "claimed") and locator against src/. List each sentence that states more than the sources support, gets a number wrong, drops a qualification that changes the meaning, or misattributes. The provenance section makes claims about how many challenges there were and what they found: check each against the challenger reports (a2-chalR; a5-chalB, a8-chalB2, a9-chalL34; c2-chalS) and against A1_LEDGER/A2_LEDGER.
 2. CONSISTENCY ACROSS SECTIONS. Notation and names: is Hypothesis (A)/(B)/(C), H0-H4 the same object everywhere; do the introduction's descriptions of Theorems 3.x match their statements in sec_reduction.tex; does the statement of Theorem 1.1 in main.tex agree exactly with what sec_proof.tex proves (the set of limit states, the observable, 'every n >= 1', the constants G(0) = 1/2 and the asymptotics, the 'Consequently' sentence, m defined as -log of the norm on the orthocomplement); do cross-references resolve to the intended statements (compile is clean, but a \ref can point at the wrong lemma); are the counts in the abstract and introduction ("five lattice hypotheses, of which two are proved", "five attempted routes", "six properties", "ten standard facts") equal to what the sections contain (count them).
 3. OVERCLAIM SCAN. Search all text for: verified, review(ed), establish(es), solve, resolve, first, novel, breakthrough, remarkable, clearly, obviously, well known, it is easy to see, we believe, Millennium, prize, Clay. For each hit judge whether it claims more than it should; list the ones that do.
 4. THE DISCLOSURE. Does any sentence in the paper present agreement among same-family sessions as review, or present the second-lineage checks as covering more than Lemma R, the Wick inequality and the free correlation? Does the paper anywhere use an operational alias (Z4, A4, B4, C4, D4, G4, 'Director') in the text (bibliography entries for the repository excepted)? List.
 5. MECHANICS: overfull boxes wider than 10pt (pdflatex log: recompile main.tex twice in a scratch copy), undefined or duplicate labels, bibliography entries not cited or cited keys missing.
Recompute with a program (predicates first) any arithmetic the director's sections do. Output report.md: a table (location: file and line, quoted words; what the source says: file, quoted words; verdict FAITHFUL / OVERSTATED / WRONG / OMITTED / INCONSISTENT; suggested wording), then the counts. Delegate to the child: item 5 and the count checks of item 2 (control question: the number of \section commands in the assembled paper, which you count first).
Budget: about 25 minutes.
