YOUR CLAIM: (C) of A1_REDUCTION.md for SU(2), d = 4, assuming (A) and (B): for a fixed lattice-local gauge-invariant slice observable (or the slice-flowed magnetic density of SPATIAL_FLOW.md at flow time s xi^2), there is D with C_beta(xi/2) <= D C_beta(xi) for all large beta; and (C+).
 This is the scaling hypothesis for one observable: the spectral measure of a local observable is regular between the lattice scale and the gap scale. It is not expected to be provable with present methods; the director wants the strongest honest chain and the exact place it stops.
SUGGESTED MECHANISMS (yours to change, but commit):
  (a) Reduce (C) to a statement about one number: by log-convexity of n -> C_beta(n), C(xi/2)/C(xi) <= exp((xi/2) * (-d/dn log C)(xi/2)); so (C) follows from a bound on the logarithmic derivative ("effective mass") at time xi/2: m_eff(xi/2) <= K m. State and prove the exact equivalences among: (C); a bound on the effective mass at times of order xi; a bound on the mean energy of the propagated vector; a lower bound on the spectral weight of psi in [m, K m] relative to its weight at higher energies after damping. Lean for the finite-sum versions.
  (b) An upper bound on the effective mass from a variational principle: m_eff(n) is the energy expectation of T^{n/2} psi; it decreases in n; any bound of the form "T^{n0/2} psi has energy at most K' / n0 for n0 << xi" (the propagated local excitation has spread and cooled like a free one) combined with monotonicity gives m_eff(xi/2) <= K'/n0... which is not enough unless n0 ~ xi. Say what is needed to bridge from n0 to xi, and whether a doubling induction (energy at time 2n is at most a fixed fraction more than half the energy at time n, for n <= xi) is equivalent to (C+).
  (c) What does a lower bound on a correlation function look like in the literature? (Infrared bounds from reflection positivity are UPPER bounds; lower bounds come from correlation inequalities, e.g. Griffiths or Simon-Lieb-type, which gauge theories with non-abelian groups mostly lack.) Find the nearest rigorous tool and say what it gives.
  (d) Can (C) be avoided? For instance by choosing psi_beta not lattice-local but local on the physical scale: psi_beta = T^{k} applied to a local observable smeared over a block of side l = lambda xi, or the slice-flowed density at physical flow time. Then part of (C) might be traded for a statement at the lattice scale. Work out exactly what replaces (C) for such a vector, and whether the replacement is weaker. In particular: is there ANY choice of vectors built from local observables by operations that keep locality on the physical scale for which the analogue of (C) is automatic? (If psi is allowed to be T^{xi} phi, then C_psi(n) = C_phi(n + 2 xi) and (C) for psi is a doubling statement for phi between 2.5 xi and 3 xi: by log-convexity and the gap, what can be said without any hypothesis?) Push this: it may show that (C) can be replaced by a statement that is true for trivial reasons, at the price of the observable being smeared in Euclidean time over one correlation length; then say what is lost (is such a vector still 'local' in the sense the Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction needs? is the limit possibly a single atom?).
