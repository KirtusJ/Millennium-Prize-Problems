## 3. Paths

### Path A: the weight bound through the slow states

Stipulation. At weak coupling in a fixed box the states of energy at most `u` are, up to a fraction `c·a·u` of their norm, functions of the coarse field, with `c` independent of β, of the mesh and of the box. The mechanism stipulated is the one seen twice: a slow state is a function of a zero mode or soft mode, the coarse field determines that mode up to massive modes at the cutoff, and the massive modes have variance of order `1/β` times a number.

Tests, in order of cost.
1. Compact toy, all low states (running: `papers/spectral-criteria/supplement/S12-compact-toy/u1kappa.py`). Expectation committed before the run: `κ = ‖GH⁻¹G‖ → 0.9208` as β → ∞ and `κ < 1` throughout. Against the path: `κ` growing with β.
2. Compact U(1) in 2+1 dimensions at the next size, 4 × 4 blocked to 2 × 2, in the quadratic approximation with flux sectors (a finite matrix computation like Appendix E plus winding). Stipulated value: the constant is `Σ 1/sinh ω` over the aliases, as in `softweight.py`. Against the path: a constant that grows with the side.
3. SU(2) in the small-torus regime. Stipulated: the slow states are functions of the spatially constant part of the potential; the coarse links determine that part up to modes at the cutoff; the weight of a slow state in the fine detail is at most a constant times `a·u`, the constant being the free value 0.911 per colour to leading order. This needs a computation that has not been designed. Against the path: slow states whose fine-detail weight does not vanish with the mesh at fixed physical energy, for instance through the non-abelian zero-momentum modes near the orbifold points, where the adiabatic approximation is reported to fail.

What the path does not give even if every test passes: Hypotheses 3.2 and 3.3, volume uniformity, the continuum identification.

### Path B: a two-scale proof of the fibre inequality

Stipulation. The weighted inequality (3.1) of Proposition 3.7 holds on the fibres of the coarse field for gauge-invariant functions, with `‖Σ^{1/2} M Σ^{1/2}‖` bounded by a constant times `e^{pβ}`, `p < 1/s`, uniformly in the volume, and can be proved by iterating a two-scale criterion inside the fibre.

Tests.
1. Read the two-scale disintegration of the two-dimensional result ([arXiv:2610.35286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35286)) and record which variable is conditioned on and how the constant depends on β. Against the path as stated: a constant that grows faster than `e^{β/s}` with no visible way to improve it.
2. Compute `J_c` in the compact toy for the scalar weight and for the best weight, as `weightedtoy2.py` did for two links, and compare with the exact rate. Stipulated: the bound stays within a factor two of the exact rate up to β = 8. Against: a bound that degrades like a power of β while the exact rate does not.
3. One link pair under a coarse link for SU(2) with the other links frozen: the fibre is a three-sphere of radius √2 with a density that is explicit. Compute the gauge-invariant Poincaré constant as a function of β and of the frozen staples. Stipulated: at most a constant over β away from staples that cancel. Against: growth at generic staples.

### Path C: change the action so that the energy controls the gradient

Finding (computed 24 November, one link): for the Wilson kernel the decay rate of spin `j`, `−log(I_n(β)/I_1(β))` with `n = 2j+1`, satisfies `(n² − 1)/(β · rate) ≈ 2` at low spin (1.94 at β = 16, n = 2) and grows without bound at high spin (4.2 at n = 129, β = 16; 10.0 at β = 4). So for the Wilson action the gradient form is not dominated by `β·a·H`, and the two-step route "fibre Poincaré inequality, then gradient bounded by energy" fails at high spin. For the heat-kernel action the ratio is constant.

Stipulation. For the heat-kernel action the first hypothesis reduces to a fibre Poincaré inequality alone, because the one-step operator is `e^{-V/2} e^{Δ/(2β)} e^{-V/2}` and the energy form dominates the gradient form up to the potential terms.

Tests.
1. Prove or refute, for one link with a potential, `E|∇f|² ≤ Cβ ⟨f, (−log A) f⟩` for the heat-kernel step. Against: a counterexample at one link.
2. Check that Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 6.4 survive the change of action (the cap lemma uses the tilted law of the Wilson weight). Against: no analogue of the cap bound.

This path changes the regulator, so it also changes what would have to be matched to the external construction, which uses the Wilson action.

### Path D: finish the free theory

Stipulation. `γ_N ≥ 0.674941…` for every even side, with equality in the limit; that is, the rate at every coarse momentum is at least its limit at zero.

Tests.

Against: a loss `ε_i` that does not decrease with the mesh.

## 5. The Banach–Tarski paradox and the axiom of choice: first look (24 November 2026; superseded by §6)

Question from the Principal: are they relevant here, given the foundations of the research program?

What was checked.
- The research program's repository was searched for "Banach–Tarski", "axiom of choice", "non-measurable", "ZFC" and "Classical.choice". There is no mention of the paradox. Choice appears only as the Lean axiom `Classical.choice` in the axiom lists of the machine-checked lemmas (the program's own pass criterion allows `propext`, `Quot.sound` and, with Mathlib, `Classical.choice`), and one review note leaves open whether a functional equation has bounded non-measurable solutions. The program's results used so far are finite and algebraic; none rests on a choice principle beyond what Mathlib uses throughout.
- In the Yang–Mills paper every set is Borel (the gates are closed sets of holonomies), every function is a class in an `L²` space of a Borel probability measure on a compact metrisable space, and the conditional expectations are projections on those classes. The pieces of a paradoxical decomposition are not measurable, so they do not define elements of these spaces and no operator in the paper sees them. The arguments need countably many choices at most (regular conditional laws, a countable dense family in the proof of Proposition 3.4).
- The Lean files list `Classical.choice` because Mathlib's real numbers and classical logic bring it in. That says which axioms the library used, not that the statements need the full axiom of choice.
- External grounding (one search, summaries only): the paradox comes from a free subgroup of rank two in SO(3) (Hausdorff 1914) and from non-amenability, as von Neumann observed; the related Ruziewicz problem, whether Lebesgue measure is the only finitely additive rotation-invariant measure on the sphere, was solved in the affirmative for the spheres S², S³ by Drinfeld and in higher dimensions by Margulis and Sullivan, and the solutions go through a spectral gap for the averaging operator of finitely many rotations on `L²` of the sphere (see the survey [arXiv:1902.02706](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02706); Lubotzky, Phillips and Sarnak give explicit elements of SU(2)). From memory and not found in that search: the paradox needs a form of the axiom of choice, and by Solovay's theorem it is consistent with dependent choice, given an inaccessible cardinal, that every set of reals is Lebesgue measurable.

Assessment. Not relevant to anything proved or open here. The one point of contact is that the gauge group SU(2) contains free subgroups, and that operators averaging over finitely many of its elements have a spectral gap on `L²(S³)`. That gap is for a different operator: the transfer step integrates each link against a smooth kernel over the whole group, and its difficulty is the coupling between links and the volume, not the mixing of one link. No statement of the paper would change in a set theory without the paradox. Dropped.

## 6. Second look, on the Principal's correction (24 November 2026)

The first look searched the program for the words and found none. That answers nothing: the question was whether the paradox and the axiom of choice bear on the foundations the program has laid and on their use here, whether or not anyone has mentioned them. Taken that way there are four points of contact. Two are substantive.

External grounding for this section (searches, summaries only; nothing read in full): Tarski's theorem, that a group or action has a paradoxical decomposition exactly when it has no finitely additive invariant probability measure on all subsets; Følner's condition and Kesten's theorem, that a finitely generated group is amenable exactly when the spectral radius of a symmetric random walk on it is one ([arXiv:2404.08270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08270) and the sources it cites); Foreman and Wehrung, and Pawlikowski, Fund. Math. 138 (1991), that the Hahn–Banach theorem alone gives a non-measurable set and the paradox; a statement in a paper of Luxemburg and Väth that the paradox cannot be proved from dependent choice; Dowker, Johnston and Surya, *On extending the quantum measure*, [arXiv:1007.2725](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.2725), that the decoherence functional on cylinder sets extends to the generated σ-algebra exactly when the associated vector measure does, and fails to for a finite unitary system. Solovay's theorem was confirmed by a further search (encyclopaedia summary and the journal record; the paper itself was not read): R. M. Solovay, *A model of set-theory in which every set of reals is Lebesgue measurable*, Ann. of Math. (2) 92 (1970), 1–56, constructs, from an inaccessible cardinal, a model of Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory with dependent choice in which every set of reals is Lebesgue measurable; S. Shelah, *Can you take Solovay's inaccessible away?*, Israel J. Math. 48 (1984), 1–47, shows that the consistency of an inaccessible cardinal is needed for that. The sources found disagree on exactly which weak choice principle Shelah's argument uses; that detail is not relied on here.

**(a) The paradox is a statement about spectral gaps.** Paradoxical decomposition, absence of an invariant mean, failure of Følner's condition and a spectral radius below one for the random walk are one property seen four ways. So the paradox on the sphere is the visible side of the fact that a walk driven by a free group of rotations loses a fixed fraction of every centred function at each step. On a non-amenable carrier a gap of the step operator is free; on an amenable one it is not. This bears on both projects.
- In the program, the carriers of the gain-graph papers (G8 to G10) are graphs on which walks are counted and a step operator acts. On a line or a lattice (amenable) the count of walks has no gap; on a tree or the Cayley graph of a free group it has one, of size `1 − 2√(d−1)/d`, for a reason that has nothing to do with gains. A result of the program that reads as "decay" or "loss" on a carrier should say which kind the carrier is.
- Here, the spatial lattice is a box in `Z³`, which is amenable: boxes are Følner sets, and that is exactly why the free theory has a gap `2π/L` in a box and none in infinite volume (Appendix E, "What the example does not show"). So no part of a Yang–Mills gap on `Z³` can come from the geometry of the lattice. It has to come from the non-abelian dynamics. This is the precise sense in which Hypotheses 3.1 to 3.3, which the free theory satisfies, cannot give volume uniformity.

Stipulated test (cheap, a control and not a path to the goal): the free theory of Appendix E on a non-amenable spatial graph of bounded degree has a gap uniform in the volume, and the refinement architecture then closes with volume uniformity. If that is so, the architecture is not what stands between a box and infinite volume; amenability is. Against the reading: no uniform gap for the free gauge field on such a graph (the gap is known for functions; for the curl–curl operator on one-forms it has to be checked).

**(b) Finitely additive set functions on an algebra are the program's own ground.** G10 takes events to be sets of histories and defines its rule on the algebra of cylinder sets (its lemma on events as sets of histories), citing the result that the quantum measure does not in general extend to the generated σ-algebra. The Banach–Tarski paradox is the classical statement of the same kind one level up: an invariant finitely additive measure need not exist on all sets, and where it exists (the line and the plane) it is obtained from the Hahn–Banach theorem, which by Foreman–Wehrung and Pawlikowski is already enough for the paradox. So the program's restriction to the cylinder algebra is not only caution: any extension of its rules to all sets of histories would be an object whose existence depends on a choice principle and whose values no finite record could fix. For the Yang–Mills work the contrast is the useful part. The Wilson weight is positive, the transfer operator is positive, and the measure on histories of the lattice field is countably additive, so the extension problem does not arise; that positivity (reflection positivity in the continuum) is exactly what the signed and complex rules of the program lack.

**(c) Limits chosen without a rule.** The external construction takes its infinite-volume limit along a subsequence that is not proved unique. Existence of such limits in separable spaces needs only countably many dependent choices. A canonical limit obtained from a Banach limit or an ultrafilter would need a choice principle of Hahn–Banach strength, and a statement that held only for such a limit could not be tested. The paper is on the safe side: Corollary 5.2 uses a lower limit, and Theorem 1.1 is stated for one component. Standard to keep: every statement about a limit here must hold for all subsequential limits or name the subsequence.

**(d) A criterion from the program's organising question.** The program asks which properties of a description can be justified by a specified set of observations. A property that changes with the choice of a non-measurable set, or that exists only by a Hahn–Banach argument, is not of that kind. Solovay's theorem is the formal version: if an inaccessible cardinal is consistent, then dependent choice is consistent with every set of reals being Lebesgue measurable, and in such a model there is no paradoxical decomposition of the ball. So nothing provable from dependent choice alone can depend on the paradox. The proviso about the inaccessible cardinal is part of the statement and, by Shelah's result, cannot be removed. Check applied: every statement of the Yang–Mills paper is about Borel sets, `L²` classes and positive kernels, and its proofs use at most countable dependent choice. `Classical.choice` in the Lean axiom lists is how Mathlib is built and does not decide the question; no tool here certifies choice-freeness.

**Assessment.** (a) is a real structural fact and gives one cheap control. (b) explains a design decision of the program and why the Euclidean lattice measure does not share its difficulty. (c) and (d) are standards the paper already meets. None of them is a route to the gap; (a) says where a gap cannot come from.

## 7. Reading done before the toy run ended (24 November 2026)

**Piazza and Shen, arXiv:2610.35286v1, read in full (13 pages; fetched, read, not stored).** This is test B1.

What the proof does. The torus is paved by blocks of `n = m × m` plaquettes. The coarse variables are the links on the block boundaries; the fibres are the interior links, independent across blocks given the boundaries. (i) Integrating the interior of a block turns its `n` plaquette weights into the `n`-fold convolution `w^{*n}` of the one-plaquette weight evaluated on the boundary word; with `w ≥ ½e^{-2t}`, `t = N²|β|`, the convolution powers approach 1 at the rate `r = 1 − ½e^{-2t}` per factor, with gradient and Hessian, so for `n` large the marginal has a Hessian below half the Ricci curvature `N/2` of the group and the curvature criterion gives a log-Sobolev constant 2 for the marginal, independent of β. (ii) Each fibre gets its inequality from the Holley–Stroock perturbation bound, with constant `K = e^{4tn}`. (iii) The two are combined by the chain rule for entropy and a bound on the gradient of the conditional expectation: covariance of `F` with the score `∇_e H_R`, estimated by `‖∇_e H_R‖_∞ ≤ 2t` times the fibre Poincaré constant (their Lemma 6.1). All functions, no gauge fixing. They state that in higher dimensions the conditional integrations produce larger non-local loops and the method does not extend, and that it uses positive Ricci curvature, so not U(1).

Outcome against the stipulation of Path B. The constant is `K t² n` with `n` of order `e^{2t}` times a logarithm, so it is doubly exponential in β. In the normalisation of our paper (`β_ours = 4β_theirs` for SU(2), so `t = β_ours`) that is `exp(c β e^{2β})`, far beyond the `e^{pβ}`, `p < 1/s ≈ 2.7`, that Remark 3.5 can absorb. The authors call their constant not optimal. So the test comes out against Path B as stated: the one existing weak-coupling proof of this type does not give a usable dependence on β, and it is two-dimensional. What survives is the shape, and two specific things to take from it:
- Their step (iii) uses the supremum of the score, of order β per link. Proposition 3.7 uses the conditional covariance of the next slice instead, which in the free theory is of order `1/β`. The difference is a factor of order `β³` in the product, and it is the reason to keep the matrix form.
- "Convolution makes the Hessian small" is the mechanism by which a coarse marginal becomes tractable at weak coupling. Its analogue in the time direction is that the conditional law of the next slice is a product kernel reweighted by `φ` (Remark 6.6).

A lower bound to keep in mind (their Section 7). At a critical point of the action that is not a global minimum and has vanishing Hessian, the log-Sobolev constant is at least `cβ^{1/3}`, also for gauge-invariant functions; their example needs `N` a multiple of 4. The same bump function gives only `Var(f) ≥ c r² E|∇f|²` with `r = β^{-1/3}`, that is, a Poincaré constant at least `cβ^{-2/3}`: no divergence, but slower than `1/β`. For SU(2) the degenerate points on the minimum itself, the flat connections where transverse directions are quartic (the small-torus literature of §1), would by the same scaling give at least `cβ^{-1/2}`. This is one more reason for Remark 3.8(b): ask for bounded constants, not for order `1/β`. (The last two sentences are my scaling estimates, not statements of the paper.)

**Grunewald, Otto, Villani, Westdickenberg, Ann. Inst. H. Poincaré Probab. Stat. 45 (2009), 302–351** (supplied by the Principal after two failed fetches; read: Sections 2.1, 2.2, Remark 16 and Section 3, that is, the set-up, Theorem 3, and its whole proof; the hydrodynamic part and the application were not read; not stored here).

Theorem 3. For `μ = e^{−H}` on a Euclidean space `X` and a linear coarse-graining `P: X → Y` with `P N Pᵗ = id`, assume (i) `κ < ∞`, where `κ` is the largest value of the mixed second derivative `⟨Hess H(x) u, v⟩` over unit `u` in the macroscopic directions and unit `v` in the fibre directions; (ii) every conditional measure satisfies a log-Sobolev inequality with constant `ρ`; (iii) the marginal satisfies one with constant `λN`. Then `μ` satisfies one with constant `ρ̂ = ½[ρ + λ + κ²/ρ − √((ρ + λ + κ²/ρ)² − 4ρλ)]`.

The proof is three lines of structure. Additivity of entropy over the disintegration; the inequality on the fibres; and, for the marginal term, a bound on the gradient of the coarse-grained function `f̄(y) = ∫ f dμ(·|y)` (Proposition 20). That bound rests on two lemmas: Lemma 21, the identity `∫ P∇f dμ(·|y) = N⁻¹∇f̄(y) + P cov_{μ(·|y)}(f, ∇H)`; and Lemma 22, a bound of a covariance `cov(f, g)` by the Lipschitz constant of `g` and the entropy of `f`, using the fibre inequality. The coupling `κ` enters as the Lipschitz constant, along the fibre, of the macroscopic component of `∇H`. Their Remark 6 describes the intended use: one step of a renormalisation group, with the fibre inequality from standard methods at a finite block size and the marginal inequality from convexification of the coarse Hamiltonian by averaging.

Comparison with Proposition 3.7 of our paper, step by step.
- Their Lemma 21 is the same identity as ours, "the derivative of `h(x) = E[g(Y) | X = x]` is `β` times a covariance with `Y`": in both, the gradient of a conditional expectation is a covariance with the score.
- Their Lemma 22 bounds that covariance by a supremum (a Lipschitz constant) times a fibre functional inequality. Ours bounds it by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality with the conditional covariance matrix `Σ_x`, and puts the fibre inequality on the other side. For a Poincaré inequality the Cauchy–Schwarz form is available and is never worse; for entropy it is not, which is why they use the Lipschitz form.
- Their coupling constant is a mixed second derivative of the Hamiltonian. For the joint law of two consecutive slices in temporal gauge the mixed second derivative between the slices comes from `β Σ_e x_e·y_e` alone and is `β` per link. So a direct transcription of Theorem 3 to the pair of slices has `κ = β`, and its constant is useful only if the fibre constant `ρ` is of order `β` or better; that is the same requirement as `P_c` of order `1/β`, which Remark 3.8 shows is impossible for all functions and which the scaling estimates above put in doubt even for gauge-invariant ones. Proposition 3.7 replaces `κ²/ρ = β² P_c` by `β² ‖Σ^{1/2} M Σ^{1/2}‖`, in which the factor `Σ`, of order `1/β` in the free theory, compensates one power of `β`. This is the concrete gain of the matrix form over the transcription.
- Their theorem needs a Euclidean space and a linear `P`; the extension to non-linear maps is the paper of Lelièvre below.

**T. Lelièvre, *A general two-scale criteria for logarithmic Sobolev inequalities*, J. Funct. Anal. 256 (2009), 2211–2221** (supplied by the Principal in an archive of a journal issue; read: statement and set-up, pp. 2211–2216; not stored here; the other eleven articles of the archive are unrelated). It states the criterion of Grunewald, Otto, Villani and Westdickenberg and extends it from linear to non-linear coarse-graining maps `ξ`, which is the case needed here, since the coarse field is a product of links. For a measure `e^{−V}` on a domain of `R^n` and a smooth `ξ` with Gram matrix `G = ∇ξ∇ξᵀ ≥ m`, its Theorem 1.2 assumes: a log-Sobolev inequality with constant `ρ` for every conditional measure on a level set of `ξ`; one with constant `r` for the image measure; and a coupling bound `‖∇_Σ F‖_∞ ≤ M`, where `F` is the local mean force (the vector whose conditional average is the gradient of the effective potential) and `∇_Σ` is the gradient along the level set. The conclusion is a log-Sobolev inequality for the whole measure with constant
`R = ½ [ rm + M²m/ρ + ρ − √((rm + M²m/ρ + ρ)² − 4rmρ) ]`,
which the paper says is optimal for Gaussian measures with linear `ξ`. In the Gaussian case the coupling vanishes exactly when the coarse and fibre variables are uncorrelated.

Bearing. (i) The coupling is measured by a derivative of the mean force along the fibre, not by its size; this is the static counterpart of using the covariance `Σ_x` in Proposition 3.7 and is what the two-dimensional proof above gives away by taking a supremum. (ii) Like Proposition 3.7 the criterion is exact for Gaussians, so the free theory is the right calibration for both. (iii) It is stated on a domain of `R^n` with the Euclidean metric; the links here live on spheres and the gauge directions have to be removed (Remark 3.8), so it cannot be quoted as it stands. (iv) It is a statement about one measure. The first hypothesis of the paper concerns a time step, and its coarse part is handled by Theorem 1.2 of the paper, not by a marginal inequality; a static two-scale inequality for the slice measure would be an input to the fibre inequality (3.1), not a replacement for the hypothesis.

**Solovay 1970 and Shelah 1984.** Not read beyond the journal records; nothing in the Yang–Mills work depends on their details.

B3 (one link pair, gauge-invariant Poincaré constant against β and staples) is still worth doing, now with the specific question whether the fibre constant behaves like `1/β`, like `β^{-1/2}` near degenerate staples, or worse. It is below 1 throughout, and `(0.9208 − κ)·β` is 0.353, 0.349, 0.347 at the last three couplings, so the values are consistent with the limit written before the run. The outcome that would have counted against Path A, a constant growing without bound in β, is not seen. This is one toy with a single coarse site; test A2 (the next size, in quadratic approximation, with the coarse comparison of Path E) is next.

## 9. A sixth path, found after Remark 5.8 (24 November 2026)

**Path F: one step on one lattice (Proposition 5.9).** A uniform gap in a box is equivalent to: (i) `I − P ≤ κH` with `κ` independent of the regulator, and (ii) decorrelation of the coarse variables over the time `t`, `‖P e^{−tH} P‖ ≤ 1 − b`, for a projection `P` of one's choice. This removes the chain, Hypotheses 3.2 and 3.3 and Path E.

Stipulations and tests.
1. There are gauge-invariant block variables, finitely many per coarse cell, for which (i) holds in the free theory with `κ` at most a constant times the coarse mesh, uniformly in the blocking factor. Tested so far: line sums fail (constant grows like `r` coarse meshes; predicate failed and kept); box averages of the plaquette field give 0.46 to 0.72 coarse meshes for `r` = 2 to 8 and are still increasing. Next: a smoother kernel (iterated box average, or a triangular weight). Against: growth for every kernel of bounded support.
2. For such variables, (ii) holds in the free theory in a box with `b` equal to the deficit (trivially, by compression), and the joint law of the coarse variables at two times has a density with respect to the product of its marginals that is bounded below, uniformly in the regulator. The second half is a Gaussian computation: it fails if some linear combination of coarse variables has correlation tending to one. Against: such a combination.
3. For SU(2) the same two statements. (i) is a statement at all scales below the coarse one and is of the kind renormalisation-group analyses control; (ii) concerns finitely many variables. Nothing is known for either.

What would make this path empty: if for every admissible finite set of coarse variables (i) forces `κ` to grow with the regulator. The free theory already shows the question is not trivial.

**Outcome of Path F, test 1 (24 November 2026).** With the box average of the plaquette field applied twice in each direction (triangular weight), the free-theory constant is `1/ω(π/r,0,0)` in fine units to six figures for `r` = 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, that is, about `1/π` coarse meshes, bounded and equal to the value forced by the resolution. The stipulation holds in the free theory. The predicate as written failed because the axial values decrease by more than its two-sided tolerance; recorded. Next: test 2 (the two-time law of these coarse variables), then what the analogue of these variables is for SU(2), where a block average of the field strength needs parallel transport to a common point.

**A reading of Path F against practice (not grounded by a search yet; from general knowledge of lattice spectroscopy, to be checked before any use in the paper).** Numerical glueball spectroscopy estimates masses from correlations in time of blocked or smeared operators on one fine lattice. Statement (ii) of Proposition 5.9 is the rigorous counterpart of what is measured there, for the whole space of functions of the coarse variables instead of a finite basis; statement (i) is what is assumed there without proof, that the blocked variables miss no light state. So Path F says where a proof would differ from a computation: (ii) is the measurable part, (i) is the part that needs an argument at all scales below the coarse one. Test to design: an estimator of the maximal correlation of a small set of block variables between two times, on the existing samplers, as a lower value for `1 − b`.

**Path F, test 1, now a theorem in the free theory (24 November 2026).** Proposition E.5 of the paper: for the twice-averaged block plaquette field the constant of statement (i) is at most 1056 coarse meshes for every blocking factor and side. What decides it is the decay of the averaging multiplier at the aliases: the weight of a soft mode in the fine detail is a sum over aliases of `(sin(k/2)/sin(p/2))^{2q}` times the alias energy, which converges for `q = 2` and diverges logarithmically for `q = 1`. For a non-abelian theory this says what a block variable must do: suppress every alias of a soft momentum at least like the fourth power of the ratio of momenta.

**Path F, test 2: outcome without a computation (24 November 2026).** The second half of the stipulation, a lower bound for the density of the two-time law of the coarse variables against the product of its marginals, is false in the free theory for a trivial reason: the variables are Gaussian and unbounded, and the ratio of a correlated Gaussian density to the product of its marginals tends to zero at infinity. Statement (ii) holds there anyway, through canonical correlations (it is the compression of the gap). So the minorisation is a sufficient condition suited to compact coarse variables, as for a compact gauge group, and the free theory neither supports nor tests it. The compact toy could test it: the law of the two coarse holonomies at two times is a function on a four-torus that the sector program can produce.

**Where Path F points for SU(2).** The block variable has to be an average of parallel transports over the block, of the kind used in renormalisation-group constructions (and, with a penalty, in the external construction), not a product of links along one line. Section 8 of the paper now says so. The two statements to aim at are then: (i) for that block variable, the part of a function it does not determine is bounded by the energy, uniformly in the fine mesh; (ii) the block variables at two times a fixed physical time apart have a joint law bounded below by a multiple of the product of its marginals, uniformly in the fine mesh. Neither is attempted here.

**Path F, test 2 in the compact toy (24 November 2026).** Done as suggested above. The minorisation constant is far below the true decorrelation at short times and within a few per cent of it after a few correlation times (table in the README of `supplement/S12-compact-toy/`). So where statement (ii) is wanted at a fixed physical time of the order of the inverse gap, proving a lower bound for the joint density of compact coarse variables loses little, in this toy.

**Path F, statement (i) in a form that can be probed (24 November 2026).** (i) with `κ = s/γ` follows if, given the block variables of a slice, the maximal correlation between that slice and the slice a time `s/2` later is at most `(1 − γ)^{1/2}`, with `s` of the order of the coarse mesh and `γ` independent of the fine mesh; and (i) implies such a bound. For SU(2) this can be probed from below by trial functions on a sampler with the block variables of one slice held fixed, exactly as Appendix A.2 of the paper did for the link-product coarse field, now at a lag of half a coarse mesh instead of one fine step. A trial function with conditional correlation tending to one as the fine mesh decreases at fixed coarse mesh would count against the path.

**Where the two statements of Path F sit relative to the scale of the theory (24 November 2026; a reading, not a result).** The coarse mesh `a_0` is ours to choose. If `a_0` is well below the scale at which the coupling becomes strong, statement (i) concerns only scales at which the coupling is weak, uniformly as the fine mesh goes to zero; that is the regime of ultraviolet-stability analyses. Statement (ii) is then the whole infrared problem, for the effective theory of the block variables at mesh `a_0`. If `a_0` is taken above that scale, (ii) is a strong-coupling statement for which decay of correlations is the expected and, for the Wilson action itself, the provable behaviour, and the crossover has moved into (i). So the one-step form does not remove the difficulty; it lets one choose on which side of the cut it lies, and in the first choice it isolates a statement, (i), that does not involve the crossover at all.

## 10. A first non-abelian data point for Path A (24 November 2026)

Test A3 asked whether, for a non-abelian group, slow states keep a fine-detail weight proportional to their energy, in particular when the energy is made very small by tunnelling between flat configurations. A version of it that can be computed exactly: finite subgroups of SU(2) on a 2 × 1 torus in 2+1 dimensions (`papers/spectral-criteria/supplement/S14-nonabelian-toy/`).

Outcome. Where the octahedral and icosahedral groups agree (β ≤ 2, marginally 3), the ratio of weight to energy for the lowest state is 0.25, 0.31, 0.36, close to the abelian toy. Beyond that the groups differ in the gap by an order of magnitude, but in each of them separately the weight follows the energy down to 2e-7 with a ratio that grows only to 0.70, and γ_opt stays above 0.74. So the outcome that would have counted against Path A, a ratio growing like the inverse of an exponentially small energy, is not seen in these models. What is seen instead is slow growth of the ratio, roughly linear in β, which the summability can absorb.

Limits. A finite group at large β is not SU(2): its flat configurations are discrete and the collapse of its gap is partly its own. The lattice has two sites. The slow state found is flux through a direction of length one. A real test needs SU(2) itself (a spin-network basis for the same ladder would do it) and at least one more site in each direction.

Next on this line: the same ladder with SU(2) in a truncated spin-network basis, to see how far in β the icosahedral numbers can be trusted; and the dependence of the ratio on β in a model where the slow state's energy is known analytically.
