## Paper A: arXiv 2111.12813 — Cao and Chatterjee, *A state space for 3D Euclidean Yang–Mills theories*

### L1: main theorems and hypotheses

Setting (p.2): G is a compact Lie group that is a finite product of tori, U(n), SU(n), O(n), SO(2n+1); dimension 3; **continuum**, on the 3D unit torus T^3 (no lattice in the results themselves; lattice or Fourier-truncated approximations are allowed as inputs, Remark 1.14, p.11). No coupling-constant regime appears in the statements (INFERRED: the results are about short-distance regularity, not weak/strong coupling). The state space X consists of distributional gauge orbits, encoded as trajectories t > 0 of the Yang–Mills heat flow modulo gauge, with the topology generated by regularized Wilson loops (Wilson loop of the heat-flowed field at time t > 0) (p.6-7).

- **Theorem 1.6 (p.7), tightness criterion.** Take a sequence of random elements of X. If, for every positive flow time t, the Yang–Mills action of the time-t flowed configuration forms a tight family of real random variables, then some subsequence converges weakly to a probability measure on X. Proved: compactness / existence of subsequential limit measures on the state space. Not proved: existence of the YM measure, mass gap, decay, or area law.
- **Theorem 1.12 (p.11), GFF-like inputs suffice.** Suppose smooth random g^3-valued fields A_n on T^3 satisfy (p.9-10): translation invariance, finite second moments with stretched-exponential tail bounds (also for quadratic forms), an approximate Wick bound on four-point functions, and a uniform covariance bound |E[A(x)A(y)]| <= C d(x,y)^{-(3-alpha)} with alpha in (5/3, 3) (i.e. at most free-field-like or somewhat worse singularity). Then the expected YM action after heat flow for any time t > 0 is bounded uniformly in n, so by Theorem 1.6 a subsequential weak limit on X exists. This is conditional: it does not show the actual YM approximations satisfy the hypotheses (that is Conjecture 1.13, p.11).
- **Theorem 1.16 (p.13), gauge-invariant free field.** Applying the above to Fourier truncations of the 3D Lie-algebra-valued Gaussian free field, the corresponding elements of X converge in law (full sequence) to a limit random variable, which they call the gauge invariant free field. It shows X contains genuinely distributional elements.
- Supporting: Theorem 3.51 (p.35) and Corollary 3.52 (p.36) on the quotient space being Lusin; Theorem 4.3 (p.51) cites the heat-flow existence from GFF-like data proved in the companion paper [8].

Quotation supporting the main L1 claim (Theorem 1.16, p.13): "the laws of XN converge weakly to the law of X" (p.13).

### L2: what is open, and 4D

- Conjecture 1.8 (p.7-8): that for some sequence approximating the YM measure, the tightness hypothesis of Theorem 1.6 holds. Conjecture 1.13 (p.11): that some approximating sequence satisfies the GFF-like assumptions of Theorem 1.12. These are the gaps between the paper and an actual 3D YM construction.
- p.5: they state the 1980s lattice/phase-cell renormalization program was not completed, so the Yang–Mills existence problem remains open. p.13: they say none of the prior works they list construct non-Abelian Euclidean YM in dimension three or more in the sense they want.
- **4D (Remark 1.15, p.11-12):** the method does not extend to 4D, because the probabilistic smoothing that makes the heat flow locally solvable from GFF-type initial data fails there; they attribute this to 4D being the critical dimension for Yang–Mills, and say the SPDE approach of Chandra–Chevyrev–Hairer–Shen hits similar obstacles. The paper makes no claim about a 4D continuum construction or a mass gap (no mass-gap mention located; INFERRED that it is out of scope).

## Paper B: arXiv 2201.03487 — Chandra, Chevyrev, Hairer, Shen, *Stochastic quantisation of Yang–Mills–Higgs in 3D*

### L1: main theorems and hypotheses

Setting (p.3, p.8): G an arbitrary compact Lie group; a Higgs field in a finite-dimensional real orthogonal representation V (dim V = 0 allowed, i.e. pure YM included, p.3); trivial bundles on the 3D torus T^3; **continuum**, with mollified space-time white noise of scale epsilon removed as epsilon -> 0. No coupling-strength regime is imposed (INFERRED: results are local in time and hold for arbitrary initial data in the state space). The object studied is the Langevin (stochastic quantisation) dynamics of the YMH action, not the measure itself.

- **Theorem 1.2 (State space), p.11.** For Hölder-type exponent eta slightly below -1/2, there is a complete nonlinear metric space S of distributions which embeds into C^eta, contains a dense image of a better Hölder space, on which the deterministic DeTurck–YMH flow extends continuously, and whose gauge-equivalence relation (defined via the flow) yields a separable, completely Hausdorff orbit space O; a Hölder gauge group acts continuously on S with a polynomial bound on the gauge transformation (points (i)-(v)).
- **Theorem 1.7 (Local existence), p.12-13.** For any choice of the extra G-equivariant mass operator and any mollifier, there are renormalisation counterterms (depending only on the mollifier) such that the renormalised stochastic YMH system started in S converges in probability, as the mollification is removed, to a limit in the space of (maximal, possibly blowing-up) solutions, and the limit does not depend on the mollifier. Proved via regularity structures: **local-in-time solutions**.
- **Theorem 1.9 (Gauge covariance), p.14.** There is a unique choice of the finite counterterm making the limiting solution gauge covariant in law (modulo finite-time blow-up), and consequently a unique Markov process on the orbit space O that agrees locally in time with the projected solution. Precise versions: Theorem 6.1 (p.76) and Theorem 7.5 (p.129); general local existence Theorem 5.1 (p.58).
- Not proved: existence of an invariant measure (i.e. the YMH measure), global-in-time existence, mass gap, decay of correlations, or area/perimeter law.

Quotation supporting the main L1 claim (Theorem 1.9, p.14): "There exists a unique Markov process X on O such that" (p.14).

### L2: what is open, and 4D

- p.3: they state that constructing the pure YM measure in d = 3, and the non-Abelian YMH measure in d = 2 and 3, is open even in finite volume.
- p.7-8 (Section 1.2): main open question is whether the Markov process on O has an invariant measure (they conjecture yes; uniqueness would follow from strong Feller and full support), which would be a candidate YMH measure on T^3; they cannot even show the process survives for all times (no global existence). Also open: whether a genuine topological gauge group acting on S can generate the equivalence relation (they suspect not with current definitions); extending to R^3 is described as completely open, even in 2D.
- **4D (Remark 2.1, p.14-15):** the state-space results of Section 2 hold for d = 2, 3; long-time existence of the deterministic 4D YM flow is known from other work, but their space would not support the GFF in d >= 4 and all later sections fail severely for d >= 4. They mention earlier 4D results only as background (U(1) lattice convergence in d = 4; ultraviolet-stability results for pure YM in d = 4 in finite volume, p.3). No claim about the 4D continuum Yang–Mills / mass-gap problem is made (INFERRED: out of scope; no mass-gap string found in either text).
