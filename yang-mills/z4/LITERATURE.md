# External grounding: where this folder's statements stand against the literature

Director Z4, 23 November 2026, at the principal's direction to ground the work externally as it proceeds. Kirtus Justus, Theoretical Physicist, Port Coquitlam, British Columbia, Canada, directs the work. Prepared with assistance from Claude (Anthropic).

**How this was gathered, and its limit.** Twelve web searches on 23 November 2026. Every entry below rests on search-result summaries and abstracts; no paper was opened and read in full in this pass. Each row says what the source is reported to contain, not what Z4 has verified in it. Rows marked *recalled* were named by a worker from memory and only then searched. This file is a map for checking, not a set of verified citations. It will be extended as the work proceeds.

## 1. The problem and what is established

| Topic | Source (as found) | Reported content | Bearing here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem statement | Jaffe and Witten, Clay Mathematics Institute, [official text](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf) (not opened); secondary: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang%E2%80%93Mills_existence_and_mass_gap), [MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Yang-MillsExistenceandMassGapProblem.html) | For any compact simple gauge group a nontrivial quantum Yang–Mills theory on ℝ⁴ with mass gap Δ > 0, satisfying axioms at least as strong as Wightman's. | The six requirements of `OVERALL_DISPOSITION.md` paraphrase this. The secondary sources name the Wightman axioms; the Osterwalder–Schrader form used in this repository should be checked against the official text. |
| Survey of the probabilistic formulation | Chatterjee, "Yang–Mills for probabilists", [arXiv:1803.01950](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.01950) | Lattice gauge theories, Wilson loops and confinement, the continuum limit problem, review of the mathematical literature; all central questions open. | The standard reference for the state of the art in the language this repository uses. To be read in full. |
| Reflection positivity, strong-coupling gap | Osterwalder and Seiler 1978 (as cited in recent preprints, for example [arXiv:2406.16881](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.16881)) | Lattice gauge theories with arbitrary compact group are reflection positive, have a mass gap at strong coupling, and SU(N) confines there. Confinement and a gap at all couplings in four dimensions are a long-standing open problem. | G4's finite-slab reflection positivity and B4's strong-coupling transfer bounds are in the territory of this 1978 result. They should be stated as re-derivations with explicit constants unless a difference is shown. |
| Ultraviolet stability in four dimensions | Bałaban, renormalization-group papers of the 1980s ([bibliography](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Tadeusz+Balaban)); exposition of the method by Dimock, [arXiv:1304.0705](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.0705) and parts I, II | Ultraviolet stability bounds for four-dimensional lattice Yang–Mills in a small-field regime, by a rigorous renormalization group. Dimock's papers explain the method on φ⁴ in three dimensions. | **Correction to this folder.** `OVERALL_DISPOSITION.md` section 5 says of a weak-coupling estimate for a fixed-size observable that "the literature has none in four dimensions". Too strong as written: Bałaban's stability bounds are weak-coupling results in four dimensions. What is, by these summaries, not available is control of correlation functions of observables in the continuum limit. The sentence is scoped here; the original stays. |
| Stability bounds for scaled plaquette fields | Faria da Veiga and O'Carroll, [arXiv:2205.07376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07376), Rep. Math. Phys. 95 (2025); earlier [arXiv:2005.00899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00899), [arXiv:1903.09829](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09829) | For U(N) in d = 2, 3, 4 on a finite lattice: thermodynamic and ultraviolet stable bounds for a scaled partition function; the generating function of scaled plaquette correlations bounded uniformly in volume, spacing and coupling; a singularity of order a^(−d) for the physical two-plaquette correlation at coincident points. | Directly comparable with C4's uniform exponential moment for β(1 − x_p), with A4's contact variance and with this folder's b⁻⁵ law (the smeared square of a field whose coincident two-point function is of order b⁻⁴). The other directors should compare constants and hypotheses with this paper before calling the bounds new. |
| Leading weak-coupling free energy | Chatterjee, "The leading term of the Yang–Mills free energy", [arXiv:1602.01222](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01222) | Explicit leading term of the free energy of lattice gauge theories in the weak-coupling limit, including dimension four, via Gaussian measures. | The Gaussian (free three-colour) structure that C4's tangent law, D4's loop covariance and the seventh round of this folder all rest on is the mechanism of this paper at the level of the free energy. |
| A non-Gaussian limit is open | Chatterjee, "A scaling limit of SU(2) lattice Yang–Mills–Higgs theory", [arXiv:2401.10507](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.10507v3) | With a Higgs field and a very small coupling scaling the gauge field converges to a massive Gaussian field; constructing a non-Gaussian scaling limit is stated as open. | Matches the reading of the seventh round and of C4's `magnetic-smearing`: every limit obtained in this repository is Gaussian, on trajectories where the coupling vanishes too fast for anything else. |

## 2. This folder's own statements

| Statement here | Literature | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| Free lattice benchmark converges to the continuum free field at positive time (`fixed-size/STATEMENT.md` S2′, proved with constants in round 9) | Gross (U(1) in three dimensions) and Driver, "Convergence of the U(1)₄ lattice gauge theory to its continuum limit", Comm. Math. Phys. 110 (1987) 479–501, [paper](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~bdriver/DRIVER/Papers/Drivers_Papers/A1-U(1)_4-Lattice.pdf); see also Garban and Sepúlveda, [arXiv:2107.04021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.04021) | **Not new.** Driver proves that the compact U(1) lattice theory in four dimensions, with a general energy function, converges to a renormalized (free) continuum theory. The benchmark of this folder is the noncompact Gaussian case, which is elementary by comparison. Its use here is as a known-answer test, which is what it was called; the review record should cite Driver. |
| Sharp-time smeared action density has no limit; a regularized version does (S1′, S3′) | Gradient flow: Lüscher 2010; Lüscher and Weisz 2011 (as described in [arXiv:1308.5598](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.5598), [arXiv:1506.00118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.00118), [arXiv:1805.11070](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.11070)) | **The established construction is different and better.** In the literature the action density at positive *flow* time needs no renormalization beyond that of the coupling, to all orders of perturbation theory, and has an unambiguous continuum limit; it is the standard fixed-physical-size observable of lattice gauge theory. This folder regularizes by Euclidean *time* evolution instead, where the challenger found a two-by-two mixing of magnetic and electric densities. S6′ should be restated with the flowed action density as the candidate observable. Whether C_b at positive flow time can be bounded rigorously is the same open problem; the gain is that the observable is the right one. |
| Wilson loop: perimeter-law self-energy, mean normalization (`fixed-size/WILSON_LOOP.md`) | Polyakov 1980; Dotsenko and Vergeles 1980; Brandt, Neri and Sato 1981 (as described in Makeenko, [arXiv:0810.2183](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.2183)) | **Textbook.** A smooth loop is finite after charge renormalization up to a factor exp(−const·length/a); cusps add a logarithmic factor depending on the angle and not absorbable in the coupling; self-intersecting loops mix. The benchmark's c₁·(perimeter)/b + c₂·log reproduces the first two for a square in the free theory. The worker cited these names from memory (*recalled*); the search confirms the attribution at the level of a review. Consequence for D4 and B4: for a loop with corners in the interacting theory the mean is not the only renormalization. |
| Units audit, electric-scale band, two-dimensional controls | No source searched yet | Two-dimensional Yang–Mills on a cylinder is exactly solved in the literature (Migdal, Witten, Driver, Sengupta, Lévy and others); the gap values 1/2 and 3/8 of section 2 of the README should be matched to a citation. Not done. |

## 3. Bearing on the other lanes (for the directors; from summaries only)

- **D4, `electric-scale` (one-link dimension, entropy, fidelity under electric cutoffs).** A preprint of this month, Mihajlovic, "SU(2) Yang-Mills: Spectral Bounds, Vacuum Concentration, and Criteria for the Continuum Limit", [arXiv:2611.11534](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11534), is reported to contain vacuum-concentration entropy bounds, fidelity obstructions for hard electric cutoffs and the growth required in retained representation spaces, with physical-transfer bounds from gauge reduction, reflection positivity and strong-coupling estimates, and to isolate the extra input needed for a volume-uniform gap without claiming one. The overlap with D4's and B4's lanes looks substantial and must be read and cited or distinguished. Unrefereed; not opened here.
- **B4, strong coupling.** Osterwalder–Seiler; and the recent strong-coupling gap results by polymer or character expansions that the search returned among unrefereed postings. One of those postings claims a Lean-checked strong-coupling U(1) gap. None was examined.
- **C4, `tangent-curvature` and `magnetic-smearing`.** Chatterjee's free-energy paper and Faria da Veiga–O'Carroll above; Gross and Driver for the abelian continuum limit.
- **Everyone.** The search returned several postings (Zenodo, preprint servers) that claim proofs of the Yang–Mills mass gap. None is refereed or endorsed by anything found, none was examined, and none is relied on here.

## 5. What this changes

1. Two sentences of this folder were stronger than the literature allows and are scoped above: "the literature has none in four dimensions", and the presentation of the free-benchmark convergence without Driver's theorem.
2. The candidate fixed-size observable should be the gradient-flowed action density, not the time-evolved plaquette density.
3. Novelty claims anywhere in the repository about strong-coupling gaps, finite-lattice reflection positivity, Gaussian weak-coupling limits and uniform plaquette moment bounds need a comparison with the sources in section 1 before they are made. This folder makes none.
