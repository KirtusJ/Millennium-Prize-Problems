# A mixing bound for sums of nonlinear Wilson observables on many cells

On one slice of the isotropic four-dimensional SU(2) Wilson measure, fix the straight spacing-two skeleton $s$. Let $F=\sum_{l=1}^M f_l$ where $f_l$ is a function of $s$ and of a finite set $S_l$ of unfrozen links. Coefficients, locations and the set of terms can depend on $s$. They are fixed during all 24 colour updates. Assume the sets $S_l$ are mutually disjoint. Divide them into $b$ families such that within a family no elementary action face contains links from two different supports. The [local theorem](../cyclic-transfer/local.md) defines $D_l$ (the product of link polynomial dimensions), the one-link product factor $\alpha_l=\prod_{e\in S_l}c_{d_e}(1+6\beta)^{-2d_e}$ and the coverage count $h_l$ for each separate multidegree. Let $\sigma_l^2=\operatorname{Var}_{H_{S_l}}f_l$ under independent Haar links with the skeleton fixed. At every skeleton and finite regulator,
$$
 J_s(F)\ \ge\ \frac{1}{24b}\sum_l h_l\alpha_l\sigma_l^2,\qquad
 V_s(F)\ \le\ \left(\sum_l\sqrt{D_l}\,\sigma_l\right)^2.
$$
The first bound applies to arbitrarily many terms, in all coefficient directions. The second allows large covariances between their conditional means. Both use the same stationary Wilson fibre. Spatial separation of supports within an action is used only after conditioning.

**Proof.** Fix a colour and a family. In each support of the family retain just the links $T_l$ updated by this colour. Condition on every raw variable outside their union. This conditions on at least the full colour exterior, and hence can only decrease expected variance. Marks outside the family are now constants because their supports are disjoint. Given the enlarged exterior the Wilson weight on the union factors over the $T_l$: no action face uses retained variables from two distinct supports. Thus the conditional variance of the sum is the sum of the conditional variances of the individual functions. This holds also for complex coefficients. Terms with $T_l$ empty contribute zero.

For each term, its conditional law on $T_l$ is exactly its one given every link outside $T_l$. Conditional independence from the other retained sets ensures this statement, even when the exterior observables are correlated before conditioning. Compare the predictor infimum on $T_l$ to Haar, then use the one-link polynomial inequality on the remaining variables of $S_l$ in the squared Haar-centered polynomial, as in the local proof. Other supports do not enter that replacement. After averaging over the true exterior, this yields $\alpha_l$ times the full product-Haar defect of colour $i$ for $f_l$. For every family the full colour loss bounds its sum; averaging these $b$ lower bounds pays $1/b$. Summing over colours covers each nonconstant Haar component of term $l$ at least $h_l$ times. This proves the numerator estimate. For the denominator center at the sum of the Haar means and use $|f_l-\mathbb E_H f_l|\le\sqrt{D_l}\sigma_l$ pointwise. No unobserved conditional covariance was assumed to vanish.

**The actual plaquette cells.** In an $xy$ coarse cell at even $z$, the support consists of its four nonskeleton spokes. Directed $x$ links have odd $y$, even $z$; directed $y$ links have odd $x$, even $z$. Their position and direction uniquely determine the coarse cell at that orientation. A fine $xy$ square belongs to just one coarse cell at fixed even $z$. A fine square in a plane containing $z$ or time can see at most one such spoke in that layer: its parallel edges differ by one in $z$ or time. The remaining spatial orientation perpendicular to a spoke does not introduce another case. Thus distinct cells in one orientation need just one family. The identical statements hold in the other orientations. Supports in different orientations are still disjoint: the direction and the unique odd transverse coordinate determine a spoke's plane. Taking the three orientations as three families suffices. These statements are checked by [enumeration](geometry.py) on both odd and even coarse sides. The vacuum slab can be chosen with outer boundary factors away from the entire updated slice.

**How many nonlinear marks can be summed?** For the transported four-plaquette average of Z4’s sampler, $D_l=1750$, $h_l=2$ and $\alpha_l=c_1^3c_2(1+6\beta)^{-10}$ are valid for every translated and oriented coarse cell. A unit quaternion reference carried by the frozen skeleton is a coarse coefficient. For nonconstant sums define
$$M_{\rm eff}=\frac{(\sum_l\sigma_l)^2}{\sum_l\sigma_l^2}\le M.$$
The two inequalities give a relative floor
$$
\frac{J_s(F)}{V_s(F)}\ge
\frac{c_1^3c_2}{12b\cdot1750}
\,\frac{(1+6\beta)^{-10}}{M_{\rm eff}}.
$$
The bound permits nonlinear marks on all these cells and coefficients of either sign. For arbitrary functions of their joint variables the additivity assumption would fail.

On a dyadic spatial sequence $N=2^n N_0$, with $a_n=\ell/N$ and $\beta_n=O(\log N)$, this floor gives $\sum_n a_n/\gamma_n<\infty$ for a class with uniform effective-count bound $M_{\rm eff}\le C N/(\log N)^{11+\epsilon}$, any $\epsilon>0$. In particular any $O(N^{1-\epsilon})$ number of equally weighted cells is allowed for fixed $0<\epsilon<1$. Here $M_{\rm eff}$ must be bounded over coarse backgrounds for the whole claimed class. For an extensive unweighted sum with $M$ of order $N^3$, this proof gives a volume-independent lower bound on the loss **per Haar variance summed over cells**. Controlling the true variance per cell to obtain a useful relative floor for that sum is an independent question.

**Why the effective count occurs.** Let a hidden fair sign $Y$ conditionally give $M$ independent SU(2) links the density proportional to $\exp(kY\,\Re U_l)$ with fixed $k>0$. Set $f_l=\Re U_l$, and redraw all visible links conditional on $Y$. Each is a bounded-degree observable with a fixed one-link tilted-Haar conditional. Write $R=I_2(k)/I_1(k)$, $p=R'(k)$. The total redraw loss of $F$ is $Mp$, whereas its variance is $Mp+M^2R^2$. A uniform relative estimate without any count or covariance input fails in this example. It illustrates the limitation of the conditional factorization argument. The hidden sign is an external common field in this example, not a constructed Wilson vacuum mode.

The matched four-dimensional clear-sector problem still includes observables outside these sums, and the low-energy spectral projection can take a local sum outside this class. The [cross-lattice bound](../cyclic-transfer/partition.md) and the refinement budget are separate obligations.
