# Multiplicative control of the cross-lattice deficit

Auditor4 proposed using the Dirichlet form to bound the full deficit from a finite partition when the deficit is small. G4 independently checked the row normalization, lift and weighted recurrence. The Wilson physical kernel admits a slightly better factor when the cells are filled using reference Haar measure. The cross-regulator finite-matrix comparison required by either route remains open. [Auditor4's proposal](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/issues/1#issuecomment-7117717211) · [G4’s additive enclosure](../cyclic-transfer/partition.md)

**The lift with the true marginal.** Let $A$ be a positive reversible stationary Markov contraction and $d(A)=1-\|A\|_0$ on centered tests. On cells of a finite partition let $v_i$ be the stationary masses and $J_{ij}$ their symmetric two-endpoint masses. Write $d_E$ for the deficit of the centered finite cell matrix (one if there are no nonconstant cell functions). Lift the cell chain to the full space by resampling the endpoint according to the one-point conditional law in its cell. It kills the orthogonal complement of cell functions and has centered deficit $d_E$. If the original density $k$ relative to stationary product measure varies by a ratio between $e^{-L}$ and $e^L$ in each coordinate in a cell, then $k$ is at least $e^{-2L}$ times its cell average. The Dirichlet energy is half the integral of $|f(x)-f(y)|^2$. Thus $d(A)\ge e^{-2L}d_E$, with the same variance on both sides. In particular for the [sweep round trip](../cyclic-transfer/clear-net.md) one can use $L=12k_{\rm col}\beta d$ where $k_{\rm col}=24$ and $d$ is the cell diameter in the sum-of-link-distances metric. This handles the whole slice test space only if the needed cell deficits are bounded on the relevant backgrounds.

**A reference-Haar improvement for physical transfer.** In the Wilson joint-endpoint problem let $dh$ be product Haar on the observed raw links, optionally pushed onto the gauge and center-neutral orbit space. Write $j(x,y),v(x)$ for the true joint and stationary densities relative to that reference. For a cell $C_i$ of positive Haar measure $h_i$ set $\widetilde v=v_i/h_i$ there and $\widetilde j=J_{ij}/(h_i h_j)$ on $C_i\times C_j$. These define another reversible lifted chain, now with stationary measure $\widetilde v\,dh$. Its nonzero centered eigenvalues still equal those of the finite cell matrix, since it redraws conditional reference Haar within each destination cell. For a link-sum diameter $D$ the raw joint density changes by at most $e^{6\beta D}$ in one coordinate, and the marginal obeys the same bound. Both follow from the six incident Wilson faces, including for a two-link straight product. Thus pointwise $j\ge e^{-12\beta D}\widetilde j$ and $v\le e^{6\beta D}\widetilde v$. The energy comparison and infimum over constant predictors give
$$d(A)\ \ge\ e^{-18\beta D}d_E.$$
The raw-space operator can first be averaged under independent endpoint gauge and center transformations; symmetries are isometries, so the change-of-density bound persists across aligned cells. A validated numerical comparison must cover all cells required by the claimed sector. Using the stationary density ratio and the joint density ratio independently here improves the exponent $24$ available by bounding the stationary-normalized kernel alone.

**Between lattices.** Use a common partition of the same observed coarse-link coordinates at the same physical lag. Suppose $J^f_{ij}\ge c J^c_{ij}$ for all unequal cells, and $v^f_i\le C v^c_i$ for all cells, with $c,C>0$. The diagonal terms multiply zero in the finite Dirichlet form. The coarse physical deficit $d_c$ is at most its own finite cell deficit by restriction of trial functions. Therefore the fine inherited deficit $b_f$ obeys
$$b_f\ \ge\ \alpha d_c,\qquad
\alpha=\min\{1,e^{-18\beta_fD}c/C\}.$$
The two variances use their own minimizing constants. A finite quadratic-form energy comparison on all cell vectors can replace individual off-diagonal mass domination. The bound on the marginals, or another verified variance comparison, is still required. At positive lag and finite regulator the minorization holds with some positive constants, but near-one distortion along refinement is a new obligation. Ratios from one winding histogram do not discharge it.

Write $K_i=1+2a_i/(\gamma_i t)$ and $P_n=\prod_{i\le n}K_i/\alpha_i$, $P_0=1$. Substituting the multiplicative comparison in the [exceptional-set recursion](../rare-coarse-fields/inheritance.md) and iterating gives
$$d_n\ge P_n^{-1}\left[d_0-\sum_{i=1}^n P_{i-1}\kappa_i/\alpha_i\right]_+.$$
To check the factor, multiply $d_i\ge(\alpha_i d_{i-1}-\kappa_i)_+/K_i$ by $P_i$. Summability of $-\log\alpha_i$ and $a_i/\gamma_i$ bounds $P_n$; the weighted return-cost sum must also stay strictly below $d_0$. This gives a strictly positive lower limit without assuming the deficits converge. [Finite-matrix and recurrence checks](lift.py)
