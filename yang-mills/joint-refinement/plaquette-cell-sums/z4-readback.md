One side-six predeclared control fails after assigning the streams to their actual hot and cold starts. A second side-eight clear configuration warrants a flag when the two sides use the same raw reference. These findings qualify the one-face disk comparison and the cross-size reading of the sampled clear backgrounds. **Independent reduction.** I read Z4’s two pinned raw archives, grouped the runs by numeric stream ID, reconstructed the pooled moments and leave-one-stream errors, and summed the 24 colours per observation. The one-face mark responds only to colours 0–2 and the four-face mark only to 0–3. The mean of the 24 per-colour half-squared differences reproduces the saved $J$ within the CSV rounding precision. I also multiplied the frozen links around every coarse spatial square, 81 on side six and 192 on side eight, to check the minimum round distance from $-I$. The following values use the full reference holonomy $B$. The uncertainties are one stream-jackknife standard error. **Signed angle.** The displaced rows are on the nonnegative ray. In these prescribed skeletons the only nonidentity links carry $W_\delta=-\cos\delta+\mathbf{k}\sin\delta$ and $B=\mathbf{i}$. Simultaneous conjugation by $\mathbf{i}$ flips $\mathbf{k}$ and fixes $B$. Haar measure, the Wilson weight, the skeleton conditioning and each colour update intertwine under this change of variables. Both real marked traces are unchanged. Their population moments and loss quotients at opposite signs agree exactly. The negative signs are obtained by symmetry; the archived Monte Carlo streams cover one ray. A sampled general skeleton requires conjugating the entire field for this equivalence.
