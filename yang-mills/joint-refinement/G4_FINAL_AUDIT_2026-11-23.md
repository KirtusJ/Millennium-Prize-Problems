### Which state is being propagated?

Let $P$ be conditional expectation onto the gauge orbit of the straight coarse links and $Q=I-P$. An inherited test must be measurable from those links alone. For a real fine mark $F$, the identity $\|QF\|^2=\mathbb E\,\mathrm{Var}(F\mid\mathcal C)$ tests that requirement. Gauge invariance and center neutrality do not remove dependence on the interior spokes.

There is an explicit check on the all-identity skeleton. Set every fine link to $I$. The average of the four elementary half-traces in one coarse square is one. Flip a single interior spoke to $-I$ while keeping every coarse link fixed. Exactly two elementary plaquettes change sign and the average becomes zero. At $\beta=0$ its conditional variance on that skeleton is exactly $1/16$. At finite bare coupling the Wilson density is strictly positive, and the two different values persist on neighbourhoods by continuity. Projecting this mark onto the coarse links can discard the very variation that made it a promising fine-state test. ### What a failed comparison establishes

Write $R_i(f)=\mathcal E_i(f)/\mathrm{Var}_i(f)$ under the stationary marginal of regulator $i$. For a common eligible test, a certified violation of $\mathcal E_f(f)\ge c\mathcal E_c(f)$ defeats that energy comparison. To defeat the proposed spectral inequality $b_f\ge\alpha d_c$ using a trial function, it suffices to certify $R_f(f)<\alpha\,\underline d_c$, where $\underline d_c$ is a **lower** bound on the full coarse deficit. A coarse trial quotient gives an upper bound.

For an exact control take two independent reversible bits with uniform stationary law. On the coarse chain their centered one-bit eigenvalues are $.9,.1$, and on the fine chain they are $.8,.8$. These are positive Markov kernels (flip probabilities $(1-\lambda)/2$). The second-bit trial quotient drops from $.9$ to $.2$, yet the full gap rises from $.1$ to $.2$ because the joint spectrum also includes the first bit and the product of the two eigenvalues. This counterexample concerns inference from a trial vector.
