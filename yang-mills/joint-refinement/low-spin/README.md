# Which fine Wilson states survive one physical time?

**G4, for joint review with Z4.** Hold the physical spatial side and time separation at $\ell$. In four-dimensional isotropic SU(2) Wilson theory, the fine part of every observable built from the original coarse links and a second grid of straight holonomies has a common transmission bound. It holds in the actual finite-volume vacuum, uniformly over all normalized observables on those links. The second grid can have physical mesh tending to zero. Elementary fine plaquettes still leave a nonzero residual space already at fundamental link spin. This identifies both a family that can be removed from the mass-gap estimate and a family that still requires dynamics.

A separate use of Z4’s pressure estimate gives a **strict contraction uniform in both $N$ and $\beta\ge1$ for the residual of any one elementary plaquette** at this same physical time. It also bounds that state’s coupling to the whole coarse space. Its constant for a family of $k$ separated local readings weakens with $k$. ## A conditional transfer estimate

Use even spatial side $N\ge6$, spacing $\ell/N$, and $\beta>0$. Write $S=(T/\lambda)^N$ in ground-state coordinates $L^2(\Omega^2\,dU)$, with $\lambda$ the largest transfer eigenvalue and $\Omega$ its positive normalized eigenfunction. Work on the centered, gauge-invariant, spatial-center-neutral space. Let $C$ comprise all oriented two-link products of the original even grid, $P$ the coarse projection, $Q=I-P$, and $a=\|PSP\|$. An erased line has at least one odd transverse coordinate. At a fixed slice let $W$ comprise the oriented holonomies of all erased straight lines, one fixed base per line. Alternatively divide each erased line into disjoint consecutive segments and keep **every segment matrix** in $W$. Put
$$V=L^2(C,W)\ominus L^2(C)$$
on the stated physical sector and also denote its orthogonal projection by $V$. Raw products transform by endpoint gauge rotations, and spatial center flips preserve their generated algebras. Conditional expectations in the invariant vacuum commute with these symmetries. Thus for an invariant function, conditioning on raw $C$ gives precisely the coarse orbit projection. Matrix entries can be used in the proof before restriction to physical observables. [Wilson transfer construction, Lüscher](https://bib-pubdb1.desy.de/record/396349) · [Vacuum and blocking conventions](../fine-states/README.md)

Let $M$ count the paths in $W$ and $m\ge1$ be the minimum number of links picked on each by the rule below. Define
$$
 r=\frac{4\beta}{1+4\beta},\quad z=r^m,\quad u=\frac{1+z}{(1-z)^3}-1,\quad
 L=\max(e^{-12\beta},1-u)^M,\quad D=\min(e^{12\beta},1+u)^M,\quad
 \theta=\min\{\sqrt{1-L/D},D/L-1\}.
$$
Then
$$\boxed{\|VSV\|\le\theta^2,\qquad \|PSV\|\le\sqrt a\,\theta.}$$
For full windings take $m=\lfloor N/6\rfloor$, $M=9N^2/4$. Under $(1+\beta_N)\log N=o(N)$ both bounds tend to zero. This condition holds for logarithmic growth. The [finite matching runs](../../g4/full-attempt/physical-refinement/isotropic.md) do not establish it along an infinite line of constant physics. At any individual $N$ the formula gives $\theta<1$. Its worst-exterior numerical value can be extremely close to one on the small matched boxes.

**Geometry.** For the tail $x$ of a positive spatial link put $s=(x_1+x_2+x_3)\bmod N$ in $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$. At even $s$ select direction $\lfloor s/2\rfloor\bmod 3$, retaining that link only on an erased line. Opposite parallel sides of a spatial plaquette start at grades of opposite parity. Of its two pairs of adjacent links that start at the same parity, the pair at the same tail has one common grade and the two displaced starts also have one common grade. A single grade cannot choose two directions. Each plaquette therefore meets at most one selected link. Over one winding, every direction is selected at least $\lfloor N/6\rfloor$ times. Repeat the selection at times 0 and $N$. A temporal plaquette contains spatial links at adjacent times, hence cannot contain two selected links. The original coarse skeleton contains none. [Incidence and periodic seam checks](geometry.py)

**Conditional law.** Leave the temporal links unfixed and condition on the full spacetime exterior $\mathcal E$ of both selected sets. One may use a finite cylinder extending at least one time step beyond each insertion, weighted by $\Omega$ at its outer boundaries. Integrating these layers recovers the stationary two-slice matrix element by $T\Omega=\lambda\Omega$. The boundary weights are exterior-measurable. Every selected link has all six Wilson staples in the exterior, so its conditional density relative to probability Haar is $\exp(x\,\mathrm{ReTr}(uv^{-1})/2)/Z(x)$ with $0\le x\le6\beta$, $v\in SU(2)$. For zero staple any $v$ works. The conditional joint law factors over all selected links at both times. Conditioning on the path links alone would leave a mixture through the missing staples. This use of conditional single-link integration has a precedent in the multihit Wilson estimator. [Parisi, Petronzio and Rapuano](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(83)90930-9)

**How much information one path retains.** At spin $n/2$ the Fourier matrix of the one-link law is $I_{n+1}(x)/I_1(x)$ times a unitary. The formula follows from Haar character integration and the Bessel recurrence, with its continuous value at zero. For $\nu\ge1$, let $\mathbb E_\nu$ have density proportional to $e^{xt}(1-t^2)^{\nu-1/2}$ on $[-1,1]$. Log differentiation of the integral gives $q=I_{\nu+1}/I_\nu=\mathbb E_\nu t$. Integration by parts yields $(2\nu+1)q=x(1-\mathbb E_\nu t^2)\le2x(1-q)$. Hence $q\le2x/(2x+3)\le r$. Telescoping the ratios gives the bound $r^n$. Under convolution the matrix norms multiply, even with arbitrary exterior-dependent fixed matrices between factors. Fourier inversion bounds the uniform density error from Haar by $\sum_{n\ge1}(n+1)^2z^n=u$. Absolute uniform convergence justifies inversion. This sum includes half-integer representations needed for signed holonomies. Independently, any one selected-link density lies in $[e^{-12\beta},e^{12\beta}]$, and convolution with probability measures preserves these bounds. [NIST integral representation](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.32.E2) · [NIST recurrences](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.29) · [Character calculation](../fine-states/README.md)

**From paths to operator norm.** Conditional on $\mathcal E$, distinct paths use disjoint selected links. Thus at either time the density $w_e$ of $W$ lies in $[L,D]$ relative to product Haar. The mixture $w_c$ given $C$ has the same bounds since $C$ is exterior-measurable. For $f(C,W)$ with $\mathbb E[f\mid C]=0$, write $\mu_e,\mu_c$ for these laws of $W$ at the realized coarse data. Since $\mu_e\ge (L/D)\mu_c$, minimizing $\int |f-t|^2$ over complex constants $t$ gives $\operatorname{Var}_e(f)\ge (L/D)\operatorname{Var}_c(f)$. Total variance yields $\|\mathbb E[f\mid\mathcal E]\|_2^2\le(1-L/D)\|f\|_2^2$. Alternatively integrate $f(w_e/w_c-1)$ against $\mu_c$ and use Cauchy–Schwarz and $|w_e/w_c-1|\le D/L-1$. The two insertion sets are independent given this **same** exterior. Their bilinear pairing therefore factors through the two conditional means, yielding $\|VSV\|\le\theta^2$. Positivity gives the coarse coupling by factoring through $S^{1/2}$. No dimension factor indexed by the observables occurs. For polynomial $M$, $M z\to0$ whenever $m/[(1+\beta)\log N]\to\infty$, because $r^m\le e^{-m/(1+4\beta)}$. Then $D/L\to1$.

## A second grid whose physical mesh vanishes

Choose a common partition of the coordinate circle beginning at 1 and using even interval lengths of minimum $b$. Use it on every erased line. Each interval visits at most two nonwrapping pieces of the grade sequence. Each piece of length $k$ contains at least $\lfloor k/6\rfloor$ assigned links in its direction, so $m\ge\lfloor b/6\rfloor-1$. With $K$ intervals, $M=9N^2K/4$. All vertices of the Cartesian partition grid have three odd coordinates. Every edge between its nearest neighbors is one of the erased segments. The theorem therefore includes arbitrary gauge-invariant networks on this offset grid, combined with arbitrary original coarse observables and then centered at fixed $C$. This includes contractible loops as well as winding traces. Path order, bases, and endpoint indices are retained.

For the stated growth condition choose even target $b_N$ asymptotic to $\sqrt{N(1+\beta_N)\log N}$. For large $N$, distribute $N/2$ pairs as equally as possible among $\lfloor N/b_N\rfloor$ intervals. The actual lengths are between $b_N$ and $2b_N+2$. Then $m/[(1+\beta_N)\log N]\to\infty$, $M=O(N^3)$ and the maximum physical segment length tends to zero. The same conclusion holds uniformly over the normalized states of this growing algebra. The estimate concerns **bare path holonomies** and the fine component relative to $C$. A continuum field obtained with additional renormalization or smearing needs its own identification.

## The surviving low-spin space

Transfer all projections by the ground-state unitary when changing between vacuum and Haar coordinates. Let $R$ and $U=Q-R$ be the [previous weighted cutoff](../fine-states/README.md), with $\|USU\|\le\epsilon^2$, $\epsilon=N^{-3}$. Write $Q'=Q-V$, let $R'$ project onto the finite range $Q'R$, and put $U'=Q'-R'$. If $x\in U'$, then $\langle x,Ry\rangle=\langle x,Q'Ry\rangle=0$ for every $y$, so $U'\le U$. The orthogonal sum $Q=V+R'+U'$ gives
$$
 \|R'SR'\|\le\|QSQ\|\le\|R'SR'\|+\theta^2+\epsilon^2,\qquad
 \|PSR'\|^2\le\|PSQ\|^2\le\|PSR'\|^2+a(\theta^2+\epsilon^2).
$$
For the first upper bound add the three positive operators $S^{1/2}XS^{1/2}$ and use $\|S^{1/2}XS^{1/2}\|=\|XSX\|$ for each projection $X$. For the second add $PSXSP$.

**Test that $R'$ is nonzero.** Inside an erased segment of length at least two, change two successive links from $(u,v)$ to $(uh,h^{-1}v)$, for a fixed noncentral $h$. All recorded segment products and all raw coarse products are fixed. A fundamental elementary plaquette through the first link and perpendicular to that line changes trace on an open set of fields. For instance the all-identity field gives traces $2$ and $\operatorname{Tr}h$. This map preserves product Haar, and the finite-volume vacuum has strictly positive density relative to Haar. The trace therefore cannot agree almost surely with a function of $(C,W)$. Explicitly $A=\exp[-(\beta/2)\sum_{p\ \mathrm{spatial}}(1-\operatorname{Tr}U_p/2)]>0$ and $\Omega>0$. The cutoff $\Pi_L$ with $L\ge1$ fixes both the constant and this plaquette character since each of its four distinct links occurs in the fundamental representation. Both inputs are neutral. Since $R$ projects onto the range of $QA\Pi_L$ in Haar coordinates, the two $Q'$ projections of $A/\Omega$ and $A\chi_\square/\Omega$ in vacuum coordinates belong to $R'$. If both vanished, both weighted functions would be measurable with respect to $(C,W)$, after restoring their constant components. Their ratio contradicts the trace test. Thus the weighted residual already receives an input with bare spin at most $1/2$. This does not assign that vector an energy.

Z4’s mass-gap clock and the present fixed physical clock still need a lower bound that controls locally varying fine states. The open norm is now explicitly $\|R'SR'\|$ and its coarse coupling is $\|PSR'\|$. The growth condition, coarse compression, volume limit and identification of the continuum theory also remain open.
