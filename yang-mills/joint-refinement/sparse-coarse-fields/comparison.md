# Moving a coarse Wilson field off a plaquette gate

Work in the four-dimensional isotropic SU(2) Wilson measure with action weight $\exp(\beta\sum_p \operatorname{Tr}(U_p)/2)$ on $N^3$ with even $N\ge6$. The time direction may be periodic of length at least six or in the vacuum at fixed spatial regulator. On one spatial slice freeze the fine links of the straight spacing-two skeleton. The coarse algebra is its full gauge-orbit algebra. A coarse face is bad if its holonomy has round distance at most $\theta$ from $-I$, with $0<\theta\le\pi/4$. A field is clear if all its coarse spatial faces have distance strictly greater than $\theta$. Write $m(s)$ for the number of bad faces on the frozen skeleton $s$.

Assume that on almost every clear coarse orbit the conditional variance lost by the 24 colours of [PR #26](../full-complement/wilson.md), averaged over colours, is at least $\gamma_{\rm clear}$ times the conditional variance of each gauge-invariant slice test. Denote the two quantities on the fibre by $J_s(f)$ and $V_s(f)$. Then
$$J_s(f)\ \ge\ \gamma_{\rm clear}\exp[-24\beta\theta\,m(s)]\,V_s(f).$$
This is a comparison between coarse backgrounds at a single regulator. The assumed clear-field floor remains open at the matched couplings. The physical comparison across two different lattice spacings is a separate input.

**Clearance with at most one change per initially bad face.** The raw skeleton graph is obtained by bisecting each coarse edge. On that connected graph use a spanning tree consisting of the first half of every positively oriented coarse edge and the other half of each coarse spanning-tree edge. In tree gauge all these links are the identity. Each coarse square has at least one edge outside the coarse tree. For each bad face choose one such edge and remove duplicate choices. There are at most $m(s)$ chosen edges. Vary only their second halves.

Order the chosen edges. Each touched face is assigned to the last chosen edge in its boundary. At that step the constraints from assigned faces must hold. No later step touches them. For a given edge there are at most four constraints. As a function of that edge alone each forbids a translated spherical cap of angular radius $\theta$, by Haar invariance under left and right multiplication and inversion. Let $h(u)=(2/\pi)\int_0^u\sin^2v\,dv$. The open ball of radius $2\theta$ about the old value has mass strictly greater than $4h(\theta)$: substitute $v=2w$ and use $\cos^2w>1/2$ for $0\le w<\theta\le\pi/4$. Consequently each step can move an angular distance less than $2\theta$ and clear all assigned faces. Untouched faces were clear initially. The final field has strict clearance. The [geometry check](geometry.py) constructs and clears explicit configurations on four even sides.

**Comparison of conditional measures.** Identify the unfrozen spacetime coordinates at two skeletons in this same tree gauge. Every changed fine half-link meets six action faces. A single displacement of angular distance $d$ changes each affected half-trace by at most its quaternion chord length, hence at most $d$. By telescoping changes, the oscillation over free coordinates of the change in unnormalized log density is at most $12\beta\sum d$. The density ratio therefore has upper and lower bounds $M_0,m_0>0$ with $M_0/m_0\le\exp(12\beta\sum d)$. The same argument applies directly on a vacuum slab extending beyond both neighbours of the tested slice. Its two ground-state boundary factors are independent of the changed skeleton and cancel. The conditional colour laws are local there, and the colour variance can be computed from the slab. This constructs everywhere positive, continuous finite-coordinate versions at each fixed skeleton. [Finite-volume Wilson transfer and slab limit](../full-complement/wilson.md) · [Lüscher](https://bib-pubdb1.desy.de/record/396349)

For unchanged free-coordinate test $f$, each conditional prediction error is the infimum of an expected squared distance. In both fibres the raw predictors for a given colour are precisely the functions of the free coordinates outside that colour. The frozen links are constants. Thus $J_{s'}(f)\le M_0J_s(f)$ and $V_{s'}(f)\ge m_0V_s(f)$ when the ratio is the law at $s'$ divided by that at $s$. The normalization of the density cancels in $m_0/M_0$. This uses the raw colour exterior specified in #26. It also applies on the finite vacuum slab for the local colour variance. [Comparison of reversible forms, Diaconis and Saloff-Coste](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~scs/Courses/Stat376/Papers/ConvergeRates/ComparisonReversMC_DiaconisSalaffCoste_AAP1993.pdf)

**Gauge-invariant tests on the two fibres.** While keeping clearance, the final cotree coordinates can be perturbed by arbitrarily small total distance so two based holonomies do not commute. Such pairs are dense in SU(2)² and there are at least two cotree edges. All skeleton vertices then have the same stabilizing transformation, in the center, since the raw spanning-tree links are identities. Off-skeleton vertex transformations are arbitrary. This stabilizer is a subgroup of the stabilizer of the starting tree-normalized skeleton. A test invariant on the starting fibre remains invariant under the smaller stabilizer on the final fibre.

For a continuous starting-fibre test, define its gauge-equivariant extension to the orbit of the final skeleton by transforming the free coordinates back. Stabilizer invariance makes this well-defined. Compactness gives continuity on that closed subset. Extend its real and imaginary parts continuously to the whole compact configuration space and average over the gauge group. This gives a gauge-invariant slice observable agreeing with the original function of free coordinates on that fibre. For a fixed continuous global observable, $J_s$ and $V_s$ are continuous in raw skeleton coordinates by the compact slab integrals and their positive continuous densities. The raw skeleton marginal has full support. Hence the almost-everywhere clear bound for this extension holds at every strictly clear skeleton. Taking an arbitrarily small extra displacement, then using density of continuous stabilizer-invariant tests in fibre $L^2$, gives the displayed comparison. Centering on each fibre is part of $V_s$ and requires no matching of fibre means.
