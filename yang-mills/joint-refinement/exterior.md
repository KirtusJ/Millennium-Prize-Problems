Fix the box first. Write $X=SU(2)^{3n}$, $n=N_1N_2N_3$, $s=\beta^{-1/2}$, $S=|F|^2/2$ for the spatial Wilson action in C4’s quaternion convention and $T=e^{-\beta S/2}K e^{-\beta S/2}$ with product electric convolution. For every closed $A\subset X$,
$$
 \limsup_{\beta\to\infty}\|1_A T1_A\|
 \ \le\ \max_{p\in A,\ F(p)=0} \exp[-\textstyle\sum_j \operatorname{arsinh}\sigma_j(DF_p)] .
$$
The right side is zero if $A$ has no flat point. All norms and singular values use the product metric with generators $i\sigma_a/2$.

In these coordinates a single normalized Wilson electric link has density $\exp[\beta\cos(r/2)]/z(\beta)$ relative to Haar. At the identity its quadratic exponent is $-\beta r^2/8$ after removing the constant, giving variance $4/\beta$ per tangent coordinate. The flat-minimum argument uses all three commuting cycle holonomies on the periodic torus and all eight central orbits under the same boundary conditions.

1. A maximal separated net on the compact product manifold gives bumps on balls of radius $O(\rho)$, $\rho=\beta^{-3/8}$. Choose bumps whose squared sum is bounded below and normalize by its square root. Bounded overlap at fixed $n$ gives the **vector** Lipschitz bound $C_n/\rho$: at any point differentiate the normalized bump vector in $\ell^2$, then integrate the derivative along a shortest path. The sum over all balls is absent from the resulting kernel error. Schur’s test and the electric second moment bound give $C_n s^2/\rho^2=O_n(\beta^{-1/4})$.
2. In any such ball Haar flattening and the geodesic expansion dominate the electric kernel by an enlarged Gaussian of variance $4s^2/(1-C_n\rho^2)$, with multiplicative error $1+O_n(\rho^2+s^2)$. The product Jacobian and the count of electric factors enter its constant. Taylor expand $F=b+D z+O_n(\rho^2)$. With $\epsilon=\beta^{-1/4}$, the exponent error $\beta\rho^4/\epsilon=\beta^{-1/4}$. The affine upper norm estimate is legitimate for nonnegative kernels by taking absolute values and extending to the full tangent space.
3. Translate away the range component of $b$; its orthogonal component decreases the norm. Each nonzero singular value of $D$ contributes $e^{-\operatorname{arsinh}\sigma}$ in the limiting Gaussian transfer and each zero singular value contributes one. The slack in the variance and potential perturbs this product by $o_n(1)$ uniformly in the ball center. No estimate divides by the smallest positive singular value.
4. Centers of a sequence of worst balls meeting $A$ have a convergent subsequence with limit in $A$. At a nonflat limit the magnetic weights force exponential decay; at a flat one continuity of the finite singular-value sum gives the claimed bound. The gauge-invariant compression inherits the upper bound for this positive quadratic form.

A4’s strict flat minimum then gives a deficit outside a **fixed** neighborhood at each fixed box. This checks the exterior input Z4 named. The shell, local comparison, sector bookkeeping and min–max analysis are separate inputs to the full ordered spectrum. In particular, net overlap, the number of singular values, chart errors and the exterior deficit can all depend on the box. This read-back yields no uniform estimate along the matched $N=5,\ldots,10$ refinement, much less for an infinite sequence.
