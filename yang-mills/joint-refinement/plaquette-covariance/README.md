# Covariance of growing families of fine plaquette states

**G4, for joint review with Z4.** At fixed physical separation $\ell$, the four-dimensional isotropic SU(2) Wilson vacuum admits an explicit family of $\lfloor N/8\rfloor^3$ independent fine plaquette contrasts. The covariance norm, the transmission of their whole span and its coupling to the entire coarse space obey bounds independent of $N$ and $\beta\ge1$. Coefficients may be arbitrary complex numbers. For a family of unfiltered plaquettes of one orientation, a sufficient upper bound reduces to **one observable**: the variance per spatial site of Z4’s sum over all plaquettes of that orientation. The remaining isotropic question is whether that variance stays bounded along physical refinement, or whether the coarse projection removes its growing part. ### The covariance along one coordinate

On the periodic $N^3\times M$ Wilson lattice, take one fixed spatial orientation and let $X_x=\beta(1-\operatorname{Tr}U_{p_x}/2)$ minus its expectation at one time. For any spatial axis, take a linear combination $F$ of translates transverse to that axis at a common longitudinal coordinate. Other transverse coordinates can be fixed or Fourier transformed. Define $c_l=\mathbb E[\overline F\,\tau_l F]$ on the length-$N$ circle. Then
$$
 c_l=c_{N-l}\ge0,\qquad c_0\ge c_1\ge\cdots\ge c_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor},
 \qquad \operatorname{TV}(c)=2(c_0-c_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor})\le2c_0 .
$$

To see the contact and reflection conditions, use the Wilson transfer $T$ in the chosen spatial direction, taking the other three dimensions as its section, including the periodic original time. On the gauge-invariant section it is compact, self-adjoint and positive. If the face lies in the section, the insertion is multiplication by $F$. At nonzero separations its thermal numerator is a sum of $t_a^{l}t_b^{N-l}|F_{ab}|^2$ in a transfer eigenbasis. If the face straddles two sections, insert the one-step kernel $B_F$ obtained by integrating the Wilson slab with the marked plaquette. Interchanging its two boundaries reverses that longitudinal link direction and preserves the real face trace at each transverse basepoint. The marked face is a closed Wilson loop. Independent gauge transformations on the two boundary sections leave the integrated marked kernel invariant by a change of the longitudinal Haar links. Complex coefficients are conjugated in the adjoint, so $B_{\bar F}=B_F^*$. Subtracting the expectation adds a scalar multiple of the unmarked kernel. For $1\le l\le N-1$ the two insertions mark distinct slabs, and the numerator is a sum of $t_a^{l-1}t_b^{N-l-1}|(B_F)_{ab}|^2$ up to an interchange of the indices. The bounded insertion kernels are Hilbert–Schmidt at finite section size. At zero powers use the identity, including on a kernel. The sums are real nonnegative and convex in the discrete variable on $1,\ldots,N-1$ by the geometric-mean inequality, including the zero-eigenvalue endpoint by continuity. Translation gives $c_{N-l}=\overline{c_l}$. At contact use $|c_1|\le c_0$ by ordinary Cauchy–Schwarz. In particular, replacing contact by a product of two straddling insertion kernels would be incorrect. The same proof holds with positive, orientation-dependent couplings: longitudinal temporal convolutions have nonnegative character multipliers and the two section weights are identical. [Lüscher’s positive Wilson transfer](https://bib-pubdb1.desy.de/record/396349) · [Related site-monotonicity results, Lees and Taggi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12666)

At fixed $N,\beta$ take $M\to\infty$ along dyadic time periods. Every covariance just used involves bounded functions of finitely many spatial links at a single physical-time slice. The physical-time thermal marginal converges to the finite-spatial-volume vacuum by the simple positive top eigenfunction and trace convergence of normalized transfer powers. Thus all inequalities pass to that vacuum. Use $K=64(s^2+2s+2)$, $s=8(60.2)$, the [uniform single-plaquette second-moment bound](../low-spin/plaquette.md) derived from Z4’s temporal forest and rectangular dissemination. It bounds $\operatorname{Var}X_0$ for every even $N\ge6$.

### Three spatial differences control a whole span

On one coordinate circle choose disjoint arcs $[a_i,b_i]$ along its positive direction, with disjoint edge interiors. Use the normalized difference column $d_i=(e_{a_i}-e_{b_i})/\sqrt2$. For fixed transverse Fourier momentum the covariance in this direction is convolution by the sequence $c$. The absolute row sum of its matrix on the difference columns is at most $\operatorname{TV}(c)$. Indeed, the entry in row $i$, column $j$ is half the difference of $c(a_j-a_i)-c(b_j-a_i)$ and $c(a_j-b_i)-c(b_j-b_i)$. For either fixed shift, the sum over $j$ of absolute endpoint differences is at most the total variation along the circle, because the arcs have disjoint interiors. Symmetry and the Schur row bound give
$$ D^*G(q_\perp)D\preceq 2c_0(q_\perp)I .$$
Here $c_0(q_\perp)$ is the Fourier eigenvalue of the covariance restricted to one fixed longitudinal coordinate, with the transverse Fourier transform normalized to be unitary on coefficient vectors. Inverting that transform gives the operator inequality with $2I\otimes G_{\rm section}$ on the right, valid on arbitrary coefficient arrays. Repeat on the two remaining axes. Each restriction retains the same single-plaquette insertion in the smaller set of spatial positions, so the one-axis argument still applies. For tensor products of three normalized differences the final covariance is bounded above by $8\operatorname{Var}(X_0)I$. Arbitrary disjoint arcs are allowed in each coordinate, including spacings that do not divide $N$.

For even $N\ge8$ set $m=\lfloor N/8\rfloor$ and take $[8i,8i+4]$, $0\le i<m$, in each direction. Fix orientation $xy$. Each of the $m^3$ tensor columns $v_i$ has eight distinct plaquette coefficients $\pm1/\sqrt8$ and disjoint support from other columns. For $O(c)=\sum_{i,x}c_i v_i(x)X_x$, let $f(c)=O(c)-\mathbb E[O(c)\mid\mathcal A]$. Here $\mathcal A$ is the original gauge-invariant two-link coarse orbit algebra, or the larger coarse-plus-erased-path algebra with disjoint straight segments of length at least two. Both use the fine vacuum marginal. Orthogonal projection cannot increase variance, so $\|f(c)\|^2\le8K\sum_i|c_i|^2$. This proof first bounds the raw covariance; an $x$-translation of the fine lattice need not preserve the coarse algebra.

**Lower bound and independence.** At each marked base $(x,y,z)$ use the upper $x$-link of its $xy$ face on the erased line with transverse coordinate $y+1$ odd. Take a consecutive neighbor on that line inside its recorded segment; choose a neighbor across no distinguished path endpoint. Keep the ordered product of the pair fixed. Every other corner used by the family is separated by at least four in some periodic coordinate. No Wilson action face meets two distinct pairs, every measured face meets only its own pair, and the pair variation fixes all recorded paths and coarse links. Repeat at the two time endpoints separated by $N$ lattice steps. Conditional on all other spacetime links and the pair products, the private variables factor across all pairs and times. The [two-link variance lemma](../low-spin/plaquette.md) gives $\delta=2^{-22}/169$ for each scaled face for every exterior. For complex coefficients variances add with squared moduli. The three differences are normalized, hence $\operatorname{Var}(O(c)\mid{\rm ext})\ge\delta\sum_i |c_i|^2$ at each endpoint. The vacuum cylinder has an extra outer layer at both ends so the ground-state boundary weights lie in the exterior. This proves $\delta I\preceq G_f\preceq8K I$ and establishes the dimension claim.

There is an additional dimension count for any private packing of $k$ individual plaquettes. Its residual Gram matrix has trace at most $kK$. For any $L>1$, at most $k/L$ eigenvalues exceed $LK$. On the complementary coefficient subspace the same two-end innovation gives a deficit at least $\delta/(LK)$, with the coupling estimates below. This identifies a large subspace using the unknown covariance. The spatial differences above specify a uniformly bounded family in advance. A second explicit choice retains a prescribed fraction of Fourier modes of a regularly spaced packing, about 90.7% in one example. [Fourier selection and its infrared exclusion](momenta.md)

### Fixed physical time and coarse coupling

Let $S=(T_{\rm time}/\lambda)^N$ in ground-state coordinates, let $P$ be the centered coarse projection and $V$ the projection onto the span of the $f(c)$. Set $\gamma=\delta/(8K)$ and $a=\|PSP\|$. For **every** coarse $p$ and $f\in V$, each endpoint of $p+f$ has conditional variance at least $\gamma\|f\|^2$ under the same exterior. The two-end transfer pairing factors through the conditional means. Cauchy–Schwarz and equality of the unconditional endpoint norms give
$$0\preceq S_{P+V}\preceq D=P+(1-\gamma)V,\qquad S_{P+V}=(P+V)S(P+V).$$
In particular $\|VSV\|\le1-\gamma$. Normalize by $C=D^{-1/2}S_{P+V}D^{-1/2}$ on $P+V$ so $0\le C\le I$. Set $A=PCP$ and $u=\min(a,1/2)$. Compressing $C^2\le C$ to $P$ gives $(PCV)(VCP)\preceq A-A^2$. Spectral calculus on $0\le A\le aI$ gives
$$
 \|PSV\|\le\sqrt{1-\gamma}\sqrt{u(1-u)},\qquad
 \|S_{P+V}\|\le1-\gamma(1-a).
$$
For the latter bound use $\|S_{P+V}\|=\|C^{1/2}DC^{1/2}\|$, $D=(1-\gamma)I+\gamma P$, and $\|C^{1/2}PC^{1/2}\|=\|PCP\|=a$. This controls mixtures of the new packets with **arbitrary coarse states**. It does not assert a bound for products of packets with arbitrary coarse-dependent coefficients. The deficit is small (about $1.18\,10^{-17}$). No coarse gap or estimate on all omitted states is supplied here.

The proof also works at every integer time lag of at least two steps, so a positive physical time can be fixed independently of spatial volume; $a$ must be evaluated at that lag. The uniform positive spatial sum is orthogonal to each difference column in coefficient space. It is a specific remaining case to study, alongside non-plaquette spin networks and coarse-coefficient products.
