# Coarse compression at equal physical time in the quadratic Wilson vacuum

On a fixed spatial torus of side $L$, refine from spacing $2a$ to $a$ in space and time, with even fine side of at least six sites and coarse side of at least three sites. In the Gaussian curvature model the inherited coarse compression satisfies
$$
 U^*S_a(t)U\ \preceq\ S_{2a}(t)\qquad(t\ge0).
$$
Here $S_h(t)$ is the vacuum transfer semigroup at spacing $h$ on all Gaussian chaoses generated by contractible spatial circulations. The isometry $U$ identifies the coarse theory with the straight two-link observation subspace of the fine theory after normalizing their covariances. The centered compression in the [inheritance criterion](inheritance.md) therefore satisfies $b_n\ge d_{n-1}$ with zero comparison error at every such refinement. Combined with the [24-colour bound](gaussian.md) this verifies both analytic inputs to that criterion for this comparison model at fixed $L$.

**Physical energies.** For dimensionless momentum $q$, set $z(q)=(\sum_i\sin^2(q_i/2))^{1/2}$. The isotropic Wilson oscillator has physical energy $E_h(q)=2\operatorname{arsinh}(z(q))/h$, obtained from its pole $4\sinh^2(hE/2)=4z(q)^2$. Each of the eight fine aliases of $k$ satisfies $2q=k$ modulo $2\pi$. The double-angle formula gives $z(k)\le2z(q)$, and concavity gives $\operatorname{arsinh}(2z)\le2\operatorname{arsinh}(z)$ for $z\ge0$. Hence $E_{2a}(k)\le E_a(q)$ for **every** alias. This uses the same physical clock on both lattices. [Wilson propagator conventions in García Pérez, Snippe and van Baal](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-lat/9608015) · [Oscillator and alias definitions](../fine-states/gaussian.md)

**Identifying the Hilbert spaces.** Use normalized first-chaos coordinates. At each nonzero coarse momentum $k$, straight two-link measurements map into the direct sum of its fine transverse alias planes by the adjoint observation map $A_k$, including the square roots of the fine vacuum covariances. A scalar coarse vacuum normalization can be included in $A_k$ without changing its polar isometry. The principal alias $q$ has $|q_j|\le\pi/2$. Its diagonal link filter $D_q=\operatorname{diag}(1+e^{iq_j})$ is invertible and carries the fine gradient $e^{iq_j}-1$ to the coarse gradient. It induces an isomorphism between the two gradient quotients. Thus $A_k^*A_k$ is positive definite on the coarse transverse plane. The isometry $V_k=A_k(A_k^*A_k)^{-1/2}$ has precisely the inherited range. Polar normalization respects conjugate Fourier pairs and the independent colour copies. The zero coarse momentum supplies no contractible circulation. Fine nonconstant aliases at that momentum remain outside the inherited first chaos.

On an alias plane the fine transfer acts by the scalar $\exp(-tE_a(q))$. All eight scalars are at most $\exp(-tE_{2a}(k))$. Compressing by $V_k$ gives the claimed operator order on first chaos. Let $V$ be the direct sum of the $V_k$. On chaos $j$, use $U_j=V^{\otimes j}$ on symmetric tensors, including the identity on constants. Its range consists of degree-$j$ functions of the straight coarse readings, by the Wick exponential identity. Compression commutes here with taking tensor powers. For positive $B\le C$, the difference $C^{\otimes j}-B^{\otimes j}$ is a sum of positive tensor products with one $C-B$ factor. Summing the chaos inequalities proves the displayed result. This argument also restricts to global colour singlets by equivariance. [Gaussian Hilbert spaces, Janson](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511526169)

As a separate check, the actual centered free deficit at time $t>0$ in the unrestricted circulation space is $1-\exp[-2t\,\operatorname{arsinh}(\sin(\pi h/L))/h]$. Its continuum limit at fixed $L$ is $1-e^{-2\pi t/L}$. This benchmark tends to zero as $L$ grows. The compact interacting theory retains dynamical holonomies and lacks the Gaussian tensor and alias diagonalization used above. Its equal-time-unit comparison and the four-dimensional mass gap remain open. For $L=1$ and $t=1/4$, the largest first-chaos eigenvalue divided by the corresponding coarse multiplier was 0.516787 on fine side eight and 0.785714 on side 24, maximizing over all nonzero coarse momenta.
