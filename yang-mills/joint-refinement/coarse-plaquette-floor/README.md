# Fluctuations of every elementary plaquette after fixing the coarse links

For the isotropic four-dimensional SU(2) Wilson vacuum, there is a volume- and coupling-independent **lower** bound on the fluctuation of every linear combination of elementary spatial plaquettes after observing the original coarse links. This includes the constant mode and all three orientations. The proof also gives an absolute loss of correlation over a fixed physical time. The uniform **upper** bound for the unfiltered susceptibility remains open. We give an [exact positive scale decomposition and an obstruction to inferring that bound from small windows](blocks.md).

Let $N\ge6$ be even, $V=N^3$, $\beta\ge1$ and let the coarse algebra $\mathcal C$ contain the gauge-invariant functions of the straight length-two links joining the even grid at one time. Here $\mathcal C$ means precisely that algebra (or any subalgebra). For each geometric spatial face use exactly one $X_p=\beta(1-\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}U_p)$. At one slice set $F=\sum c_p X_p$, where each complex coefficient is square-integrable and measurable with respect to $\mathcal C$. Write $f=F-\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal C]$, let $p_0$ be any square-integrable coarse observable and put $H=p_0+f$. In the stationary vacuum and for integer time lag $d\ge2$,
$$
 \|H\|^2-\operatorname{Re}\mathbb E[\overline{H(0)}H(d)]
 \ \ge\ \delta \sum_p\mathbb E|c_p|^2,\qquad
 \|f\|^2\ \ge\ \delta\sum_p\mathbb E|c_p|^2,\qquad
 \delta={1\over 31104}.
$$
For one orientation the constant is $1/10368$. The [mixed Haar derivative proof](score.md) gives these constants. The finite-shift argument below independently proves the same inequalities with $e^{-4}/(24\cdot36864)$ and $e^{-4}/(8\cdot36864)$. Take $d=\max(2,\lceil t_0/a_t\rceil)$ for fixed physical time $t_0>0$. The proof concerns the original coarse skeleton. Conditioning on extra path holonomies that use the varied links can invalidate its argument.

### Two links private to one face

For an $xy$ face use its unique corner with both $x,y$ odd and select its two incident face edges there. The $x$-edge has odd $y$, the $y$-edge odd $x$. Neither lies on an original coarse path. Use the same rule in $yz,zx$. Colour by spatial orientation and by the three parities of the canonical basepoint. In any colour, distinct faces differ by a nonzero even translation. Their vertex sets are disjoint on the even torus. Parallel chosen edges of the same direction have bases differing by that even translation and cannot be opposite edges of a unit square. Perpendicular edges from different pairs cannot meet. Thus no action plaquette meets two different pairs. The only face containing both links within a pair is the selected face. At two slices $d\ge2$ a temporal action plaquette cannot meet chosen links at both ends.

Fix the spacetime exterior of one colour of pairs. The Wilson density factorizes over the pairs. This argument works in the vacuum cylinder by leaving the boundary slices outside all varied links. At fixed regulator the boundary ground-state factors depend only on that exterior. Equivalently use the finite cylinder vacuum distribution directly. Coarse products are fixed. Every measured face contributes to at most one pair, so conditional variances of the resulting pair functions add. A coarse summand and the coarse conditional mean of $F$ are fixed.

### The two-link inequality

Orient the selected links consecutively around their shared plaquette, inverting the canonical variable when needed. Haar probability is unchanged. Its half-trace becomes $\Re(uvw)$ in unit quaternion variables, with $w$ fixed. Write $L$ for the log of the unnormalized conditional density on $(u,v)$. The six left/right unit generators are orthonormal for the product of round unit three-spheres. Each varied link belongs to six action faces. Along each of its three generators the second derivative of any incident half-trace equals its negative, including inverses. Hence $|\Delta L|\le36\beta$. Integration by parts on the compact group gives
$$\mathbb E_\mu |\nabla L|^2=-\mathbb E_\mu\Delta L\le36\beta.$$
The set $G=\{|\nabla L|\le\sqrt{72\beta}\}$ has conditional probability at least one half for every exterior. This local use of Haar differentiation is independent of volume. [Wilson transfer positivity](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01614090)

Choose $a,b$ among the three imaginary quaternion units and signs $\sigma,\tau=\pm1$. Set $T_{\sigma\tau}(u,v)=(u\exp(\sigma ha),\exp(\tau hb)v)$ at $h=1/(4\sqrt\beta)$. On $G$ the initial derivative of $L$ along the path is at most $12\sqrt\beta$ in absolute value. Ten incident faces involve just one link and have second derivative bounded by one before scaling. The shared face has two pure terms and two mixed terms, each bounded by one. Consequently $|L''|\le14\beta$ along the path. Taylor's inequality gives $|L(T_{\sigma\tau}x)-L(x)|\le12\sqrt\beta h+7\beta h^2<4$ on $G$. Changing Haar variables in the integral therefore costs at most $e^4$.

Let $Y$ be the entire pair contribution. The mixed sign sum $D_{ab}Y=\sum_{\sigma,\tau}\sigma\tau Y(T_{\sigma\tau}x)$ cancels every term except the common face, even if the other measured faces carry larger coefficients. Its modulus is $4\beta\sin^2h\,|c_p|\,|\Re(uabvw)|$. Writing $z=vwu$, the nine real coordinates have squared sum $3z_0^2+2|\vec z|^2\ge2$. Conversely, subtract the common mean $\mathbb E_\mu Y$ in all four terms, apply Cauchy–Schwarz, and change variables. This gives $\int_G |D_{ab}Y|^2d\mu\le16e^4\operatorname{Var}_\mu Y$. Averaging over the nine choices and using $\mu(G)\ge1/2$ yields
$$\operatorname{Var}_\mu Y\ge e^{-4}\beta^2\sin^4h\,|c_p|^2/9
 \ge \kappa |c_p|^2,\qquad\kappa=e^{-4}/36864,$$
since $\sin h\ge h/2$. The full integration is on original gauge variables. The final observable and the coarse conditioning are gauge invariant.

### Adding colours and controlling coarse coupling

For each single colour, condition simultaneously at the two endpoints. Its contribution to the expected conditional variance of $H$ at either endpoint is at least $\kappa\sum_{\rm colour}\mathbb E|c_p|^2$. Conditional independence across endpoints and $2\operatorname{Re}(\bar z w)\le |z|^2+|w|^2$ give the same lower bound on the transfer loss. Total variance gives it also on $\|f\|^2$. Average these inequalities over colours. Each uses its own exterior and bounds the same left side. This proves the estimates with the finite-shift constants, including for coarse-dependent coefficients. Substitution of the conditional $1/1296$ bound from the mixed Haar derivative proof proves the larger displayed constants.

For deterministic coefficients the residual map is injective and has dimension $3V$. The trace of its covariance is at most $3VK$ by the [single-plaquette second moment](../low-spin/plaquette.md). For every $L>1$, the residual covariance has an eigenspace sum of dimension at least $3V(1-1/L)$ on which its eigenvalues are at most $LK$. Every vector in that subspace has relative loss at least $\delta/(LK)$, even with an arbitrary additive coarse observable in the absolute loss inequality. This dimension estimate does not identify the low-momentum eigenvectors. For the *entire* deterministic residual span a regulator-uniform susceptibility ceiling $\chi_*$ would give $\gamma=\delta/(3\chi_*)$, by the [zero-mode maximum](../plaquette-covariance/pressure.md). For example, if $P$ projects onto centered coarse states, $W$ onto this residual span and $a=\|PSP\|$ for the normalized lag operator $S$, then $\|(P+W)S(P+W)\|\le1-\gamma(1-a)$ and $\|PSW\|\le\sqrt{(1-\gamma)u(1-u)}$ with $u=\min(a,1/2)$. The [positive compression argument](../plaquette-covariance/README.md) applies also to the bounded-eigenvalue subspace with $\gamma=\delta/(LK)$. For random coarse coefficients only the absolute estimate above has been established.

### Cross-check on the auditor's checkerboard

For $xy$ faces select the $x$-links with $y+z$ odd. This avoids the coarse skeleton. In the [single-link proof](../isotropic-variance/README.md) the integrated coefficients appear as $c_xa_++c_{x-e_y}a_-$. Summing their expected squared norms gives $\|c\|^2+2R\Re\sum_{y+z\ {\rm odd}}\bar c_x c_{x-e_y}$ for deterministic $c$, where $R=\mathbb E(a_+\cdot a_-)\ge0$ is the reflected rectangle. The parity multiplier shifts momentum by $(0,\pi,\pi)$. On the half-open band $-\pi/2\le q_y<\pi/2$ that shift has disjoint support. The result is $\sum_q(1+R\cos q_y)|\hat c(q)|^2\ge\|c\|^2$. Thus the auditor's checkerboard constant $2^{-22}/49$ applies on this band. The two-link result above adds its complement, staggered modes and all orientations with the finite-shift constant; the mixed derivative further improves both constants.
