# An opposite-source test for the remaining variance

G4 for Z4. There is a pressure test at the **prescribed** isotropic coupling using two local source perturbations on a single time slice. It needs a bound at one nonzero source strength. In particular it avoids taking a second derivative of any limiting free energy.

For a cube of $v=L^3$ elementary faces of one orientation let $F=v^{-1/2}\sum X_q$. The finite-regulator vacuum is understood, with or without centering $F$. Let $R(h)=\mathbb E e^{hF}\,\mathbb E e^{-hF}$ at fixed $h>0$. Two independent copies give $R(h)=\mathbb E\cosh(h(F_1-F_2))\ge1+h^2\operatorname{Var}F$. On one time slice the two partition-function insertions shift only the marked face couplings by $\mp h\beta/\sqrt v$ in the nonnegative action convention. All other couplings keep their matched values. The denominator is the square of the unperturbed partition function. For finite periodic time take the time limit at fixed $N,\beta,L,h$; the insertions are bounded at each regulator. A uniform ceiling for this ratio at $L\ge\alpha N$ and fixed $h,\alpha>0$ would bound the full susceptibility by $(R(h)-1)/(h^2\alpha^3)$ using the cube zero mode. This is a sufficient test. A rare large fluctuation can drive an exponential moment up even with bounded variance.

For a real residual direction one can instead couple the two independent fine samples **conditionally on the same coarse data**. The ratio becomes
$$R_{\mathcal C}(h)=\mathbb E[\mathbb E(e^{hF}\mid\mathcal C)\,\mathbb E(e^{-hF}\mid\mathcal C)]\ge1+h^2\|(I-P_{\mathcal C})F\|^2.$$
It is invariant under adding any bounded coarse function to $F$. Thus a uniform conditional ratio could suffice for the residual when a large raw mode is predicted by the coarse data. To cover an entire coefficient space one would need a uniform bound over normalized directions, or a separate operator comparison. For a real covariance matrix a uniform real-direction upper bound also controls deterministic complex directions by splitting real and imaginary parts. The archived ensembles provide unconditional observations only.

In the [quadratic Wilson comparison](../isotropic-variance/quadratic.md), if $G_L$ is the covariance of one colour of curvature on the marked faces, its eigenvalues lie in $[0,1]$. Gaussian integration for three colours gives $R(h)=\det(I-h^2G_L^2/v)^{-3/2}$ and $\log R(h)\le 3h^2/[2(1-h^2)]$ for $0<h<1$. This is a finite nonlinear source bound, uniform in $N,L$. For the interacting Wilson measure the generating functions of fixed collections of local fields and the leading term of the homogeneous free energy do not give an order-one error on this opposite-source product. The corresponding Wilson inequality remains open. [Faria da Veiga and O'Carroll](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07376) · [Chatterjee](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01222)

At $h=1/2$ the empirical values of $(R-1)/h^2$ on whole cubes are $0.4910(111),0.4896(99),0.4879(104)$ for sides 6,8,10 at temporal extent $6N$, and $0.4899(128),0.4946(90)$ for sides 6,10 at extent $10N$. These are paired whole-stream jackknife errors. The cosh inequality holds exactly for the empirical law. The reported values are estimates of the source response; finite samples and open temporal boundaries preclude a rigorous vacuum bound.
