# A full low-energy band where the proposed rate can be calculated

**Benchmark theorem.** Consider the massless free real scalar field on a periodic three-dimensional spatial lattice of even side $N\ge6$, spacing $a=\ell/N$ and continuous time. Remove its spatial zero mode before constructing the normalized Gaussian vacuum. At spacing $2a$ make the same zero-mode convention. Observe the field on every second site in each dimension and subtract the spatial average of these sampled values. This is a precise coarse map between the two mean-zero spatial coordinate spaces. It is a benchmark for G4’s H3 and H1. The Wilson observation blocks link products and its vacuum is interacting. [H3](slow-band.md) · [Harmonic lattice dispersion and covariance](https://academic.oup.com/ptep/article/2026/6/061A01/8675224)

**Result.** For every $v$ in the full free spectral band $[0,E_*]$, once $E_*<\sqrt2/a$,
$$\|Qv\|^2\le(7/\sqrt2)\,a\langle v,Hv\rangle.$$
Here $H$ is vacuum-subtracted. The exponent of $a$ is sharp for point sampling when the band contains a fixed nonzero physical momentum at all fine spacings. At any positive common lag the inherited deficit also satisfies $b_f\ge d_c$ for this particular model. Thus the two inputs of the proposed fixed-box argument can hold together. The true gap of this massless benchmark tends to zero when the physical box grows.

**Proof on linear observables.** Let $k$ denote physical momentum, with each coordinate an integer multiple of $2\pi/\ell$. Fourier transformation of the nearest-neighbour Laplacian gives
$$\omega_a(k)=\frac2a\left(\sum_{j=1}^3\sin^2(ak_j/2)\right)^{1/2}.$$
The vacuum variance of a Fourier coordinate is proportional to $1/(2\omega_a(k))$ with a common normalization. Real and imaginary modes are paired by conjugation; the computations may be made in the complexified one-particle space. Subsampling sums the eight aliases $k+\pi z/a$, $z\in\{0,1\}^3$, with equal coordinate weights. Index each class by its representative in the reduced Brillouin zone $[-\pi/(2a),\pi/(2a))^3$. Every shifted alias has at least one coordinate with sine modulus at least $1/\sqrt2$ in the dispersion formula, hence frequency at least $\sqrt2/a$. Deleting the zero class of the coarse observation removes no low nonconstant fine mode: the unshifted zero mode is absent and the seven shifted aliases are high.

In a class containing a low mode $k$, the one-particle projection $p$ onto the observations is the projection onto the vector with entries proportional to $\omega_a(k+\pi z/a)^{-1/2}$ in standardized coordinates. For its unit low-mode vector $e_k$, put $R_k=\omega_a(k)\sum_{z\ne0}\omega_a(k+\pi z/a)^{-1}$. Its lost squared norm is $R_k/(1+R_k)\le(7/\sqrt2)a\omega_a(k)$. There is at most one low mode per class. Different classes are orthogonal, so the same inequality holds as a quadratic form throughout the low one-particle space. For fixed $k\ne0$, the ratio of that loss to $a\omega_a(k)$ tends to
$$\sum_{j=1}^3\binom3j/(2\sqrt j)=(3+3/\sqrt2+1/\sqrt3)/2.$$
Consequently a uniform faster power fails for that observation if the physical band contains this mode.

**Lift to every low-energy state.** In the Gaussian vacuum representation the $j$th Hermite chaos is the symmetric $j$-particle space. The free Hamiltonian there is the sum of its one-particle frequencies, as follows by diagonalizing the independent oscillators. Conditional expectation onto linear Gaussian observations acts as $p^{\otimes j}$ on this chaos. To see the latter identity, condition the Wick exponential $\exp(X(h)-\|h\|^2/2)$ on the observed field. The independent Gaussian component cancels its own normalization. What remains is the Wick exponential with $h$ replaced by $ph$; comparing powers and polarizing gives the tensor formula. A vector of total energy at most $E_*$ lies in the tensor product of the low one-particle spaces because each frequency is positive. The commuting projections $p_i$ on distinct tensor factors obey $I-\prod_i p_i\preceq\sum_i(I-p_i)$. Apply the one-particle quadratic-form estimate to each factor, sum the energies, and sum the orthogonal chaoses. The vacuum contributes zero on both sides. This proves H3 for arbitrary superpositions throughout this band.

**Inherited physical transfer.** At lag $t$ the vacuum one-particle transfer is diagonal with entries $\exp(-t\omega_a)$. Compressing onto each surviving coarse class averages those entries with weights proportional to $1/\omega_a$. Each alias has $\omega_a(k+\pi z/a)\ge\omega_{2a}(k)$: componentwise $2|\sin(ak_j/2)|$ and $2|\cos(ak_j/2)|$ both dominate $|\sin(ak_j)|$. Hence each inherited one-particle eigenvalue is at most its coarse counterpart. At chaos order $j$ the positive transfer compresses to the symmetric $j$-fold tensor product of the one-particle compression. Its largest centered eigenvalue over all chaoses occurs at order one. The two marginal Gaussian laws can have different variances; standardization in each Hilbert space is part of this calculation. Thus H1 holds with $\alpha_n=1$. As an independent check, the full spatial-box gap is $2\sin(\pi/N)/a\to2\pi/\ell$. Taking $\ell\to\infty$ removes it.

**What Wilson still needs.** The proof uses independent Fourier modes, Gaussian conditional expectation on all Hermite chaoses and exact additivity of oscillator energies. None of these three properties has been established for the interacting gauge vacuum. The calculation gives a concrete reason to try the linear power of $a$, and a counterexample to universally improving that power even in a free field. [Reproduction](alias.py)
