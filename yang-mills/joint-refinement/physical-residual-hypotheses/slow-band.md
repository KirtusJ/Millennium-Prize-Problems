# My first Wilson hypothesis to try to disprove

**Wilson test.** A necessary inequality for H3 uses the covariances at $2jt$ and $(2j+1)t$, plus two physical replicas sharing the full coarse orbit at time zero. The spectral filter has an explicit leakage allowance. [Derivation and rejection rule](wilson-test.md)

**Analytic evidence.** For a mean-zero free scalar in three spatial dimensions, point sampling at twice the lattice spacing satisfies H3 on the entire low-energy band, with constant at most $7/\sqrt2$. The squared prediction error has exactly linear leading order in $a$ on a fixed nonzero Fourier mode. H1 holds on the same benchmark at every positive lag. [Proof and limits of the analogy](gaussian.md)

**H3 — energy cost of information lost on a low-energy state.** Fix the physical box and time from S1. For a concrete test take $\lambda_*=3/4$ and $E_*=-\log(3/4)/t$. Write $H_n$ for the physical Hamiltonian above the vacuum. My candidate is that along the matched sequence there is a finite $C_\ell$, independent of refinement, such that for **every** centered, neutral $v$ in the spectral band $[0,E_*]$,
$$\inf_{g\ {\rm coarse}}\|v-g\|^2=\|Q_nv\|^2\ \le\ C_\ell\, a_n\,\langle v,H_nv\rangle.$$
Use units with time and lattice spacing in the same physical units. The exponent one is my stipulation. The measured Wilson data give no estimate of it. If the band is empty at some regulator then the desired clipped deficit already holds there. The constant may depend on the chosen physical time, box and calibrated trajectory.

My motivation is a question about reconstructing slowly evolving gauge-invariant fields. How much of a normalized low-energy state can be recovered by observing the straight links at twice the spacing? The left side is an exact prediction error. A constructive route would exhibit an approximation from those links for every vector in the physical band, bound its squared error by the displayed energy, and prove that the constants stay bounded. The conditional mean is the optimal approximation, so an explicit, less accurate approximant can suffice for an upper bound. Existing results for fixed low-spin observables do not supply such an approximation for spectral vectors. A low-energy state that distinguishes fine configurations with nearly identical coarse data is the main way H3 could fail.

**Conditional consequence.** H1 and H3 alone give $\bar d_n\ge\alpha_n\bar d_{n-1}/(1+C_\ell a_n/t)$. To see this, take $\lambda_*<r<\|S_n(t)\|$ and a centered unit vector in the spectral projection on $[r,1]$ of $S_n(t)$. Its energy expectation is at most $-\log(r)/t$. Positivity gives $1-b_n\ge r\|P_nv\|^2$. Hence
$$b_n\le(1-r)+(C_\ell a_n/t)\,r(-\log r)\le (1-r)(1+C_\ell a_n/t).$$
The last step follows from $\log x\le x-1$ at $x=1/r$. Send $r$ to the centered spectral norm and apply the clipping argument. Dyadic refinement makes $\sum a_n/t$ finite. H3 replaces H2 or H2F in this proof; it does not establish either one. A bound $C_\ell t(a_n/t)^p\langle v,H_nv\rangle$ with any fixed $p>0$ would also suffice.

**Falsification standard.** A positive lower bound for the prediction error divided by $a_n\langle H_n\rangle$ that grows without bound along a sequence of certified band vectors rules out a uniform $C_\ell$. A nonzero residual for a zero-energy centered band vector would violate H3 already at one level. A few measured quotients can challenge a specified constant. Small average energy alone does not establish spectral support in the band. A variational implementation needs a certified spectral filter or explicit leakage errors, and a lower bound on the *optimal* coarse prediction error. The error of a fitted predictor is normally an upper bound on that optimum. Conditioning only on four winding bins loses information and can give a false violation.

**Known-answer check.** In the two-sign example with $\lambda=.9,\mu=.4$, the band consists of $X$. Its lost squared norm is $4^{-n}$ and $t\langle X,H_nX\rangle=-\log(.9)$. With $a_n/t=2^{-n}$ this meets H3. Holding the correlation of $X$ and $Y$ fixed below one violates H3 as $a_n$ decreases even though the full model stays gapped. H3 is a claim about a chosen coarse observation and rate. Its failure would direct attention to another reconstruction or a slower rate before drawing any conclusion about the prize.
