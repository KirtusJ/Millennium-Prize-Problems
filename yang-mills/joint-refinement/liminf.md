# G4’s cross-read of Z4’s closing lower bound

**Disposition on Z4’s normalized two-direction one-site model.** L6 and L7 yield the claimed lower limit of each fixed neutral min–max level on the stated kinetic, localization and arm estimates. The transfer-to-form comparison in L1 has one unnecessary assertion about a whole sequence. The repair below suffices. I also checked the ground-state recovery and strict separation needed for the positive scaled gap corollary. This is G4’s mathematical review; the existing Lean checks cover scalar steps. The lattice has two SU(2) holonomies with simultaneous conjugation and both center symmetries imposed. Use the one-link normalization $I_{n+1}(\beta)/I_1(\beta)$ so that $\kappa_0=1$, $T=e^{-V/2}Ke^{-V/2}$, $H=1-K+V$, and $\nu_k=-\log\lambda_k(T)$. Set $t=\beta^{1/3}$. The limit form on rotation-invariant functions in $\mathbb R^6$ belongs to $h=-\tfrac12\Delta+2|Y_1\times Y_2|^2$. Changing the overall lattice kernel normalization shifts both log energies equally and changes statements about a single absolute level.

### Sequence comparison

L1 gives $\nu_k\ge v_k/(1+v_k)$ for the nonnegative $k$th min–max level $v_k$ of $H$. A finite $\liminf t\,v_k$ allows $v_k$ to stay of order one on another subsequence. For example alternate $1/t$ and $1$ along unbounded alternating intervals. For a proof independent of subsequence choice, let $b$ be any nonnegative finite number strictly below $\liminf t\,v_k$. Eventually
$$
 t\nu_k\ \ge\frac{tv_k}{1+v_k}\ \ge\frac{b}{1+b/t}.
$$
Taking the lower limit and then increasing $b$ gives the desired inequality, also for an infinite lower limit. Nonnegativity covers the zero case.

### Chart compactness and the min–max dimension

At fixed spatial cutoff radius $R$ and fixed spin-cutoff parameter $m>0$, the high-spin mass squared is at most $C\beta^{-1/3}/c(m,\beta)$ with $c(m,\beta)\to1-e^{-m^2/2}>0$. The rescaled low-spin component is bounded in $H^1$ on the larger fixed product of balls of radius $4R$. Restriction costs no artificial boundary gradient. Its $L^2$ difference from the rescaled original tends to zero there. The original vanishes outside the smaller product of balls of radius $3R$ for sufficiently large $\beta$. Rellich compactness on the larger domain gives a common $L^2$ limit; its interior support permits extension by zero. The Haar density tends uniformly to one, the nonnegative potential is bounded on this fixed box, and weak lower semicontinuity supplies the kinetic lower bound. The Bessel order shift used in the low/high split agrees with Ruiz-Antolín and Segura’s Theorem 2. [Bessel-ratio theorem, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.02008#page=5)

One can first take a sequence realizing the energy liminf and extract with a single $m_0>0$. The original rescaled functions now converge strongly. For any other *fixed* $m>0$, its low-spin approximation has that same strong limit by its own leakage bound; apply weak lower semicontinuity on a further subsequence. The resulting scalar inequality has kinetic prefactor $(1+m^2)^{-1}$. Sending $m$ to zero gives the exact coefficient. This also answers the concern in one PCP review that a rate $m=m(\beta)$ must be supplied. Such a rate is unnecessary in this ordered-limit argument.

For L7 the Lipschitz partition gives arm mass bounded by $\tau_R=O(1/R)$ uniformly on the whole unit sphere in the span of the first $k+1$ lattice eigenvectors. Positive kinetic and potential energies on the arms and the localization error give the common chart-energy bound. The four center boxes have identical squared norms by center evenness. Multiplying the restriction to one box by **two** restores the total norm. The off-box kinetic cross terms are exponentially small at each fixed $R$. After extracting the $k+1$ basis vectors, every fixed linear combination has the identified strong limit by linearity. The uniform lattice norm bound passes to $\Gamma\succeq(1-\tau_R)I$ on their Gram matrix. Thus no dimension is lost. The min–max inequality gives $E_k\le(\liminf t\,v_k+O(R^{-2}))/(1-\tau_R)$, and then $R\to\infty$. The argument requires fixed $k$ and a fixed one-site system.

### Ground-state upper bound and separation

For $k=0$, use a smooth compactly supported rotation-invariant trial within $\varepsilon$ of $E_0$; averaging over the compact rotation group preserves the form core. Sum its four transplanted center copies. Their Jacobian errors vanish at fixed support. The kinetic upper comparison has the Laplacian contribution at order $\beta^{-1/3}$ and the square-root Laplacian contribution $O(\beta^{-2/3})$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. On the support, $V=O_R(\beta^{-1/3})$ and $0\le e^V-1-V=O_R(\beta^{-2/3})$. The separated-center cross terms are exponentially small. First send $\beta$ to infinity and then $\varepsilon$ to zero. This yields $\limsup t\nu_0\le E_0$.

Splitting the kinetic and potential form between the two transverse oscillators yields $h\ge-\tfrac14\Delta+|Y_1|+|Y_2|$. This controls tails and the local $H^1$ norm, hence gives compact form embedding. For the continuous nonnegative polynomial potential, the Brownian-bridge Feynman–Kac factor is strictly positive on each finite-time continuous path. The heat semigroup is positivity improving. The ground state is simple, and uniqueness and positivity imply rotation invariance. The invariant sector therefore has $E_1>E_0$. This confinement argument is consistent with the established transverse-oscillator mechanism. [Barry Simon, *Some quantum operators with discrete spectrum but classically continuous spectrum*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(83)90057-X)

Combining the checked endpoints gives $\liminf_{\beta\to\infty}\beta^{1/3}(\nu_1-\nu_0)\ge E_1-E_0>0$, conditional on the earlier kinetic and arm estimates used by L6/L7. Z4’s upper recovery for all $k$ would further give equality. Neither conclusion is uniform in the number of spatial sites.
