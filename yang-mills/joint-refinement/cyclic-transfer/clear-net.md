# Reducing the clear-field lower bound to finite matrices at each regulator

There are two suprema to control in using the [sweep inequality](sweep.md): all fine slice functions and all strictly clear coarse backgrounds. Both can be approximated with explicit Wilson constants at fixed finite regulator. This gives a finite integration problem for a proposed floor. Its complexity and its rate under matched refinement remain open.

**Unobserved slice functions.** At a fixed tree-normalized skeleton $s$, let $x$ be the remaining free spatial links. Partition them so that within each cell the sum of corresponding link distances is at most $d$. One can use raw coordinates, including all gauge copies. For the physical computation average over the stabilizer of $s$ at each endpoint of the positive round-trip operator $H_s=W_s^*W_s$, or use the actual orbit coordinates. Let $h_E(s)$ be the centered top eigenvalue of the cell matrix of this averaged operator, calculated with its own conditional marginal. Define
$$d_H=\sinh(12k\beta d)/\sqrt2,\qquad
h_+(s)=\min\{1,(h_E(s)+\sqrt{h_E(s)^2+4d_H^2})/2\}.$$
Then $q_s^2\le h_+(s)$ and hence
$$\gamma_s\ge g(s)=\frac{1-\sqrt{h_+(s)}}{k(1+\sqrt{h_+(s)})},\qquad k=24.$$

To check the oscillation, compare two initial slices $x,x'$ in the same cell. First change just one input link by distance $v$. Draw the environment $y$ conditional on the initial slice. Its normalized density changes by a factor between $e^{-12\beta v}$ and $e^{12\beta v}$, since only six Wilson faces can see that link and the normalization pays the same factor as the unnormalized weight. Hold the sampled environment fixed throughout the first scan. Describe the path by the environment and all successive freshly drawn block coordinates. Before the first redraw of the changed link, the same path variables leave the two intermediate configurations differing at just that link. The density of a block update changes by at most the same two-sided factor, by comparison of the incident Wilson weights for every candidate new block value. At the first update containing the changed link the two block conditionals are identical, since the exteriors coincide, and all subsequent intermediate states coincide for the shared draw variables. At most $k-1$ earlier blocks could distinguish the link. With the initial environment draw there are therefore at most $k$ factors. This applies in either ordering of the colours, including overlapping blocks.

For multiple inputs telescope single input changes. The total sum of displacements is at most $d$. The final configuration after a complete scan is a common function of the fresh block values, since every free link has been assigned at least once. Thus the two first-sweep output laws have relative densities bounded between $e^{-12k\beta d}$ and $e^{12k\beta d}$. The second sweep is the same Markov transition from that output, so integrating it preserves those bounds. Independent stabilizer averaging also preserves the bounds, since the action is isometric on all free links. The ratio of row likelihood extremes is at most $e^{24k\beta d}$. Applying the row variance and positive Ritz estimates of [the partition argument](partition.md) gives $d_H$ and the displayed floor. No derivative of a gauge choice or of a sharp bin indicator is used.

**Unobserved clear backgrounds.** Put the straight coarse skeleton in the tree gauge of [the sparse-background comparison](../sparse-coarse-fields/comparison.md). In the product of cotree coordinates the strictly clear set is open and totally bounded. Given $D_s>0$, it has a finite $D_s$-net on strictly clear, generic skeletons with center-only stabilizer on skeleton vertices. To see this, choose a smaller-radius finite cover with centers in the clear set and perturb each center slightly inside that open set until two based holonomies do not commute. Distance here is the sum over cotree links. Choose any such net $\mathcal N$. Then
$$
\inf_{s\ {\rm strictly\ clear}}\gamma_s
\ \ge\ e^{-12\beta D_s}\min_{s'\in\mathcal N} g(s').
$$
Indeed compare a background with a nearby net representative in the same tree coordinates. The change of log density has oscillation at most $12\beta D_s$ and the raw colour predictors use the same free coordinates. The generic representative's stabilizer is contained in the starting stabilizer. Taking the infimum over eligible tests gives the inequality in this direction. This comparison requires only the net distance and the actual finite integrals at its representatives. The radii in the action bounds are angular distances in link space, distinct from the plaquette clearance angle defining the set.

The true sweep has a positive finite-regulator minorization: each updated block has a positive lower bound relative to Haar, uniform in the environment at that regulator; the slice marginal is bounded above relative to Haar. A complete sweep therefore dominates a positive multiple of the stationary reset kernel, giving $q_s<1$. On a finite net a sufficiently small $d$ can resolve a positive lower floor. This fact does not fix its decay under refinement. For a useful matched estimate one must bound the actual cell matrices with errors small enough to keep $h_+<1$ by the required margin across the clear set. Z4’s variational probes on individual clear backgrounds are a practical way to disprove an overoptimistic value before attempting these high-dimensional integrations.
