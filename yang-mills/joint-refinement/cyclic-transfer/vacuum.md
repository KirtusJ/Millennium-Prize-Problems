# Finite-period and integration errors for the cell matrices

At finite spatial side $N$ put $V=N^3$. In the usual symmetric Wilson transfer, the two half spatial actions together contribute at most $3\beta V$ in magnitude and the time-like faces at most $3\beta V$. Integrating temporal links with Haar probability preserves the bounds $m=e^{-6\beta V}\le T(x,y)\le M=e^{6\beta V}$ on the kernel relative to spatial product Haar. Overall state-independent transfer factors may be removed. This deliberately crude estimate works before passing to orbit variables. [Wilson transfer construction](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01614090)

Set $R=M/m$. For the top eigenfunction $\phi>0$ normalized by $\int\phi^2 dh=1$, the eigenfunction equation gives $\sup\phi/\inf\phi\le R$, hence $1/R\le\phi\le R$. The top eigenvalue lies in $[m,M]$ by integration of the eigenfunction equation. With vacuum marginal $d\nu=\phi^2 dh$ the one-step Markov density $s_1=T/(\lambda_0\phi(x)\phi(y))$ lies between $R^{-3}$ and $R^3$. Thus this transition contains at least $\eta=R^{-3}$ times stationary redistribution, and its residual after subtracting this component and dividing by $1-\eta$ is stationary Markov. It contracts centered bounded functions in supremum norm by at most $1-\eta$ per step for the original transition. In particular
$$\sup_{x,y}|s_l(x,y)-1|\le u_l=\max(1,R^3-1)(1-\eta)^{l-1},\qquad l\ge1.$$
Apply the contraction $l-1$ times to $s_1(\cdot,y)-1$. At $\beta=0$ the vacuum law factors in one step directly.

For a finite observation slab, let $l$ be the number of time steps in the complementary arc closing the periodic path. Relative to the corresponding vacuum slab, its periodic law has the additional factor $s_l(y,x)$ between the outer slices, divided by its mean. This follows by multiplying the symmetric transfer kernels and cancelling the two ground-state factors. It remains true for slab observations including time links after their usual conditional disintegration. When $u_l<1$, a normalized slab law has density ratio in $[1/h,h]$, $h=(1+u_l)/(1-u_l)$. Fixing a skeleton, a full starting slice, or both and then normalizing still costs only $h$: the factor before normalization was in $[1-u_l,1+u_l]$. Positivity and the finite compact group integral versions permit these comparisons at prescribed boundary fields.

For the [physical two-endpoint matrix](partition.md) each joint cell mass on the periodic geometry is within a factor $h$ in either direction of its vacuum mass. For the [clear-field round-trip matrix](clear-net.md) one can take $y$ to contain only the neighbourhood needed by the slice heat baths. Real conditional integration of this neighbourhood given the current slice and frozen skeleton pays a factor $h$ per sweep. At a fixed environment the block draws have identical local laws in the two preparations. There are two environment draws in the round trip; the conditional stationary starting-slice law contributes one more factor. Thus its joint cell masses differ by at most $h^3$ in either direction. This bound also holds under endpoint stabilizer averaging. Each factor uses the appropriate complementary length for the slab containing the affected faces. No assumed uniform ground-state convergence across regulators enters this calculation.

**Passing a mass error through normalization.** Let $J$ and $\widehat J$ be symmetric nonnegative joint cell matrices with positive row sums. If $J/F\le\widehat J\le FJ$ entrywise for $F\ge1$, their respective symmetric Markov matrices $D_{ij}=J_{ij}/\sqrt{v_iv_j}$ have entrywise relative factors between $F^{-2}$ and $F^2$. Therefore
$$\|\widehat D-D\|\le F^2-1.$$
Indeed the absolute bilinear error on unit vectors is at most $(F^2-1)\,|u|^T D|v|$, and $D$ is a self-adjoint Markov contraction in this representation. Symmetric eigenvalue perturbation now bounds the error in the second largest algebraic eigenvalue by the same quantity even though the two constant vectors in normalized coordinates differ. Insert this error above the measured cell eigenvalue before applying the positive Ritz enclosure. If interval integration gives additional entrywise ratios bounded by $F_{\rm int}$ and its reciprocal, multiply $F$ by $F_{\rm int}$. Ordinary Monte Carlo errors from four streams are not validated entrywise enclosures.

This supplies a finite-regulator alternative to an unknown vacuum boundary function. The elementary factor $1-e^{-36\beta V}$ makes the required period extremely large at matched coupling. It cannot validate time-vacuum accuracy of the measured $2\ell$ or $4\ell$ runs. The observed period dependence is reported separately in [the winding experiment](measurements.md).
