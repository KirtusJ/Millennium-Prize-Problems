# A Wilson bound on the modes missed by a full-skeleton partition

At fixed positive physical lag consider either the fine transfer observed on its straight coarse links or the coarse transfer on all its spatial links. Use the time vacuum at each fixed isotropic regulator. Write $A$ for its positive stationary endpoint operator, $\nu$ for its one-slice marginal, and $\lambda$ for the centered norm in the desired gauge-invariant sector. For the center-neutral sector average the joint endpoint density under independent spatial center twists of the two endpoints. One may also treat the resulting transfer on the raw coarse-link space, where it kills the orthogonal complement of invariant functions. Its nonzero centered spectrum is unchanged. A quotient by the endpoint symmetries gives the same operator on the invariant functions.

Let $E$ be conditional expectation on a finite partition of these coordinates, and let $\lambda_E$ be the centered norm of $EAE$. Suppose that within each cell every pair of configurations can be aligned by endpoint symmetries so that the **sum** of round SU(2) distances over corresponding observed links is at most $D$. It suffices to partition every raw coarse link into angular cells of diameter $h$, taking the product partition and $D=3N_c^3 h$ for coarse side $N_c$. Then
$$
\delta=\frac{\sinh(6\beta D)}{\sqrt2},\qquad
\lambda_E\ \le\ \lambda\ \le\
\min\!\left(1,\frac{\lambda_E+\sqrt{\lambda_E^2+4\delta^2}}2\right).
$$
Here $\beta$ is the bare coupling of the ensemble being observed, including when its observed links are two fine links long. The largest missed contribution is at most $\delta$, and at most $\delta^2/\lambda_E$ if $\lambda_E>0$. The number of cells can be very large. Four bins of a winding do not enforce this diameter bound at a useful scale.

**Wilson comparison of rows.** Write $j(x,z)$ for the joint density relative to raw product Haar. On the fine lattice parameterize a straight pair with product $x_e$ by $(u,u^{-1}x_e)$. Product Haar disintegrates into independent Haar on $u$ and $x_e$, because distinct observed edges use disjoint halves. Changing $x_e$ at fixed other coordinates moves just one fine spatial link by the same round distance. On the coarse lattice it moves one ordinary spatial link. In either case at most six Wilson faces are incident, and each half-trace changes by at most the angular displacement. Place both vacuum slab boundaries beyond the neighbours of the two insertions. No changed link appears in a boundary factor. Integration over unseen variables preserves the multiplicative bounds
$$e^{-6\beta D}\le j(x',z)/j(x,z)\le e^{6\beta D}.$$
This also works at one lattice step of lag, since no link on the other time slice is changed. Independent endpoint gauge invariance follows from the integrated Wilson law. The neutral average acts by isometries on the link products, so each term obeys the same inequalities after alignment. At fixed finite regulator all the density versions involved are positive. [Wilson transfer](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01614090) · [Single-slice local action comparison](../sparse-coarse-fields/comparison.md)

Let $P_x$ be the conditional law of the other endpoint. Its likelihood ratio $r=dP_{x'}/dP_x$ has mean one under $P_x$; its essential maximum divided by its essential minimum is at most $L=e^{12\beta D}$. The row normalizations cancel in this ratio of extremes. If $m\le r\le M$, integration of $(r-m)(M-r)\ge0$ gives $\operatorname{Var}(r)\le(M-1)(1-m)$. Maximizing with $M\le Lm$ gives $(L-1)^2/(4L)=\sinh^2(6\beta D)$. Cauchy–Schwarz now bounds $|Af(x')-Af(x)|^2$ by $\sinh^2(6\beta D)P_x|f|^2$. The conditional variance of $Af$ in a cell equals half its mean squared difference between two independent draws from that cell. Integrating over cells and using stationarity gives $\|(I-E)A\|\le\delta$.

**The missed eigenfunctions.** Compactness follows from the bounded finite-regulator endpoint kernel. For $\lambda>0$, take a centered unit top eigenfunction $g$. Then $\|(I-E)g\|\le\delta/\lambda$. If $Eg\ne0$, positivity gives $A\succeq\lambda\,|g\rangle\langle g|$. Testing on $Eg/\|Eg\|$ yields $\lambda_E\ge\lambda\|Eg\|^2\ge\lambda-\delta^2/\lambda$. If $Eg=0$, $\lambda\le\delta$ already suffices. Solving the quadratic proves the upper estimate. This reasoning also permits raw partition cells that split orbits, when the operator used is the independently symmetry-averaged endpoint law. [Finite matrix stress test](partition.py)

**What this buys for refinement.** Compute a full-skeleton cell matrix on the fine side and any justified centered Rayleigh lower bound on the coarser side. Subtract that lower bound from the displayed fine upper bound and take the positive part. This bounds the actual cross-lattice error at the same physical time using finite integrals. For both signs one can construct the two finite matrices and apply the enclosure to each. Choosing $D_n\le\operatorname{arsinh}(\sqrt2\,\eta_n)/(6\beta_n)$ makes the upper approximation error at most a prescribed $\eta_n$. Its sum can be made arbitrarily small along a refinement sequence. The actual differences between the two regulators' finite matrix norms still require estimates. This removes an uncontrolled spectral truncation at each regulator without giving the desired uniform bound on those differences.

For the side-ten ensemble at $\beta=3.493705679$ observed on a side-five skeleton, taking raw link diameters of $10^{-6}$ radians gives $D=0.000375$ and $\delta<0.00556$. This is an illustration of mesh cost. The side-ten full-orbit cell matrix has yet to be evaluated. A small cell-average error, a uniform bound on each marginal observable, or the sampled mass of typical cells alone supplies no such enclosure.
