# A polynomial mixing floor for bounded-spin functions of a growing set of links

In the four-dimensional isotropic SU(2) Wilson law, freeze any straight spatial skeleton $s$ on a slice. This includes frustrated as well as strictly clear backgrounds. A slice test may depend arbitrarily on $s$. On each fibre suppose it is a polynomial in quaternion coordinates of a set $S$ of unfrozen spatial links, of degree at most $d_e$ separately in each $e\in S$. The set itself may be chosen from the coarse data. Counts and degree bounds below must hold on every fibre considered. Finite SU(2) spins $j$ are covered via matrix coefficients of degree $2j$ in the fundamental link. We assume the finite spatial geometry of [the 24-colour construction](../full-complement/wilson.md), in which each elementary Wilson face uses each of its links only once. Work at finite periodic time or in the fixed spatial-regulator vacuum.

Put $D_d=\sum_{j=0}^d(j+1)^2$, $D_S=\prod_e D_{d_e}$, and let $h_S$ be the minimum, over these links, of the number of colours covering that link. There are explicit positive constants $c_d$, depending only on the degree, such that the averaged colour loss satisfies
$$
J_s(f)\ \ge\ \frac{h_S}{24D_S}
 \left[\prod_{e\in S}c_{d_e}\right](1+6\beta)^{-2\sum_{e\in S}d_e}\,V_s(f).
$$
Here $S$ is nonempty; constants have zero variance. This is a rate on a class of functions uniform in volume and background. No lower bound on the probability of a chosen skeleton is needed.

**One-link estimate.** Given its complete raw exterior, an unfrozen link has tilted Haar law on $S^3$ with parameter $0\le k\le6\beta$. Rotate coordinates to align the tilt with the north pole. For every degree-$d$ spherical polynomial $f$,
$$\int |f|^2d\nu_k\ge c_d(1+k)^{-2d}\int |f|^2dH.$$
Here is a direct construction of the constants. Let $r=(1+k)^{-1/2}$. The radial Haar integral gives $e^{-k}Z(k)\le10(1+k)^{-3/2}$. For $k\le1$ use $e^{-k}Z\le1$. For $k\ge1$, bound $\sin^2\theta\le\theta^2$ and $1-\cos\theta\ge2\theta^2/\pi^2$, then extend the Gaussian integral to the half-line. In stereographic coordinates $z$ about the north pole (omitting the south pole), the scalar part is $(1-|z|^2)/(1+|z|^2)$ and Haar probability is $(4/\pi^2)(1+|z|^2)^{-3}dz$. On $|z|\le r$ the tilted density relative to Haar is at least $e^{-2}r^{-3}/10$. Write $P(z)=(1+|z|^2)^df(U(z))$; this is a polynomial of degree at most $2d$. The perpendicular quaternion coordinates are $2z/(1+|z|^2)$. Rotation preserves the degree and Haar norm.

Let $b$ be the monomial coefficients of $P$, $M_d=\binom{2d+3}{3}$ and $g_d>0$ the smallest eigenvalue (or a positive lower bound for it) of the degree-$2d$ monomial Gram matrix of uniform probability on the unit three-ball. This matrix is strictly positive since no nonzero polynomial vanishes almost everywhere on the ball. The squared integral of $P(ru)$ on the unit ball with that probability is at least $g_d r^{4d}\|b\|^2$. On the whole stereographic space every monomial of degree at most $2d$ divided by $(1+|z|^2)^d$ has magnitude at most one, so $\int |f|^2dH\le M_d\|b\|^2$. Changing variables $z=ru$ in the lower integral cancels the $r^{-3}$ factor. Thus one may use
$$c_d=\frac{4e^{-2}}{10\pi^2}2^{-3-2d}\frac{4\pi}{3}\frac{g_d}{M_d},\qquad c_0=1\ \text{by convention for constants}.$$
Related work on sphere polynomial norm inequalities is given by [Dai et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11854-020-0084-9); the displayed coefficient here comes from the ball argument.

**Many links and the conditional predictors.** The one-link quadratic inequality also holds conditional on fewer exterior coordinates, for a polynomial whose coefficients are fixed by those coordinates: the reduced conditional law of the link is a mixture of the original one-link laws. To replace several variables by independent Haar draws, replace the first, integrate its fresh Haar value separately, then use this mixture observation on the next marginal. Repeating proves the product bound for positive squared tests of the stated multidegrees, also with any remaining exterior fixed. Effective actions from integrating out links need not be Wilson actions.

For a given colour take $T\subset S$ to be the links this colour updates. Conditioning on everything except $T$ can only decrease the expected colour loss. In this conditional variance, apply the Haar replacement on $T$ to $|f-c|^2$ for every constant predictor $c$, before taking the infimum. This bounds it below by the corresponding product factor times $\operatorname{Var}_{H_T} f$. Write this last variance as $\int|f-\int f\,dH_T|^2dH_T$. For each independent integration over $T$, the centered integrand inside the absolute value has the original degree bounds on $S\setminus T$. The mixture argument therefore replaces the remaining links in $S$ by Haar too. Each colour now has the same factor $\prod_e c_{d_e}(1+6\beta)^{-2d_e}$. In the product-Haar orthogonal decomposition every nonconstant component loses its variance in at least $h_S$ colours. Summing and dividing by 24 yields the stated numerator comparison.

For the denominator, center at the **Haar** mean. The supremum of the squared centered polynomial is at most $D_S$ times its Haar variance: the sum of squared values of an orthonormal basis of the product polynomial space is constant by transitivity of translations, and its integral is the dimension. On $S^3$ the homogeneous harmonic space of degree $j$ has dimension $(j+1)^2$, by subtracting the degree-$j-2$ polynomial dimension from the homogeneous degree-$j$ dimension. The ordinary variance under the true slice law is no larger than this supremum. These arguments apply to complex tests and arbitrary coarse-dependent coefficients. The local conditional laws in a vacuum slab have the same six incident action factors.

**Growth along refinement.** On a dyadic sequence $a_n=a_0\,2^{-n}$ with $\beta_n=O(n)$ and a uniform degree cap $d\ge1$, the restricted floor obeys $\log(1/\gamma_n)\le2d|S_n|\log n+O_d(|S_n|+1)$ (with a constant also depending on the coupling bound). Thus $\sum a_n/\gamma_n<\infty$ when $|S_n|=o(n/\log n)$, or whenever $\limsup 2d|S_n|\log n/n<\log2$. This supplies the required rate for all observables in each such stencil class. Linear superpositions with a union of supports comparable to the whole spatial volume can lie outside the class. Projecting the short-time transfer onto its low-energy subspace need not preserve local support or bounded spin. The full-complement inheritance argument still needs control there.

## Two checks for Z4's frozen-orbit measurements

The four-face average in the unchanged sampler uses four free interior spokes. Its three other spatial paths and the transverse reference outside those spokes lie on the frozen skeleton, except that one transport uses the $y$ spoke from $(1,0,0)$ once in each direction. Counting quaternion factors gives degrees at most $(1,1,1,2)$ up to permutation. Each spoke appears in two colours. The relative floor for this nonlinear trial space is consequently at least $c_1^3c_2(1+6\beta)^{-10}/(12\cdot1750)$, on **every** skeleton and even side $N\ge6$. The [rational Gram calculation](local.py) certifies $g_1\ge1/1000$, $g_2\ge1/100000$. The resulting prefactor exceeds $9.0\times10^{-37}$. This provides an asymptotic rate and is numerically weak at the matched points.

For the single elementary plaquette, only colours 0, 1 and 2 can change the mark; only 0 through 3 can change the four-face average. These are the zero-based colour indices in the Rust instrument for the given origin and orientation. The other losses vanish per draw. For each of the three active single-mark colours at least one updated free link occurs exactly once. With its raw exterior fixed, the mark is a linear functional of a unit quaternion with unit coefficient. Its one-link variance is at least $R'(6\beta)$ where $R(k)=I_2(k)/I_1(k)$. Thus $J_s(f_{\rm single})\ge R'(6\beta)/8$ before normalization by the variance. At the side-six and side-eight couplings the two raw-loss floors are approximately .00047188148 and .00043866320. They concern population means.

For completeness, put $p=R',q=R/k$. The tilted covariance has eigenvalues $p,q$ and the Riccati identity is $p=1-R^2-3q$. Near zero $R=k/4-k^3/96+O(k^5)$, hence $p<q$ and $p'<0$ there. Since $q'=(p-q)/k$, at a first equality of $p$ and $q$ their difference would have derivative $-2Rp<0$, excluding contact from below. At a zero of $p'$ its derivative is $p''=-2p^2+6(p-q)/k^2<0$, excluding a first contact from the negative side. Thus $p$ is the smaller covariance eigenvalue and decreases with $k$. This confirms the portion of the two auditors' tilted-Haar argument required here.
