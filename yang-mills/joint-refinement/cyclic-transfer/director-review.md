## Auditor3’s stronger disk probe

On the [four-face SU(2) disk](../sparse-coarse-fields/disk.md) let the intermediate path vertices be $q_j=g_1\cdots g_j$, with endpoints $1,W$. Take $X_j=\Re(B q_j)$ for $j=1,2,3$, $W=-\cos\delta+e_z\sin\delta$ and $B=e_x$. The $j$th adjacent-pair bath changes only $X_j$. Hence the averaged loss matrix $J$ is diagonal and its diagonal is a third of the expected conditional variances. Axial symmetry centers all three marks. Auditor3 proposed an optimized real linear combination. G4 independently computed the full covariance matrix $V$ and diagonal loss matrix $J$, including the middle bath. [Auditor3's proposal](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/issues/1#issuecomment-7117363654)

Write $p$ for a two-link product of angle $t$ and axis inclination $u$ to $e_z$. The two pair factors are $Z(\beta L_1)Z(\beta L_2)$, with $L_1=|1+p|$, $L_2=|p+W|$. Put $R_i=I_2(\beta L_i)/I_1(\beta L_i)$, $b_i=R_i/(\beta L_i)$ and $X=\sin^2t(1-u^2)/2$. First take $p=q_2$. Given $q_2$, the neighbours $q_1,q_3$ are independent spherical tilts in directions $(1+p)/L_1,(p+W)/L_2$. The covariance therefore contains the mean of $Xhh^\top$, where $h=(R_1/L_1,1,R_2/L_2)$. Add the expected conditional variances $3J_{11},3J_{33}$ to entries 11 and 33, obtained from the spherical tilt covariance in #28.

For the middle bath instead take $p=g_2g_3$, $a=g_1$. Given $p$, $g_2$ and $a$ are independent tilts with directions $v=(1+p)/L_1$ and $(1+Wp^{-1})/L_2$, respectively. The middle mark is minus the transverse component of $a g_2$. Its conditional variance given $a,p$ is $b_1+(R'_1-b_1)(av)_x^2$. The azimuthal mean of the squared transverse component of the product of the two unit tilt directions is $2(1+\cos\delta)X/(L_1L_2)^2$, by expanding $(1+W p^{-1})(1+p)$. Thus averaging over $a$ gives $b_2+(R'_2+R_2^2-b_2)2(1+\cos\delta)X/(L_1L_2)^2$ for $(av)_x^2$. The density of $t,u$ has the same pair factors in this representation. These are finite-$\beta$ identities, and lead to a two-dimensional outer integration with one-dimensional radial integrals.

At fixed $c=\beta\delta$, the maximum path has $q_j=\cos(j\pi/4)+\mathbf n\sin(j\pi/4)$ with the axial tilt in #28. Write $s_j=\sin(j\pi/4)$ and $A=\mathbb E n_x^2$ in this limiting law. Then $V\to A ss^\top$ and $\beta J$ tends to the diagonal with entries $(1-As_j^2)/(3\sqrt2)$. The conditional direction is that of the sum of the neighbouring vertices and its tilt strength divided by $\beta$ tends to $\sqrt2$. Off any fixed neighbourhood of the maximizers the probability is exponentially small. The limiting diagonal is positive. Thus the smallest generalized eigenvalue of $(\beta J,V)$ tends to the reciprocal of the top eigenvalue of $(\beta J)^{-1/2}V(\beta J)^{-1/2}$ in the limit. This avoids inverting the singular limiting $V$. It gives Auditor3’s coefficient $1/\{3\sqrt2 A[(1-A/2)^{-1}+(1-A)^{-1}]\}$.
