# An explicit class of trajectories for sharp Wilson contours

The fixed-mesh diagonal uses only the fixed-cell theorem. C4's subsequent growing-box Ward and reflection estimates give a second route with predetermined parameters. A sharp surface has diverging free variance; applying convergence in a negative Sobolev space directly to the surface delta would leave a gap. Instead we compare *absolute moments* with the cutoff-dependent, infinite-lattice Gaussian moments, before Wick subtraction. [Growing-box Ward estimate](../../central-quotients/magnetic-smearing/ward.md) · [Stopped-walk decomposition](../../central-quotients/magnetic-smearing/smoothing.md) · [Moment and reflection argument](../../central-quotients/magnetic-smearing/ordinary-profile.md)

Let $r=1/b$ run through powers of two and $H=\min(L^{1/10},\beta^{1/40})$. Fix an even $d\ge2$. For a fixed finite list of rectilinear dyadic polygons and bounded physical insertion times, all the readings through total vector degree $d$, including their finite-mesh Wick counterterms, have the same separated-time limits as on the diagonal whenever
$$
 H\ge r^{d(3d+6)}
$$
eventually. The square two-point, square triangle, cubic two-point and their calibration-independent ratios all use $d\le6$. A single all-degree example is $L=\lceil e^{(\log(2+r))^2}\rceil$ and integer $\beta=L$. More generally, $L$ and $\beta$ can each grow faster than every fixed power of $r$. The bounds here deliberately retain elementary powers; they give no sharp boundary for the onset of a free sector. In particular they do not transfer C4's smoother-test exponents to a singular surface.

**The tests.** Fill the contours by a fixed finite number of coordinate rectangles with signed multiplicities, subdivided at mesh $1/r$. To obtain such a filling, commute the finitely many axis segments of each physical contour and cancel opposite traversals. On bounded physical sets the number of faces counted with multiplicity is $O(r^2)$, the largest face multiplicity is bounded, and the number of boundary links is $O(r)$. For a real linear combination $g$ of the resulting oriented face arrays with color coefficients, $\|g\|_1\le Cr^2,\ \|g\|_2\le Cr,\ \|g\|_\infty\le C$. Constants below can depend on the finite geometry, degree and coefficients. Root all faces and edges in C4's coordinate tree. In a box of radius $O(r)$ each gauged edge differs from one by $O_q(r/\sqrt\beta)$ in $L^q$. In the boundary product, the linear imaginary link sum approximates $\sqrt\beta(U_C-1)$ within $O_q(r^4/\sqrt\beta)$ by telescoping and the unit-quaternion scalar defect, including reversed links. The same expansion on each four-edge face costs $O_q(r^2/\sqrt\beta)$. Summing the faces cancels internal oriented links. Consequently the difference between the rooted face sum and the scaled contour is $O_q(r^4/\sqrt\beta)$. Products with transported connectors obey the same estimate for each fixed geometry.

**Remove the long-wavelength remainder quantitatively.** First take one scalar array of one orientation at one time. Pick a spatial axis in its face. Write $M=\lfloor H\rfloor$ and translate this array by $j\lceil cr\rceil$ along that axis for $1\le j\le M$, with $c$ large enough that the supports are disjoint and lie on the positive side of the site plane through the root. Use the arithmetic mean of their weights, keeping the color root fixed. Denote its rooted scalar observable by $Y$. Its Gaussian counterpart has standard deviation at most $Cr/\sqrt M$ because the copies are disjoint in $\ell^2$ and $P$ is a contraction. The original array minus the block mean is a first difference with a compact line antiderivative of $\ell^1$ norm at most $Cr^3M$: the mass on each line cancels and the line support is at most $CrM$. The same bounds hold for the positive mean minus its reflected array, after the one-cell shift to the positive orientation. Reflecting the spatial face moves its tail along this same axis and changes its sign. In particular each transverse line retains its signed mass after converting to the positive orientation. For several components choose the axis separately, decompose each difference, and add their Hodge pieces.

Here are estimates for a linear combination $g'$ of these **difference arrays**. Take the stopped walk with $s=\lceil H^6\rceil$ steps and stopping radius $\lceil\sqrt{24s\log(s+2)}\rceil$. The whole construction fits in a coordinate patch of radius $R\le C H^4$ when $H\ge r^2$. There is no wrap for large $r$, since $L\ge H^{10}$. The exact stopped decomposition $g'=dh+d^*k+w$ satisfies
$$
 \|h\|_1+\|k\|_1\le C r^2 H^3,\qquad
 \|w\|_1\le C(r^3 M/\sqrt s+r^2 s^{-12})\le C r^3/H^2 .
$$
Indeed the residual for the unabsorbed infinite-lattice walk is bounded using a first difference of its heat kernel and the line antiderivative. The stopped-versus-unrestricted endpoint error is bounded by twice the exit probability times $\|g'\|_1$. These estimates use the componentwise convolutions of C4's stopped Green kernel.

Put $X=F(g')$, $v=\|Pg'\|_2^2\le Cr^2$ and apply the rooted Haar identity to the polynomial $X^{m-1}$. For $1\le m\le d$,
$$
 \left|\mathbb E X^m-(m-1)v\mathbb E X^{m-2}\right|\le C_d r^{2m+4}H^{-2}.
$$
Omit the second term at $m=1$. For details, the action-score and Bianchi errors in every fixed $L^q$ are bounded by the Ward factors times $\|h\|_1,\|k\|_1$; the frame derivative of $X$ adds the $\|g'\|_1/\sqrt\beta$ term. The residual contribution is bounded by $C_q\|w\|_1$ and the covariance error by $\sqrt v\,\|w\|_2$. Since $\|X\|_q\le C_q r^2$ from the face bounds, Hölder for the polynomial and its derivative bounds the total by
$$
 C_d(1+r^{2m-2})(1+Cr)\left[\|w\|_1+
 (\|h\|_1+\|k\|_1)(R(1+\|g'\|_\infty)+\|g'\|_1)/\sqrt\beta\right].
$$
Insert the displayed estimates and $\sqrt\beta\ge H^{20}$. This calculation needs no bounded-variance assumption. Gaussian integration by parts obeys the same recursion without the error; induction and $v\le Cr^2$ give the same absolute error bound, with a degree-dependent constant, for the difference of the two raw moments.

**Return to undifferenced surfaces.** Let $Z=-\theta Y$ for site reflection in the chosen axis. For even $d=2q$, apply reflection positivity to $\operatorname{sgn}(Y)|Y|^q$ to obtain $\|Y\|_d\le\sqrt2 q\,\|Y-Z\|_d$. This is the signed site-reflection form in C4's proof. The positively oriented reflected mean differs from $Z$ by at most $C_q r^2 R/\sqrt\beta$ in $L^d$, using the one-edge base change and the sum of absolute weights. The difference of the positive and reflected arrays has Gaussian $L^d$ norm $O_d(r/\sqrt H)$, by disjointness separately in each half. Applying the degree-$d$ absolute moment bound to that difference gives
$$
 \|Y\|_d\le C_d\big(r/\sqrt H+r^{2+4/d}H^{-2/d}+r^2H^{-16}\big)
      \le C_d r^4 H^{-1/d}.
$$
Subtract such a mean separately for every scalar component of an arbitrary linear combination. The Gaussian mean removed from each has the same $O_d(r/\sqrt H)$ bound. By telescoping powers and Hölder, the $m$-th moments of the original and difference observables differ by at most $C_d r^{2(m-1)+4}H^{-1/d}$ on each side, for $1\le m\le d$. Polarization yields the mixed-moment comparison for the rooted face tests with error at most $C_d r^{2d+4}H^{-1/d}$. Converting back to contour variables costs at most $C_d r^{2d+2}/\sqrt\beta$ for monomials of degree at most $d$, by the contour estimate and $\|\sqrt\beta(U_C-1)\|_q\le C_qr^2$. For the scalar defect use the exact squared quaternion distance with the same product bound.

In a total degree-$d$ Wick product, each covariance counterterm is $O(r^2)$, and all coefficient products have size $O_d(r^d)$. The resulting absolute comparison error is at most $C_d r^{3d+4}H^{-1/d}$, hence at most $C_d/r^2$ on the stated trajectory. Centering a scalar defect by its actual mean has the same control: expanding centered moments as products of raw moments preserves the degree count. More precisely for a raw term of degree $m\le d$ use the unrounded error $C_d r^{2m+4}H^{-1/d}$; a product with remaining raw moments of total degree $d-m$ costs at most $C_dr^{2(d-m)}$. This also covers the centering of the oriented word. All comparisons here are analytic, with degree- and geometry-dependent constants. The quadratures in this lane evaluate the Gaussian reference kernels; no equilibrium simulation of these extremely large finite regulators was performed.

A subsequent local Green-function comparison improves the sufficient rates for fixed total degree $d$: $L/(r^{3d}\sqrt{\log(2+r)})\to\infty$ and $\beta/(r^{16d}\log(2+r))\to\infty$ suffice. It applies a uniform spacetime second-moment estimate to the residual of the compact lattice inverse before summing sharp surface terms. The reflected-array proof above supplies a separate route.
