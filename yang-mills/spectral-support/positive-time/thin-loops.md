# Two separated thin Wilson loops

Take the finite, isotropic four-dimensional SU(2) Wilson measure and unnormalized fundamental traces $F_1,F_2$ of deterministic closed spatial contours. Each contour uses every unoriented edge at most once. Spatial periods are at least three. The two times are distinct and nonadjacent in either direction around the temporal circle. With contour lengths $L_i$, let $D_i=13$ for a spatial contour and $D_i=7$ if it lies in one coordinate plane. Put $\theta_i=L_i/[D_i(4\beta+1)]$. Then

$$|\operatorname{Cov}(F_1,F_2)|\le 4 e^{-\theta_1-\theta_2}.$$

This extends A4’s one-loop conditioning estimate. The coupling, action, group, trace normalization and distinct-edge assumptions are the same. Classical perimeter estimates concern the expectation of one Wilson loop. Here the quantity is the centered covariance of two separated loops and its behavior under cutoff-dependent normalization. [A4’s conditional link argument](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/tree/7c8a3d695372b87824473bfb6a3ad11d796fa30d/yang-mills/observable-normalization/thin-loops) · [Simon and Yaffe, 1982](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(82)90815-2)

## Proof

1. Join contour edges when a common action face contains them. A spatial edge has four incident spatial squares, each with at most three other contour edges. Its two temporal squares contain no other edge of that contour. The conflict degree is at most 12. Within one coordinate plane only its two coplanar squares can contribute, giving at most six neighbors. For a vertex-simple contour at most two other contour edges touch its two endpoints in total. Only the opposite spatial edges remain (at most four in space or two in the plane). Then $D_i=7$ in space and $D_i=5$ in a coordinate plane are also valid. [B4’s count](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/issues/1#issuecomment-7091215511) · [C4’s count](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/issues/1#issuecomment-7091227230)

   A greedy choice keeps $k_i\ge L_i/D_i$ mutually noncofacial edges on each contour.
2. A temporal face only joins consecutive slices. Under the stated time separation the union of the two retained sets is still noncofacial. Condition on *all* other links. The Wilson density then factors over the retained links since each action face contains at most one. The two ordered loop products depend on disjoint retained sets and are conditionally independent.
3. For each retained link A4’s conditional matrix mean has operator norm at most $q=1-1/(4\beta+1)$. The other factors are unitary. Conditional independence within each contour and single occurrence give $|\mathbb E(F_i\mid\mathcal E)|\le2q^{k_i}\le2e^{-\theta_i}$ for the common exterior $\mathcal E$. This applies also at $\beta=0$, when each retained Haar link has zero conditional mean.
4. The law of total covariance gives $\operatorname{Cov}(F_1,F_2)=\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbb E[F_1\mid\mathcal E],\mathbb E[F_2\mid\mathcal E])$. Centering each conditional mean can only decrease its $L^2$ norm. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the stated constant four. The Lean lemma `exteriorPairBound` checks this last Hilbert-space step with the conditional identity and norm bounds explicit. Wilson factorization, the graph bound and their identification with those premises are analytic. [Lean source](PositiveTime.lean)

**A divergent renormalization can still be too small.** For the same contour at two such times with scalar normalization $Z_k$, the centered covariance has magnitude at most $4(|Z_k|e^{-\theta_k})^2$. This tends to zero whenever $|Z_k| e^{-\theta_k}\to0$, irrespective of the equal-time norm. For distinct contours the product $4|Z_{1,k}Z_{2,k}|e^{-(\theta_{1,k}+\theta_{2,k})}$ suffices. A cutoff-independent lower bound $c>0$ on a renormalized separated autocorrelation would require $|Z_k|\ge (\sqrt{c}/2)e^{\theta_k}$ there. This is a necessary cost. It supplies no prescription that would produce a finite nonzero limit. The result concerns thin contours and their stated separations; spatially thickened or flowed loops have different conditional factors.

**How far the bare spectral weight moves.** Now assume a zero-temperature positive transfer representation for the same centered loop, normalized to unit variance, with spectral measure $\mu$ and time spacing $a>0$. Write $V$ for its original variance. A4’s one-sided conditioning applies even at the neighboring time and gives $C(a)\le 2e^{-\theta}/\sqrt V$. At separation $2a$ the two-sided bound gives $C(2a)\le4e^{-2\theta}/V$ when the slices are nonadjacent. For every real $s$,

$$\mu\{aE\le\theta-s\}\le\min\{2e^{-s}/\sqrt V,\ 4e^{-2s}/V\}.$$

Indeed $\mu\{aE\le R\}\le e^R C(a)$ by positivity, and the two-step version uses $2R$. Along any trajectory with $\theta\to\infty$, A4’s first and second character estimates give $V\to1$. For every $0<\delta<1$, the spectral weight below $(1-\delta)\theta/a$ then vanishes. This locates the escaping weight of this one bare loop. No lower edge for the spectrum of other states follows. [Checked Markov bound](PositiveTime.lean)

## Adverse checks

* **Adjacent times.** A temporal plaquette can couple retained copies of the same spatial bond on neighboring slices. Fix its two temporal links to identity on a single-face test. For the two other links $U,V$ the density is proportional to $e^{\beta\operatorname{Tr}(UV^{-1})/2}$. The variables $UV^{-1}$ and $V$ are independent, with $V$ Haar. Both trace marginals have mean zero but their covariance is $r(\beta)>0$ for $\beta>0$, by the fundamental conditional mean and Haar character orthogonality. Conditioning on a shared face as if the two selected links were independent would miss that term. On a three-slice periodic lattice every pair of distinct slices is adjacent.
* **The covariance constant.** With a uniform exterior sign $G$ and independent signs $A,B$ of means $2/5,3/7$, take $F_1=2GA$ and $F_2=2GB$. The conditional product estimate is attained at $24/35$. This is an exact equality example for the probability inequality. The Wilson inputs have further structure.
* **Link geometry.** Enumeration on complete periodic $3^4$, $4^4$, and $5^4$ lattices recovers 12 neighbors within a full spatial slice and six within the coordinate plane. Across slices, a spatial edge conflicts only with its same-bond copies at the two adjacent times. The general proof is the face-incidence count above. [Exact checker](check_slices.py)
