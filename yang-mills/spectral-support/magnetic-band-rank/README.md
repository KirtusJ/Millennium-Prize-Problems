## A test with an absolute tolerance

Let $C(t)=\int e^{-tE}\,d\mu(E)$ be a positive spectral reading with $0<C(T)<\infty$. It may be the connected correlation of a centered observable. Normalize the seven readings as $m_j=C((j+1)T)/C(T)$ for $j=0,\ldots,6$. In a discrete transfer take $T$ to be an integer multiple of its physical time step. If
$$
 \max_{0\le j\le6}|m_j-(j+1)^{-8}|\le 1/400000000 ,
$$
the spectral measure has support on at least three distinct energies in the **open** interval above. A compact finite-box transfer must have at least three different eigenvalues there with nonzero weight in this observable. This test uses no equal-time norm or information about its possible divergence. A known error on a raw measurement must first be propagated through the common denominator to obtain an error on these ratios.

Indeed $x=e^{-TE}$ and positive-time weighting $e^{-TE}d\mu(E)/C(T)$ give a probability measure with moments $m_j$. Put $a=1/1024$, $b=1/2$ and $g(x)=(x-a)(b-x)$. For $0\le i,j\le2$, form
$$G_{ij}=-m_{i+j+2}+(a+b)m_{i+j+1}-ab\,m_{i+j}.$$
For every vector $v$, $v^*Gv$ integrates $g(x)|v_0+v_1x+v_2x^2|^2$. If at most two distinct transfer values inside $(a,b)$ carried the measure, a nonzero polynomial of degree at most two could vanish on all of them. The integral would then be nonpositive. This also handles atoms at the interval endpoints and a vacuum atom at $x=1$. Positive definiteness therefore certifies the three values. This is the one-variable localizing matrix from the moment problem. [Lasserre, moment and localizing matrices](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2006/12/1549.pdf) More generally, if a localizing matrix has $h$ positive eigenvalues, fewer than $h$ distinct in-band values cannot suffice: their evaluations impose fewer than $h$ linear conditions on its positive subspace.

At the rational moments $(j+1)^{-8}$ the three leading principal minors of $G-I/70000000$ are strictly positive by exact arithmetic. An error of at most $\delta$ in each moment changes each entry by at most $3075\delta/2048$. Its spectral norm is at most three times that. At the stated tolerance the remaining lower bound on the least eigenvalue is $3469/1146880000000>0$. Lean proves the combined measure theorem: after removing any two nominated transfer values, the remaining open band has positive measure. It derives the conclusion directly from the seven moment bounds and integrability of the powers under a positive measure. ## All positive energy intervals

The radial density satisfies $h(E)=J^2 E^3/(6\pi^2)+O(E^5)$ near zero. It is nonnegative and real analytic on $E>0$: compact support makes the Fourier transform entire and its power series and derivatives locally uniform in direction. Since its leading coefficient is positive, its positive-axis zeros are isolated. The limiting density of the centered $Q$ is $\tfrac32(h*h)(E)$, continuous and strictly positive at **every** $E>0$, by integration of positive factors off a null set in $(0,E)$. This conclusion needs the entire covariance, rather than just the infrared exponent.

For each bounded interval with $0<E_1<E_2$, split its interior into any prescribed finite number of disjoint open subintervals. Each has positive limiting mass and hence eventually positive regulator mass by C4’s weak spectral convergence (equivalently use positive-time-damped measures). Compactness of the finite-box transfer means there are only finitely many distinct transfer eigenvalues in the corresponding band at each fixed regulator. Their number seen by this one vector goes to infinity. Cover a compact positive interval by finitely many short pieces to see that its maximum distance to a visible regulator level tends to zero. The largest individual atomic weight there tends to zero as well: otherwise a subsequence of its locations converges and every closed neighborhood of the limit would have a positive limiting mass bounded below by the same constant. The continuum measure is atomless.

There is also a finite positive-time witness for any prescribed rank $h$ in any one fixed band, without measuring an energy projector. Choose a positive base time and step, and transfer endpoints $0<a<b<1$ for that band. Work with the damped measure of finite mass on $[0,1]$. Set $x_0=(a+b)/2$ and multiply each of $1,x,\ldots,x^{h-1}$ by $p_N(x)=[1-(x-x_0)^2]^N$. Their matrix with the factor $g(x)$ uses finitely many positive-time moments. On a closed interval strictly around $x_0$ inside $(a,b)$, $g$ is bounded below and the monomial Gram matrix has strictly positive least eigenvalue because the measure has positive density. On that interval $p_N\ge c_1^N$ for a $c_1$ larger than the maximum $c_2$ of $1-(x-x_0)^2$ outside $(a,b)$. For a unit coefficient vector the negative part of the integral is bounded in magnitude by $h\|g\|_\infty c_2^{2N}$ times the total damped mass, while the interior yields at least a fixed positive constant times $c_1^{2N}$. Some finite $N$ therefore makes the matrix positive definite. Convergence of the finite time readings preserves strict positivity. The explicit seven-reading example avoids choosing this degree.

In particular, if $w_i$ denotes the spectral weight at a **distinct energy** within a fixed band (combine all degeneracies), $(\sum_iw_i)^2/\sum_iw_i^2\to\infty$ since the total mass tends to a positive limit and $\sum_iw_i^2\le(\max_i w_i)\sum_iw_i$. These are consequences along the free magnetic scaling sector at C4’s stated fast bare coupling. They provide no interacting continuum construction, interacting mass gap, or rate of level spacing on an ordinary logarithmic trajectory.
