# Complementary curvature spaces, boundaries and contact terms

On a primal rectangular four-box with its complete boundary let $B$ be the face-by-chord matrix for the additive curl after tree gauge fixing. Its range is the exact two-cochains. A primal plaquette based at $x$ in directions $a<b$ has a dual in the complementary positive directions $c<d$ based at $x+e_a+e_b$. Its orientation sign is that of the permutation $(a,b,c,d)$. Dual lattice coordinates are displaced by $-\tfrac12(1,1,1,1)$ in physical space. For primal side lengths $L_i$ cells, take dual lengths $L_i+1$. Keep the cells of the relative complex, meaning those not contained in the dual boundary. In the compact model fix every boundary link to the identity. A rooted forest through the interior fixes the remaining pure gauge freedom. Its curl matrix is $G$ in primal face coordinates, including the orientation signs.

The dual relative edges are the primal three-cells. Their incidences on the dual relative faces are the primal two-to-three coboundary transposed, up to changes of orientation. Therefore $B^\top G=0$. Contractibility gives $\ker d_2=\operatorname{im}d_1$ on the primal box, so the two ranges together span face space. Removing a gauge tree or rooted forest retains the curl range. Equivalently relative cohomology of the dual four-ball vanishes in degrees one and two. The result holds for all positive side lengths. Under this map the 24 two-faces of a four-cube become the 24 faces incident to the dual vertex at its center.

For positive diagonal $W$ in the common face ordering define the unweighted-coordinate covariances
$$
 K=B(B^\top W B)^{-1}B^\top,\qquad J=G(G^\top W^{-1}G)^{-1}G^\top .
$$
They obey $J=W-WKW$. Indeed, $W^{1/2}B$ and $W^{-1/2}G$ have complementary orthogonal ranges; add their orthogonal projections and conjugate by $W^{1/2}$. At unit weights these are complementary Euclidean projections. Define $\ell_P(w)=\log Z_P(w)-\log Z_P(1)$ for the Gaussian partition function and use the same normalization for the dual complex. Then, with $s$ independent colors,
$$
 \ell_P(w)=\ell_D(1/w)-\frac{s}{2}\sum_p\log w_p .
$$
To prove this, take orthonormal columns spanning the two ranges and write $W$ in the joint orthogonal basis. Its leading block is the primal compression. The dual compression of $W^{-1}$ is the inverse of the complementary Schur complement of that block. Their determinants give the displayed relation. Normalization removes the covolumes of the tree bases. This is Gaussian electric–magnetic duality of cochains. Compact Wilson interaction terms are computed separately. For pairwise disjoint plaquette masks $f_i$ put $w=1+\sum_i t_i f_i$. A mixed derivative using each source once sees a minus sign per source in $1/w$, since all mixed derivatives of each individual reciprocal face weight vanish. For $m\ge2$ the logarithmic contact term vanishes as well. The dual connected $m$-point equals $(-1)^m$ times the primal one. This gives equality of pair covariances and cancellation of the averaged odd cumulants at every finite mesh. It works for arbitrary disjoint masks, with no need to choose momenta or tune a coefficient. With overlapping masks use $w_p=e^{u_p}$ to retain an exact identity: $\{\ell_P(e^u)+\ell_D(e^u)\}/2+(s/4)\sum u_p$ is an even functional. Derivatives in $u$ then include the contact insertions from the exponential.

**Ordinary periodic boundary.** On a torus with $V$ sites, the exact and coexact two-cochains leave six constant harmonic two-forms per color. In the unit-weight Maxwell law with these modes omitted the dual projection, pulled back with the signed face map, is $I-P-H$ where $H_{pq}=\mathbf1_{\mathrm{plane}(p)=\mathrm{plane}(q)}/V$. For disjoint unit masks, write $\mathcal T(M)=s\sum_{p,q,r} f_p g_q h_r M_{pq}M_{qr}M_{rp}$. The paired free triangle is $[\mathcal T(P)-\mathcal T(P+H)]/2$. In particular fixed supports have an $O(1/V)$ defect. A Fourier computation on a $6\times4^3$ torus gives $-0.000318936251994486\ldots$, recovered directly from the harmonic projection. This concerns the linear Gaussian ensemble with the stated flux sector. A compact untwisted torus can also have flat holonomy modes.

**Bulk and twist.** On $\mathbb Z^4$ the exact and coexact Fourier projections span all of two-form space at almost every momentum. The single zero momentum has zero measure. Cube and vertex-star triangles can consequently be evaluated in a single translation-invariant Gaussian law. On the spatially twisted torus used here every adjoint color is antiperiodic in at least one direction. Its allowed momenta all have a nonzero lattice difference. The same exact complement holds at *every* allowed momentum, now at finite volume. Crossing a twisted seam may change a component's sign in the face map. A squared color norm and every closed Wick cycle are invariant under these local changes of basis. **Passage from absolute boxes to the infinite projection.** Let the boxes exhaust $\mathbb Z^4$ about a fixed finite set and extend $P_L f$ by zero for compactly supported $f$. The norms are bounded by $\|f\|_2$. Any weak limit is closed on each fixed cube, and is therefore in the $\ell^2$ closure of exact fields by the almost-everywhere Fourier decomposition. Testing against the curl of any finite one-cochain gives $\langle P_L f-f,dh\rangle=0$ eventually. These tests identify every weak limit as $P_\infty f$. The identity $\|P_L f\|^2=\langle P_L f,f\rangle$ gives strong convergence. This argument controls all entries needed in the finite-support difference formula below; separate convergence of extensive diagram sums is a different question.
