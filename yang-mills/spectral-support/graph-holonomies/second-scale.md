# The second staple scale in the infinite-time Wilson vacuum

Take the infinite-time isotropic $SU(2)$ transfer vacuum on periodic cubic spatial boxes. Then take $\beta,L_{\rm box}\to\infty$. This note uses two specific results from C4: a uniform exponential moment for $Y_p=\beta(1-x_p)$ in all orientations and the fixed-cell, joint moment limit of the rooted plaquette curvatures with three independent color covariances equal to the lattice exact-two-form projection. The one-plaquette mean tends to $3/4$. Unlike the independent finite-circle argument, these inputs allow any spatial side, including odd sides. [Moment bound](../../central-quotients/tangent-curvature/moments.md) · [Gaussian covariance](../../central-quotients/tangent-curvature/gaussian.md) · [Removal of the constant mode](../../central-quotients/tangent-curvature/reflection.md)

Use the path, marked bonds, positive conditional means $s$, and reference $s_0$ from the [deterministic normalization calculation](mean-normalization.md). Let $H_j=\beta(1-x_j)$ and $B_j=\beta(1-x_j^2)/2$, with $x_j=|S_j|/6$. The six oriented incident plaquette deficits bound $H_j$ by their average after multiplying by $\beta$. Convexity gives $\sup\mathbb E\exp(H_j/8)<\infty$. Also $H_j-B_j=H_j^2/(2\beta)$.

### The local coefficient is one half

Condition on the complement of one marked link. If $r=6\beta x$, conditional integration and the exact staple identity give
$$\frac16\sum_{p\ni e}\mathbb E[\beta(1-x_p)]=\mathbb E H_e+\tfrac16\mathbb E[r(1-q(r))].$$
Here $r\to\infty$ in probability. The continuous function $r(1-q(r))$ is bounded and tends to $3/2$. Therefore $\mathbb E H_e\to3/4-1/4=1/2$, and $\mathbb E B_e\to1/2$. An independent check in the tangent field uses the six incident curvatures $F_a$ with their signs for one oriented link. The local limit of $B_e$ is $B^*=(1/72)\sum_{a<c}|F_a-F_c|^2$. Indeed $S_{ea}S_{ec}^{-1}$ has the same real trace as the relative product of their two oriented incident plaquette holonomies based at the common endpoint. The scalar components of those holonomies differ from one by $Y_p/\beta$ with uniformly bounded moments. Thus the quadratic limit and its joint second moments follow from C4's finite-cell result. Each $F_a$ has mean squared norm $3/2$. The oriented sum has mean squared norm 18, since $P d\delta_e=d\delta_e$ and $\|d\delta_e\|^2=6$ in each color. The Euclidean pair-difference identity gives $\mathbb EB^*=(6\cdot6\cdot3/2-18)/72=1/2$ again.

### Below the second scale

Put $l_j=1-q(6\beta x_j)/q(6\beta)$. For each fixed finite $p\ge1$ the single-bond estimate is
$$\|4\beta^2 l_j-B_j\|_p=O_{p}(1/\beta).$$
Here the subscript denotes dependence of the deterministic norm bound on $p$. On $x_j\ge1/2$, expand the two Bessel terms as in the preceding note. Replacing $1/x_j-1$ by $(1-x_j^2)/2$ costs $O(H_j^2/\beta)$ after scaling. The second Bessel coefficient costs $O((1+H_j)/\beta)$. Replacing the log ratio $D_j$ by $1-e^{-D_j}$ costs at most $D_j^2/2$. Uniform exponential moments bound these errors in $L^p$. On the bad event the scaled quantities are $O(\beta^2)$ and its probability is exponentially small in $\beta$.

Write $B=m^{-1}\sum_j B_j$. With no independence assumption on the exteriors,
$$\left\|\frac{4\beta^2}{m}(1-s/s_0)-B\right\|_2=O(\beta^{-1}+m/\beta^2).$$
Indeed $0\le\sum l_j-(1-\prod(1-l_j))\le(\sum l_j)^2/2$, and $\|\sum l_j\|_4\le Cm/\beta^2$. It follows for arbitrary marked locations and $m=o(\beta^2)$ that
$$\mathbb EF=s_0\left[1-\frac{m}{8\beta^2}+o(m/\beta^2)\right].$$
The placement must still obey the private-bond geometry. In particular $\beta\ll m\ll\beta^2$ gives a diverging relative sharp-time variance and an explicit second factor in the mean. Uniform exponential tails also remove the dyadic restriction from the ordinary polar Brownian crossover in this vacuum by a union bound on $O(\beta)$ bonds.

For clarity about physical time, $B_j$ is a real time-reflection-even observable supported on the three slices centered at its spatial link. For center separations $n\ge2$ its connected autocorrelation has the slab transfer representation $\langle v,T^{n-2}v\rangle$ for a centered slab vector and a positive contraction $T$. This follows by integrating its two outer half histories to their boundary slices, or directly by the reflection inner product of a slab with its translate. At separation two the slabs can share boundary links but no interior action. For every fixed integer $n\ge2$ the covariance converges to that of the two translated local quadratics $B^*$. The Fourier matrix of the limiting curvature covariance is bounded and integrable on the momentum torus, so its distant coefficients tend to zero. Wick's formula then makes the limiting quadratic covariance tend to zero as $n\to\infty$. Transfer monotonicity, taking a fixed $n$ first, proves vanishing at **every** growing lattice separation in the joint limit. For different spatial translations, Cauchy–Schwarz in the positive form of $T^{n-2}$ bounds the mixed covariance by the same diagonal quantity. Thus the result holds for any convex spatial average, including $B$. Conditional independence of the two paths, the uniform $L^2$ bound for $B$, and the norm comparison above now give
$$\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(F(0),F(t))}{(\mathbb EF)^2}
=o\!\left((m/\beta^2)^2\right)$$
at fixed $t>0$ when $b\downarrow0$, and more generally when $n\to\infty$. This statement does not supply a rate in $n$. No physical mass can be read from it.

### At order $\beta^2$ marked links

There is an observable crossover in the deterministic **relative mean**. Suppose $m/\beta^2\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)$ and mark consecutive strings, or a union of consecutive strings for which the shortest run length tends to infinity. Runs may be spread across a fixed physical path. Then
$$\frac{\mathbb EF}{q(6\beta)^m}\to e^{-\kappa/8}.$$
To justify the spatial averaging, for any fixed $K$ a run average of length $M$ differs in $L^2$ by $O(K/M)$ from an average of length-$K$ sliding windows, using translation invariance and the uniform second moment. Its centered norm is bounded by that of one such window plus the endpoint error. At each fixed $K$ the latter converges to the norm for the stationary limiting Gaussian quadratic. Its covariance at large spatial separations tends to zero by the same Fourier and Wick argument. Cesàro averaging makes the limiting window variance tend to zero with $K$. First let $M,\beta,L_{\rm box}\to\infty$, then let $K\to\infty$. The result is uniform in the placement of each run. A convex combination proves $B\to1/2$ in $L^2$ for the stated markings.

Meanwhile $\sum l_j=(m/(4\beta^2))B+o_{L^2}(1)$ and $\mathbb E\sum l_j^2=O(m/\beta^4)$. The chance of any $l_j>1/2$ vanishes. On its complement $|\log(1-l_j)+l_j|\le 2l_j^2$. The product $s/s_0$ consequently tends to $e^{-\kappa/8}$ in probability and in every finite $L^p$, since it lies in $[0,1]$. Conditional independence then also gives vanishing deterministic mean-normalized connected covariance for two nonadjacent time copies. This last conclusion allows fixed disjoint-slice separations. A single physical path on a logarithmic bare trajectory contains far more than $\beta^2$ links; it can retain its full physical extent while the chosen marks form increasingly long runs distributed along it. No rate derived here controls the *mean* when $m/\beta^2\to\infty$.

A two-state control shows why the run-averaging argument matters. Let an exterior choose a common deficit $H=0$ or $1$ with equal probability at every mark on both slices, with independent conditional link noise between slices and $x=1-H/\beta$. This has limiting local mean $1/2$ and bounded moments. At $m/\beta^2\to\kappa$ the relative mean tends to $(1+e^{-\kappa/4})/2$ and the connected ratio tends to $\tanh^2(\kappa/8)>0$. The example lacks C4's spatially mixing Gaussian limit and is not the Wilson law. It rules out replacing the spatial argument by a single first moment.
