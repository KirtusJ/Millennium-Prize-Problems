# Coarse graphs, shared words and winding lines

Take a fixed finite graph of coordinate line segments in a four-dimensional physical box or torus. Work on meshes on which it is represented exactly, or move its vertices by $O(b)$ while preserving the indicated rectilinear graph and limiting nonzero segment lengths. Split intersections, changes of direction and overlaps into a common graph. Each independent oriented edge has one holonomy variable; a backward traversal uses its inverse. Treat periodic identifications before counting segments.

Along a single straight segment, distinct collinear links never share a unit plaquette. For the fixed graph one can retain all except $O_\Gamma(1)$ links on each segment. Omit the end links near any meeting vertex. Perpendicular segments can threaten one another only near a common endpoint or crossing after splitting; disjoint segments stay apart on sufficiently fine meshes. Distinct collinear pieces are already noncofacial, and distinct parallel lines stay at positive transverse distance. The same argument uses periodic distance on the torus. Thus all retained links on all segments form a single noncofacial set. Condition on its complement, denoted by $Y$. In particular the unretained links of the paths are fixed by $Y$.

The segment products are conditionally independent because their retained variables are disjoint and the action factors over those variables. For segment $i$, write $k_i$ for its retained count and $\delta_i$ for the [uniform density error](mixing.md). With respect to independent Haar edge variables the conditional density is $\prod_i w_{i,Y}$, $|w_{i,Y}-1|\le\delta_i$. For a fixed graph and $b(1+\beta)\to0$ this converges to one in the uniform norm, uniformly in $Y$. Subdivision, removing unused edges, reversal, and composing consecutive edge variables push the product laws to the corresponding coarse laws by Haar invariance. Hence all cylindrical readings on the rectilinear path system have the projectively consistent product-Haar limit. This identifies the restriction of the Ashtekar–Lewandowski law to this path system. It makes no assertion for arbitrary continuum path embeddings or for a topology on classical smooth connections. [Ashtekar and Lewandowski, Projective Techniques and Functional Integration](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9411046)

For bounded or integrable functions of the graph one must evaluate the **same Haar variable** at each occurrence of an edge. For example, $UU^{-1}=I$ exactly. In the finite-group control $A,B$ are independent uniform permutations of three letters. The commutator $ABA^{-1}B^{-1}$ equals identity in 18 of the 36 pairs and each 3-cycle in nine. It never equals a transposition. In the standard two-dimensional representation its normalized mean trace is $1/4$, despite the zero integral of a single nontrivial character. This tests shared-word accounting; the connected Lie group hypothesis of the convergence proof does not apply to this finite control group. [Generated enumeration](GraphHolonomies.lean)

## Many paths and a fixed physical cross-section

For a growing family with a simultaneous private-link selection, the forward squared $L^2$ density error is at most $\prod_i(1+\delta_i^2)-1$ by independence and unit mass. In particular $\sum_i\delta_i^2\to0$ suffices for joint total variation convergence, without requiring the sum of the errors to vanish. Uniform conditional convergence against arbitrary unit-variance nonlinear tests uses the reverse estimate in the [reconstruction note](reconstruction.md).

There is a simple simultaneous selection for **every** straight winding line in a chosen coordinate direction. Color the transverse lattice sites properly with at most seven colors by the greedy degree-six bound on the three-dimensional transverse torus. At longitudinal index $j$ retain only the bonds on lines of color $j\bmod 7$. Distinct parallel bonds can share a face only at the same longitudinal index on neighboring transverse sites. Thus the set is noncofacial, and every length-$L$ line has at least $\lfloor L/7\rfloor$ retained links. Consecutive longitudinal indices, including across the periodic seam, do not cause a conflict. In a **spatial** slice there are only two transverse dimensions and five colors suffice. With $M$ lines, $M\exp[-2c\lfloor L/7\rfloor/(1+\beta)]\to0$ is sufficient for total variation convergence to $M$ independent Haar variables. This covers a polynomially growing family on the logarithmic bare trajectory at fixed physical side length. For spatial winding lines it also excludes normalized correlations with the other slices. For temporal lines the graph-law assertion still applies.

More generally take any edge-disjoint family of edge-simple paths with lengths $\ell_i$. In the conflict graph of their bonds, face-sharing has degree $D\le18$ in four dimensions. Give every vertex an independent continuous random priority and select strict local minima. The selection is independent as a vertex set. On each path one may first mark at least $\ell_i/(1+D^2)$ vertices at pairwise graph distance at least three by a greedy packing. Their closed neighborhoods are disjoint, so their local-minimum indicators are independent with probabilities at least $1/(D+1)$. Write $J=(1+D^2)(D+1)$. The probability some path receives fewer than $\ell_i/(2J)$ selected links is at most $\sum_i e^{-\ell_i/(8J)}$ by the independent Bernoulli lower-tail bound. If this sum is below one a suitable simultaneous choice exists, fixed before seeing the gauge field. No intersection restriction at vertices is needed. This applies, for example, to the four families of all axis windings simultaneously when the sides are comparable and grow. Constants for the single-direction construction are better.

## Thick blocks with point junctions

The same obstruction applies to a class of **spatially thick, nonlinear** transporters. A coarse path is an ordered product of group-valued block maps $F_j$ joining marked lattice vertices. Each map can use all links in its block and must be equivariant under site gauge transformations, with the ordinary endpoint action. Suppose one can mark $k$ terminal vertices, at most one per factor, with the following geometric property. At each such vertex the incident links used by that block form a nonempty set private to that factor in the whole observable family. No elementary action plaquette and no selected link is shared by two marked partial stars. These conditions hold for a chain of edge-disjoint boxes joined at a single marked vertex when the chosen terminals are sufficiently separated. For a family of paths use a simultaneous selection.

In each marked block, apply a gauge transformation with parameter $h$ at its terminal **only to the block's incident links**. The block transport is multiplied on its terminal side by $h^{-1}$, while every other transport is unchanged. In the product-Haar reference law this orbit has a Haar coordinate independent of its invariants. Indeed choose one incident oriented link as anchor, use its value or inverse as the group coordinate, and transform the others to the frame with anchor identity. Conditional on the anchor the transformed other links have product Haar law by invariance. The selected stars are disjoint, so this disintegration can be performed simultaneously. If a block map is defined only almost everywhere, use an equivariant version there.

Expose all orbit invariants and all remaining links. A plaquette meeting two altered bonds at the vertex is invariant, since the inserted matrices there cancel in its trace. A plaquette with one altered bond contributes a translated Wilson trace of $h$ or $h^{-1}$. At a four-dimensional vertex at most 24 plaquettes can contribute. By the no-common-face condition the conditional orbit laws factor, and each has the same Hessian and supremum estimates as in [the link proof](mixing.md), with 24 replacing six in the uniform bounds. Each marked block contributes one Haar coordinate multiplied by fixed group elements. Thus the conditional density, product-Haar graph law, and all unit-variance decorrelation estimates apply with $k$ marked junctions in place of $k$ bare bonds.

For instance, within each block take any nonnegative weighted sum of defining $SU(2)$ parallel transports over detours with common endpoints and divide the resulting quaternion by its norm. Use a designated path at exact cancellation. This is a gauge-equivariant group-valued map and can use arbitrarily many internal detours. If a physical edge is partitioned into such blocks of physical length $\ell_b$ with a private marked terminal every bounded number of blocks, then $\ell_b(1+\beta)\to0$ suffices for the same Haar limit. The block widths in lattice units may diverge. This result identifies the cost of repeatedly joining at a point. A flow whose neighborhoods overlap every junction, or a diffusion with no prescribed intermediate vertex, does not satisfy the geometric argument just given.
