# Separating the scalar and temporal stress readings with Wilson link stars

The two summands of the electric density can be measured with different combinations of actual cutoff links. With the normalization of the slab result, put $k=|p|$ and suppress the common factor $\mathbf 1_{E>k}/(49152\pi^2)$. The scalar reading has weight $(E^2-k^2)^2$. The traceless temporal reading has weight $8k^4/15$. Their mixed two-point measure vanishes. These are the vacuum free-gluon spectral shapes. The Wilson-regulator argument supplies the observables and the simultaneous parameters on which the two shapes emerge.

**Cutoff insertions.** For each direction $\mu=0,1,2,3$ at site $z$, average $W_e^2$ on the two links from $z$ in directions $\pm\mu$. For a negative link use the corresponding preceding positive link and invert its orientation; all face traces and the conditional squared reading are invariant under reversing this convention. Denote the centered average by $O_\mu$. Define $A=\frac14\sum_\mu O_\mu$ and $T=O_0-A$. All are time-reflection even about the central spatial slice and fit inside the two slabs from $t-1$ to $t+1$. The forward/backward temporal pair interchanges under reflection. A spatial link's two temporal faces also interchange, while its scalar reading is invariant. Consequently the transfer insertions are self-adjoint and the intervening propagation for two such windows is $S^{n-2}$ for $n\ge2$. At physical separation the lag eventually exceeds two. For every such separated pair the selected target links share no action face. Conditional integration gives the covariance of their exterior variances term by term. This uses the original spatial and temporal Wilson weights.

These combinations can also be formed entirely from bounded functions of ordinary plaquette traces. A4 subsequently constructed a common bounded even function $h$ with conditional mean $V(R)$ for $X=\beta\sum_{p\ni e}\operatorname{tr}(U_p)/2$. For a spatial target link the identical six-face Haar identity applies, since the spatial site is interior to the two-slab window and its central spatial weight is complete. For a temporal target the one-slab identity composes with the neighboring transfer. Hence each $h(X)$ can replace $W_e^2$ in the corresponding two-slab kernel before taking the orientation combinations. This transfers the separated spectral results to bounded readings of ordinary trace data at each regulator. Their sharp-time noise can differ. A4's local pair-distance identity and the [uniform point estimate](local-moments.md) apply to every orientation. A spatial link introduces two temporal as well as four spatial faces. The reflected halves and all one-cell shifts have the same separated continuum kernel. To see the time Fourier limit, start from $dG_0d^*$, the exact-curvature projector. Its components consist of second lattice differences of the scalar Green function. For nonzero temporal displacements exceeding the stencil, spatial differences multiply the spatial transform by $e^{\pm i\theta_j}-1$, and temporal differences shift $e^{-n\omega}$ by one step, giving factors $e^{\pm\omega}-1$. Each factor is bounded by $C|\theta|$ on the spatial Brillouin zone. The same $C|u|e^{-ct|u|}$ bound as for the electric case therefore passes these kernels to their continuum derivatives. No finite-box space/time rotation of the vacuum is assumed.

**Covariance between orientations.** At a nonzero separation $X=(t,x)$ write $I_{\mu\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}-2X_\mu X_\nu/|X|^2$. Differentiating the scalar Green kernel gives the one-color curvature covariance on oriented faces $a<b,c<d$,
$$
 \frac{I_{ac}I_{bd}-I_{ad}I_{bc}}{\pi^2|X|^4}.
$$
The local tangent quadratic from one link has coefficient $-1/48$ on the sum of squared curvatures incident to its direction, centered. The two oppositely oriented faces of each pair give the same square. Internal contractions are removed by vacuum centering. For two orientations the color sum and the two cross-pairings give $6/48^2=1/384$. The matrix on two-forms is the exterior square of the orthogonal reflection $I$. For unit directions $u,v$, the squared overlap of the subspaces $u\wedge u^\perp$ and $v\wedge v^\perp$ is $1+2(u\cdot v)^2$. Choose $u=e_0$ and $v=c e_0+s e_1$. The common $01$ bivector contributes one and the two projected bivectors $v\wedge e_2,v\wedge e_3$ contribute $c^2$ each. The parallel case follows by continuity. Replacing $v$ by $I e_\nu$ yields
$$
 \lim\beta^4r^8\,\operatorname{Cov}(O_\mu(0),O_\nu(X))
 = \frac{1+2I_{\mu\nu}^2}{384\pi^4|X|^8}.
$$
Each row sums to $6/(384\pi^4|X|^8)$. Thus the scalar covariance is $F_A=1/(256\pi^4|X|^8)$, the mixed reading is zero, and $F_T=F_{00}-F_A$. At $|x|=t$, $F_T$ is negative even though its joint spectral measure is positive. The zero mixed reading asserts two-point orthogonality.

**Inverse transform and thresholds.** Invert the proposed temporal stress density $k^4\mathbf 1_{E>k}/(92160\pi^2)$. The temporal Laplace transform is $k^4e^{-kt}/(92160\pi^2t)$. In three dimensions the inverse spatial transform of $e^{-kt}$ is $t/(\pi^2(t^2+|x|^2)^2)$. Applying the spatial Laplacian twice therefore gives
$$
 \frac{\Delta_x^2(t^2+|x|^2)^{-2}}{92160\pi^4}
 =\frac{3t^4-10t^2s+3s^2}{768\pi^4(t^2+s)^6}=F_T(t,x),
 \qquad s=|x|^2 .
$$
For radial functions of $s$, the Laplacian is $6\partial_s+4s\partial_s^2$. Subtraction from the full [electric density](spectrum.md) gives $\rho_A$. The joint-measure argument applies to each symmetrized probe with exponent $n-2$, and to their mixed measure by polarization. For mixed spatial phases one can take complex combinations of the insertion vectors. At $k>0$ the scalar density vanishes quadratically on approaching the threshold from above. The temporal stress density is constant in $E$ above threshold. At $k=0$ the latter vanishes identically. These pointwise fiber statements concern the limiting density. Identification of a renormalized stress tensor at finite cutoff requires additional normalization and Ward identities.

**Literature and normalization.** Meyer evaluated the zero-temperature finite-momentum spectral functions of pure gauge theory at leading perturbative order. His equation (2.19) gives the $00,00$ coefficient $2d_A/[15(4\pi)^2]$ on $k^4$. For $d_A=3$, division by $48^2$ gives precisely $1/(92160\pi^2)$. Equation (2.17) gives the scalar coefficient on $(E^2-k^2)^2$. Removing the squared trace-anomaly prefactor displayed there and dividing $d_A/[4(4\pi)^2]$ by $48^2$ gives $1/(49152\pi^2)$. The laboratory scalar here is the action quadratic with that fixed normalization. The spectral shapes and their free normalization thus have an independent published derivation. Our cutoff-specific conclusions concern the squared conditional link innovations, the common rate sufficient for their separated correlations, and joint convergence of the measures visible to those insertions.
