# A separated free-field null test and a nonzero fourth-order control

Write $A$ for the centered scalar orientation average and $T_\mu=O_\mu-A$ for any traceless diagonal orientation. At pairwise distinct physical insertion times, **every limiting moment containing an odd number of scalar factors and any number of the traceless factors vanishes** on the sufficiently weak trajectories below. In particular the scalar three-point reading is zero. A nonzero fourth cumulant gives an internal check that the scalar signal has survived the limit.

For a prescribed product of $m\ge2$ local insertions take $h\gg r^{2m}$ sufficiently slowly, with $L/(r^{6m}\sqrt{\log(2+r)})\to\infty$ and $\beta/(r^{32m}\log(2+r))\to\infty$. Multiply each centered reading by $\beta^2r^4$. The product of the $m$ bounded readings from A4's reconstruction can replace the product of the squared innovations whenever their two-slab windows have disjoint interiors. This follows by composing the equal boundary kernels, or by conditioning on all targeted links in each term in the expansion. The polynomial radial-variance expansion used for two slabs also holds in each fixed $L^q$: on $R\ge3\beta$ its remainder is a fixed polynomial in the local deficit divided by $\beta$, and the complement has exponential probability. Uniform local moments then control every product. The [point estimate](local-moments.md) has the same order for degree $2m$, with a degree-dependent constant. The total Wick comparison error after physical scaling is $O_m(r^{4m}(h^{-2}+h^2/\sqrt\beta))=o(1)$. At each vertex actual centering removes its internal pairing in the Gaussian comparison. The cross-contractions have finite physical limits.

**Duality at separated points.** On Euclidean oriented two-forms let $J$ denote the Hodge star, with $J^2=1$. The curvature kernel for displacement $X\ne0$ is proportional to $\bigwedge^2 I(X)$, where $I(X)$ is an orientation-reversing orthogonal transformation. Thus $J$ anticommutes with every cross-kernel. The bivector coefficient for $A$ is a scalar multiple of the identity. The coefficient for $T_\mu$ is proportional to $P_\mu-\frac12 I$, where $P_\mu$ selects faces containing $\mu$. The Hodge star exchanges such faces with their complements, so it anticommutes with that coefficient. A Wick graph of centered quadratic vertices is a disjoint union of cycles, allowing a double edge for a pair. A cycle with $l$ vertices and $b$ traceless coefficients accumulates sign $(-1)^{l+b}$ upon Hodge conjugation of its matrix trace. This equals $(-1)^{\#\text{ scalar vertices in the cycle}}$. A cycle with an odd scalar count therefore has zero trace. With an odd total there must be such a cycle. This proof concerns separated correlations and uses no duality of the full Wilson action. The older conformal-field literature already discusses electric–magnetic duality and odd correlations of the Maxwell Lagrangian. [Nikolov, Stanev and Todorov, especially §4](https://cds.cern.ch/record/617903/files/0305200.pdf)

**Positive controls.** Take four sites on one time axis with distinct times, and let $C_{ij}$ denote the scalar covariance at sites $i,j$. Let $\kappa_4$ be the fourth centered moment minus the three products of pair covariances. There are three unoriented cycles through the four labeled vertices. Then
$$
 \frac{\kappa_4(A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4)}
 {\sum_{(ijkl)\,{\rm cycles}}\sqrt{C_{ij} C_{jk} C_{kl} C_{li}}}
 \ \longrightarrow\ \frac29 .
$$
For temporal collinear separations each cross-kernel is a positive scalar times the same diagonal matrix of three electric minus signs and three magnetic plus signs. Include the three independent colors to get $N=18$ scalar components. On an even cycle the signs cancel. Each of its sixteen labeled-slot assignments has color and face weight $N$, and a two-point pairing has weight $2N$. Thus each cycle gives $16N/(2N)^2=2/9$ in the displayed normalization. In contrast the pure electric $O_0$ has nine components, with a negative cross-kernel and the negative sign of the radial variance coefficient. Its centered triangle divided by the square root of its three pair covariances tends to $+\sqrt{8/9}$, from eight assignments. All three ratios (zero, $2/9$, and $\sqrt{8/9}$) can be formed from centered, bounded ordinary-trace functions. No sharp-time second moment or bare amplitude occurs in their definitions.

**An interacting comparison with a specified coefficient.** A perturbative short-distance target exists for the scalar null test. Fix the sign by using $S=-A$, whose free scalar quadratic has positive coefficient. For the canonically normalized continuum $F^2$ used by Becchetti and Bochicchio, the identity coefficient of its two-point function at leading order is $48d_A/(\pi^4 |x|^8)$. At $x\ne0$ the scalar $F^2$ coefficient in $F^2(x)F^2(0)$ begins with $4\beta_0g^2/(\pi^2 |x|^4)$ in their coupling convention. The contact distribution in that OPE is a separate term. Project the close pair onto its scalar part by spherical averaging around the origin, take the third operator farther away, and normalize by the square root of the three two-point functions. At leading order in the squeezed-distance expansion the scalar Wilson coefficient divided by the square root of the identity coefficient is
$$
 \frac{\beta_0 g^2}{\sqrt{3d_A}}
 \quad\text{and hence}\quad \frac{\beta_0g^2}{3}\ \text{for }SU(2).
$$
This is a perturbative continuum comparison for the scalar component. The short-pair and long-pair distances should both be perturbative and the expansion must be used where its scale logarithms are controlled. Powers of the ratio of distances and higher perturbative orders are omitted. Identity contributions are removed by connected centering, and angular projection matters for separating the spin-two channel. Finite positive rescalings of $S$ cancel from the ratio. Matching this prediction to the bounded Wilson insertion on a trajectory that retains a renormalized interaction requires control of operator matching, cutoff errors smaller than the signal, and the order of the continuum and short-distance limits. The estimates above prove the free null result; they do not resolve the term of relative order $g^2$ in the regulator. [Becchetti and Bochicchio, JHEP 03 (2019) 088, equations (3.4)–(3.7), (4.5) and (4.9)](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP03(2019)088)
