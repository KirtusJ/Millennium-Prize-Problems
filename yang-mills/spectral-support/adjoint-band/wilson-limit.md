# The same adjoint witness on a simultaneous Wilson trajectory

Fix the spatial spacing, the two Hamiltonian coefficients and a physical time $\tau>0$. Let $n$ index a subsequence of growing periodic boxes along which the centered Hamiltonian adjoint measures converge weakly. At box $n$ use the dyadic temporal spacing $a_j=\tau 2^{-j}$. Divide the positive Wilson transfer by its largest eigenvalue and call the resulting positive contraction $T_{n,j}$. The adjoint test vector is centered in its own transfer vacuum. B4’s fixed-box convergence of these vacua and bounded-observable correlations applies to the pullback of this test from $SO(3)$. ## Choosing the spacings

Measure the positive contraction $T_{n,j}^{\,2^j}$ in the test vector, giving a finite scalar measure $\eta_{n,j}$ on $[0,1]$. The common coordinate is $u=e^{-\tau E}$. For every integer $k\ge0$, its $k$-th moment converges, as $j\to\infty$ at fixed $n$, to the $k$-th moment of the pushforward Hamiltonian measure $\eta_n$. The case $k=0$ uses convergence in norm of the fixed-box vacuum. A zero eigenvalue of a cutoff transfer is allowed.

Enumerate the moment readings, including the zeroth, and any additional countable family of bounded observable readings whose finite-volume Hamiltonian values converge on the chosen subsequence. For every $n$ choose $J(n)\ge n$ so that all $j\ge J(n)$ approximate the first $n+1$ finite-box readings within $1/(n+1)$. Each fixed reading on any eventually finer choice $j(n)\ge J(n)$ converges to its Hamiltonian volume limit by the triangle inequality. The Lean theorem takes these two ordinary convergences as its premises. For several bounded observables, passage to one common subsequence can also be obtained by repeated subsequence extraction and diagonal selection. [Formal selection and control](AdjointBand.lean)

All polynomial integrals therefore converge to those of the limiting $\eta$. The zeroth moments are bounded and polynomials are uniformly dense in $C([0,1])$, so $\eta_{n,j(n)}\Rightarrow\eta$. As a pushforward from finite Hamiltonian energies, $\eta$ has no atom at $u=0$. In particular the exact limiting compact band $[e^{-\tau U},e^{-\tau L}]$ carries mass at least $w$. For any $0<L'<L\le U<U'<\infty$, the **open** transfer window $(e^{-\tau U'},e^{-\tau L'})$ has cutoff mass with liminf at least $w$ by Portmanteau. An exact endpoint may carry an atom, so this last cutoff assertion uses slack on both sides. The selected path has no stated rate of temporal refinement. The positive one-atom array $\eta_{n,j}=\delta_{1/2}$ for $j>n$ and $\delta_1$ for $j\le n$ has identical row limits but gives $\delta_1$ along the prescribed $j=n$. B4 now gives an explicit alternative at fixed mesh when the Hamiltonian gap is uniformly positive. With fixed coefficients and spatial side $N$, $aN^6\to0$ suffices there for true-vacuum correlations. The general quantitative estimate also applies to shrinking gaps if the vacuum approximation error is controlled relative to each finite-box gap. B4 subsequently supplied a finite-box heat-kernel lower bound $d_n$ and an explicit spacing at arbitrary finite coupling. Rounding that spacing down to a dyadic subdivision preserves its upper bounds. Applied to this bounded adjoint probe, it carries the same window and filtered correlations along a specified temporal path. The abstract selection above also applies when only row convergence is available. ## Physical time readings

For a physical time $t>0$, an actual Wilson reading at $\lfloor t/a_j\rfloor$ steps integrates $u^{\,2^{-j}\lfloor t/a_j\rfloor}$. These functions converge uniformly on $[0,1]$ to $u^{t/\tau}$ because the exponents stay in a compact subinterval of $(0,\infty)$. At zero time the reading is total mass. Hence the unfiltered readings converge pointwise, and uniformly on bounded physical-time intervals. They are monotone and the limiting Laplace transform is continuous, so a finite time partition controls the whole interval.

There is also uniform **absolute** convergence on the entire half-line for the filtered readings. Fix a physical $s>0$, put $m=\lfloor t/a_j\rfloor$ and $h=\lfloor s/a_j\rfloor$, and take transfer readings at $m,m+h,m+2h$. Their second difference is the integral of the nonnegative multiplier $u^{m2^{-j}}(1-u^{h2^{-j}})^2$. It decreases with $t$. Pointwise convergence follows as above, including at zero. The limiting multiplier kills $u=1$, so the limiting difference tends to zero at late time, even with an arbitrary vacuum atom. A finite partition on $[0,T]$ and monotonicity beyond $T$ now give uniform absolute convergence. Equivalently, for $m>0$ and mass at most four the cutoff tail is at most $16s^2/[e^2(ma_j)^2]$, using $(1-e^{-y})^2\le y^2$ and $\sup_{v\ge0}v^2e^{-v}=4/e^2$. A zero transfer eigenvalue contributes zero at these positive step counts.

The limiting filtered readings obey the [positive band floor](README.md). Uniform *absolute* error can still exceed an exponentially small signal at late times. A common physical exponential rate on a dense family of states, as required for a mass gap, needs additional estimates. Spatial spacing remains fixed throughout.
