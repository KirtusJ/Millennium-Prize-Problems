# A positive discrepancy in the isotropic Wilson vacuum

Take periodic cubic space of even side $L\ge4$, infinite discrete time, and the four-dimensional $SU(2)$ Wilson measure at fixed finite $\beta\ge0$. Use unit quaternions with their round metric and the action $\beta\sum_p(1-\operatorname{Re}U_p)$. Write $\Phi_u$ for negative gradient flow of the unit-coupling **spatial** plaquette action. At one fixed based spatial $12$ plaquette define
$$
 H(U)=\tfrac12|\operatorname{Im}U_p|^2,\qquad
 J_\mu(U)=\tfrac12\left|\int\operatorname{Im}(\Phi_u U)_p\,d\mu(u)\right|^2 .
$$
Here $\mu$ is any fixed probability on $[0,\infty)$, including the free first-passage law. Gauge transformations conjugate each based holonomy by the same group element. Equivalently the dot product of the two imaginary parts of $P,Q$ equals $(\operatorname{Re}(PQ^{-1})-\operatorname{Re}(PQ))/2$. This is an observable on one time slice for each finite regulator. Spatial gradient flow is used in glueball spectroscopy. The comparison below concerns a specifically defined bilinear observable and the raw magnetic target. [Sakai and Sasaki, *Glueball spectroscopy in lattice QCD using gradient flow*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15176) · [Lüscher, *Properties and uses of the Wilson flow in lattice QCD*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.4518)

For each $n\ge1$, a constant $c(\beta,\mu)>0$ independent of spatial size and of $n$ satisfies
$$
 \left\|J_\mu^c\Omega-\widehat T^{\,n}H^c\Omega\right\|^2
 \ \ge\ c(\beta,\mu).
$$
The stated independence of $n$ holds for a **fixed** prediction law. Choosing $\mu=\operatorname{law}(T_n)$ makes the lower constant depend on $n$. Here $\widehat T$ is the spatial-box transfer divided by its largest eigenvalue. The vacuum probability on the slice is the squared positive ground state relative to link Haar measure. Conjugating $\widehat T$ by the ground state gives the reversible Markov operator $P$ on this probability space. For gauge-invariant functions $P^nH$ is the conditional mean on slice $n$ given slice zero. This follows either by inserting the ground-state factors in the slab path integral or by taking arbitrarily long time boundaries with the vacuum on each end. The discrepancy displayed above is $\operatorname{Var}(D)$ with $D=J_\mu-P^nH$. [Lüscher, *Construction of a selfadjoint, strictly positive transfer matrix for Euclidean lattice gauge theories*](https://bib-pubdb1.desy.de/record/396349)

### The conditional reading stays away from both endpoints

One spatial edge of the terminal plaquette belongs to six four-dimensional Wilson faces. Given all other links, its density relative to normalized Haar measure is at least $\kappa=e^{-12\beta}$, since each face deficit lies in $[0,2]$. This also works in the stationary two-sided path measure by local conditioning, or first on long periodic time circles and then by passage to the vacuum. Holding all other links fixed, the plaquette holonomy is Haar when that link is Haar. For its scalar part $x$, $E_{\rm Haar}x^2=1/4$. Hence the Haar means of $H$ and $\tfrac12-H$ are $3/8$ and $1/8$. The time-zero links are disjoint from the selected terminal link. Conditioning once more gives almost everywhere
$$
 3\kappa/8\ \le P^n H\le\ 1/2-\kappa/8.
$$

### Spatial flow fixes two configurations with extreme magnetic readings

For the trivial configuration $J_\mu=0$. For the other configuration choose one quaternion unit $i$ with $i^2=-1$. In coordinates $a,b,c\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}$, take $U_2(a,b,c)=i^a$, $U_1(L-1,b,c)=i^{-Lb}$, all remaining links equal to one. Each positive $12$ face has exponent $1$ modulo four. Away from the $a$ seam the exponent increments by one. On the seam it is $1$ except at the double seam where it is $1-L^2\equiv1\bmod 4$. All $13,23$ faces are trivial. In the common circle subgroup the two incident faces in each spatial plane contribute opposite imaginary parts to every link velocity. The perpendicular Lie directions have zero derivative since the face imaginary parts are parallel to $i$. Thus this configuration is stationary for the full spatial flow and $J_\mu=1/2$. The seam is necessary on sizes congruent to two modulo four. [Finite exact enumeration](check_flux.py)

### Uniform probability and persistence of the two readings

Choose $U$ with $\mu((U,\infty))\le\kappa/128$. Use the link graph in a spatial slice with two links adjacent if they share an elementary face. Let $R$ satisfy $\sum_{j>R}(16U)^j/j!\le\kappa/512$ and take $0<\delta\le e^{-16U}\kappa/256$. Around either stationary configuration require the chord distance to be at most $\delta$ for every link within graph distance $R$ of the four measured links. There are at most $v_R=3(2R+7)^3$ such links even with periodic wraparound.

For clarity, the left-trivialized velocity at a link is a signed sum of the imaginary parts of four incident based plaquette products, transported to the endpoint by cyclically writing each loop from that link. The difference of each product is bounded by the sum of the four individual chord differences, using unitary multiplication and inversion. For the chord between two evolved links the common left multiplication is skew and contributes zero to its upper derivative. These estimates give the upper differential inequality $d'\le A d$ componentwise, with the nonnegative incidence-count matrix $A$ supported on the link graph and its diagonal, each row summing to at most 16. The integral inequality iterates to $d(u)\le e^{uA}d(0)$. At each measured link this is at most $\delta e^{16U}+2\sum_{j>R}(16U)^j/j!$ for $u\le U$. A plaquette contributes four such bounds. Beyond $U$ the imaginary-part distance is at most two. The integrated imaginary vector is therefore within $3\kappa/64<\kappa/16$ of its corresponding stationary value. On the unit ball the half squared norm is 1-Lipschitz, giving the same upper error for $J_\mu$.

Each cylinder has stationary probability at least $p=[e^{-12\beta}h(\delta)]^{v_R}$ where $h(\delta)>0$ is the normalized Haar mass of the chord ball. To see this, condition on the exterior of those spatial links in the full history. At most $6v_R$ action terms occur and each deficit is at most two. The joint density minorizes product Haar by $e^{-12\beta v_R}$. Taking the time-zero marginal preserves the estimate. This bound allows the two cylinders to have entirely different exterior fields.

On the low cylinder $D\le\kappa/16-3\kappa/8$. On the high cylinder $D\ge1/2-\kappa/16-(1/2-\kappa/8)$. These ranges are separated by at least $\kappa/4$. For any centering constant at least one entire cylinder is at distance $\kappa/8$ or more. Integrating its squared distance proves the assertion with $c=p\kappa^2/64$. The measure-theoretic step for general finite measures is kernel-checked. The result can be tested using three connected measurements,
$$
 C_{JJ}(0)-2C_{JH}(n)+C_{HH}(2n),\qquad C_{AB}(j)=\langle A^c\Omega,\widehat T^{\,j} B^c\Omega\rangle.
$$
This is a strict obstruction to exact reconstruction of the unflowed target at a fixed Wilson regulator. The constant degenerates as coupling or the clock tail changes. Its positivity in growing boxes supplies neither continuum control nor a lower bound on the interacting physical spectrum.

### What a sequence of actual compact Wilson measurements can approximate

Fix a physical time $t>0$ and positive physical baseline $s_0$. Replace the continuum Poisson law in the free reconstruction by finite positive atomic laws $\eta_j$ converging weakly to it. For the component $B_{12}$ use the actual compact prediction formed by averaging the imaginary parts at physical spatial flow times $s_0+u$ for $u$ in the support of $\eta_j$, then taking one half the squared norm. The target uses the same compact magnetic component at baseline $s_0$. In a spatial box of lattice spacing $1/r$, multiply both by $\beta r^4$ and use transfer lag $n=\lfloor rt\rfloor$. There are sequences of even spatial sides $L=\beta\to\infty$, mesh tending to zero, and $j\to\infty$ on which the squared discrepancy divided by the variance of the transferred target tends to zero. The limiting denominator is strictly positive.

Indeed for fixed $j,r$ all the required mixed imaginary parts use finitely many fixed lattice clocks. In a common finite tree frame the Lie–Euler comparison for each based plaquette controls the $\sqrt\beta$-scaled imaginary part in every fixed $L^p$; its linearization is the heat-evolved Gaussian magnetic component. A simultaneous frame is needed for magnetic dot products at different flow times on the same slice. It follows by taking the union of the finitely many patches before the Taylor expansion. Gauge invariance permits a common frame when taking the lagged scalar correlations as well. Products converge by Hölder's inequality and the moment bounds. Let the even side and $\beta=L$ go to infinity at this fixed mesh before taking $r\to\infty$. Integration over temporal momentum leaves a spatial bound $C|\mathbf p|e^{-c s_0|\mathbf p|^2}$ for each covariance, uniform in the nonnegative lag. The three entries of the squared discrepancy therefore converge to those of the continuum Gaussian at this $j$. In the Gaussian expression the multipliers of $\eta_j$ are bounded by one and converge pointwise to $e^{-t|\mathbf p|}$. Another dominated limit yields zero by the free transfer identity. The denominator is the strictly positive finite magnetic covariance at lag $2t$. Selecting a diagonal in this order gives the assertion for actual finite Wilson measurements. This construction selects a weak trajectory and a baseline-smoothed target. The all-coupling lower bound above uses the raw target and fixes the regulator. Uniform fourth-moment control along one prescribed interacting trajectory for the full unbounded clock law remains open.
