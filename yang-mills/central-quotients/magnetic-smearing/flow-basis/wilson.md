# What survives for compact Wilson observables

### Fixed bare parameters and finite spatial volume

Consider the defining $SU(2)$ Wilson vacuum on a finite spatial torus. Use the negative gradient of the sum of spatial plaquette deficits, with the unit round metric on unit quaternions and lattice flow clock $u$. For a spatial plaquette, let $\psi(u)$ be its centered flowed deficit times the original vacuum vector. Every open interval of positive clocks spans, after closure, exactly the same Hilbert subspace as the entire family $\{\psi(u):u\ge0\}$ at this regulator. Consequently an energy projection annihilates all clocks in one interval if and only if it annihilates the whole family. If it detects even one clock, its zeros in any finite interval are isolated. The assertion applies to every bare coupling for which the stated vacuum is defined. It concerns this family of observables; other physical states may be orthogonal to the entire family. [Wilson flow equation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1006.4518) · [Spatial prescription](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.15176)

Here is a volume-independent bound on the complex-clock neighborhood in **lattice units**. Complexify the four coefficients of each quaternion and use the supremum over links of their coefficient $\ell^1$ norm. Quaternion multiplication is submultiplicative for that norm, and the algebraic quaternion conjugation extending real inversion is a linear isometry. Each real unit quaternion has norm at most 2. Each link velocity in the ambient ODE is a sum of at most six degree-five products: left multiply the link by the signed imaginary projection of a based face holonomy. Coordinate permutations, conjugation and the imaginary projection have norm at most one. On the ball of radius 1 around any real configuration the uniform speed bound is $6\cdot3^5=1458$ and a Lipschitz bound is $6\cdot5\cdot3^4=2430$. The Picard integral map on bounded holomorphic paths maps this ball into itself on the complex disk $|z|\le r_0=1/5000$ and is a contraction there. These conservative constants also cover the four spatial faces incident to a spatial link in this flow.

Restarting at every real clock proves real analyticity with this uniform local disk. The complexified deficit has absolute value at most $M=1+3^4=82$. Passing to its vacuum vector and centering are bounded linear maps from the uniform-norm observable space to the vacuum Hilbert space, each with the requisite contraction bound (centering in the Hilbert norm). Thus the centered vector has the same holomorphic disk bound. This proof also works for an infinite spatial lattice whenever the original Wilson state and time-slice Hilbert space exist: Picard iteration at the initial time gives measurable finite-patch approximants to each link component; restarting and taking the uniform limits at finite real times gives bounded measurable slice functions. Testing against an arbitrary vector perpendicular to the interval span and applying analytic uniqueness proves the span assertion. If the projected vector is nonzero at one clock, its scalar product with that nonzero value supplies the scalar analytic function for the isolated-zero claim.

**A quantitative interval estimate.** Let $P$ be a spectral projection or a contraction, $h\le r_0/8$, and let $M X$ bound $\|P\psi\|$ on an interval of lattice-clock length $h$, with $0\le X\le1$. The supremum on an adjacent interval of length $h$ is at most $2M X^\alpha$, where $\alpha=\log4/\log48$. To see this, interpolate at $n+1$ equally spaced nodes of the known interval. On either adjacent interval the sum of absolute Lagrange coefficients is at most $(2n)^n2^n/n!\le12^n$. Each distance in the interpolation remainder is bounded by $2h$; the Cauchy derivative estimate gives a remainder at most $M(2h/r_0)^{n+1}$. The real divided-difference bound applies to Hilbert values by scalar duality or its integral formula. For $n=0$ use the single endpoint. With $n=\lfloor\log(1/X)/\log48\rfloor$ the two terms are each at most $M X^\alpha$. If $X=0$ pass to arbitrary $n$. Iterating $j$ steps from an interval therefore bounds the norm at a destination clock by $M\,2^{1/(1-\alpha)}X^{\alpha^j}$. Inverting this bound gives a nonzero interval floor from a nonzero anchor, uniform over any class with common $M$ and common anchor norm. One can use a smaller subinterval and more steps to fit an arbitrary prescribed nondegenerate finite interval.

For example, G4 proves a bare plaquette has projected norm at least $1/4$ in the specified band of normalized transfer eigenvalues $|\lambda/(\beta/4)^4-1|\le\beta^{1/4}$, uniformly for $0<\beta\le2^{-256}$ and even tori of side at least four, including the strong-coupling vacuum limit. Applying the estimate backwards from any positive lattice-clock interval to zero gives a uniform positive supremum in that interval for the **ordinary** flowed plaquette. The clock achieving it may vary with the regulator. The derivative bound $M/r_0$ also gives a uniform positive lower bound for the interval average of the **squared projected norms**: if the interval length is $h$ and its supremum is at least $d$, integrate the lower bound $(d/2)^2$ over a set of length at least $\min(h,d r_0/(2M))$ adjacent to a maximizing point. Averaging the vectors themselves could introduce cancellations. G4 owns the stronger pointwise long-clock Haar comparison. C4 independently checked the action-gradient identity and sent a localization estimate to that lane. [G4's band and constants](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/blob/815f24ddaaebcffce7b3554dcb16a620dc9e0240/yang-mills/g4/spatial-flow-variance/concentration.md) · [Long-clock commission](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/issues/1#issuecomment-7097887071)

For fixed $\beta$ in this strong-coupling range the original band has a volume-uniform positive floor at each clock **outside a countable exceptional set**, without choosing a clock separately for each volume. In G4's proof the bare mass is at least $1/4-d_0$ and the mass outside the band of relative half-width $\eta=\beta^{1/4}$ is bounded by a second-moment Markov estimate of at most $1/32$. Using $\eta/2$ quadruples this last bound, leaving at least $1/4-1/32-4/32=3/32$ inside. Choose a continuous multiplier $0\le f\le1$ of the vacuum-normalized transfer, equal to one in this inner band and zero outside the original band. The vector $f(T)\psi(u)$ is analytic and nonzero at zero in each volume and its infinite-volume limit. Remove the union of their discrete zero sets. At every remaining clock its infinite-volume norm is positive. The finite-volume norms converge to it: approximate $f^2$ uniformly on $[0,1]$ by polynomials and approximate the finite-clock observable uniformly by local slice functions using the finite-range flow equation. Every resulting term is an ordinary fixed-time-separation correlator and converges in the unique strong-coupling vacuum. A positive limiting norm and the finitely many remaining positive finite-volume norms have a positive infimum. This argument makes no assertion that exceptional zeros exist. ### Weak trajectories at fixed physical flow times

At zero spatial momentum choose the real $L^2$-normalized growing spatial packets used in the earlier compact Wilson limit. Use the same packet for every clock at each cutoff. For any finite set of physical clocks in $[a,b]$ and fixed finite time lags, the previous proof applies to **joint** moments: the finite collection of fixed lattice-clock Euler approximants can be put in a common finite tree frame, the Gaussian inputs coincide, and each filtered curl has its own heat multiplier. In the subsequent large-radius lattice integral the lower bound $a>0$ gives common Gaussian domination. The packet limit at zero momentum yields the mixed spectral matrix above, including the equal-time Gram matrix. Polarization or finite entrywise convergence then gives weak convergence of the positive measures of any fixed real linear combination. [Finite-packet and order-of-limits proof](../flow-momentum/spectrum.md)

For each $m$ first fix the exact signed coefficients of $F_m$. Choose the packet extent, inverse mesh and finally the even box size and inverse coupling $L=\beta$ so that the normalized compact spectral window probabilities approximate those of that fixed $m$ to within $1/m$. The target law is absolutely continuous at its two endpoints, so the weak limit suffices. Box size can also be required to dominate the packet extent in cells. One may select a countable sequence of scaled-energy distribution tests by the same diagonal procedure. Thus along selected actual compact $SU(2)$ vacua, centered, unit-normalized vectors formed only at clocks $a\le s\le b$ have more than half their spectral mass at $0<E\le4/\sqrt{(b-a)m}$ for all sufficiently large $m$. Centering removes the finite-volume vacuum, and the margin follows from the limiting second moment giving an upper bound $15/32$ on the complement. This transfer uses bounded spectral probabilities. It makes no assumption of convergence of the unbounded second energy moments of the compact observables. The physical-clock interval is fixed, but the spatial packet extent grows to select zero momentum, and the necessary accuracy depends on the coefficients.

The lattice analyticity disk above has physical radius $a_{\rm lat}^2/5000$. It supplies no fixed physical-clock analytic continuation bound after the cutoff is removed. The selected weak trajectories have no independently set interacting length scale. Neither the variational upper tests nor the strong-coupling transfer bands prove a lower spectral gap in a constructed four-dimensional interacting continuum theory.
