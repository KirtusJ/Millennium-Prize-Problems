# What an estimated variational energy establishes

Let $w_i$ be individually normalized, centered spatial-flow vectors in a positive vacuum transfer representation. At separation $t\ge0$ set $G_{ij}(t)=\langle w_i,e^{-tH}w_j\rangle$. For real $c$ with nonzero vector $v=\sum c_iw_i$, the quotient $R(t)=c^\top G(t)c/(c^\top G(0)c)$ is the Laplace transform of its spectral probability. If $t>0$ and $R(t)>0$, $-t^{-1}\log R(t)$ bounds the infimum of its spectral support from above. Maximizing the quotient over a finite basis gives the largest generalized eigenvalue on the non-null Gram subspace. A lower bound on the complete physical gap needs control of states invisible to the basis. The standard generalized eigenvalue method in lattice spectroscopy uses correlator matrices and Euclidean times to resolve energy levels. Here the issue is specifically the cost of changing the trial vector with energy resolution in a massless control. [Blossier et al., §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.1265) · [A4's separate full-gap trace bounds](../../../observable-normalization/replica-gap/README.md)

For a uniform absolute entry error $\delta$ in the matrix normalized by the true individual equal-time variances, the error of a quadratic form is at most $\delta(\sum_i|c_i|)^2$. Relative to the true equal-time norm this is $\delta\chi^2$. The constant is sharp: adding a positive spectral atom of weight matrix $\delta\,\sigma\sigma^\top$ at energy $\Lambda>0$ with $\sigma_i=\operatorname{sgn}c_i$ changes each entry by at most $\delta$ at every nonnegative time, and changes the equal-time form by exactly $\delta(\sum_i|c_i|)^2$. The example is an admissible matrix of spectral measures. It need not come from an actual Wilson flow as a function of the continuous clock. If errors at $0,t$ are bounded by $\delta_0,\delta_t$ and $\delta_0\chi^2<1$, elementary division gives
$$
 |\widehat R(t)-R(t)|\le
 \frac{(\delta_t+\delta_0R(t))\chi^2}{1-\delta_0\chi^2}.
$$
Correlated errors or direct sampling of the combined observable can behave differently. This is an entrywise worst-case bound, without a model for Monte Carlo error. Numerically stable formulas for one known analytic kernel do not make a variational eigenvalue measured from noisy entries equally stable. [Sharp quadratic-form statements in Lean](FlowBasis.lean)

At rest the normalized free Gram matrix is $G_{su}(0)=(2\sqrt{su}/(s+u))^{5/2}$. The largest single-clock probability below $\epsilon$ occurs at $b$ and is at most $8b^{5/2}\epsilon^5/(15\sqrt\pi)$. The triangle inequality for a projection gives the quick necessary bound $\eta\le\chi^2\,8b^{5/2}\epsilon^5/(15\sqrt\pi)$. The Laguerre proof strengthens this polynomial conditioning floor to an exponential one. For the explicit family on $[1,4]$ at $m=32$, for example, $\log_{10}\chi\simeq42.51$, so worst-case entry errors of order $10^{-85}$ can compete with the normalized signal. This is a property of that family in the control, not a precision requirement asserted for a particular simulation.

Spatial gradient flow as an operator for glueball spectroscopy is established in the literature. Sakai and Sasaki compare spatial and four-dimensional flow, study flow-time dependence and report finite-error glueball masses. Their interacting simulation does not have the radial Gaussian spectral coordinate used in the concentration theorem. Teper discusses constructing a variational basis from different spatial shapes, smearing and blocking levels. Such an enlarged basis is outside the one-parameter restriction in the moving-momentum bound.
