# The spacetime curvature as a random distribution

There is a four-dimensional field result under the first-order box and coupling conditions. For each positive face orientation $ij$ and color $c$, put
$$
 \mathcal F_{r,ij,c}=r^{-2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z^4}
              F_{ij,c}^\beta(x)\,\delta_{x/r}.
$$
Use one lift of the periodic spatial box centered at zero, extend by zero beyond it and use all time slices of its vacuum. Each face is rooted along the ordered coordinate paths. Faces at the spatial boundary may be omitted with this convention; they are eventually outside every fixed physical compact set. The atomic measure is locally finite for each regulator. The normalization is the four-volume element $r^{-4}$ times the curvature scaling $r^2$. For every $a>2$ these random distributions converge in law in $H^{-a}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb R^4)$, jointly over faces and colors, to centered Gaussian Maxwell curvature. In a fixed color its covariance on real compact smooth two-form tests is
$$
 \int_{\mathbb R^4}
 \widehat\phi(p)^*
   \frac{p\wedge\iota_p}{|p|^2}
 \widehat\chi(p)\,\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}.
$$
There are three independent colors. The formula uses the Euclidean inner product on positively oriented faces. Values of the multiplier at $p=0$ do not affect the integral.

**Finite tests.** The weights $g(x)=r^{-2}\phi(x/r)$ obey $\|g\|_1\le C_\phi r^2$, $\|g\|_\infty\le C_\phi/r^2$ and $\|g\|_2\le C_\phi$. For each component choose a spatial axis belonging to its face; every face has at least one. Perform the positive-block translation and the odd site reflection of the [ordinary-profile proof](ordinary-profile.md) along this axis. The signed differences admit line antiderivatives bounded in $\ell^1$ by $C_{\phi,M} r^3$, at all time slices together. The [stopped-walk bound](smoothing.md) applies separately in the selected axes and adds for a finite collection; the compact differential operators always act on the total cochain. The root comparison uses the same one-cell base change and is $O_{\phi,M,q}(r^3/\sqrt\beta)$ in $L^q$. Ordered coordinate paths to every site in a spatial half-space stay in that half-space, including their time-axis segments along the reflecting plane.

The full four-dimensional cubical projection is bounded by one at every momentum. Its symbol uses $e^{ik_j}-1$, and after $k=p/r$ it tends to the displayed real exterior-algebra projection for $p\ne0$. Poisson summation gives $r^2\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z^4}\widehat\phi(p+2\pi r\ell)$ for the discrete transform. The two factors of $r^2$ cancel $d^4 k=r^{-4}d^4p$. Rapid decrease supplies an integrable dominating function. Translations and reflected blocks add only bounded phase factors or reflected profiles. The free variance of block means vanishes as $M\to\infty$ by dominated convergence in the selected spatial momentum. The characteristic and moment arguments therefore apply jointly to every prescribed finite group of spacetime tests. The same reasoning for sharp spatial slices gives all magnetic face orientations: their mixed numerators are $p_i p_k\delta_{jl}-p_i p_l\delta_{jk}-p_j p_k\delta_{il}+p_j p_l\delta_{ik}$ for faces $ij,kl$, divided by $2|p|$ and multiplied by $e^{-|t||p|}$. The bounded positive-time measurements use these sharp magnetic slices directly.

**Local tightness.** Fix a compact physical box $K$. Uniformly over scalar weights $g(x)=r^{-2}u(x/r)$ supported there with $|u|\le1$, the second moment of their rooted scalar sum is bounded by a constant depending on $K$. Here one block translate is enough. Both its difference with the test and its difference with the positive-orientation reflected translate have the same $\ell^1,\ell^\infty$ and first-antiderivative bounds uniformly over $u$. Their free variances are bounded by $\|g_{\rm diff}\|_2^2\le C_K$ regardless of how fast $u$ varies. The even moment estimate for $n=2$ then bounds both difference norms uniformly, without using any free covariance limit for these particular weights. Site reflection bounds the translated block by its reflected difference, with a uniform one-edge frame error. Adding the first difference proves the claim for the original test. Real and imaginary parts give the bound for complex weights.

Multiply the field by a compact smooth cutoff supported inside a coordinate torus and test its Fourier modes there. Their expected squared coefficients are bounded uniformly in the mode and regulator by the preceding estimate. For $s>2$, $\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z^4}(1+|n|^2)^{-s}<\infty$, hence the expected local squared $H^{-s}$ norm is uniformly bounded. Given $a>2$, choose $2<s<a$. Markov's inequality and compact embedding $H^{-s}\hookrightarrow H^{-a}$ on this torus give tightness. Compactness here follows directly by cutting off the Fourier series: the $H^{-a}$ tail on a bounded $H^{-s}$ ball tends to zero uniformly. Exhaust space by such cutoffs, with summable exceptional probabilities; diagonal extraction gives local tightness. The finite test laws identify every limit in this separable local Sobolev space, which proves convergence of the whole sequence.

The color gauge at the common root can rotate the three components together. The Gaussian law has that invariance. The spatially coherent squared norm at a fixed time removes the root rotation exactly at each regulator. These statements specify a free scaling limit of selected rooted curvature fields on sufficiently rapidly weakening pure Wilson trajectories. They leave the continuum Yang–Mills construction at a fixed nonzero interaction scale open.
