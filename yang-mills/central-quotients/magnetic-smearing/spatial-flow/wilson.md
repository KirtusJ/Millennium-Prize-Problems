# A simple-group test on periodic four-dimensional cubes

Consider the defining $SU(2)$ Wilson action in quaternion coordinates, with $V(U)=1-\operatorname{Re}U=\|U-1\|^2/2$ and the induced tangent metric. Write $\ell$ for the even side in **lattice cells**, and take $\beta=\ell\to\infty$ in the first limit below. This is a sequence of actual isotropic periodic hypercubes. The general argument for transporting reflection positivity through an arbitrary full flow can be tested at any such cutoff, regardless of how a continuum interaction scale would be chosen. The result here uses the program's original local Wilson estimates in the specific ways described below.

### 1. The microscopic tangent on cubes

The existing microscopic curvature theorem first took the infinite-time vacuum at fixed spatial box. To use cubic periodic histories directly, let $P=3\ell^3$, $z(\gamma)=\int e^{-\gamma V(U)}dU$. For the unnormalized periodic partition function one has
$$
 Z_\ell(\beta)\ge z(\beta)^{P\ell}e^{-C P\ell},\qquad
 Z_\ell(\gamma)\le z(\gamma)^{P(\ell-1)} .
$$
The first inequality follows by keeping the top eigenvalue in the transfer trace and using the product-bump Rayleigh bound for the normalized transfer. Its positive top eigenfunction is gauge invariant, hence survives the projection. The second follows by dropping the nonnegative spatial action and the last layer of temporal plaquettes from the action, then integrating spatial links successively from the last slice backwards. Conditioned on temporal links each of the $P$ final spatial links appears in just its own retained temporal face, giving $z(\gamma)$ per link. [Transfer and trial bound](../../lie-curvature/wilson-groups.md)

Since $z(\beta)\asymp\beta^{-3/2}$, $(P\ell)^{-1}\log[Z_\ell(\beta/2)/Z_\ell(\beta)]\le C'+O(\log\beta/\ell)$. The original site-reflection copying estimate is already a finite-period inequality: for a face $p$, $(\mathbb E e^{\beta V_p/8})^J\le Z_\ell(\beta/2)/Z_\ell(\beta)$ with $J\ge c\ell^4$. Thus the local exponential bound is uniform along the chosen cubes. Compact-tree frame bounds follow by filling each fixed link loop with finitely many faces. The original finite-link Haar variations now pass to subsequential limits directly on these measures. They identify a closed Gaussian curl with projection covariance plus an independent constant mode. The original sine half-test on the even periodic cube is reflection positive at every cutoff. Taking the cutoff limit first and then taking the half-test's displacement to infinity removes the constant mode exactly as in the vacuum proof. Polynomial moments follow from the exponential bound. This extends the same microscopic three-color law to these cubes; no assertion of uniform convergence in flow time follows. ### 2. Passing a fixed nonlinear flow to the tangent

Take the product metric and let the Wilson flow be negative gradient of $\sum_p V_p$, using all faces or just the spatial faces and links for slice flow. It commutes with gauge change and time reflection. At each link the left-trivialized velocity $v_e(U)$ is a sum of at most six signed, conjugated skew parts of plaquettes. In particular $\sup_e\|v_e(U(0))\|_{L^q}\le C_q/\sqrt\beta$ for each fixed finite $q$. This velocity is a smooth function of a uniformly bounded number of neighboring compact-group variables, with neighborwise Lipschitz constants independent of $\ell,\beta$. Consequently for $0\le u\le\tau$ its supremum of individual-link $L^q$ norms is at most $C_{q,\tau}/\sqrt\beta$. Indeed the change in velocity is bounded in that norm by a constant times the integral of the supremum of the velocity norms. Apply Grönwall. The supremum is outside the expectation.

Approximate a fixed $\tau$ by $M$ explicit Lie-Euler steps $U_e\mapsto\exp[(\tau/M)v_e(U)]\,U_e$. A single exact step from time $t$ differs in an individual-link $L^q$ norm from the update with frozen initial velocity by at most $C_{q,\tau}(\tau/M)^2/\sqrt\beta$: integrate the neighborwise velocity change and use the bi-invariant metric. One numerical step is Lipschitz with factor $1+C_\tau\tau/M$ in the supremum of the individual-link norms, by smoothness, bi-invariance and the finite neighbor count. Discrete Grönwall bounds the global error by $C_{q,\tau}/(M\sqrt\beta)$, also for an observed four-link plaquette. Each evolved face stays $O_{L^q}(\beta^{-1/2})$ from the identity by integration of the four link speeds and the initial bound. The difference of the exact and discrete **scaled actions** $\beta V$ is therefore $O_{L^q}(1/M)$ by the quadratic norm formula, Hölder and the same estimates at $2q$. Constants may grow with fixed $\tau$.

For fixed $M$ the numerical observable uses a finite patch. Put its initial links into the same ordered tree frame. Every link in the patch differs from identity by $O_{L^q}(\beta^{-1/2})$. Taylor expansion of the smooth finite update at identity has scaled remainder $O_{L^q}(\beta^{-1/2})$ for the linear curvature, using the moment bound at $2q$ (or chart localization with higher moments). The Hessian of $\sum_p V_p$ there is $d^*d$ on link coordinates. The face derivative of the $M$ steps on an initial exact curl is $(I-\tau dd^*/M)^M$; use spatial $d$ and magnetic faces for the spatial version. The fixed-patch tangent law identifies its limiting input with the microscopic Gaussian curl. On closed two-forms $dd^*$ acts as the scalar Hodge Laplacian $\Delta$. For each fixed $\tau$, boundedness of the lattice Laplacian gives uniform Fourier-multiplier convergence to $e^{-\tau\Delta}$ as $M\to\infty$. The initial Gaussian has bounded spectral density, so this gives convergence in all finite Gaussian moments for the filtered variables. The uniform numerical error gives joint moment convergence for the original evolved face deficits to one half the squared norm of the filtered three-color Gaussian. The same reasoning at fixed flow time gives the spatial alternative.

### 3. A negative form in a finite simple-group history

Take $R$ through positive multiples of four. For each **fixed** $R$, choose $\tau=R^2$ in lattice units, mark the same spatial face at times $R/4$ and $R/2$, and first let $\ell=\beta\to\infty$ through even cubes. The normalized reflected action form converges to the analogous squared-covariance expression of the lattice Gaussian. Its single-color covariance is the Brillouin-zone integral of the finite-torus symbol in the [abelian computation](finite-torus.md). The color factor cancels after variance normalization.

Now take $R\to\infty$ only in that Gaussian expression. Under $p=Rk$, $R^4 K_{R^2}(n)$ tends to the four-dimensional flowed magnetic covariance at flow time one and physical separation $n/R$. Indeed the projection is bounded by one, $R^2\sum_j4\sin^2(p_j/(2R))\ge c|p|^2$ on the expanding domain, and the rescaled multiplier tends to $(p_1^2+p_2^2)/|p|^2$ away from zero. The heat factor gives integrable domination. The reflected form divided by the equal-time connected variance tends to $-0.0150600460\ldots$, strictly negative by concavity, at reflected separations $1/2,3/4,1$. Thus some fixed finite $R$ already gives a negative Gaussian form, and then all sufficiently large even $\ell$ for this $R$ give a negative **actual** finite-volume Wilson form. The original single-plaquette actions are bounded at each regulator and the chosen positive coordinates fit inside its positive half. [Strict analytic sign and normalization](spectrum.md)

This argument quantifies over some finite regulators. It supplies neither a negative form at every finite coupling nor a conclusion about full flow on a particular logarithmic trajectory. It tests the universal finite-cutoff assertion and the support assumption in the proposed proof. Its compact simple-group step is a written extension of the program's tangent proof. It is not independently machine-verified end to end.

### 4. Positive spectral weight for the original nonlinear spatial operator on selected trajectories

The fixed-flow argument also applies directly in the original finite-spatial-box *infinite-time vacuum*, using the existing vacuum tangent and its uniform moments. At physical spacing $1/R$ define
$$
 H_R(n)=R^4\beta\sum_{1\le i<j\le3}V(U^{\mathrm{sp}}_{ij}(n,0;sR^2)).
$$
Here $U^{\mathrm{sp}}$ is the actual compact nonlinear spatial-flow field at fixed physical flow time $s>0$. The construction uses a single slice, without the thin shared transporter of the earlier angle. For each fixed $R$ all its fixed time readings have Gaussian-tangent limits as $\beta=L\to\infty$ in the vacuum. In the second limit $R\to\infty$, the magnetic covariance tends to the continuum spatial-heat result. To justify this even at coincident time, perform the temporal Fourier integral before scaling: for the $12$ component its factor is $(q_1^2+q_2^2)e^{-|n|\omega}/(2\sinh\omega)$, where $\omega=2\operatorname{arsinh}(|q_{\rm sp}|/2)$. Multiplied by $e^{-2sR^2 |q_{\rm sp}|^2}$ and rescaled, it is dominated by $C|p|e^{-cs|p|^2}$ in the three spatial variables. Polarization gives the other entries, and the half-link phases tend to one. Wick moments converge because these are finite Gaussian arrays.

Consequently there exist diagonal trajectories for the original nonlinear operator with limiting mean $d/(32\pi^2s^2)$ and connected point covariance $3d\,c_3(t)^2/2$ ($d=3$). To see the quantifiers, enumerate the nonnegative rational times, take $R$ through multiples of four, and choose an even $L=\beta$ so large that the first $R$ mean, equal-time variance and rational-time covariance tests differ by less than $1/R$ from their *fixed-$R$* limits after the displayed rescaling. This selection can additionally impose $L/R\to\infty$. The same selection can include the cubic-history reflection tests in section 3. In the vacuum, positivity of the original transfer makes the covariance decreasing at positive time. Squeezing between rational times extends convergence to every fixed real $t>0$. Total mass converges at zero. Under $E\mapsto e^{-E}$ the integer-time moments and polynomial density now identify the weak limit of the raw connected physical-energy measures as the local self-convolution in the [free calculation](spectrum.md), with nonzero density arbitrarily near zero. Rounding $Rt$ at each time costs a vanishing uniformly bounded Laplace-kernel error for a fixed positive time.

This gives one family of actual nonlinear, spatially smoothed Wilson observables with a calibrated positive-energy reading on selected trajectories. The choice of $\beta(R)$ is existential. A Wilson vacuum with an independently measured interaction scale and a prescribed bare trajectory requires separate uniform estimates; the free time law and the lattice fit of a glueball mass cannot provide them. The power-five zero-spatial-momentum measure in the accompanying calculation is a free infinite-volume control; this diagonal proof is for a point reading.
