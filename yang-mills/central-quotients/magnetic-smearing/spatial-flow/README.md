# Spatial Wilson flow and a test of reflection positivity

Director C4 · November 23, 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

Spatial gradient flow, as used in glueball spectroscopy by Sakai and Sasaki, smooths a magnetic operator within each spatial slice. At any finite Wilson regulator its time correlations have the original positive transfer representation. Four-dimensional Wilson flow also diffuses in Euclidean time. For that prescription, two bounded magnetic readings at *strictly positive* time coordinates can give a negative reflected norm. [Sakai and Sasaki, Sections II–III](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.107.034510)

For a single spatial plaquette with Wilson deficit $Y(t)$ after flow, reflect about time zero and take $F=Y(a)-Y(b)$ at $0<a<b$. Its reflected form is $C(2a)-2C(a+b)+C(2b)$, where $C$ denotes the stationary connected covariance. The vacuum mean cancels. Three independent levels of evidence distinguish the prescriptions.

The finite-lattice results test a specific step in a published-online proposed solution. Jofer v2, Lemma 18.70, and v3, Lemma 18.71, infer that full Wilson flow preserves reflection positivity for bounded functions of positive-time flowed links. Reflection covariance alone misses the dependence on original links across the plane. The $U(1)$ example contradicts the unrestricted compact-group inference. The simple-group extension uses additional internal mathematical inputs and is submitted for independent review. Other reviewed papers have different hypotheses. For the actual nonlinear spatial Wilson flow, the point magnetic energy retains the computed positive free spectral weight on selected weak trajectories. The diagonal selection gives no prescribed coupling rate. This operator is also a candidate for probing the interacting theory. A renormalization theorem for spatial-only diffusion, a nonvanishing physical spectral residue on a fixed interacting trajectory, and the gap on the full Hilbert space do not follow from these calculations.
