# A compact Wilson counterexample at a fixed regulator

Use normalized Haar link variables on the periodic $16^4$ lattice with $\beta\sum_p(1-\cos f_p)$, where $f_p$ is the oriented plaquette angle modulo $2\pi$. Write $d$ for the oriented cubical coboundary on real cochains, with adjoint $d^*$. In lattice units use the negative gradient of the sum of plaquette deficits with the product metric on link angles. This fixes the flow clock independently of $\beta$. A different constant normalization of that clock just changes the positive flow time in the example.

The multiplicative coboundary is a homomorphism of compact tori. Its pushforward of product Haar is exactly Haar on its compact connected image $H$, whose real tangent space is $\operatorname{im}d$. This integrates out the gauge and flat holonomy directions without fixing a sector. The action restricted to $H$ has exactly one zero, the identity plaquette configuration. In an exponential chart of $H$ its Hessian is the identity on the tangent subspace in the inherited face norm. Off a fixed chart about the identity the action has a strictly positive minimum. Finite-dimensional Laplace rescaling and dominated convergence therefore give $\sqrt\beta f\Rightarrow F$ with all polynomial moments in the chart, where $F$ is a centered Gaussian with covariance the orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{im}d$. Exponentially rare complements times fixed powers of $\beta$ vanish. This reasoning uses the compact image itself, so no multiplicity of flat links or choice of logarithmic flux sector alters the calculation.

On angle lifts the full Wilson flow near the minimum obeys $\dot f=-dd^*\sin f$. Its derivative at zero at time $\tau$ is $e^{-\tau dd^*}$ on $\operatorname{im}d$, equal there to the scalar cubical heat semigroup because $df=0$. The time-$\tau$ map is smooth on the compact image. Taylor expansion in the chart gives the tangent limit for $\sqrt\beta f(\tau)$ and $\beta(1-\cos f_p(\tau))\Rightarrow F_p(\tau)^2/2$, including joint moments. Uniform integrability follows by a local Lipschitz bound for the flow, the quadratic deficit and the same Gaussian domination. For spatial flow the spatial curvature obeys the corresponding slice coboundary equation and scalar spatial heat equation at first order.

Put $q_j^2=2-2\cos k_j$ and $A=\sum_{j=1}^4q_j^2$, with $k_j=2\pi l_j/16$. Spatial axes are 1–3. For orientation $12$, the independent-time double heat factor and covariance projection give
$$
 K_\tau(n)=16^{-4}\sum_{k\ne0}\frac{q_1^2+q_2^2}{A}\,e^{-2\tau A}\cos(nk_4).
$$
The zero mode of the exact projection is zero. For spatial flow replace $A$ in the exponential by $q_1^2+q_2^2+q_3^2$ and retain the four-dimensional denominator. Wick's formula for the squared field gives $C(n)=K_\tau(n)^2/2$. Hence the normalized form uses $[K(2)^2-2K(3)^2+K(4)^2]/K(0)^2$. At $\tau=6$ the three normalized squared covariances have outward enclosures $(0.913457,0.913459)$, $(0.822440,0.822442)$ and $(0.721557,0.721559)$. They give an upper bound $-0.009862$. The spatial version is positive. The code uses exact multiplicities, algebraic cosines and outward interval arithmetic; its spatial check evaluates the temporal Green function by a different formula. There is also an analytic sign check independent of the numerical enclosure. Set $\omega=\pi/8$ and $a=2-2\cos\omega$. The slowest visible shell of the full flow has four spatial modes of eigenvalue $a$, independent of $n$. The first time-dependent visible shell has a unit spatial momentum in direction 1 or 2 and a unit temporal momentum. Its eight sign and axis choices have projection $1/2$ and eigenvalue $2a$. Other visible modes on this shell are independent of time. Thus the leading time-dependent contribution to $K_\tau(n)^2$ is $32\cdot16^{-8}e^{-6\tau a}\cos(n\omega)$, up to time-independent terms and $o(e^{-6\tau a})$. Its reflected sign is the sign of $2\cos(3\omega)(\cos\omega-1)<0$. This also gives strict negativity for every sufficiently large fixed $\tau$ before taking $\beta\to\infty$.

Each regulated $Y=\beta(1-\cos f_p(\tau))$ is bounded. Replacing it by the unscaled deficit multiplies the reflected form by a strictly positive constant. Reflection preserves the orientation of the spatial face, and flow commutes with this reflection. Therefore this is a gauge-invariant member of the nominal positive-time *flowed* algebra. The original Wilson measure has positive half-space forms on the original link algebra. The two algebras differ for full flow.
