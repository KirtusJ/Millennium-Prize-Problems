# A positive low-energy test on compact Wilson trajectories

Use the actual nonlinear spatial-flow magnetic energy in the $SU(2)$ finite-spatial-box, infinite-time Wilson vacuum. It is a function of one slice, so centering it gives a vector orthogonal to the vacuum of the original positive transfer operator. Normalize that vector to unit norm. For the free point magnetic energy after spatial flow at physical $s$, the two energies in its normalized spectral measure are independent with individual density proportional to $p^3 e^{-2sp^2}\,dp$. Thus the scaled total energy $\sqrt{s}E$ has a fixed probability law $\nu$ on $(0,\infty)$ with normalized Laplace transform
$$
 q(u)=\left(2\int_0^\infty y^3 e^{-y^2-u y/\sqrt{2}}\,dy\right)^2,\qquad q(0)=1 .
$$
Its mean is $3\sqrt\pi/(2\sqrt2)$. This gives the spatial-flow cusp in the [original-plane test](shifted-plane.md). The quantum energy here is physical transfer energy; it is distinct from the flow clock. ### Increasing the spatial smoothing radius

Choose length units and assign the original regulator spacing $a_n=1/n$, physical flow time $s_n=n^2$, and therefore the spatial lattice-flow clock $n^4$. There exist even box sides and couplings $L_n=\beta_n$ with $L_n/n^2\to\infty$ for which the spectral probability measures of $\sqrt{s_n} E$ for the actual centered compact Wilson readings converge weakly to $\nu$. This is a selected weak trajectory. Specifying both lengths makes explicit the freedom to change physical units before an interacting scale has been fixed.

For the construction, the effective smoothing radius in cells is $r_n=n^2$. Apply the earlier fixed-clock Wilson-to-Gaussian limit at each $n$ before increasing $n$. Choose $L_n=\beta_n$ sufficiently large that at lags $k r_n$ for $k=0,1,\ldots,n$ the scaled connected covariances differ by at most $1/n$ from their infinite-lattice Gaussian comparators. Here one can use $r_n^8$ times the covariance of the $\beta_n$-scaled energy, as in the original point-energy limit; the limiting equal-time variance is strictly positive. Impose $L_n\ge n r_n$ as well. For each fixed $k$, the Gaussian momentum integral converges at radius $r_n\to\infty$. Dividing by the equal-time variance gives convergence to $q(k)$. The normalized compact measures exist for all sufficiently large $n$. With $x=e^{-\sqrt{s_n} E}$, these are all integer moments of probabilities on $[0,1]$. Polynomial approximation identifies the weak limit with the pushforward of $\nu$. That limit has no atom at $x=0$, so the scaled energies themselves converge weakly on $[0,\infty)$. This argument uses only the established fixed-clock comparison and the already evaluated spatial spectrum. It requires no uniform-in-clock nonlinear perturbation theorem.

There is a direct low-energy reading that uses just the separation $\sqrt{s_n}$ and the variance. For any probability $\rho$ on energies and $t>0$,
$$
 \rho([0,3/t])\ \ge\
 \frac{\int e^{-tE}\,d\rho-e^{-3}}{1-e^{-3}} .
$$
Use $t=\sqrt{s_n}$. Jensen gives $q(1)\ge\exp[-3\sqrt{\pi}/(2\sqrt2)]>e^{-2}$. Consequently for all sufficiently large chosen regulators the centered compact observable has **at least $1/12$ of its normalized spectral mass at $0<E\le3/\sqrt{s_n}$**. Indeed $(e^{-2}-e^{-3})/(1-e^{-3})=1/(e^2+e+1)>1/12$. Positivity improvement of the finite-volume transfer gives its unique vacuum; centering removes that atom. The transfer gap in the assigned length units is at most $-\log(q(1)+o(1))/\sqrt{s_n}$. For orientation $q(1)\simeq0.1705309063$, and the two-time lower bound tends to about $0.1270703$. The unnormalized variance shrinks with increasing radius. This is a statement about the normalized spectral weight of actual bounded, slice-local probes.

A finite spatial-flow clock preserves reflection positivity on every original Wilson vacuum. This weak-trajectory example gives nonzero low-window weight and excludes a *uniform positive gap over these arbitrarily assigned trajectories*. Clay's problem asks for a nontrivial interacting continuum theory with a positive spectral gap for every compact simple gauge group. The couplings selected here have no independent physical scale calibration. Spectra on a trajectory with an independently fixed interaction scale and a lower bound throughout its reconstructed Hilbert space remain open.
