# Private straight bonds in the original Wilson measure

Keep a closed interval strictly inside the empty slab, for example $[3a,5a]$ on the spatial axis in the [specified geometry](README.md). Write $P$ for the ordered path from the root to its left endpoint, $W$ for the product of its $m$ full positive axis-1 links, and $B$ for the right packet computed starting at its right endpoint. Then $X_g=\operatorname{Ad}_{P W} B$. Put $A=\operatorname{Ad}_{P^{-1}} X_f$. Neither $A$ nor $B$ uses any marked link. All left faces and paths lie before the interval, and all right paths use the same marked product exactly once. In particular $h=\langle\widehat A,\operatorname{Ad}_W\widehat B\rangle$. A change of gauge acts orthogonally in each endpoint frame and leaves $h$ invariant. This construction allows neighboring links along the path to share sites.

The packets have nonzero norm almost surely. The squared norm of each packet is real analytic on the connected product of spatial link groups. At the identity configuration the differential of a single curvature projection in a link direction $Y$ is its signed incidence times $Y$, since $Db(1)$ is the identity for the Wilson metric. Path differentiation at that configuration acts on a zero curvature. For a packet with face array $g$ the resulting differential is $\sqrt\beta(d^*g)_eY$. Some edge coefficient is nonzero. Otherwise both discrete tangent-direction differences of the $12$ array vanish, but on at least one transverse line the sampled bump is nonzero and the support is proper. Thus the squared norm is not identically zero. Analytic coordinate charts transfer the Lebesgue-null zero-set theorem to smooth Haar measure. The fixed-box vacuum has density $\Omega^2$ with respect to product Haar; finite temporal periods also have absolutely continuous slice marginals. Mark the straight segment on slices $0$ and $n\ge2$, with temporal period exceeding $n+1$. Two parallel bonds of one plaquette have the same longitudinal coordinate and differ transversely by one. No plaquette contains two selected bonds. Conditional on the exterior $\mathcal E$, all selected links are independent with their original one-link Wilson densities. This assertion holds in the finite periodic history and the vacuum estimates below follow by sending the period to infinity. Fixed-volume convergence for bounded measurable cylinder insertions follows by trace convergence of powers of the normalized compact transfer to the top projection, including bounded insertions integrated over a fixed number of slices.

For a one-link density $p=e^\Phi/Z$, the six incident Wilson terms give $|X^2\Phi|\le C\beta$ for any unit invariant direction, uniformly over the exterior. Finite positive character mixtures obey the same estimate by summing their Hessian bounds. Haar integration by parts gives $\int|X\log p|^2p\le C\beta$. The density overlap with a translate by $\exp(sX)$ is at least $1/2$ for $|s|\le c/\sqrt{1+\beta}$. For $M=\int\pi(g)p(g)\,dg$ and unit $v$, integration of the triangle inequality for $\pi(g)v$ and $\pi(g\exp(sX))v$ about $Mv$ gives
$$
 \|\pi(\exp(sX))v-v\|^2\le 8(1-\|Mv\|^2).
$$
On any fixed finite sum of nontrivial irreducibles the sum of the left sides over an orthonormal Lie basis is at least $c_\pi s^2$, uniformly in $v$, by the strictly positive minimum Casimir and Taylor expansion at zero. Take $s=c'/\sqrt{1+\beta}$ and multiply the $m$ independent Fourier matrices. On the non-invariant parts of the adjoint and its tensor square the norm of their product is at most
$$
 \delta=\exp[-\kappa m/(1+\beta)]
$$
for one common $\kappa>0$. These finite-representation bounds have unit prefactor. The Haar projection is the identity on invariant subspaces. The adjoint representation has no invariant vector. The Haar conjugation average of $\widehat B\widehat B^T$ is $I/d$. Indeed it is symmetric, its eigenspaces are invariant ideals of the real simple Lie algebra, and its trace is one. The matrix coefficient of $\widehat A^{\otimes2}$ and $\widehat B^{\otimes2}$ on the complementary space has both endpoint norms at most one. Consequently
$$
 |\mathbb E[h\mid\mathcal E]|\le\delta,\qquad
 |\mathbb E[h^2\mid\mathcal E]-1/d|\le\delta .
$$
In particular $|\mathbb E h|\le\delta$ and $|\operatorname{Var}h-1/d|\le\delta+\delta^2$. With both times marked the conditional angular readings are independent given $\mathcal E$. Their covariance equals that of their exterior-dependent conditional means. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $|C_r(n)|\le\delta^2$ for the connected covariance at every $n\ge2$. Positivity of the vacuum transfer gives $C_r(n)\ge0$. Its spectral Cauchy–Schwarz inequality also yields $C_r(1)\le\sqrt{C_r(0)C_r(2)}\le\delta$. There is no independence assumption on adjacent time slices.

Let $\nu_r$ be the connected raw spectral measure of $h$ at lattice energy $E_\ell=-\log\widehat T$. From $\int e^{-2E_\ell}d\nu_r=C_r(2)$, for $s=\kappa m/(1+\beta)$ and $0<\theta<1$,
$$
 \nu_r([0,(1-\theta)s])\le e^{-2\theta s}.
$$
Physical energy is $rE_\ell$. If $\beta=o(r)$, $s\to\infty$ and the mass below physical energy $(1-\theta)rs$ tends to zero while the total tends to $1/d$. The threshold is of order $r^2/(1+\beta)$. On the compactified physical-energy axis the entire raw measure converges weakly to $(1/d)\delta_{+\infty}$, since every bounded interval loses its mass and the total mass converges. Thus the limiting sharp-time variance and the right limit of the positive-physical-time covariance are different. The bound concerns the spectral measure of this operator. Other gauge-invariant vectors can have low energy.

In the defining $SU(2)$ case the adjoint action is transitive on the two-sphere. A Haar connector yields a uniform angle on $[-1,1]$ whatever the two unit vectors. D4 and B4's uniform path-density bound gives conditional total-variation error $O(e^{-c m/(1+\beta)})$ once $m\ge C(1+\beta)$. At any fixed number of pairwise nonadjacent lattice times, joint exterior conditioning on all their trunks gives convergence in total variation to the product uniform law if $\beta=o(r)$. In higher rank the conditional Haar law can depend on endpoint orbits, even though its first two moments are universal.
