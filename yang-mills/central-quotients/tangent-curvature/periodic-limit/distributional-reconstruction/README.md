# Distributional reconstruction with one smeared test for a nonzero field

Director C4 · 23 November 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)  
Research program directed by Kirtus Justus

Z4’s conditional reduction controls the modulus of continuity of every order of correlation on separated configurations. For the **distributional** conclusion, the growth bound already gives subsequential compactness. One positive smeared two-point norm can then ensure a nonvacuum state survives. This yields a Wightman reconstruction under weaker continuity requirements. A local, reflection-positive spin example below explains why the normalization at one pair of lattice sites alone cannot serve as the smeared test under the weaker hypotheses. ## Reconstruction sources and conventions

Osterwalder and Schrader’s original **1975** sufficient theorem is in §IV.1, p. 287, Theorem $E'$ (or $E''$) $\to R'$, part (a). Its full hypothesis list is $E0'$ (or $E0''$) and $E1$–$E4$. Revision one establishes clustering before its reconstruction step, so it can invoke the full statement there. Section II, eq. (2.1), uses the weight $(1+|x|^2)^{p/2}$ with total derivative order at most $p$. The draft uses $(1+|x|)^p$. The latter is bounded by $2^{p/2}$ times the former by $(1+r)^2\le2(1+r^2)$. At $p=ks$ this costs only an exponential in $k$, absorbed into the allowed factorial growth. For example $k!\ge2^{k-1}$ for $k\ge1$. Equation (4.1) uses joint tests flat at all coincidence diagonals. The separate product-test condition (4.2) is stronger. OS II, introduction, remark 5 addresses the field-multiplet extension. [OS II, §§II and IV.1](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/communications-in-mathematical-physics/volume-42/issue-3/Axioms-for-Euclidean-Greens-functions-II-with-an-Appendix-by/cmp/1103899050.pdf)

OS I, §2, uses test functions with all derivatives vanishing outside the open ordered positive-time chamber. Their support can touch its walls. Shift the $j$th argument’s support forward by $j\varepsilon e_0$. The new supports have first time and successive time differences at least $\varepsilon$, and the functions converge in Schwartz topology as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Thus positivity proved for the draft’s strict supports gives positivity on the original test space by continuity of the paired distribution. The OS adjoint reverses the order of the first block in addition to conjugation. Reverse its species labels as well, then simultaneous permutation symmetry gives the draft’s unreversed scalar formula. OS I, §4.1, especially eqs. (4.3)–(4.9), gives the null quotient, completion, and translation on test vectors inducing the positive-time semigroup. Use the corrected 1975 theorem for the reconstruction. [OS I, §§2–4](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/communications-in-mathematical-physics/volume-31/issue-2/Axioms-for-Euclidean-Greens-functions/cmp/1103858969.pdf)

## A weaker sufficient criterion

Consider Z4’s finite family of real bounded centered local scalar fields with its reflection conventions. Assume H0, the positive transfer gap H1, its closing H2, and H3(a,b) along a sequence, with lattice spacing $a=m\downarrow0$. Keep the uniform factorial and separated-point bounds in H3(b). Write $S^a$ for the normalized mesh functional of that revision. Impose the following two conditions.

1. For the irrational coordinate-plane rotation $R$ in H4, $S^a_\iota(f\circ R)-S^a_\iota(f)\to0$ for every smooth compact test supported off the diagonals, at every order and species tuple. The original H4 suffices.
2. For some species $i$ and some fixed smooth compact positive-time one-point test $v$, the reflected norm has $\liminf_{a\downarrow0} S^a_{ii}((\Theta v)^*\otimes v)>0$. A lower bound along a subsequence suffices if the other assumptions hold there.

Then a subsequence converges on every coincidence-flat Schwartz test. Its distributions are those of a Wightman theory with a unique vacuum up to scalar multiple, a nonzero orthogonal complement, and invariant mass at least one on that complement. This criterion asserts distributional convergence; it does not give pointwise convergence or fix the value of a limiting two-point function at unit distance.

**Compactness.** At fixed $k\ge2$ let $r$ be the minimum pair distance, take $M=qk+1$, $P=4k+2$, and $s=q+6$ with integer $q\ge1$ as in Z4. Taylor expansion from the closest diagonal gives, for $r<1$, a bound on $|f(x)|$ by $2^P(4k)^M r^M(1+|x|)^{-P}|f|_{ks}/M!$. H3(b) costs at most $r^{-qk}$. For $r\ge1$ no Taylor factor is needed. The sum of $(1+|x|)^{-P}$ with mesh weights is at most $2^P$ times its integral for $a\sqrt{4k}\le1$, by comparison on each mesh cube. This integral is $|S^{4k-1}|/[(4k)(4k+1)]$; the sphere areas are bounded in this sequence of dimensions. Since $(4k)^M/M!\le(4e)^M$, these bounds give
$$
 |S^a_\iota(f)|\le C^k(k!)^{b'} |f|_{ks}
$$
with constants independent of sufficiently fine mesh at each order. Coincidences make no contribution. The centered one-point functions and vacuum entry are immediate. The flat Schwartz space is a closed, separable subspace of Schwartz space. Convergence on countable dense sets follows by diagonal extraction for all orders and field labels; the uniform bounds extend convergence to each test and yield $E0'$. No estimate of differences of lattice correlations enters this step.

**Motions and positivity.** Compact tests supported a positive distance from all diagonals are dense in the flat space. First cut off at a large radius in Schwartz topology. Then cut out tubes of radius $\eta$ about the diagonals; derivatives of the cutoffs cost powers of $\eta^{-1}$ of finite order, absorbed by arbitrary Taylor powers from flatness on the bounded support. The smeared rotation condition thus gives rotation invariance of the limit. To derive it from Z4’s H4, replace the mesh sum on such a compact test by integration against the nearest-site step function. The error is $O_f(a)$ since the field values on the enlarged separated compact are uniformly bounded and only the test varies inside a mesh cell. Apply H4 to the resulting integral and change variables by $R$. Hypercubic symmetry is exact. The closure of the generated rotation group is $O(4)$ by Z4’s coordinate-plane argument, and rotations act continuously on Schwartz tests. Approximate each common continuum translation by mesh translations and use the uniform seminorm bound. Reality and simultaneous permutation symmetry pass directly to the limit.

For reflection positivity use the positive-half lattice sums in Z4’s Step 6. Reflected first-block and positive second-block tests have a tensor product flat at every diagonal. Pass the finite quadratic form to the limit; positivity applies to the whole sum. The ordered-chamber approximation above handles the original OS boundary convention.

**Gap and cluster.** For positive-time compact tests or finite families thereof, let $B_{f,g}(t)$ be the centered reflected form with the second support translated forward by $t$. At mesh times $an$, lattice transfer gives
$$
 |B^a_{f,g}(an)|\le e^{-an}\sqrt{B^a_{f,f}(0)B^a_{g,g}(0)} .
$$
This uses the norm on the vacuum complement and holds for a self-adjoint one-step transfer even if it has a negative part. With $n=\lfloor t/a\rfloor$, continuity of test translation and the uniform seminorm estimate give the same bound in the limit. It holds also for compact tests with all insertion times positive but internally unordered and flat at collisions, by the same lattice proof.

For a large spatial displacement rotate its direction to time, translate the compact blocks to opposite sides of a fixed time buffer, then apply the last estimate with separation equal to the length of the displacement minus twice the buffer. The reflected first block can be written as the adjoint of a positive-time test. The resulting norms are bounded uniformly over displacement directions: rotations of fixed compact tests range over a compact set in Schwartz space, the chosen buffer may be the same, and $E0'$ bounds their paired forms. For general ordered tests truncate to compact support; reflection positivity and spatial-translation isometry bound the truncation errors by Cauchy–Schwarz uniformly in displacement. This yields $E4$ before invoking OS II. In the reconstructed space the exponential time bound excludes spectral mass below one for vectors orthogonal to the vacuum in the dense test domain, hence on the whole complement. Lorentz invariance and the forward spectral cone exclude all masses below one there by boosting any such spectral point to energy below one. Condition 2 and centering give a nonzero vector on this complement.

For practical use, H3(c) only for **one two-point function in a neighborhood of the normalized pair**, with a mesh-uniform modulus there, suffices for condition 2. By lattice translation invariance the relevant variable is the separation. The value at $aN e_0\to e_0$ is one. Thus on two fixed small reflected balls about $\pm e_0/2$ every cross correlation eventually exceeds $1/2$. A nonnegative unit-integral test on the positive ball gives the required lower bound by a Riemann sum. Full H3(c) controls far more than this specific inference needs.

## A bounded site field whose normalized clock disappears on smearing

On $\mathbb Z^4$ place one independent symmetric two-state Markov chain on **each** coordinate line in **each** of the four directions. Each transition has probabilities $(1+e^{-m})/2$ to keep the spin and $(1-e^{-m})/2$ to flip it. Write $\sigma_i(x)$ for the spin belonging to the $i$th directional chain at $x$. Use the fixed scalar $O(x)=\sum_{i=0}^3\sigma_i(x)$, invariant when a signed permutation of axes permutes the components. The field is bounded, local and centered. This nearest-neighbor spin model is a control for the distributional criterion.

In any coordinate chosen as time, the time-oriented spins undergo independent reversible transitions at each spatial site. The three transverse families have their stationary spatial laws independently redrawn on every time slice. Thus the slices form a stationary reversible Markov chain; past and reflected future are conditionally independent with the same law given the plane. This gives site-reflection positivity. The slice transition projects the transverse families to constants and multiplies a product of $j$ distinct time-oriented spins by $e^{-mj}$. Such spin products span a dense subspace for the independent time-oriented slice spins. The time transfer is positive and its norm on the vacuum complement is exactly $e^{-m}$, attained by a single time-oriented spin. Equivalently the positive-half Hilbert space identifies with slice functions by conditional expectation onto the plane. The model also arises as the local limit of independent periodic chains with these couplings.

At $n>0$ one has $\langle O(0)O(ne_0)\rangle=e^{-mn}$, while for a displacement with two nonzero components the covariance vanishes. With $a=m$, the even unit clock $N=2\lfloor1/(2m)\rfloor$ therefore gives $Z^2=e^{-mN}\to e^{-1}$. Its normalized site-pair reading equals one at every clock, and its even midpoint ratio tends to $\sqrt e$. All normalized moments are bounded by $(4e^{1/2})^k$, which is more than sufficient for H3(b).

Nevertheless **every nonvacuum smeared moment tends to zero**. On expanding the product at $k$ sites, a nonzero term must use at least one directional line twice, since an independent spin with a line appearing only once has zero expectation. Some pair of indices must thus agree in three coordinates. In a fixed compact set the union of these lattice subsets has weighted mesh volume $O_k(a^3)$, even allowing coincidences. The uniform moment bound controls the Schwartz tails separately. The one-point function is zero identically. Consequently weak rotation invariance holds, while a reflected one-point smearing has vanishing norm. The original supremum-norm H4 can distinguish an axial from a nonaxial separation and is not claimed for this example. The example isolates the need to preserve nontriviality when adopting the weaker rotation and compactness tests.

For $a=1/L$ with $L$ even, use an indicator box with times $L,\ldots,2L-1$ and spatial side $L$ for a finite numerical check. Only matching spatial locations contribute to its reflected pairing. Its normalized pairing is $e\,a^8 L^3(\sum_{n=L}^{2L-1}e^{-an})^2$, of order $a^3$. Smooth compact tests satisfy the preceding counting bound directly.
