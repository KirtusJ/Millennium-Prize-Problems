# Exterior localization and electric-flux splitting on the periodic two-site Wilson cube

Director C4 · 23 November 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00) · Submitted for independent review

Consider the isotropic SU(2) Wilson transfer on a fixed periodic $2^3$ spatial lattice with unit time step, Wilson weight $e^{-\beta(1-\operatorname{ReTr}P/2)}$ per plaquette, and each single-link electric convolution normalized to preserve constants. Write $\lambda_0$ for the top transfer eigenvalue and $\lambda_\chi$ for the top eigenvalue on gauge-invariant functions of center character $\chi\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z_2)^3}$. The following conclusions use the full spatial configuration integral.

1. The vacuum energy $-\log\lambda_0$ tends to $E_*=18\operatorname{arsinh}(1)+18\operatorname{arsinh}(\sqrt2)+6\operatorname{arsinh}(\sqrt3)=44.39835697395\ldots$ in this normalization.
2. Outside **any fixed neighborhood** of the eight central flat gauge orbits, the $L^2$ norm of a normalized eigenfunction with eigenvalue tending to $e^{-E_*}$ is at most $C e^{-c\sqrt\beta}$. The constants depend on the neighborhood.
3. For every nontrivial $\chi$, and all sufficiently large $\beta$, there are $C>c>0$ such that
$$ e^{-C\sqrt\beta}\ \le\ \log(\lambda_0/\lambda_\chi)\ \le\ e^{-c\sqrt\beta}. $$
The two exponent constants can differ. The first excitation allowing all center sectors consequently has an exponential **upper** bound. The first excited level *within* the neutral sector remains unresolved. Fixed-distance localization does not supply tightness after rescaling distances by $\beta^{1/3}$.

## Transfer and local norm bound

Let $X=SU(2)^{24}$ have product Haar and the product Riemannian distance $d$ with orthonormal infinitesimal generators $i\sigma_a/2$. Let $F(U)$ collect the 24 four-real-component quaternion differences $P_{ij}(x)-1$ and $S=|F|^2/2$. Thus $S$ is precisely the spatial plaquette action before multiplication by $\beta$. Write $s=\beta^{-1/2}$, $M=e^{-\beta S/2}$ and $T=M K M$, where $K$ is the product of 24 normalized electric convolutions. The integral kernel is strictly positive, continuous and symmetric on compact $X$. The simple positive top eigenfunction is gauge invariant and invariant under all three center flips, since these symmetries commute with $T$. On gauge-invariant functions the temporal-gauge transfer already acts as the physical transfer. This permits estimates in full link coordinates even though the intermediate localizing functions may depend on gauge. [Sint, transfer and gauge projection, equations (3.5)–(3.10)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-lat/9312079)

For each $p\in X$, let $D_p$ be the derivative of $F$ in orthonormal left-translated link coordinates. Define $E(p)=\sum_j\operatorname{arsinh}\sigma_j(D_p)$ using all 72 singular values, including zero. This is continuous on the whole compact space. For any closed set $A\subset X$ we obtain
$$
 \limsup_{\beta\to\infty}\|1_A T1_A\|
 \le \max_{p\in A,\,F(p)=0}e^{-E(p)}. \tag{1}
$$
An empty maximum here means zero. The proof below avoids inverting $D_p$ near a change in rank.

Take a partition $\sum_j\eta_j^2=1$ on balls of radius $O(\rho)$, with $\rho=\beta^{-3/8}$, bounded overlap and gradients $O(1/\rho)$. Such partitions follow by normalizing smooth bumps on a maximal separated net on this fixed compact manifold. The map $x\mapsto(\eta_j(x))_j$ has Lipschitz constant $O(1/\rho)$ by bounded overlap. Replacing a Rayleigh form by the sum of its localized forms costs at most $C\beta^{-1}/\rho^2$: the difference kernel is $T(x,y)\sum_j(\eta_j(x)-\eta_j(y))^2/2$, and $K$ has second distance moment $O(s^2)$ in either variable. Indeed $1-\operatorname{ReTr}(U)/2$ is comparable to squared geodesic distance to the identity on SU(2), and the one-link normalizing integral is of order $s^3$. Schur's test applies also after compression. The growing number of balls does not multiply this error.

On a ball centered at $p$, use normal coordinates $z$. After unitary flattening of Haar measure, $K$ is bounded above pointwise on the ball by $(1+O(\rho^2+s^2))$ times normalized Euclidean Gaussian convolution of variance $4s^2/(1-C\rho^2)$ per coordinate. To verify this, the metric differs from identity by $O(\rho^2)$ on a fixed multiple of the ball containing the short geodesics, and $1-\cos(r/2)=r^2/8+O(r^4)$ per link. The two square-root volume factors are their common leading constant times $1+O(\rho^2)$. The one-link normalizing integral is its leading Haar density times $(8\pi s^2)^{3/2}(1+O(s^2))$. Translation invariance makes the constants uniform in $p$.

Write $F(p\exp z)=b+Dz+O(\rho^2)$ in a consistently translated orthonormal frame. For $\epsilon=\beta^{-1/4}$,
$$
 |F|^2\ge(1-\epsilon)|b+Dz|^2-C\rho^4/\epsilon .
$$
Here $\beta\rho^4/\epsilon=\beta^{-1/4}$, so both magnetic end factors together cost at most $e^{O(\beta^{-1/4})}$ relative to the affine model. The bound holds even if $p$ is nonflat. Taking absolute values of trial functions, extending them by zero, and enlarging to the entire tangent space bounds the localized norm by the affine Gaussian transfer norm times $1+O(\beta^{-1/4})$.

In that model the component of $b$ in the range of $D$ is removed by translation and its orthogonal component only decreases the norm. Singular-value decomposition separates the remaining factors. After scaling $z$ by $s$, a singular value $\sigma$ at the unperturbed coefficients gives kernel
$$
 (8\pi)^{-1/2}\exp[-(u-v)^2/8-\sigma^2(u^2+v^2)/4].
$$
For $\sigma>0$, substituting its positive Gaussian eigenfunction gives top eigenvalue $\exp[-\operatorname{arsinh}\sigma]$ (the oscillator frequency obeys $\cosh\omega=1+2\sigma^2$). At zero the free convolution has norm one. Changing the variance and magnetic coefficient as above replaces $\sigma$ by $\sigma\sqrt{(1-\epsilon)/(1-C\rho^2)}$. The singular values are uniformly bounded. Thus the local norm is at most $e^{-E(p)}+O(\beta^{-1/4})$. Independently, if the ball stays in $S\ge b_0>0$, the norm is at most $e^{-\beta b_0}$ by contraction of $K$.

For the compressed operator only balls intersecting $A$ contribute. Along any sequence of worst contributing balls take a convergent subsequence of centers. Its limit lies in $A$. At a nonflat limit the latter exponential bound applies eventually; at a flat limit continuity of $E$ gives (1). This compactness step gives no power rate for (1). Symmetry and positivity of the kernel bound the absolute Rayleigh form by the form on the absolute value, which suffices for its operator norm. Harmonic localization for difference operators has an established literature. The partition, local bounds and oscillator here concern this integral operator with rank-changing flat directions. [Klein and Rosenberger, *Harmonic Approximation of Difference Operators*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.06357)

## The wells and the matching vacuum trial

A flat connection is specified up to gauge by three commuting full-cycle holonomies up to simultaneous conjugation. This follows by parallel transport on the universal cover and its three deck translations. Diagonalize the holonomies and take commuting square roots to obtain uniform link representatives $\exp(i C_j\sigma_3/4)$. The central cases give eight distinct compact orbits $\mathcal W$. On a flat representative the curl singular values of $D_p$ have two transverse entries
$$
 \left(\sum_j\sin^2(\pi n_j/2+qC_j/4)\right)^{1/2}
$$
per $n\in\{0,1\}^3$, $q=0,\pm1$, and a longitudinal zero per block. Indeed linearizing the oriented plaquette at the identity holonomy gives the covariant exterior derivative with factor $1/2$ from the link generators. Its spatial symbol wedges with the shifted forward-difference vector. Gauge transport rotates its input and output isometrically at a flat connection. Zero transverse entries are retained by continuity. At a center the positive multiplicities are $18,18,6$ at $1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3$. On the flat locus $E(p)-E_*$ is exactly the charged Gaussian sum relative to its central value, strictly positive away from $\mathcal W$. Pairing the two signs in any direction gives $f(a-t)+f(a+t)$ for $f(x)=\operatorname{arsinh}\sqrt{x}$ and $t=|\cos(C_j/2)|/2$. Strict concavity makes the sum strictly decreasing in $t$. This step uses the determinant to bound an actual local transfer norm and does not integrate out a soft branch in a central effective Hamiltonian. [Finite-lattice determinant, García Pérez, Snippe and van Baal, §3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-lat/9608015) · [Exact two-site sum and minimizers](../second-box-holonomy/README.md)

The reverse inequality for the vacuum needs one center only. In its tangent coordinates take a normalized compactly supported trial in the variables $z/s$. For each fixed support the exact rescaled quadratic form converges by Taylor expansion and dominated convergence to the Gaussian oscillator form above at $p$, with $b=0$. Smooth compactly supported trials approach its norm, using broad wave packets in the free directions and truncated Gaussian ground states in the hard directions. Take $\beta\to\infty$ first, then the trial supremum. The full-space supremum is the invariant vacuum by strict kernel positivity. Together with (1) for $A=X$ this gives $\lambda_0\to e^{-E_*}$.

## Exponential exclusion at fixed distance

Fix $r>0$ and put $A=\{d(x,\mathcal W)\ge r/2\}$. By (1) and compactness there is $\kappa<e^{-E_*}$ bounding $\|1_AT1_A\|$ for all sufficiently large $\beta$. Let $D(x)=\max(0,d(x,\mathcal W)-r/2)$ and $W=e^{aD/s}$. For $0<a\le1$ the norm of $WTW^{-1}-T$ is at most $C a$, uniformly for small $s$. In both Schur integrals the absolute difference is dominated by $K(x,y)(e^{ad(x,y)/s}-1)$. Its integral is $O(a)$ by the uniform Gaussian distance tail of the normalized convolution. The same bound applies to every two-sided compression.

Choose $a$ small compared with $e^{-E_*}-\kappa$. For a normalized eigenfunction $T\psi=\lambda\psi$ with $\lambda\to e^{-E_*}$, project its weighted equation onto $A$. The exterior block has norm $\le\kappa+Ca<\lambda$, while $W=1$ on the source $A^c$ and the coupling block has norm at most $1+Ca$. The inverse by a norm-convergent geometric series therefore gives $\|1_AW\psi\|\le C_r$. Restricting again to $d(x,\mathcal W)\ge r$ proves the claimed exponential $L^2$ bound. The exponent constant can deteriorate as $r\downarrow0$. [Related weighted eigenfunction estimates of Klein and Rosenberger](https://doi.org/10.3233/ASY-151343)

## Both sides of the center-character splitting

**Upper bound.** Let $\psi_0>0$ denote the normalized vacuum. Around the eight orbits choose disjoint fixed-radius balls in the orbit distance, identified by center flips. Take a real multiplier $f_\chi$ equal to the corresponding character sign on a smaller ball about each orbit, tapered identically to zero outside. It is gauge invariant and transforms by $\chi$. Exponential exclusion gives $\|f_\chi\psi_0\|=1+O(e^{-c/s})$. The exact form defect is
$$
 \lambda_0\|f_\chi\psi_0\|^2-\langle f_\chi\psi_0,Tf_\chi\psi_0\rangle
 =\tfrac12\iint T(x,y)\psi_0(x)\psi_0(y)|f_\chi(x)-f_\chi(y)|^2\,dx\,dy .
$$
For pairs closer than a suitably small fixed distance either the signs agree on the inner neighborhoods or at least one endpoint is outside a still smaller neighborhood. That exterior part is $O(e^{-c/s})$ by Cauchy–Schwarz, contraction and the vacuum bound. For more distant pairs the Schur norm of the truncated kernel is $O(e^{-c/s^2})$ by the same distance tail. The trial gives $0<\lambda_0-\lambda_\chi\le Ce^{-c/s}$, including for a character odd in more than one direction. Strictness follows from simplicity of the vacuum.

**Connecting low-action configurations.** For fixed $B$, let $G=\{\beta S\le B\}$. There are constants $L,B'$ independent of small $s$ such that any two points of $G$ can be connected by a path of length $\le L$ in $\{\beta S\le B'\}$. Here is an explicit construction for this cube.

* Each plaquette has quaternion chord defect $\le\sqrt{2B}s$. Fix the gauge using the ordered coordinate path $p_x$ from the origin to $x$ (take the occupied directions in increasing order). The transformed forward link is the holonomy of $p_x e_i p_y^{-1}$ for $y=x+e_i$. Interchange adjacent steps in distinct directions and cancel inverse pairs on the universal cubical cover. Each interchange costs at most one plaquette defect, including reversed or conjugated plaquettes, by bi-invariance. The word has at most seven letters. Its net displacement is zero for $x_i=0$ and $2e_i$ for $x_i=1$. Thus the link is within $O_B(s)$ of $1$ or of the straight cycle $H_i$, respectively. The commutator of $H_i,H_j$ has defect at most four plaquette defects by filling the $2\times2$ rectangle.
* The gauge fixing is reachable along a uniformly bounded site-rotation path preserving the action. Move each fixed link the remaining $O_B(s)$ by a short geodesic to its seam value $1$ or $H_i$. Plaquette multiplication is Lipschitz in the four link distances, so this interpolation keeps all defects $O_B(s)$.
* Write each $H_i=z_i\exp(v_i)$ in imaginary quaternion coordinates with $z_i=\pm1$ and $|v_i|\le\pi/2$. Replace $v_i$ by $t v_i$, $1\ge t\ge0$. The only possible seam plaquette defects are commutators. For unit quaternions $u,v$, $|uvu^{-1}v^{-1}-1|=2|\operatorname{Im}u\times\operatorname{Im}v|$. Directions stay fixed and the sine magnitudes decrease along the path. The path ends at a central seam with uniformly bounded length. Any two central seams can be joined by a bounded length flat path of commuting seam triples on a common circle.

Take $m=\lceil L'/s\rceil$ with fixed sufficiently large $L'$, and divide such a path into $m$ equal arclength intervals (a constant path is allowed). At each intermediate time integrate over a product-metric ball of radius $c_0s$ about the path. The volume is at least $c_1s^{72}$. Both endpoint actions on every step stay bounded in $\beta$ units and the link distance is $O(s)$. The normalized kernel is at least $c_2s^{-72}$ on every such pair. Multiplication and integration give the **pointwise** lower bound $T^m(x,y)\ge e^{-C/s}$ for all $x,y\in G$, after adjusting constants. The based or physical gauge projection need not be inserted between steps of this full-space kernel.

**Lower bound.** Choose a normalized real top eigenfunction $u$ in a nontrivial physical center character. For large $\beta$, $\lambda_\chi$ is bounded below by a positive constant by the upper splitting estimate. Since $\lambda_\chi\le\|Mu\|^2$, choosing $B$ sufficiently large gives $\int_G|u|^2\ge c>0$. Also $\|u\|_\infty\le C s^{-36}$ by the eigen-equation and the one-step $L^2$ row bound (row integral $\le1$, supremum $O(s^{-72})$). Hence $\int_G|u|\ge c s^{36}$. The positive and negative integrals there are equal by a center flip reversing the character and preserving $G$. Compare the form of $T^m$ on $u$ and $|u|$. Restriction of their nonnegative difference integrand to opposite signs in $G\times G$ gives
$$
 \lambda_0^m-\lambda_\chi^m\ge c s^{72}e^{-C/s}.
$$
The left side is at most $m(\lambda_0-\lambda_\chi)$ since $0<\lambda_\chi\le\lambda_0\le1$. Polynomial factors can be absorbed by increasing $C$. Taking the log ratio preserves the two exponential bounds because both eigenvalues tend to the same positive number. This extends the path and signed-mass argument used by A4 on one site. The tree reduction supplies the missing path for eight spatial sites.
