# Ordered neutral spectrum on the full periodic two-site Wilson cube

Director C4 · 23 November 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00) · Submitted for independent review

For the fixed periodic $2^3$ spatial cube, take the isotropic SU(2) Wilson transfer with probability-normalized single-link electric convolution. Let $E_{j,\chi}$ be the ordered energies in a physical center character, indexed from zero with multiplicity. My result, using the local estimates at the revisions cited below, is
$$
 E_{j,\chi}=E_*+\tfrac12\beta^{-1/3}e_j+o(\beta^{-1/3})\qquad(j\ \text{fixed}).
$$
Here $E_*=44.39835697395\ldots$ is the central hard vacuum energy and $e_j$ are the ordered eigenvalues on simultaneous-rotation-invariant functions of $H=-\Delta/2+2\sum_{i<j}|Y_i\times Y_j|^2$ on $(\mathbb R^3)^3$. In particular the first excited **neutral** gap has coefficient $(e_1-e_0)/2>0$. At every fixed ordered index the differences between characters are exponentially small. The latter statement supplies an upper bound on those differences. The two-sided electric-flux result previously obtained concerns index zero. No continuum limit or volume-uniform estimate is taken.

The missing comparison was on configurations whose distance from a central orbit shrinks more slowly than $\beta^{-1/3}$. Two estimates overlap there. The central product approximation continues to a ball of radius $h^{2/3}$, where $h=\beta^{-1/3}$. Outside half this radius the full-link local oscillator loses at least order $h^{2/3}$ in transfer norm relative to $g_*=e^{-E_*}$. This loss dominates the energy scale $h$ for the actual low levels.

## 1. Normalizations and the inputs being used

Put $s=\beta^{-1/2}=h^{3/2}$. The transfer is $T=MKM$ with $M=e^{-S/(2s^2)}$, $S=\sum_p(1-\operatorname{ReTr}P_p/2)$, and $K$ the product of 24 normalized electric convolutions. We use the based gauge chart in quaternion logarithms. At a central orbit its coordinates split as a constant triple $c$ and a mean-zero transverse field $v$ of dimensions 9 and 42. The fixed chart and its eight disjoint center translates are saturated under site gauge transformations. Norms in the physical Hilbert space use product Haar with invariant functions. The local flat-slice identification includes the square root of the quotient density. On the slice the remaining invariance is simultaneous color conjugation. A constant triple contributes $8|c|^2$ to the sum of squared link logarithms. Norms in these coordinates and the orbit distance are uniformly comparable on a sufficiently small fixed chart, with the appropriate constant factors.

Three earlier estimates are used:

* The based electric kernel on a slice ball of radius $d$ differs in compressed operator norm from variance-$s^2$ Gaussian convolution in quaternion coordinates by $O(d^2+s)$, uniformly for small $d$. The signed-cubic refinement replaces $s$ by $s^2$; the weaker bound suffices. ## 2. Enlarging the central patch

Let $r=h^{2/3}$ and use rectangular cutoffs $|c|,|v|<b r$ with any fixed $b>0$. Put $c=hX$, $v=sz$, $L=br/h=b h^{-1/3}$ and $V(X)=16\sum_{i<j}|X_i\times X_j|^2$. The exact constant-field action is the same quartic in $c$ with each pair multiplied by $\operatorname{sinc}^2|c_i|\operatorname{sinc}^2|c_j|$. The weights are $1+O(r^2)$ uniformly. Therefore
$$
 \big|e^{-S(c)/(2s^2)}-e^{-hV(X)/2}\big|\le C_b r^2 .
$$
This holds at commuting configurations, where both multipliers equal one, and for unbounded $hV(X)$ by $\sup_{u\ge0}u e^{-u/2}<\infty$. A bound obtained by replacing the difference of half-weights by the absolute difference of the actions loses accuracy on this patch.

For the mean-zero modes, the exponent remainder after subtracting $S(c)/s^2+z^TM_0z/2$ is at most $C(r^2|z|^2+s|z|^3)$, by the second input and a bounded third derivative. Both fast exponents dominate a positive multiple of $|z|^2$. The corresponding multiplier error is $O_b(r^2+s)$. Compressing the based electric comparison to the rectangle, inserting the two magnetic weights and extending the hard Gaussian past the cutoff gives
$$
 \|T_{br}-D_h\otimes G\|
 \le C_b(r^2+s)+C_b e^{-k_b r^2/s^2}=o(h).
$$
All comparisons are after unitary dilation and zero extension. The hard oscillator transfer $G$ has isolated simple invariant vacuum with top $g_*$. On the slow ball $|X|<L$, $D_h$ is Gaussian convolution with Fourier multiplier $e^{-h|\xi|^2/16}$ between the two half-weights $e^{-hV/2}$, compressed at both ends. The larger hard cutoff costs only the indicated exponential because the Gaussian half-weight already decays there.

**Compactness on the larger slow ball.** For unit $f$ supported there with bounded form of $(I-D_h)/h$, set $g=e^{-hV/2}f$ and extend by zero. The Fourier kinetic and positive potential forms give $\|f-g\|=O(\sqrt h)$, $\|\sqrt V g\|=O(1)$ and $\int\min(|\xi|^2,h^{-1})|\widehat g|^2\le C$. Convolve $g$ with a smooth radial nonnegative unit-mass bump at radius $\sqrt h$, obtaining $u$. The Fourier bound implies $\|u-g\|=O(\sqrt h)$ and $\|\nabla u\|=O(1)$. For the potential, commute $\sqrt V$ through this convolution: it is the norm of a vector of quadratic polynomials, hence Lipschitz with constant $C(L+1)$ on the enlarged support. Young's inequality bounds the commutator in $L^2$ by $C(L+1)\sqrt h\|g\|=O_b(h^{1/6})$. Thus $\|\sqrt V u\|$ stays bounded. Using $\|\sqrt V\|_\infty\|u-g\|$ on this larger ball would fail.

The full slow form of $H_b=-\Delta/16+V$ is compactly embedded in $L^2$. For example, cycle through the three pairs $(i,i+1)$ modulo three. Freezing $X_i$, the kinetic energy in $X_{i+1}$ and its cross-product potential dominate a positive multiple of $|X_i|$ by the two transverse harmonic oscillators. Summing and reserving half the kinetic energy gives both a uniform gradient bound and a first radial moment. Rellich compactness on balls and the resulting tail bound give compactness on the whole space. Hence every bounded-energy sequence of the original $f$ is precompact. Bounded Fourier and configuration cutoffs in the exact forms give the liminf with coefficients $1/16$ and $V$; compact smooth tests give the opposite min–max inequality. The same arguments work on invariant slow functions. The dilation $Y=2X$ turns $H_b$ into $H/2$. Its lowest state is simple and invariant by positivity of the Schrödinger semigroup. The hard spectral separation consequently gives, at every fixed index in the physical rectangle, $\lambda_{j,br}=g_*[1-he_j/2]+o(h)$. This recovers A4’s result on the enlarged domain.

## 3. The exterior loss on the matching annulus

First work near a single central orbit. At a spatially constant triple the derivative $D_cF$ commutes with spatial translations (between the link and plaquette representations). Thus the constant input sector and each nonzero Walsh block have orthogonal output blocks, also for noncommuting triples. There are 42 singular values near the strictly positive central cluster, all in nonzero blocks. The sum of their inverse-hyperbolic-sine values is a smooth function near zero: take the separated positive spectral cluster of $(D_cF)^*D_cF$ by a fixed contour and use the smooth analytic branch of $\operatorname{arsinh}\sqrt{x}$ on it. The scalar is invariant under simultaneous color rotations; its differential at zero is an invariant linear functional on three color vectors, hence zero. Its value is therefore $E_*+O(|c|^2)$.

There is also at least one singular value of size $k|c|$ or larger in the constant block when $c\ne0$ is sufficiently small. Select an $i$ with maximal $|c_i|$, choose $j\ne i$, and vary $c_j$ in a unit direction perpendicular to $c_i$. In quaternion coordinates the $(i,j)$ commutator plaquette has expansion $1+2c_i\times c_j+O(|c|^3)$ up to the fixed choice of sign, with derivative remainder $O(|c|^2)$. This one output gives the lower bound. Conversion of a constant additive variation to a translated adjoint-angle variation and normalization over eight sites changes its norm by bounded factors, uniformly at small $c$. The largest constant-block singular value is also $O(|c|)$ by smoothness and its vanishing at zero. Since all other singular-value contributions are nonnegative, shrinking the fixed chart gives
$$ E(c)\ge E_*+k|c|. $$
The argument uses the derivative of the **plaquette map**. A nonflat magnetic Hessian includes second derivatives times the plaquette defects and cannot be substituted in this inequality.

For a nonconstant configuration with based coordinates $(c,v)$, $E(p)\ge E(c)-C|v|$. Indeed the derivative map varies Lipschitz-continuously, singular values are Lipschitz in matrix norm, and under a fixed site gauge rotation the derivative’s input and output transform isometrically. The based chart is uniformly Lipschitz on the saturation of a smaller fixed neighborhood (use compactness of the based group and smooth slice inverse). Neighboring points in full-link balls there have coordinate displacements $O(\ell)$.

Consider states in that fixed chart supported where $\max(|c|,|v|)\ge a r$ for fixed $a>0$. Localize once, in **full link space**, to balls of radius $O(\ell)$ from input 3. A contributing center lies in a slightly larger fixed chart. If $|v(p)|>C_0\ell$ with $C_0$ large compared with the ball and inverse-chart constants, the entire ball has $|v|\ge k\ell$. Its magnetic half-weights bound its transfer norm by $e^{-k\ell^2/s^2}=o(1)$. Otherwise $|v(p)|=O(\ell)$ and its intersection with the support implies $|c(p)|\ge ar-O(\ell)$. Thus $E(p)\ge E_*+k_a r-O(\ell)$. The ball norm is then at most $g_*-k'_a r+O(\ell+h^{3/4})$, for some $k'_a>0$ independent of $h$. The sum of localized Rayleigh forms costs another $O(h^{3/4})$. As $h^{3/4}=o(r)$, the annulus norm is bounded by $g_*-k''_a r$ at all sufficiently small $h$. Taking absolute values before localization suffices to bound the norm of each compressed positive-kernel operator. This argument retains the full nonflat affine remainder; using just the strictly positive flat-locus barrier would leave the nonflat balls untreated.

## 4. Combining patches without an extra multiplicity

Use fixed squared partitions of unity on the space of link configurations: one outer cutoff vanishes on smaller fixed neighborhoods of the eight orbits, and eight congruent cutoffs are supported in disjoint based charts. Subdivide each chart into an inner and an annular piece with a sine-cosine cutoff in $\max(|c|,|v|)/r$, shifting from inner to annular between $r/2$ and $r$. These Lipschitz cutoffs can be taken gauge invariant and permuted exactly by the center. The sum of squared gradients is $O(r^{-2})$ on the full space. The transfer localization identity from input 3 therefore costs $O(s^2/r^2)=O(h^{5/3})=o(h)$ in the **global** Rayleigh comparison. The coarser $O(h^{3/4})$ error in §3 is charged only against the annulus deficit; using it as the global error would fail.

Fix a character $\chi$. For any vector in that sector its eight inner localized vectors are center translates of one another with signs $\chi$. Impose orthogonality of the identity-well inner vector to the first $j$ physical eigenvectors of the radius-$r$ rectangle. This costs at most $j$ linear constraints in the full sector, including when a level is degenerate. All eight inner pieces then have Rayleigh form bounded above by $\lambda_{j,r}$ times their squared norms. The annular pieces have upper bound $g_*-k r$ and the fixed exterior has bound $g_*-\eta$. Both lie below $\lambda_{j,r}$ for small $h$. Summing by the squared partition proves $\lambda_{j,\chi}\le \lambda_{j,r}+o(h)$. The reverse inequality follows by lifting the first $j+1$ rectangle eigenvectors to the disjoint translates with signs $\chi$, normalized by $1/\sqrt8$. Off-diagonal well blocks cost $O(e^{-k/s^2})$ by fixed separation and the Wilson tail. The min–max principle and §2 yield the stated ordered energies. This closes the shrinking annulus in the fixed-box argument without requiring an unproved shell hypothesis.

**Character comparison at the same ordered index.** For fixed $j$, the top $j+1$ full eigenvalues in each character now tend to $g_*$. Their normalized eigenvectors have $L^2$ tail $O_j(e^{-k/s})$ outside any fixed union of sufficiently small central neighborhoods by the weighted exterior estimate at `41f9f35`. The estimate extends to unit vectors in the entire span by Cauchy–Schwarz with factor $\sqrt{j+1}$. Restrict to eight disjoint, slightly larger, congruent fixed balls. Removing the tail changes both squared norms and Rayleigh forms by $O_j(e^{-k/s})$ since $T$ is a contraction. Multiply on each retained ball by the character ratio $\chi'(z)/\chi(z)$, where $z$ labels the center translate. This is an isometry on the restricted space that gives physical $\chi'$ vectors, preserves each diagonal block and changes only cross terms of norm $O(e^{-k/s^2})$. The resulting trial space has dimension $j+1$ since the restriction is injective at small $s$. Min–max in both directions proves $|\lambda_{j,\chi}-\lambda_{j,\chi'}|+|E_{j,\chi}-E_{j,\chi'}|\le C_j e^{-c_j\sqrt\beta}$. This comparison makes no identification of individual eigenvectors inside a degenerate multiplet.
