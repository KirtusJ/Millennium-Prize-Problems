## A direct precedent for Z4’s plaquette estimate

Bhattacharjee, Bhattacharya, Lockwood and Romanov study the same four-dimensional fundamental SU(2) Wilson action on periodic tori of side $L=2^k\ge4$. Their Theorem 140 applies to arbitrary sets of unit four-cubes, a stronger choice of event than one specified plaquette. They call a cube bad if any of its 24 plaquettes satisfies $\delta(U_p,I)>\rho$, with $\delta^2=4(1-\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}U_p)$. For $0<\rho\le1$ their joint bound is $q^{|A|}$ with $\log q=\min(0,6(\log\beta+C_0)-\beta\rho^2/16)$. The proof already uses reflection through sites, fourfold face incidence and a lower bound on the partition function. The authors’ later archive describes the weak-coupling bound required for their continuum route as open. [November 2 preprint, Lemma 139, Theorem 140 and Corollary 141](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00341v6) · [November 13 archive, v5.3.0](https://zenodo.org/records/24210329)

Write $Y_p=\beta(1-\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}U_p)$ and set $t=\beta\rho^2/4$. In this common normalization their exponent is $6(\log\beta+C_0)-t/4$. It has a steeper large-$t$ slope, but its upper bound becomes 1 at fixed $t$ as $\beta$ grows. Z4’s pressure **difference** cancels the bulk $\log\beta$ term. The two estimates can be combined for an arbitrary set of cubes. [Z4’s pressure calculation and single-face result](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/blob/2d3e65698f41a398bee4264ccfe685999ecb0a9f/yang-mills/z4/attempt/paper/sec_proof.tex#L168-L205) · [Fröhlich–Israel–Lieb–Simon, §4](https://www.math.caltech.edu/SimonPapers/93.pdf)

### Joint bad-cube bound on the scale of the Wilson action

For a finite set $A$ of unit cubes put $B_x(t)=\{\max_{p\subset x+[0,1]^4}Y_p>t\}$. For every fixed $\beta\ge1$, every dyadic periodic thermodynamic limit $\mu_\beta$, and $t\ge0$,
$$
\mu_\beta\!\left(\bigcap_{x\in A} B_x(t)\right)
\le \min\{1,\exp(60.2-t/8)\}^{|A|}.
$$

**Derivation.** On the dyadic torus write $V=L^4$ and $f_L(b)=V^{-1}\log Z_L(b)$. The event uses all faces of a closed cube and is carried to the corresponding event by each chessboard reflection. Theorem 140’s dissemination inequality therefore bounds the probability for $A$ by the probability that all cubes are bad, raised to $|A|/V$. When all cubes are bad, choosing a bad face in each cube marks at least $V/4$ distinct faces. Thus $\beta S\ge Vt/4$. Reserve half the action in the integral. The all-bad probability is at most $e^{-Vt/8}Z_L(\beta/2)/Z_L(\beta)$. Z4’s pressure bound gives $\limsup_L[f_L(\beta/2)-f_L(\beta)]<60.2$ at fixed $\beta$. The finite intersection defining the strict event is open, so the probability in the limit is bounded by the limit inferior of the finite-volume probabilities. Capping by 1 gives the displayed result. The finite-volume expression retains the pressure difference; the constant 60.2 here uses the stated order of limits.

**Consequence.** Join cubes whose distinct lower corners are at $\ell^\infty$ distance one. If the bad cluster at a specified root has at least $r$ cubes, it contains a connected rooted set of exactly $r$. Each such set can be encoded by a depth-first walk of length $2(r-1)$ with 80 choices per step. A union bound gives $6400^{r-1}q^r$, where $q=\min(1,e^{60.2-t/8})$. At $t=560$, $6400q<0.355$ uniformly in $\beta\ge1$ after the thermodynamic limit. This bounds the size of connected sets of high-action defects. A transfer-spectrum estimate would also have to control correlations through the complementary region. Boundary-conditioned mixing is a further, distinct estimate.

**Checks.** Direct enumeration at $L=4,8$ gave 24 faces per cube and four incident cubes per face (1,536 and 24,576 distinct plaquettes respectively). The existing three-generation PCP calculation for the [single-face and anchored-face geometry](README.md) replayed with four accepted theorems and four rejected adverse controls. The joint-event argument above is a mathematical derivation. The worker replay covers the earlier checks.
