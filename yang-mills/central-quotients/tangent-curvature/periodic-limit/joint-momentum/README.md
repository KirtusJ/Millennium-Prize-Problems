## Which translations enter the spectral measure?

Write D(v) for the path taking the signed steps of v along axes 1, 2, 3 in that order. Use B4's packet with spatial face profile f_R = R²(I−τ_{Re₃})³ A_R, where A_R is the fourfold convolution of the normalized counting measure on {0,…,R−1}³. On a slice take X to be the weighted sum of the rooted √β Im U₁₂ curvatures, and set O = exp(−s|X|²) for fixed s > 0. The centered vector is ψ = (O−μO)Ω. The time-first trunk conjugates the entire vector on a slice by the same group element. This leaves O invariant. Spatial translations act unitarily by state invariance, preserve the reflection inner product and commute with the positive time transfer T. The joint spectral theorem gives, up to the choice of momentum sign,

$$\langle\psi,T^n U(z)\psi\rangle=\int e^{-nE+i z\cdot k}\,d\rho(E,k).$$

At n = 0 the integrand's time factor is one, including on ker T (E = +∞). The joint mass is at most one. The translated observable uses paths based at z, ending at z+v. Conjugate its entire vector back along D(z), within this slice. The resulting paths are D(z) followed by the translate of D(v). B4's common-root comparison uses D(z+v). Sorting the concatenated word by axis requires exactly

$$N(z,v)=|z_2||v_1|+|z_3|(|v_1|+|v_2|)$$

swaps of distinct perpendicular signed steps before same-axis inverse pairs are erased. Each swap changes the transporter by a conjugate of one elementary spatial plaquette or its inverse. Same-axis cancellations are exact for arbitrary link assignments. In particular z parallel to e₁ gives **exact translates at finite coupling** for every face in B4's one-band matrix. For a general z, bi-invariance and the triangle inequality bound the L² norm of the transporter difference by CN(z,v)/√β. This uses the uniform one-face L² bound on 1−Uₚ from the pressure estimate, no assumption of independence among plaquettes. A difference between adjoint transports of the same curvature is bounded by twice its norm times that transporter difference. Cauchy–Schwarz and the uniform L² bound on the scaled curvature therefore give a per-face L¹ error ≤ CN(z,v)/√β. Unitary transport on the time trunk preserves all these norms. The support has |v|₁ ≤ CR and Σ|f_R| ≤ 8R². For |z|₁ ≤ LR the resulting error for X is ≤ C L R⁴/√β in L¹. The map X ↦ exp(−s|X|²) is globally Lipschitz. Here B_R is B4's connected Gaussian covariance, with exp(i z·q) in the single-color spatial Fourier integral. The comparison accommodates any bounded real L. ## The limiting joint measure

In the following formulas W is the finite momentum measure with density

$$\prod_{j=1}^3\left|\int_0^1e^{iup_j}\,du\right|^8 |1-e^{ip_3}|^6\,\frac{p_1^2+p_2^2}{2|p|(2\pi)^3}.$$

Values at a single point can be set by continuity. Define C(t,y) = ∫ exp(−t|p| + i y·p) W(dp), D = 1+2s C(0,0). For n/R → t and z/R → y, B_R(n,z) → B(t,y) = [D²−4s² C(t,y)²]⁻³ᐟ² − D⁻³. In the spatial integral the dominating function is C|p|∏ⱼ(1+|pⱼ|)⁻⁸. The phase has modulus one. The same dominated convergence along every convergent sequence z/R gives uniform convergence on compact sets of y at each fixed t. This includes t = 0. [B4's spatial scaling integral](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/blob/4e29b71788868f507bbc8abc19d931008138b0f7/yang-mills/transfer-operator/periodic-time/packet-bands/proof.md)

Push W forward by p ↦ (|p|,p) and denote that measure by κ. The desired measure is the ordinary additive convolution series

$$
\nu=\sum_{\ell\ge1}b_\ell\,\kappa^{*2\ell},\qquad b_\ell=D^{-3}\frac{(3/2)_\ell}{\ell!}\left(\frac{4s^2}{D^2}\right)^\ell.
$$

Here $(3/2)_\ell$ is the rising factorial. The positive series converges in total mass since 2sC(0,0)/D < 1. Its joint Laplace–Fourier transform is B(t,y) by the binomial expansion. The triangle inequality confines every term to ε ≥ |P|. Equality for two or more nonzero legs requires parallel momenta pointing the same way, a product-Lebesgue-null event. Thus the boundary has zero ν-mass. Every open neighborhood of (ε,P) with ε > |P| has positive κ*κ-mass. Choose a perpendicular w with |w| = √(ε²−|P|²)/2 and take p = P/2+w and q = P/2−w. This gives total momentum P and total energy ε, including when P = 0. By continuity the preimage of the neighborhood contains an open subset of ℝ⁶. The W density is positive almost everywhere, so the preimage has positive measure even when the chosen central momenta lie at zeros of the magnetic multiplier. Since the interior is dense in the closed cone, this also identifies the full support.

## Why scaled momentum does not fold

Testing only translations z = Rj would identify momenta differing by 2π in Rk. We instead test every integer shift z. Put x = exp(−RE) ∈ [0,1]. For g smooth of compact support in ℝ³, take its 2πR-periodization. For every sufficiently large R this agrees with g(P) on the representative cube [−πR,πR)³. Its exact Fourier series on the momentum torus is

$$g(Rk)=\frac{1}{(2\pi R)^3}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^3}\widehat g(z/R)e^{iz\cdot k},\qquad \widehat g(y)=\int g(P)e^{-iy\cdot P}\,dP.\tag{2}$$

The rate of decay of ĝ is faster than any power. The absolute coefficient sum is bounded uniformly for R ≥ 1 and its portion |z|₁ > LR tends to zero uniformly as L → ∞. For the latter bound one may compare the summand to (1+|z|/R)⁻⁶ and sum shells of thickness R. Equation (2) follows by integrating the periodization over a fundamental cube to compute its coefficients. [Sutherland, Fourier series of a periodization, Theorem 17.8](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.785/2018fa/LectureNotes17.pdf)

For each integer m ≥ 0, pair (2) with xᵐ dρ. On |z|₁ ≤ LR, (1) applies at n=mR and the free covariances converge uniformly to B(m,z/R). The resulting Riemann sum tends to (2π)⁻³∫ ĝ(y) B(m,y) dy = ∫ exp(−mε)g(P) dν, after sending L to infinity. The omitted tails are controlled by the uniform mass bounds on both measures. At R⁴/√β → 0 this proves convergence for every xᵐg(P). Finite sums of these tests are dense in the continuous functions vanishing at spatial infinity on [0,1] × ℝ³ (polynomial approximation in x and smooth approximation in P on compact sets). Total masses converge by (1) at n=z=0. This yields tightness and weak convergence of finite measures, uniformly in the choice of periodic state. The limit has no mass at x=0, so no weight is lost at infinite energy when returning to the ε coordinate.

For the endpoint choose for each requested neighborhood a product bump f(x)g(P) between zero and one, supported inside its image and positive at an interior point. Its ν-integral a is strictly positive by the two-leg argument. Approximate f uniformly by a polynomial on [0,1]. Both measures have bounded total mass, so the two approximation errors can be made less than a/8 in sum. Choose L large enough that the weighted Fourier tails for this polynomial, for both measures, cost less than a/8. The finitely many free-lag Riemann-sum errors are less than a/8 for sufficiently large R. The remaining Wilson error is bounded by C times R⁴/√β from (1), the polynomial coefficient sum and the absolute coefficient sum in (2). At R = ⌊cβ¹ᐟ⁸⌋ take c small enough to make it less than a/8. This proves the lower bound with η = a/2. Use the smallest c in a finite list and increase the coupling threshold once. The order of choosing L and c matters because C_{s,L} can grow with L.
