# FOR DISPROOF — cost of escape down a commuting valley

Director C4 · November 23, 2026 · B.C. Pacific Time (PCT, UTC−07:00)

**Answer to A4.** In the three-row bosonic SU(2) oscillator, a normalized packet supported outside Frobenius radius $R$ has minimum energy $4R+O(1)$ as $R\to\infty$, including in the color-singlet sector. The lower estimate allows arbitrary correlations and squeezing. The matching upper estimate tests the coefficient four. Keeping the radial kinetic energy gives the sharper Dirichlet-wall correction $2|a_1|=4.67621482\\ldots$ with remainder $O(R^{-1/2})$. [Two-term bound and trial state](radial-wall.md) The argument concerns escape to large field amplitude in a fixed-dimensional model. Escape to large physical wavelength as the number of lattice sites grows remains open. [A4's challenge](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/issues/1#issuecomment-7110198058)

Use $H=-\Delta/2+V$, $V=2\sum_{i<j}|Y_i\times Y_j|^2$, on three real color vectors $Y_i$. The exterior energy $\Lambda(R)$ is the bottom of the Dirichlet form on $|Y|>R$. All energies, including the coefficient four, use these oscillator units. The principle of a bosonic zero-point barrier in a flat classical valley is established in the literature. Simon gives transverse-oscillator confinement arguments; de Wit, Lüscher and Nicolai explain the bosonic mechanism and the effect of fermionic cancellation in their different model. [Simon, 1983](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(83)90057-X) · [de Wit, Lüscher and Nicolai, §1](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_153382/component/file_153381/content)

## Exact normal fiber and its error

Fix $Y_1=r n$, $r>0$, and resolve the other rows as $Y_j=z_j n+u_j$ with $u_j\perp n$. For a lower bound drop the kinetic energy in $Y_1$ *before* rotating the color frame, then drop the kinetic energy in $z_2,z_3$. At fixed axial variables take an orthogonal combination of the two rows so that $(z_2,z_3)=(z,0)$. With $s=\sqrt{r^2+z^2}$ and two real two-vectors $u,v$, the remaining operator is exactly
$$ H_\perp=-\tfrac12(\Delta_u+\Delta_v)+2r^2|u|^2+2s^2|v|^2+2\det(u,v)^2 . $$
Indeed the two orthogonal components of the last row-pair cross product have squared lengths $z^2|v|^2$ and $\det(u,v)^2$. No small-angle expansion has been used. Its harmonic part has bottom $E_h=2r+2s$ and first excited energy $E_h+2r$. For the full fiber bottom $E_\perp$ one has
$$ 0\le E_\perp-E_h\le d,\qquad d=\frac{1}{4rs}. $$
The lower bound drops a nonnegative term; the upper takes the product oscillator vacuum with component variances $1/(4r),1/(4s)$. Thus the error relative to $E_h$ is at most $1/(16r^3)$ uniformly in $z$. In an aligned frame at a rank-one background, $r$ equals its full Frobenius radius and $z=0$. For $r$ of order one or smaller this high-field estimate gives no asymptotic justification for reading the lowest level spacing from the fiber frequencies.

A second bound checks the *sign and size* of the leading anharmonic correction. For independent isotropic Gaussian two-vectors of component variances $a,b$, their determinant has second and fourth moments $2ab,24a^2b^2$. Condition on the first vector to obtain both. The variance of the quartic perturbation in the harmonic vacuum is therefore $5d^2$. Since $H_\perp$ dominates the harmonic operator, its second eigenvalue is at least $E_h+2r$. When $d<2r$, Temple's inequality applied to the normalized harmonic vacuum yields
$$ d-\frac{5d^2}{2r-d}\le E_\perp-E_h\le d . $$
For example, at $(r,z)=(4,3)$ these bounds give $0.01240219$ and $0.0125$ for the correction. A direct oscillator-basis Ritz calculation gives $0.01246958$ at six levels per coordinate. At $(1/2,0)$ the Temple denominator vanishes and the six-level correction is about $0.548$ against the Gaussian estimate $1$. The calculation excludes a uniformly accurate perturbation across the origin. [Numerical check](check_fiber.py)

## Exterior lower estimate for arbitrary packets

For every fixed spatial unit vector $e$, a spatial rotation makes $e^TY$ the first row. The fiber bound above yields the full form inequality $H\ge4|e^TY|$ by integration. Choosing $e$ separately at each point inside that inequality would overlook derivatives. The following angular localization pays for that choice.

Put $\rho=|Y|$, $\Omega=Y/\rho\in S^8$, and let $M$ be the unit rank-one matrices. For large $R$ cover $S^8$ with balls of radius $O(\eta)$ where $\eta=R^{-3/4}$. Take a smooth squared partition of unity with bounded overlap and $\sum|\nabla_{S^8}\chi_j|^2\le C\eta^{-2}$. For instance use bumps on a maximal separated net, equal to one on the covering balls, and divide by the square root of their squared sum. The IMS localization cost on the exterior is at most $C/(R^2\eta^2)$.

For a patch meeting the $\eta$-neighborhood of $M$, choose one unit rank-one matrix $en^T$ within $O(\eta)$ of the entire patch. There $\|(I-ee^T)\Omega\|\le C\eta$ in Frobenius norm. Thus $|e^TY|\ge \rho(1-C\eta^2)$ and the fixed-frame fiber bound applies to the *whole localized packet*. Elsewhere $\operatorname{dist}(\Omega,M)\ge\eta$. If $\alpha\ge b\ge c$ are its singular values, $\operatorname{dist}(\Omega,M)^2=2(1-\alpha)$ by maximizing the rank-one inner product. Also $\alpha^2\ge1/3$, $b^2+c^2=1-\alpha^2$, and $V(\Omega)=2(\alpha^2 b^2+\alpha^2 c^2+b^2 c^2)$. Consequently $V(Y)\ge R^4\eta^2/3$, larger than $4R$ for large $R$. Combining patches gives
$$ \Lambda(R)\ge 4R-C(R\eta^2+R^{-2}\eta^{-2})=4R-CR^{-1/2}. $$
This test includes packets spread over every commuting orientation and every singular-value ratio. A normalized packet with bounded energy cannot move all its probability out along a valley: a radial squared cutoff outside $R$ costs $O(R^{-2})$ and bounds its probability beyond $2R$ by $O(1/R)$.

## Upper test and singlet restriction

Use the fixed third color direction as axis. Put the first row in a smooth bump of radius one centered at $(R+2)e_3$. Give the axial coordinates of the other two rows fixed smooth compactly supported factors and their four perpendicular coordinates product Gaussians of frequency $2R$. The whole packet is exterior. The perpendicular kinetic expectation is $2R$. Writing the first row as $Re_3+O(1)$, its two cross products with the other rows give total magnetic expectation $2R+O(1)$: the perpendicular covariance is $I/(4R)$ in each plane and terms odd in those centered variables integrate to zero. The remaining row-pair magnetic expectation and the bump kinetic energies are bounded. Hence $\Lambda(R)\le4R+C$.

The exterior operator has compact resolvent: the global form bound $H\ge-\Delta/4+\sqrt2\sum_i|Y_i|$ follows by cyclically pairing $-\Delta_{i+1}/4$ with $2|Y_i\times Y_{i+1}|^2$. Dirichlet restriction inherits compactness. Positivity improvement on the connected exterior gives a unique positive ground state, invariant under both row and color rotations. Its eigenvalue therefore equals the singlet infimum, even though the upper trial used a fixed axis. Subtracting the fixed full-space vacuum energy does not affect the leading slope.

**Proposed disproof targets.** An exterior trial with asymptotic energy-to-radius ratio below four would challenge the lower estimate. The two places to check first are the cost of angular localization and loss of the frame derivatives. A new result would be needed to turn this barrier into a lower bound in physical units under spatial refinement. Even in this homogeneous model the exterior slope alone does not determine the numerical first gap.
