# Testing the inverse bounds

## An interacting quantum dimer

Take $H=-3Z_1Z_2-2(X_1+X_2)$ and observe $Z_1,Z_2$ on separate sites. In the symmetric Bell basis the ground-state block is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-3&-4\\-4&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The connected cross measure is $(4\delta_2-\delta_8)/5$ and each diagonal mass is one. Thus $C(0)=3/5$ and $S(t)=(4\sin(2t)-\sin(8t))/5$. The cancellation of the linear term matters for short times. From $\sin(4u)=4\sin u\cos u\cos(2u)$ and $|1-\cos u\cos(2u)|\le5u^2/2$ one gets $|S(t)|\le16 t^3$. Take $m=7$, $T=1/4$. The static filter error is at most $8T^3=1/8$, since $\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2\theta\,d\theta=\pi/4$. Using $e^{-7/4}<1/5$ in the translated-probe bound certifies at least $11/32$ of the mass in $(0,7)$. With the actual exponential the lower bound is 0.36458. Independent diagonalization gives $4/5$. This example has an interaction and a signed cross measure.

## A continuum control with a strictly positive energy band

In the mass-one, speed-one free real scalar field in $1+1$ dimensions take $f=1_{[-1/2,1/2]}$ and $A=\sin\Phi(f)$, $B$ its spatial translate by eight. These are bounded centered observables. Step smearing is admissible by smooth approximation in the one-particle norm, with supports tending to the same interval. The commutator vanishes for $|t|<7$ by finite propagation of the Klein–Gordon equation. Choose $T=7$, $\ell=10^{-8}$, $m=2$, $s=1$, $h=20$. Only three strictly positive Euclidean observations, at 1, 21 and 41, enter the test.

The field variance $v$ is at most $1/2$, since the equal-time covariance operator is $(2\sqrt{1-\partial_x^2})^{-1}$ and $\|f\|_2=1$. For a center separation $r$ and Euclidean lag $\tau$, the field cross covariance $c(r,\tau)$ is the convolution with $f$ at both ends of $K_0(\sqrt{r^2+\tau^2})/(2\pi)$. The bounded sine covariance is $e^{-v}\sinh(c(r,\tau))$, by the Gaussian characteristic function. Its single-probe variance $V=(1-e^{-2v})/2\le1/2$. These formulas follow equivalently by continuing the positive-energy two-point function. [Massive free-field propagator](https://doi.org/10.1088/1402-4896/adbfd3) · [Integral representation of $K_0$](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.32.E9)

A rational lower certificate avoids relying on quadrature. Use $K_0(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{-z\cosh u}\,du$ restricted to $0\le u\le1/3$. There $\cosh u\le18/17$ by its power series. All separations in the smeared $(8,1)$ reading have Euclidean norm below $91/10$. Because $\pi<4$ and $\int f=1$, the sine reading exceeds $e^{-1/2-(91/10)(18/17)}/24$. The 41-time reading is nonnegative. The 21-time reading costs at most $V e^{-21}$ using the known mass of this calibration field. Hence the triple exceeds the preceding lower bound minus $e^{-21}$.

In the [three-time test](three-readings.md), $q_l\le (20\ell)^2+28\ell$, $q_H\le e^{-14}+3e^{-42}$ and $U\le9$. For the last estimate every $b_j\ge\sqrt{50}/7$, so $(b_j+1)/(b_j-1)<200$ and $\log(200)<6$. With $V\le1/2$ and zero commutator error these inputs certify **more than $1.2\times10^{-7}$ of unnormalized single-probe mass in $(10^{-8},2)$**. The code encloses each exponential using rational Taylor sums and a geometric bound on the remaining terms. As a separate numerical check, the three-time reading is $1.82842\times 10^{-5}$. The certified lower bound uses only the more conservative analytic readings above. The calibration uses a free field in two spacetime dimensions. It supplies no numerical reading for four-dimensional Yang–Mills.
