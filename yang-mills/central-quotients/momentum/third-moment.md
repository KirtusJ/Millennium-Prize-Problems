# Why the third-moment matrix has finite spatial range

Use the [finite graph, vacuum and Hamiltonian](README.md). Put \(B_p=[H,F_p]\), a real skew-adjoint differential expression on smooth tests. More precisely its integration-by-parts adjoint on this common domain is \(-B_p\). Its coefficients involve only the four links of \(p\). On a finite graph the smooth ground state and the smooth insertions used here belong to all required power domains. Write \(M_{3,pq}=\langle (F_p-\langle F_p\rangle)\Omega,Q^3(F_q-\langle F_q\rangle)\Omega\rangle\). Ground-state stationarity gives
\[
 M_{3,pq}=\langle B_p\Omega,QB_q\Omega\rangle
   =\tfrac12\langle\Omega,[B_p^*,[H,B_q]]\Omega\rangle .
\]
For the last identity, expand the four terms and use \(H\Omega=E_0\Omega\). The two surviving terms give the sum of the preceding entry and its conjugate. The entries are real because \(H,\Omega,F_p,F_q\) are real. Complex test coefficients are then handled by the Hermitian quadratic form. A4 derived the same two-neighborhood support for fundamental smeared SU(2) plaquettes. Here the local operator estimate covers smooth quotient observations on any of the stated compact groups. [A4’s smeared moment argument](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/tree/e49cee4f36d69be30d041c91e05afe4f7eb49b55/yang-mills/observable-normalization/smeared-plaquettes/moment-bounds) · [A4's third-moment derivation](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/tree/c90393318d82bdd823af53010cae0cc252752619/yang-mills/observable-normalization/hamiltonian-variance/excited-band/third-moment)

Define two faces to be neighbors if they share a link, with each face its own neighbor. The electric part of \([H,B_q]\) is supported on the links of \(q\). A magnetic term can contribute only if its face touches \(q\), and its commutator with \(B_q\) is a multiplication operator. Consequently \(M_{3,pq}=0\) unless \(p,q\) are within two neighbor steps. This proves exact sparsity of this **odd spectral moment**, even when the equal-time correlations have long range.

## Volume-independent diagonal bound

For any edge \(e\), put \(K_e=\int|\nabla_e\Omega|^2\). A4's local trial argument gives \(\kappa K_e\le4\lambda\,\operatorname{osc}(v)\). In detail, replace the wavefunction by \(\phi(y)=(\int\Omega(x,y)^2\,dx)^{1/2}\), constant on that link. It is normalized, its edge kinetic energy vanishes, and the other kinetic energies can only fall by Cauchy–Schwarz. The exterior marginal and exterior potential expectation are unchanged. Only four magnetic terms can change, each by at most \(\lambda\operatorname{osc}(v)\). The variational principle gives the stated bound. It applies at \(\lambda=0\) as well. [A4's removal of one link](https://github.com/KirtusJ/Millennium-Prize-Problems/tree/a751b0bf6d99fc55067963d52075c91af216571a/yang-mills/observable-normalization/hamiltonian-variance)

One can specify a finite diagonal constant without finding individual eigenvalues. On \(G^4\) let \(K_p=-\kappa\sum_{e\in p}\Delta_e\), form \(\tfrac12[B_p,[B_p,K_p]]\), and write this real formally selfadjoint operator as \(-\operatorname{div}(A\nabla)+w\) relative to product Haar. It has order at most two by the differential-operator commutator rule. Subtracting the divergence expression with its symmetric principal coefficient leaves a real formally selfadjoint operator of order at most one, whose first-order part must vanish. Thus \(\alpha=\sup\|A\|\) and \(\omega=\sup|w|\) are finite and depend on the single plaquette, \(f\), the metric and \(\kappa\). Take maxima over the three orientations if necessary. For a magnetic face \(q\) touching \(p\), let \(\eta\) bound in absolute value the multiplier \(\tfrac12[B_p,[B_p,\lambda v(U_{\partial q})]]\). This is a function of at most eight compact link variables with smooth coefficients. Only finitely many oriented incidence types occur, including \(q=p\). There are at most thirteen such \(q\). Integration by parts and the edge energy bound give the usable upper constant
\[
 d_3=\omega+\alpha\,16\lambda\operatorname{osc}(v)/\kappa+13\eta,\qquad
 0\le M_{3,pp}\le d_3.
\]
No dependence on the total number of spatial links enters the suprema. Positivity of the third spectral form yields \(|M_{3,pq}|\le d_3\) by Cauchy–Schwarz.

## Row count and adverse geometry

A face has thirteen neighbors on these tori. The two-step neighborhood has size at most 63. Encode each face by its normal and the integer coordinates of its lower corner. In one neighbor step each coordinate changes by at most one. In two steps all lower corners therefore lie in \([-2,2]^3\) relative to the starting point. Reduction modulo side five identifies no two points in this range. Every path on a torus lifts, step by step, to the infinite graph with the same link incidences, so a smaller period can only identify endpoints. Counting the incidence neighborhoods on side five gives 13 and 63. On sides three and four the two-step counts drop to 51 and 61. These counts cover all three orientations. A4’s independent finite graph check also returned 51, 61 and 63 on these periods. The measured worker and Lean independently obtain the side-three and side-five counts. [Geometric enumeration](geometry.py) · [Formal certificate](Momentum.lean)

Thus the absolute row and column sums of \(M_3\) are bounded by \(c=63d_3\). The elementary Schur estimate, or \(2|u_pu_q|\le|u_p|^2+|u_q|^2\) followed by summation, gives \(M_3\preceq cI\) in coefficient space. Translation-invariant Fourier blocks inherit the same bound for all polarizations.

The magnetic second neighbor step can enter the operator calculation. Take two parallel \(xy\) faces bounding a single cube in \(SU(2)\), with identical transverse link configurations on its two terminal squares, vertical links equal to the identity, and one transverse link per square equal to \(\exp(i\pi\sigma_3/4)\). The horizontal holonomy angles on the two faces can be chosen the same. Each connecting side face has identity holonomy. With \(f=(1+2\cos(2\theta))/3\), \(v=1-\cos\theta\), and the unit-round metric, the two horizontal gradients have only Cartan components in these coordinates. Contracting the mixed Hessian of the sum of the four side potentials with the two gradients gives \(-4 f'(\pi/4)^2=-64/9\). The four opposite edge variations enter the side angles with opposite signs. The two faces share no edge. This tests the support argument at the operator level.
