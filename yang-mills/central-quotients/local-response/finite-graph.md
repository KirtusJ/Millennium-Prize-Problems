# Which Yang–Mills states can a quotient-link history observe?

**Finite Hamiltonian result.** On a connected spatial \(SU(2)\) lattice graph of cycle rank at least two, take positive electric coefficients, product Haar measure, Gauss' law and a real smooth gauge-invariant potential \(V\). At each time observe the \(SO(3)\) links up to vertex gauge. The closed Hilbert space generated by these observations throughout positive time is exactly the gauge-invariant cover space fixed by the central link flips that preserve \(V\). This characterizes the cyclic space generated by the vacuum and all bounded quotient observations.

For a Wilson potential with a nonzero coefficient on each elementary square of an open cubical box, this is the **entire physical cover Hilbert space**. For a periodic spatial three-torus with the same local couplings, it is the sector invariant under the three independent winding-center flips. Both conclusions hold at each fixed finite regulator. The proof below includes the geometry needed to commute the Gauss restriction with the observation analysis.

## Statement and identification of the observable algebra

Let \(\Gamma\) have \(e\) edges and \(v\) vertices, and put \(r=e-v+1\ge2\). Orient one representative of each edge. Work on
\[
 \mathcal H=L^2(SU(2)^e,dU)^{SU(2)^v},\qquad H=K+V,\qquad
 K=-\sum_{\ell=1}^e\kappa_\ell\Delta_\ell,\quad \kappa_\ell>0.
\]
Here the bi-invariant Laplacians have nonpositive spectrum. The operator is the restriction of the selfadjoint smooth elliptic Hamiltonian on the compact product. Its simple positive ground state \(\Omega\) is gauge-invariant by uniqueness. Write \(E_0\) for its energy. A quotient-link observation is any bounded gauge-invariant function of the \(SO(3)\) edge labels. Define \(\mathcal M\) as the smallest closed space containing \(\Omega\) and invariant under these multiplication operators and all \(e^{-s(H-E_0)}\), \(s\ge0\). Products of such operators give the positive-time cylinders in the stationary ground-state history. Thus \(\mathcal M\) is the OS image of the exact history pushforward in this Hamiltonian model. [Transfer convention](transfer.md)

Edge signs form \(\{\pm1\}^e\), with vertex signs acting through the coboundary map. On the cover orbit space the effective group is \(D=\{\pm1\}^e/\delta\{\pm1\}^v\cong(\mathbb Z_2)^r\). Quotienting by it gives the \(SO(3)\) orbit space. Indeed every vertex gauge transformation of the quotient graph lifts vertex by vertex, and any remaining ambiguity in matching the edges is an edge sign. Maximal-tree gauge identifies the cover orbit space with \(SU(2)^r/SO(3)\), where \(SO(3)\) simultaneously rotates the imaginary parts of all \(r\) unit quaternions. Residual chord signs independently multiply these quaternions by \(\pm1\). [Maximal-tree Hamiltonian reduction, D’Andrea et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.109.074501)

Let \(D_V=\{d\in D:V(dx)=V(x)\text{ for every }x\}\), and let \(U_d f(x)=f(dx)\). Then the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal M\) on \(\mathcal H\) is
\[
 P_{\mathcal M}=\frac1{|D_V|}\sum_{d\in D_V}U_d .
\]
When all of \(D\) preserves the potential this specializes to the usual equal-time projection. A potential that breaks a central symmetry allows histories of center-even observables to reach additional cover states.

## A connected, full-measure region where all sheets separate

Remove the commuting \(r\)-tuples from \(SU(2)^r\). They can be specified by a common axis (two parameters) and \(r\) angles, giving dimension at most \(r+2\). At each remaining tuple two imaginary parts are nonparallel, so simultaneous \(SO(3)\) conjugation has trivial stabilizer.

For each nonidentity chord sign \(d\), also remove tuples for which \(dq\) is conjugate to \(q\). A flipped quaternion must have zero scalar part. Its unit imaginary vector is sent to its negative by the conjugating rotation. That rotation must be a half-turn about an axis perpendicular to the vector. After its axis has been chosen, each flipped quaternion lies on an equatorial circle. Each unflipped quaternion lies in the circle subgroup fixed by the rotation. The dimension is again at most \(r+2\). All sets being removed are compact semialgebraic sets, and there are finitely many. Their codimension in the \(3r\)-dimensional product is at least \(2r-2\ge2\).

The complement is open, connected and of full Haar measure. For connectedness, stratify the excluded semialgebraic sets into finitely many smooth strata. A path between two points of the complement can be perturbed relative to its endpoints to be transverse to every stratum. Since the path has dimension one and each stratum has codimension at least two, the perturbed path misses them. Passing to the free \(SO(3)\) orbit space gives a connected smooth manifold \(X\). The effective sign group acts freely on \(X\), so \(X\to X/D\) is a finite covering. The full physical \(L^2\) space is represented on \(X\): integrating out the tree variables leaves product Haar measure on chord variables, and the removed sets have measure zero.

The induced physical measure on \(X\) has a smooth positive local density. In local slices of the free effective vertex-gauge action, the principal symbol of \(K\) restricted to horizontal covectors is positive definite because every \(\kappa_\ell>0\). Hence the physical kinetic operator on \(X\) is elliptic, with smooth local coefficients, and commutes with every deck sign transformation. The global operator and its domain continue to be defined upstairs on the compact group product. The local proof uses smooth functions supported away from the removed strata and introduces no boundary condition there.

## The commutant calculation

Let \(A\) be bounded and commute with all quotient multipliers and the transfer semigroup. On each evenly covered chart, the multipliers act by arbitrary scalar functions of the base on the \(|D|\) sheets. Their commutant consists of measurable matrices of functions of the base. To see this, take matrix entries between two sheets. Each entry commutes with multiplication by every indicator. On a finite-measure chart its action on an indicator is that indicator times its action on the constant one. Simple-function approximation extends this identity to \(L^2\), and boundedness makes the multiplying coefficient essentially bounded. The sheets have identical local measure because the deck group preserves Haar measure. Label the coefficients intrinsically by the transformation from \(x\) to \(dx\). This gives
\[
 (Af)(x)=\sum_{d\in D}a_d(x)f(dx).
\]

Each \(a_d\) is smooth on \(X\). In a smaller chart choose a smooth test equal to one on the \(d\)-sheet over that chart and zero on the other sheets, supported compactly in the larger chart. Its image under \(A\) there equals \(a_d\). The test belongs to every power domain of \(H\), as its invariant lift extends smoothly by zero to the compact group product. Commutation with the selfadjoint semigroup gives commutation with spectral projections and hence preservation of every power domain. Interior elliptic regularity makes its image locally smooth. Uniqueness of the labelled matrix entries lets the smooth representatives agree on overlaps.

Now fix \(x\in X\) and localize \(f\) to the sheet through \(dx\). In \((HA-AH)f=0\), choose \(f(dx)=0\) and prescribe its first derivative at \(dx\) arbitrarily. The kinetic terms involving the second derivative of \(f\) cancel because \(K\) commutes with the deck transformation. Terms multiplying \(f(dx)\) vanish. The remaining principal-symbol pairing of \(da_d(x)\) with the arbitrary pulled-back differential must vanish. Ellipticity gives \(da_d(x)=0\), separately on real and imaginary parts. Connectedness makes \(a_d\) a global constant.

Using a test constant near \(dx\) next gives \(a_d[V(x)-V(dx)]=0\) on \(X\). By density and continuity, a nonzero coefficient implies \(d\in D_V\). Conversely every \(U_d\) with \(d\in D_V\) commutes with the multipliers and the Hamiltonian. This proves that the entire commutant is the constant linear span of those deck transformations.

The space \(\mathcal M\) reduces the semigroup and observation algebra, since the former is selfadjoint and the latter is closed under complex conjugation. Its orthogonal projection lies in this commutant. Each \(d\in D_V\) fixes the unique positive normalized vacuum, and commutes with every generating operation. Thus \(\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal H^{D_V}\). Write \(P_{\mathcal M}=\sum_{d\in D_V}a_dU_d\). Since \(P_{\mathcal M}\Omega=\Omega\), the constants sum to one. The projection consequently fixes each \(D_V\)-invariant vector. This gives the reverse inclusion and the stated projection formula.

## Wilson faces, surviving winding sectors and checks

Let \(V=c-\sum_p\lambda_p t_p\), \(t_p=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr} U_{\partial p}\), and keep the active faces with \(\lambda_p\ne0\). Suppose their mod-two boundaries on the graph are nonzero and distinct, as on ordinary cubic boxes and periodic cubes of side at least three. Each \(t_p\) transforms by its boundary character \(\eta_p\), and is nonzero at the identity configuration. Orthogonality of the distinct finite-group characters applied to \(V(dx)-V(x)\) gives \(D_V=\bigcap_p\ker\eta_p\). Repeated boundary characters can instead be grouped and their combined coefficient functions tested.

Attaching the active faces to the graph gives a complex \(Y\). The condition above says the edge sign cochain must vanish on every active boundary. Dividing by the vertex coboundaries yields \(D_V=H^1(Y;\mathbb F_2)\). Higher-dimensional cells do not affect this group. For an open cubic box with all square faces, it is trivial. For a periodic spatial three-torus it has dimension three. The orthogonal projection there averages eight winding-center transformations. Charged winding sectors are excluded even when all local Wilson coefficients are nonzero.

## Implication for the mass-gap program

At each finite open-box regulator in this Hamiltonian family, using the complete exact quotient history preserves every physical energy, with multiplicity. On a periodic regulator it preserves exactly the indicated neutral sector. This comparison does not furnish a lower bound on the energies or a cutoff-uniform family of observations with nonvanishing overlaps. In particular, density proved using a time derivative and localization in configuration space may become ill-conditioned as the regulator changes. The one-plaquette example computes a spectral amplitude tending to zero explicitly. A continuum construction still needs common physical time, surviving observable norms and uniform spectral estimates on the same trajectory. [One-plaquette amplitude](rotor.md)

For analytic potentials there is a second proof using continuation of eigenfunctions on the connected group product. It covers other finite central quotients and a fixed time mesh. [Compact connected groups](analytic-groups.md)
