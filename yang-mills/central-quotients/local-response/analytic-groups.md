# Finite central quotients for compact connected gauge groups

For a real analytic potential, the finite-graph history theorem extends to **every compact connected Lie gauge group \(G\)** and every finite subgroup \(Z\) of its center. It applies at any graph cycle rank, including one. Only one regular coordinate chart with separated sheets is required. Analytic continuation of each Hamiltonian eigenfunction supplies the missing global step.

Let \(\Gamma\) be finite and connected with \(e\) edges, \(v\) vertices and \(r=e-v+1\). Use the physical space \(\mathcal H=L^2(G^e)^{G^v}\) with product normalized Haar measure. Take \(H=-\sum_\ell\kappa_\ell\Delta_\ell+V\), \(\kappa_\ell>0\), with bi-invariant metrics, and \(V\) a real analytic, real-valued, vertex-gauge-invariant potential. All these coefficients are analytic on \(G^e\). At each time the observation algebra consists of all bounded functions of the \(G/Z\) edge tuple invariant under the vertex gauge group. Work in the stationary ground-state transfer realization.

Edge-center translations modulo vertex-center gauge form the finite abelian group \(D=Z^e/\delta Z^v\cong Z^r\), with \(Z^r\) denoting a Cartesian power of the finite group. Write \(D_V=\{d:V\circ d=V\}\). The positive-time quotient histories span exactly \(\mathcal H^{D_V}\). In fact, for every fixed \(s>0\), allowing observations only on the nonnegative time mesh \(s\mathbb N\) generates the same closed space.

## Proof for analytic potentials

1. **Find a chart.** On a maximal tree remove the tree links by vertex gauge. The cover orbit space becomes \(G^r/\operatorname{Ad}G\) and \(D\) translates the chords by elements of \(Z\). The all-identity tuple and its \(D\)-translates lie on distinct conjugacy orbits, since conjugation fixes central elements. The orbit space of this compact group action is Hausdorff, so there is a neighborhood of the identity orbit disjoint from all its nontrivial translates. The open dense principal orbit type meets this neighborhood. Choose a small connected coordinate ball there with disjoint translates. On this ball the gauge quotient is smooth, its measure has a smooth positive density and the reduced kinetic symbol is positive definite. These assertions follow from a local slice of the principal orbit type and the positive symbol on the group product. Each connected component of a nonempty lift of the ball to the group product is open. For rank zero the deck group is trivial and the orbit space is a point. [Palais, slice theorem](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970335)

2. **Compute on the ball.** Let \(A\) commute with all bounded quotient multipliers and the selfadjoint transfer semigroup. On the ball \(Af(x)=\sum_{d\in D}a_d(x) f(dx)\). The measurable matrix representation follows by commuting with all scalar indicator multipliers on the sheets. Smooth cutoffs on individual sheets, preservation of all power domains of \(H\), and interior elliptic regularity make the \(a_d\) smooth there. Commuting with \(H\) on tests supported on a single sheet with value zero and arbitrary gradient forces the principal-symbol pairing with \(da_d\) to vanish. Therefore \(a_d=c_d\) is constant on this connected ball. Tests constant near the selected point give \(c_d(V-V\circ d)=0\) there. These are local statements and require no global covering of the regular locus. [Detailed local commutant argument](finite-graph.md)

3. **Extend upstairs.** For every physical eigenfunction \(\phi\) of \(H\), the vector \(A\phi\) belongs to the same eigenspace. Both are physical eigenfunctions of the elliptic operator on the full compact group product, because the vertex-gauge subspace reduces it. Analytic elliptic regularity gives globally real analytic representatives. Every translate \(\phi\circ d\) is analytic as well, whether or not that translate is an eigenfunction. On the lifted chart the analytic difference \(A\phi-\sum_dc_d(\phi\circ d)\) vanishes almost everywhere and hence everywhere by continuity. The identity theorem on connected \(G^e\) makes the difference zero globally. The coefficients came from the single operator \(A\) and are the same for every eigenfunction. Compact resolvent gives a complete physical eigenbasis, proving \(A=\sum_dc_d U_d\) on the entire physical Hilbert space. The analytic function \(V-V\circ d\) also vanishes globally if \(c_d\ne0\). Thus the commutant consists precisely of the linear span of \(U_d\) for \(d\in D_V\). [Morrey and Nirenberg, analytic elliptic regularity](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.3160100204)

4. **Identify the measured space.** The ground state is unique, positive and invariant under \(D_V\). The orbit of the vacuum under quotient multipliers and transfers therefore lies in \(\mathcal H^{D_V}\). Its closed span is reducing. The projection onto it is in the computed commutant and fixes the nonzero vacuum, hence its constant coefficients sum to one. It fixes every \(D_V\)-invariant vector. Thus the projection is the group average over \(D_V\). For the assertion about a particular mesh, \(x\mapsto e^{-s(x-E_0)}\) is injective on the spectrum of \(H\). The single bounded selfadjoint transfer at that spacing generates all the spectral projections of \(H\) by Borel functional calculus. A closed invariant space for this transfer reduces it, so the same commutant and cyclic-space proof apply. Mesh history means arbitrarily many observation times on that mesh.

## Application to Wilson potentials

For an irreducible unitary representation \(\rho\), the center acts through a scalar character. On a face \(p\), the normalized trace \(\tau_p\) transforms by the character \(\eta_p\) obtained by evaluating that central character on the oriented product of edge-center variables around \(p\). For \(V=c-\sum_p\lambda_p\operatorname{Re}\tau_p\), assume active nontrivial boundary characters are pairwise different up to inversion. For characters of order greater than two, the \(\eta_p\) Fourier component is \(-\lambda_p\tau_p/2\). For order two it is \(-\lambda_p\operatorname{Re}\tau_p\). Both functions are nonzero at the identity. As a result \(D_V=\bigcap_{p\ \mathrm{active}}\ker\eta_p\). Trivial characters impose no condition. If characters coincide up to inversion, take the nonzero Fourier components of the entire potential first. In general the stabilizer is the intersection of the kernels of the characters with nonzero component. This follows directly by applying the finite Fourier projection to \(V\circ d-V\).

For \(SU(N)\) with any subgroup of its center and fundamental Wilson traces, the defining central character is faithful. For distinct ordinary elementary faces on open boxes or tori of side at least three, the preceding condition on characters holds for a nontrivial center. The kernel test on each active face is precisely that its oriented central product be trivial. Thus \(D_V=H^1(Y;Z)\) for the graph with the active faces attached. For a periodic spatial three-torus with all faces active this is \(Z^3\). On a simply connected filled box it is trivial. If a representation only detects part of the center, use its actual character kernels. A noncyclic center need not be detected by one irreducible representation.

This theorem identifies exact observable sectors and their spectra at fixed finite spatial regulator for the stated Hamiltonian. In particular it does not require an a priori lower bound on the mass. The theorem is qualitative in the length and norm of the histories needed to approximate a state. It supplies no spatial continuum limit or comparison with a separately specified quotient action. The four-dimensional existence and positive mass-gap obligations remain open.

For \(SU(2)\) there is also a quantitative result for one directly measured quotient plaquette, uniform in box size at fixed coupling and time. [Adjoint plaquette weight](adjoint-weight.md)
