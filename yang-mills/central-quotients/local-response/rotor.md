# The full quotient history detects the first excitation of an interacting single plaquette

Consider one continuous-time \(SU(2)\) plaquette in its gauge-invariant class-function sector. Its center quotient observes \(r=|\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}U|\) at each time. For every nonzero fundamental Wilson-potential coefficient, the closed space generated by histories of that observation is the entire cover class-function Hilbert space. At zero coupling it is precisely the even subspace. In particular the lowest accessible excitation changes at zero coupling even though the cover eigenvalues vary continuously.

## Hamiltonian and observations

Write \(t=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}U=\cos\theta\). Haar class measure is \((2/\pi)w(t)\,dt\) with \(w(t)=\sqrt{1-t^2}\) on \((-1,1)\). Fix \(\kappa>0\), put \(p(t)=(1-t^2)w(t)\), and take the selfadjoint class restriction of the smooth group Hamiltonian on \(SU(2)\),
\[
 H_\lambda f=Kf-\lambda t f,\qquad Kf=-\frac{\kappa}{w}(pf')'
             =-\kappa[(1-t^2)f''-3tf'].
\]
This normalization absorbs the electric coefficient of the square. The standard one-plaquette Hamiltonian admits a radial spectral equation. The question here is which eigenmodes the quotient history can observe. [Single-plaquette radial Hamiltonian, D’Andrea et al., Phys. Rev. D 109 (2024), radial gauge-singlet equation](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.109.074501)

The compact connected group has a positivity-improving heat kernel. The bounded smooth potential preserves positivity improvement by the positive Feynman–Kac weight. Compact resolvent and elliptic regularity therefore give a simple, strictly positive smooth ground state \(\Omega\) with energy \(E\). Conjugation invariance and uniqueness put it in the class sector. Smooth class functions have bounded first \(t\)-derivative at the endpoints when applied here to the radial ground state. Indeed their restrictions to a great circle are smooth and even near each pole, so the angular derivative is \(O(\sin\theta)\).

Let \(\mathcal M\) be the smallest closed subspace containing \(\Omega\) and invariant under \(e^{-s(H_\lambda-E)}\) for \(s\ge0\) and under multiplication by bounded functions of \(|t|\). This is the image of the complete quotient-history OS space in the ground-state transfer realization. Products of time-ordered observation multipliers and transfer steps on \(\Omega\) give the finite cylinder vectors. Taking their span and closure gives exactly this invariant space. Bounded Borel functions can equivalently be obtained by strong approximation from continuous observations. A single equal-time observation gives only \(F(|t|)\Omega(t)\).

## Proof of full observability at nonzero coupling

Split the radial line into two sheets \(t=\pm r\), \(0<r<1\), with identical measure on each. Write \(u(r)=\Omega(r)\) and \(v(r)=\Omega(-r)\). The two ground-state equations are
\[
 \kappa(pu')'=-(E+\lambda r)wu,\qquad
 \kappa(pv')'=-(E-\lambda r)wv .
\]
For the weighted Wronskian \(W=p(u'v-v'u)\), subtract the second equation times \(u\) from the first times \(v\). The derivative terms \(pu'v'\) cancel and \(W(1)=0\) because \(p(1)=0\) and both derivatives are bounded. Thus
\[
 W(r)=\frac{2\lambda}{\kappa}\int_r^1 x\,w(x)\,u(x)v(x)\,dx .
\]
For every \(0<r<1\), this expression has the strict sign of \(\lambda\). In particular the vectors \((u,v)\) and \((u',v')\) are linearly independent there.

It remains to produce both directions from admissible observations. The square \(f(t)=t^2\) is an even smooth observation. Both \(\Omega\) and \(f\Omega\) belong to \(\mathcal M\), and \(f\Omega\in D(H_\lambda)\). Taking the strong derivative of its transfer orbit at zero puts \((H_\lambda-E)(f\Omega)\) in \(\mathcal M\). Subtracting the even multiplier \((Kf)\Omega\) gives
\[
 (H_\lambda-E)(f\Omega)-(Kf)\Omega
    =-4\kappa t(1-t^2)\Omega'(t).
\]
On the two sheets this is \(-4\kappa r(1-r^2)(u',v')\). Hence \(\mathcal M\) contains arbitrary bounded scalar multiples, depending on \(r\), of two pointwise independent vectors. A vector orthogonal to the whole space has zero pointwise inner product with each one almost everywhere, by testing against indicators of measurable subsets. Solving the two-by-two linear system makes both components zero. This density argument does not divide by a determinant near an endpoint. It proves \(\mathcal M=L^2_{\rm class}(SU(2))\).

## Accessible gap and spectral weight

At \(\lambda=0\) the vacuum is constant. Even multipliers generate the full even space, and the free transfer preserves parity. The normalized characters \(U_n(t)=\sin((n+1)\theta)/\sin\theta\) have parity \((-1)^n\) and electric energies \(\kappa n(n+2)\). The first nonconstant even mode has energy \(8\kappa\), whereas the cover class gap is \(3\kappa\). For each \(\lambda\ne0\) the quotient history sees the full class gap \(E_1(\lambda)-E_0(\lambda)\). The bounded perturbation has norm \(|\lambda|\), so the min–max principle puts that gap within \(2|\lambda|\) of \(3\kappa\). It tends to \(3\kappa\) as \(\lambda\to0\) through nonzero values. The gap of the history sector at zero itself equals \(8\kappa\).

A fixed observation illustrates the change in spectral weight. Orient the first excited state \(\psi_1\) continuously from \(U_1\). In the character basis \(t U_n=(U_{n+1}+U_{n-1})/2\) with \(U_{-1}=0\). The free eigenvalues are simple and the perturbation is bounded, so ordinary first-order perturbation theory gives, in \(L^2\),
\[
 \Omega=U_0+\frac{\lambda}{6\kappa}U_1+O((\lambda/\kappa)^2),\quad
 \psi_1=U_1+\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}\left(-\frac16 U_0+\frac1{10}U_2\right)
        +O((\lambda/\kappa)^2).
\]
For the center-even adjoint observation \(U_2=4t^2-1\), \(U_2U_1=U_3+U_1\) gives \(\langle\psi_1,U_2\Omega\rangle=4\lambda/(15\kappa)+O((\lambda/\kappa)^2)\). Vacuum centering leaves this amplitude unchanged. Thus the first mode has positive but vanishing weight for this same observation at sufficiently small nonzero coupling. Full history observability holds for every nonzero coupling by the Wronskian argument.

At \(\kappa=1,\lambda=0.001\), character cutoffs 18 and 32 both gave gap \(3.00000011667\) and amplitude \(0.00026666666\). The check also exercises zero and negative coupling. This one-square result identifies a loss of spectral weight that an equal-time projection can conceal. Extending it to a spatial lattice with shared plaquettes requires controlling many coupled sign sectors. Even an exact finite-volume observability result would still require quantitative bounds uniform in regulator, volume and physical time for a continuum conclusion.
